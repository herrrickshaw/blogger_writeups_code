Chloride in fuel ethanol: IS 15464 sets 3 mg/kg for 2G ethanol and no limit for 1G

Skip to article content
Text Size

Top line

  • No limit on chloride in 1G ethanol, made from molasses and grain, which is almost all the ethanol India blends. IS 15464:2022 caps only 2G ethanol, at 3 mg/kg.
  • 570 ppm vs under 3 is the gap in the 2026 E20 dispute: the highest reading in automakers’ internal emails (Rajasthan) against oil-company tests of distillery, terminal and pump samples.
  • 60 litres of tap water (250 mg/L chloride) left in a 20,000-litre E20 tank can breach a 1 mg/kg limit. A 500 mg/kg reading would need 30,000 litres, more than the tank holds.

India’s chloride limit: 3 mg/kg for 2G ethanol, none for the 1G ethanol it blends

What it means: Almost all of India’s blending ethanol is 1G, so the 3 ppm limit that officials cited in August 2026 has no published basis in the standard, and this blog found no amendment, notification or contract clause that applies it.

What to do: BIS should extend the 3 mg/kg chloride limit, with the sulphate and water limits, to 1G ethanol, or publish the instrument that already does.

E20 chloride readings: automakers up to 570 ppm, oil companies 0 to 3 ppm

What it means: The two sides sampled different things, fuel from failed vehicles against ethanol and petrol at distilleries, terminals and pumps, so the numbers do not contradict each other as directly as the headlines suggested.

What to do: Both sides should publish their laboratory reports with sampling points and methods, and test the same pumps and vehicles under one agreed method.

The 2026 dispute: a letter, a withdrawal and two sets of tests in three weeks

  1. Before JulyMercedes-Benz India raises fuel-contamination concerns. SIAM says automakers had raised chloride at the BIS fuel committee for over two years.
  2. 26 JulyAutomakers discuss their findings privately with the petroleum ministry, a Maruti Suzuki executive later tells Reuters.
  3. Late JulySIAM writes to the ministry: up to 500 mg/kg chloride in fuel from vehicle tanks, 350 mg/kg at stations, and moisture above 10,000 mg/kg against a 3,000 limit.
  4. 4 and 5 AugustSIAM withdraws the letter, saying some numbers need authentication and there is “no cause for concern”.
  5. 7 and 8 AugustOil companies publish tests: refinery petrol at or below 1 ppm, ethanol from 80 distilleries under 3 ppm, 160 station reports at 0 to 3 ppm. Two or four outlets are suspended, depending on the statement.
  6. 13 AugustReuters publishes the automakers’ internal emails with state-wise readings of 1.4 to 570 ppm.

What it means: The dispute ended with a withdrawn letter and two sets of numbers, neither published with its sampling method, so the evidence leaves it unresolved.

What to do: Fleet owners and fuel buyers should treat chloride as an open quality risk until a joint test with a published method settles it.

What it means: A few dozen litres of tap water left after cleaning can push E20 past a 1 to 3 mg/kg limit, but readings in the hundreds point to a saltier source, to separated water sampled on its own, or to a measurement problem.

What to do: Rinse ethanol and E20 tanks with low-chloride water, drain and dry them completely, and test the first receipt after cleaning for chloride.

What the rules say about cleaning water
  • IS 10500, drinking water: up to 250 mg/L chloride, or 1,000 mg/L where there is no other source, and at least 0.2 mg/L free chlorine. Safe to drink; not chloride-free.
  • Oil-company depots: ethanol tanks must be water-free and cleaned every two years or as needed, and the first receipt after cleaning is tested. The ministry does not say what water is used.
  • API 650, Annex S (stainless tanks): test water under 50 ppm chloride, standing no more than 21 days, then drained and dried completely.
  • Water UK guidance: strongly chlorinated water is acceptable for disinfection only if it is “well flushed through the system immediately afterwards or pitting can occur”.

Why a few mg/kg of chloride matters

With water and dissolved oxygen, chloride drives pitting and stress corrosion cracking in steel tanks and pipes. One study in a 2021 review found cracking with chloride below 1 mg/L in ethanol.

ASTM’s chloride test method gives the reason for testing plainly: chlorides “may be found in filter plugging deposits and fuel injector deposits”.

Five fixes, in order of effort

  1. Put chloride on every tank truck’s certificate, next to purity, water, density and sulphur.
  2. Specify tank-cleaning water: low-chloride rinse, full draining and drying, a chloride test on the first receipt.
  3. Make sure BIS-recognised laboratories can run the test (ASTM D7319 or EN 15492).
  4. Amend IS 15464 to apply the chloride, sulphate and water limits to 1G ethanol.
  5. Add a chloride limit for finished E20, so fuel can be tested at the pump against a published number.
What this piece could not establish
  • The legal basis of the 3 ppm limit for 1G ethanol: no amendment, notification or contract clause was found.
  • The oil companies’ outlet-level tables at the Centre for High Technology, which could not be opened.
  • The automakers’ sampling method, known only through press coverage.
  • Current editions of foreign standards: the US figure is from a 2018 summary of ASTM D4806; China’s GB 18350 was not checked.
  • What water oil companies and dealers use to clean tanks.

Top line

  • +2.5% was the 40-day drift gap between the best and worst earnings-day reactions, pooled over 8,803 events in 19 markets; the saved per-market notes give no gap or monotonicity score by market, so a country ranking of the gap cannot be built.
  • +0.242 to +0.010 is the range of the illiquidity-drift rank correlation across the markets with a recorded event count, Brazil (109 events) to the US (4,075); it is positive in 8 of the 12 markets the repo tabulates.
  • 6.5% to 7.3% is the best-minus-worst drift reported in two Indian working papers over about three months; one peer-reviewed 2021 paper finds no drift, and the studies measure different things.

What a reader choosing where to look should and should not conclude

  • Reasonable: drift is not a US constant. Studies find it in the UK, India, Japan, China, Korea and Latin America, while the US shows little in large stocks, and this blog’s own US numbers are near zero.
  • Reasonable: if drift lives anywhere it is where trading is costly, and the blog’s link is positive in 8 of 12 markets. The US with true filing dates (313 events) shows +0.102, so even the US result depends on the event date.
  • Not supported: a ranking of countries. Per-market counts run from 53 to 4,075 over about one year, event days are volume spikes outside the US, and no per-market drift gap was saved.
  • Not supported: that any market can be traded on this. Drift sizes of a few per cent over weeks come before trading costs, and this is not investment advice.

What would fix this

  1. This blog’s study owner: write one file with each market’s event count, quintile gap, monotonicity, volume surge and illiquidity link, and have the post’s pooled figures recomputed from it.
  2. This blog’s study owner: replace the volume-spike stand-in with announcement dates from each exchange or regulator, as the US filing-date run did.
  3. This blog’s study owner: extend the price history from about one year to at least five before ranking any country.
  4. NSE and BSE: publish a machine-readable file of board-meeting results dates with the time stamp, so that Indian event dates need not be proxied.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

Method and definitions
  • Events are days when volume exceeded 2.5 times its recent average on a move of more than two standard deviations, on a liquidity-filtered universe (top 40% by dollar volume). Drift is market-adjusted return from day 2; the pooled run used 40 days, the US-only run 60.
  • The illiquidity link is a rank correlation between pre-event illiquidity and drift multiplied by the sign of the surprise. The markets table only lists markets with at least 40 events.
  • The standard error shown is 1 divided by the square root of events minus one, an approximation that treats events as independent.
  • Regional figures are event-weighted means of the market figures and are computed here; the study did not publish them.
  • The page marks a market under 100 events ‘too few to read’. Finland has 108, so it is plotted, but the blog’s earlier post called it too few: that is a conflict in how the cut-off was applied, and its +0.26 rests on 108 events.
  • Papers are labelled by how they were read. ‘Excerpt only’ means the page text came through a search excerpt or a fetch route outside the allowed hosts; no figure from such a read is printed.
What this piece could not establish
  • The per-market quintile drift gap and monotonicity score for any market: the saved notes give them only pooled and for the US.
  • Event counts for Australia, Canada and Germany, the identity of two further markets in the 12, and anything for the seven markets with under 40 events.
  • That the pooled +2.5%, +0.029 and 8,803 events can be rebuilt from per-market rows. The seven markets with counts cover 5,671 events; the pooled +0.029 differs from their weighted +0.036, and the gap cannot be traced.
  • Whether India is among the 19 markets or what its row would show.
  • Country-by-country drift sizes from the cross-country papers (Dou et al., Guo and Holmes, Chen, Hung et al.): only abstracts or excerpts were read, so no cross-country magnitude is given.
  • Any Korean, Japanese, UK or European drift magnitude from a paper read in full on an allowed host.
  • Any direct retail-versus-institutional drift split for China.
  • The 2025 US papers disputing Martineau; they were not read.
  • Whether Kim and Kim (2003) is a Korean study: it is not, so it is not used.

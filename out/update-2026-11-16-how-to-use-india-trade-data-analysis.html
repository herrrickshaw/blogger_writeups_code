Why it is useful

  • Who benefits: students, journalists and policy analysts who need a sourced, dated view of India's trade position without stitching together four agencies' sites.
  • What it replaces: manual downloads from TRADESTAT, RBI and MoSPI. The repo ships the extracts as JSON plus ready-made charts.
  • Evidence from our read: the five-year synthesis file shows imports up 26.58% against exports up 4.68% over FY2021-22 to FY2025-26.
  • It cross-checks itself: a validation file compares TRADESTAT totals with the Commerce Ministry TIA portal and notes a roughly 2% gap in exports.
  • Everything is in US$ million, which makes cross-source comparison simple.

What it is for

The repo records what the data show: CPI and WPI by state, GDP growth with revisions, forex reserves, the rupee, the goods and services balance, and imports and exports by HS chapter, from FY2018-19 to FY2025-26. A five-year synthesis re-cuts all threads to one window and a sample FY2025-26 report standardises units.

It is the data half of a split. The prescriptive recommendations live in india-trade-sector-policy-recommendations. This is economic analysis, not investment or legal advice.

Who it is for

  • Students and researchers studying India's external sector
  • Journalists verifying a trade or reserves figure against a dated source
  • Analysts who want JSON extracts for their own notebooks
Not for.
  • Real-time figures: the snapshot is dated 2026-07-18 and FY2025-26 is provisional
  • Investment, trading or legal decisions
  • Treating the policy commentary as official findings

Quick start: commands and what you should see

Print the five-year import and export totals

python3 -c "import json;d=json.load(open('data/five_year_trade_currency_synthesis_2026-07-18.json'))['trade_totals_usd_million'];print(d['years']);print(d['export']);print(d['import']);print(d['export_growth_5yr_pct'],d['import_growth_5yr_pct'])"

Five fiscal years of exports and imports in US$ million, then 4.68 and 26.58 percent growth.

Sample prompts for your AI assistant

  • Read README.md in this repository and explain in plain English what it contains and how the files relate to each other.
  • Open the main data file and summarise the three most important findings with the exact numbers and the file they come from.
  • List every caveat or data-quality warning the author documents, and tell me which ones affect the figures I plan to quote.
  • Show me how to load one of the JSON files in Python (or Excel) and make a simple table of the top 10 rows by value.
  • Explain how I would update this analysis with newer official data, and what I would need to re-check by hand.

Limits and honest caveats

  • Figures are a snapshot retrieved 2026-07-18; FY2025-26 is provisional on all sources.
  • The cross-validation file itself notes TRADESTAT and the TIA portal differ by about 2% on total exports, probably vintage or revision.
  • We read committed files only and did not re-pull MoSPI, RBI or TRADESTAT data or run the Colab notebook.
  • Policy commentary is the author's interpretation, not an official finding.
  • Economic analysis only, not investment or legal advice. Services trade runs one year behind goods.

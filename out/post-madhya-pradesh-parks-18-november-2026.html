Madhya Pradesh parks 18 Nov 2026: Rs 42,587 crore listed, Rs 70,650 crore announced

Text Size

Top line

  • Rs 28,063 crore is the gap between the Chief Minister’s total of Rs 70,650 crore and the five park figures he gave (Rs 42,587 crore); the jobs gap is 140,000, and no report read explains either.
  • 4.8 times is the investment per job at the bio-pharma park (Rs 72 lakh) against the chemical park (Rs 15 lakh), on the cabinet’s own figures.
  • 30 Nov 2026 is the deadline for state proposals to BHAVYA-Rasayan, which will fund three chemical parks nationally; Kurlasi was cleared by the state 12 days earlier, and no central selection is reported.
KindWhat this piece includes
DecidedOn Wednesday 18 November 2026 the Madhya Pradesh cabinet, meeting at Gandhi Sagar in Mandsaur district, approved five industrial parks, the second phase of a telecom manufacturing zone at Gwalior, a new industrial area at Pandhurna and a battery storage and floating solar project at Omkareshwar.
TargetsInvestment and jobs for each park, as the Chief Minister gave them. They are projections of what private firms might bring, not construction budgets.
OutcomesNone. No plot allotment, tender or investment commitment for any of the five parks was found.
ConflictThree totals are in print for the same meeting (Rs 70,650, 55,650 and 42,450 crore), and one report reads the chemical park as approved under a central scheme that has not yet chosen its parks.

What it means: The bio-pharma park projects 4.8 times the capital per job of the chemical park, so its Rs 18,000 crore carries the highest capital per job among the four parks that give a jobs figure.

What to do: The industries department should state, for each park, whether jobs are direct, indirect or both and the year they are expected, so that the ratios can be compared.

Chemical park: public support is about 10% of the projected Rs 15,000 crore

  1. 24 Jul 2026 (Friday)Union Cabinet approves BHAVYA-Rasayan: Rs 3,030 crore over 2026-27 to 2030-31 for three dedicated chemical parks chosen by challenge (PIB).
  2. 10 Aug 2026 (Monday)Scheme notified in the Gazette of India; each park at least 8 sq km of encumbrance-free land (gazette).
  3. 18 Nov 2026 (Wednesday)Madhya Pradesh cabinet approves a 3,500-acre chemical park at Kurlasi, reported ‘under BHAVYA’ (ANI, Business Standard).
  4. 30 Nov 2026 (Monday)Last date for states to submit proposals to the Department of Chemicals and Petrochemicals, 12 days after the state cabinet (scheme page).

What it means: The cabinet approved a state proposal and not a central selection, because only three parks nationally will be chosen by challenge and state proposals close on 30 November, 12 days after the meeting.

What to do: The Madhya Pradesh industries department should state whether the Kurlasi proposal has been submitted under BHAVYA-Rasayan and what contribution the State has committed.

What it means: The chemical park uses 58.3% of the land for 35.2% of the investment, so it has the lowest investment per hectare, Rs 10.6 crore, among the four parks that give a jobs figure.

What to do: The industries department should publish a site-development cost for each park, so that the public cost of the land and infrastructure can be set against the projected private investment.

What would fix this

  1. Madhya Pradesh industries department: publish the cabinet note of 18 November 2026 with every project behind the Rs 70,650 crore and 3.14 lakh jobs, and say which items are not parks.
  2. Madhya Pradesh industries department: state for each park whether the jobs are direct, indirect or both, and the year they are expected; give areas in hectares with the acre conversion.
  3. Madhya Pradesh industries department: state in each release which BHAVYA scheme a park falls under, and whether a state approval is a proposal or a central selection.
  4. Department of Chemicals and Petrochemicals: publish the list of state proposals received by 30 November 2026 and the marks given on each criterion when the three parks are chosen.
  5. Madhya Pradesh industries department: publish a site-development cost and a funding source for each of the five parks.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

Method and definitions
  • Park figures are as the Chief Minister gave them at the press conference after the cabinet, as carried by Business Standard, ANI, IANS and Swarajya. They are projections of private investment and jobs, not construction budgets or commitments.
  • Sums, differences, per-job and per-hectare ratios and shares are computed here and each is checked by an assertion in the builder. Acres and hectares were converted at 0.40468564 hectares to the acre.
  • BHAVYA-Rasayan terms are from the official release, the gazette notification and the Department of Chemicals and Petrochemicals; the match between that scheme and the Kurlasi park is an inference from the 3,500-acre size and is marked as one.
  • Business Standard, ANI, IANS, Swarajya, Patrika and Raj Express were read as excerpts, several of them the full opening of the article; the Times of India was read as its headline and first paragraph. News sites are secondary, and DesiMachines is a trade blog.
  • Weekdays were checked against the calendar.
What this piece could not establish
  • What makes up the Chief Minister’s Rs 70,650 crore and 3.14 lakh jobs, or the Times of India’s Rs 55,650 crore and 2.7 lakh jobs, beyond the five parks; the cabinet note and the state release were not found.
  • Whether the Ratlam figure is Rs 18,000 crore or Rs 18,100 crore; the state text is reported as 18,000 by a trade blog, which is secondary.
  • Whether the Kurlasi proposal has been submitted under BHAVYA-Rasayan, the State’s own contribution, and the weights of the selection criteria.
  • Any site-development cost or funding source for any park except the Ujjain research park, and any plot allotment, tender or investor commitment.
  • The text of the Raj Express figures of Rs 45,000 and over Rs 50,000 crore beyond their headlines, and the Times of India article beyond its first paragraph.
  • The cost of the Omkareshwar phase II project, and the items behind the Rs 2,700 crore of allocations.

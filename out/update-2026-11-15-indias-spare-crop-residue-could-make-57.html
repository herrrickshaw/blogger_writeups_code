Biochar from India's spare crop residue: 57 million tonnes a year possible

Skip to article content
Text Size

Top line

  • 210 Mt of crop residue is left spare each year after fodder, fuel and other uses, enough for about 57 Mt of biochar counting bagasse at half.
  • 47.7% of Soil Health Card samples in 2024-25 had organic carbon below 0.5%.
  • 2 of 119 crop-state pairs stay in the top ten under all six weightings: Odisha's rice straw and Maharashtra's cotton stalk.

Updated 15 November 2026: rewritten as charts; figures unchanged.

What would fix this

  • Publish the Soil Health Card results as a state and district table, with the share of samples low, medium and high for each nutrient, not only on a dashboard that has to be clicked through state by state. EnviStats’ index is the only complete state table, and it leaves out Telangana.
  • Finalise the biochar methodology under the Carbon Credit Trading Scheme with default organic-carbon values for common Indian residues, so a small kiln operator does not need a laboratory to estimate credits.
  • Let crop-residue money pay for char, not only machines. A per-tonne payment for straw delivered to a registered kiln would reach states with high soil need that the machinery subsidy does not.
  • Run biochar trials on the acid soils of Jharkhand and Chhattisgarh, where this search found no work since 2022 and the soil case is strongest.
What this piece could not establish
  • District surplus. Surplus fractions exist by state for rice, and only nationally for other crops; wheat outside Punjab and Haryana uses an assumed 20%. A district’s actual surplus can be far from its state’s.
  • Sugarcane trash, jute sticks, millets and minor pulses are left out for want of reliable residue ratios or surplus fractions. So is rice husk, most of which already goes to boilers. Including them would raise the totals, chiefly in Uttar Pradesh, Maharashtra and West Bengal.
  • Imputed soil figures. Telangana takes Andhra Pradesh’s soil indices. Chhattisgarh, Assam and Uttarakhand take the all-India N:P:K ratio.
  • Char carbon content beyond PAU’s rice-straw figure is an assumption. Under the methodology it has to be measured.
  • Openness rests on what a web search could find, so states with work published only in regional languages or internal reports will score too low. Fire counts cover only six states.
  • The index weights are a judgement. The sensitivity table shows which conclusions survive changing them.

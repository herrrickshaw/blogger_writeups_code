CORRECTION NOTICE — 4 August 2026

A re-derivation of this analysis against DGCI&S TradeStat on 2 August 2026 found that several HS-8 codes cited below do not exist in India’s tariff schedule, and the import values attached to them are not supported by the data. Specifically: ethylene was cited as 29011100 / $2,800M (the real line is 29012100, $44M); propylene as 29011200 / $1,400M (real: 29012200, $14M); polyethylene as 39021010/30 / $8.5bn (real 3901 lines total ~$2.8bn; 3902 is polypropylene); lecithin as 29239090 / $100M (real: 29232090, ~$19M); polypropylene as 39021100 (real: 39021000, value $1,372M is correct).

Chapter 38 is labelled “Textile Auxiliaries, $620mn” below. Authentic DGCI&S imports for Chapter 38 in FY2025-26 are $8,857M — the figure shown understates the chapter by 93% — and the chapter carries a $5.32bn gross deficit across its 77 deficit-running HS-8 lines ($1.74bn net). Two single lines each exceed the $620mn shown.

The $8.3–11.5bn FY30 savings headline is therefore retracted pending a from-scratch substitution-target exercise against corrected import bases. What would fix this

  • DGCI&S should publish, alongside TradeStat, a concordance of current and retired ITC-HS eight-digit codes with a flag on any code that carries no trade in the current year, so errors like using 29011100 instead of 29012100 for ethylene are caught before analysis, not after publication.
  • The Department of Chemicals and Petrochemicals should publish an annual reference table for key petrochemicals — ethylene, propylene, polyethylene, polypropylene, TPA and para-xylene — giving the correct HS-8 codes, import value and domestic capacity, so substitution targets start from an agreed base.
  • The Department of Chemicals and Petrochemicals should maintain a public quarterly tracker of large petrochemical projects, starting with BPCL's Andhra Pradesh cracker and Bina unit, showing approval, land allotment, environmental-clearance filing and commissioning dates, so a gap like an unfiled clearance after a foundation stone is visible officially.
  • The Department of Chemicals and Petrochemicals should publish site-wise milestones and spend for the three BHAVYA Rasayan Parks, including the date each site is selected, so the ₹3,030 crore commitment can be tracked against its FY28–30 timeline rather than reported only as “site selection ongoing”.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

PM-KUSUM: 24% of grid plants built; agrivoltaics estimates span 5 to 2,129 GW

Skip to article content
Text Size

Top line

  • 2,426 MW of the 10,000 MW sanctioned for grid-connected solar plants under PM-KUSUM Component A was installed by 30 September 2026, 24%; solar pumps reached 91% of their sanction.
  • 5 to 2,129 GW is the spread of agrivoltaic estimates: the MNRE Secretary expects more than 5 GW in 3 to 4 years, a slide says 3 to 14 GW, and a TERI study puts theoretical potential at 1,192 to 2,129 GW.
  • 25 to 30% is how much more the minister says elevated agrivoltaic structures cost than ground-mounted solar; the next phase of PM-KUSUM is to include an agrivoltaic component.

What it means: Farmer-facing pumps scaled while plants that need land, tariffs and grid connection did not, which is the pattern the Centre for Science and Environment describes for Phase 1.

What to do: MNRE should publish the reasons for each state’s shortfall in Component A with the land, tariff or grid cause, before the second phase allocates new capacity.

What it means: Capacity rose by 699 MW between 30 June and 30 September, but a Centre for Science and Environment figure of about 2,400 MW for the end of Phase 1 in March does not fit a 30 June total of 1,727 MW, so the dates attached to the figures need checking.

What to do: MNRE should publish the installed capacity by month and state with the commissioning date, so that the dates of the 1,727 MW, 1,986 MW and 2,426 MW figures can be reconciled.

What it means: A spread of about 700 times between the lowest and highest estimate means the numbers describe different things, with the large ones measuring land that could host panels and the small ones measuring what states are likely to build.

What to do: MNRE should publish a single agrivoltaic potential with its land, crop and tariff assumptions, so that a target in the next phase of PM-KUSUM can be set against it.

What it means: A generation cost that is less than half the average supply cost would make the 25 to 30% structure premium worth paying, but the figure holds only under specific assumptions that the minister did not list.

What to do: MNRE should publish the assumptions behind the Rs 3.20 to 3.30 figure, including tariff, farm yield and structure cost, so that bidders and states can test them.

What would fix this

  1. MNRE: publish Component A installed capacity by month and state with the commissioning date, and reconcile the figures given on different dates.
  2. MNRE: publish the cause of each state’s shortfall (land, tariff or grid) before the next phase allocates capacity.
  3. MNRE and TERI: publish one agrivoltaic potential with stated assumptions.
  4. MNRE: publish the assumptions behind the Rs 3.20 to 3.30 generation cost.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

Method and definitions
  • Component A is decentralised grid-connected solar plants up to 2 MW; B is standalone solar pumps; C is solarisation of grid-connected pumps, by individual pump (IPS) or by feeder (FLS).
  • Installed share is installed divided by sanctioned on the portal date; the yearly additions are summed to check them against the reported total.
  • The 700-fold spread is TERI’s upper figure of 2,129 GW divided by the low end of MNRE’s slide of 3 GW.
What this piece could not establish
  • The reason Component A capacity for March 2026 (about 2,400 MW per CSE) exceeds the 30 June total in the Rajya Sabha reply.
  • Whether the 25 to 30% premium and the Rs 3.20 to 3.30 cost refer to the same plant design.
  • How much agrivoltaic capacity exists today: 37 pilots and a 100 kW plant are reported, with no total.
  • The final outlay of PM-KUSUM 2.0, which the CSE says remains subject to approval.

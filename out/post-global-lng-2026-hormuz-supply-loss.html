Global LNG in 2026: supply forecast down 3%, with Qatar contracting US LNG

Text Size

Top line

  • -3% is the IEA’s forecast for global LNG supply in 2026, the steepest yearly fall on record. Qatar and UAE loadings fell by over 60 bcm from March to October; output elsewhere rose 14% and offset about two-thirds.
  • $25.60/MBtu was the average Asian spot LNG price in Q3 2026, up 95% on a year earlier, against an estimated $12-14/MBtu for oil-indexed LNG.
  • 7 Mtpa is the maximum volume of US LNG in QatarEnergy’s October contracts with Cheniere (up to 4, 22 October) and Woodside (up to 3, 29 October), with deliveries due from 2030 and 2031.

What it means: New US, Canadian and African capacity replaced about two-thirds of the Gulf loss, which left global LNG production around 20 bcm lower from March to October.

What to do: Importers should plan 2026/27 winter purchases on the IEA’s two winter cases, a fall in global LNG supply of around 3% or 7%, rather than on a return to Gulf loadings.

What it means: Pakistan took 98% of its 2025 LNG from Qatar and cut imports most, while India replaced Qatari cargoes with US, Omani and Nigerian supply and imported 4.4% more.

What to do: Importers that depend on one exporter should contract a second, destination-flexible source before the next outage.

QatarEnergy’s October purchases: up to 7 Mtpa of US LNG, delivered from 2030 and 2031

What it means: The contracts place up to 7 Mtpa of Qatar’s portfolio outside the Strait of Hormuz, about 9% of its 77 Mtpa capacity, from 2030, and add no LNG for this winter.

What to do: Asian and European buyers of Qatari LNG should ask QatarEnergy which sources and delivery points will back their contracts during a Gulf outage.

What would fix this

  1. International Energy Agency and member governments: agree in advance the triggers for gas demand reduction and joint action in a Gulf LNG outage.
  2. QatarEnergy: publish monthly loadings and the scope of its force majeure by buyer, so importers can plan winter cargoes.
  3. India’s Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas, through PPAC: publish monthly LNG imports by supplier country and contract type.
  4. European Commission: publish the gas demand reduction that applies if EU storage falls below a stated level in January; the IEA says storage could end the winter at record lows.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

Method
  • Volumes and prices are from the IEA Global Gas Security Review 2026 (2 November 2026) and the IEEJ LNG Review October 2026. Contract terms are from Cheniere’s press release and Woodside’s ASX announcement.
  • Computed here: Qatar’s nine-month fall (60 to 22 Mt = 38 Mt); the three named exporters’ gain (16+7+2 = 25 Mt); the 7 Mtpa total (4+3); 9.1% of Qatar’s capacity; the price ratio of 1.8 to 2.1 (25.6 divided by 14 and by 12); Egypt (+84.6%), Europe (-2.9%) and India (+4.45% from the volumes, reported as 4.4%).
  • Where the IEA gives a threshold (“over 60 bcm”, “more than 40 bcm”), the post keeps the threshold.
What this piece could not establish
  • Source disagreements. The IEA gives the fall in global LNG production from March to October as 6% in one section and 5% in another, both about 20 bcm.
  • China and Ras Laffan. For China, IEEJ shows -5.8% for January to September and the IEA -10% for January to October. Reports date the Ras Laffan damage to 18-19 March (IEA) or to April (one Indian outlet); the post uses the IEA date.
  • Mixed periods. The importer chart combines January-September, April-September and January-October changes, each labelled. Egypt and Europe each rest on one secondary page. For India, PPAC volumes show +4.4% for April-September, while Kpler data reported by the Economic Times show +9% for March-September.
  • Contract volumes. The Cheniere and Woodside volumes are maximums: one million tonnes a year of the Cheniere total depends on a Sabine Pass expansion decision, and Woodside’s volume reaches 3 Mtpa only when Train 3 starts.
  • Dates and prices. Delivery dates are QatarEnergy’s (2030 and 2031) as reported by Reuters; Cheniere says “early 2030s”. Contract prices beyond Cheniere’s Henry Hub formula were not published.
  • Qatar’s other purchases. Reuters reports QatarEnergy buying other non-Gulf LNG as well; no other contract was confirmed here.
  • Current flows. Tracker data on October loadings and the status of the Strait change daily and were not used for any figure.

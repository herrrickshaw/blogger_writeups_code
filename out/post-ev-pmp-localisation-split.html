On 3 September 2026, the Ministry of Heavy Industries issued two notifications — one for electric trucks, one for electric buses — that on their face "tighten" India's localisation rules for EV traction motors. Look at what they actually did and the picture is more interesting: the deadline didn't move forward, it split. Most of the traction-motor build stays due from 1 September 2026 as already scheduled. The one piece the industry actually can't source domestically yet — the rare-earth magnet, and the same problem in the motor controller — got pushed to 1 April 2027. That is not government indecision. It is a forced-localisation mandate running directly into a real, external supply constraint, and finding the only place it can still hold the line.

A scheme built to force manufacturing, not just subsidise purchases

PM E-DRIVE (the Electric Drive Revolution in Innovative Vehicle Enhancement scheme) was approved by the Cabinet on 11 September 2024 with a ₹10,900 crore outlay running from 1 October 2024 to 31 March 2026. It funds demand incentives across the EV fleet — two-wheelers, three-wheelers, buses, ambulances and, from July 2025, trucks — plus 72,300 fast chargers. But a demand subsidy alone doesn't force where the vehicle gets built. That job belongs to a separate, older mechanism bolted onto the scheme: the Phased Manufacturing Programme, which sets out, component by component, what must be domestically manufactured for a vehicle to keep qualifying for the incentive at all. The same PMP mechanism was already in use for xEV parts under the earlier FAME-India Scheme-II; PM E-DRIVE extended the model to e-bus and e-truck traction motors specifically.

Three postponements, then a split instead of a fourth

The localisation deadline for e-bus and e-truck traction motors has already moved twice before this month's notifications, and industry asked for a third move that would have delayed it wholesale.

DateEvent
30 Sep 2025First postponement: original PMP localisation timeline pushed to March 2026
13 Mar 2026Second postponement: manufacturers allowed to keep importing traction motors (with rare-earth magnets) until 31 August 2026, pushing compliance to 1 September 2026
22 Jul 2026SIAM (the industry body) formally asks the Ministry for a third postponement, to 1 April 2027, citing continued rare-earth-magnet supply disruption
3 Sep 2026Ministry responds: not a blanket fourth extension — a split. Base assembly still due from 1 Sep 2026; magnet/shaft fitment and full motor-controller PCB assembly move to 1 Apr 2027

Why: China controls the one input that actually matters

China's export restrictions on heavy rare-earth elements — dysprosium and terbium among them — and on permanent magnets took effect from April 2025 and disrupted global supply chains for the magnets that go into EV traction motors. That is the input Indian component makers cannot yet substitute at scale, which is exactly why SIAM's extension request in July 2026 named rare-earth magnets specifically, not the traction-motor build as a whole. The rest of the traction motor — rotor and stator assembly, bearings, the enclosure, connectors and cables — does not depend on that single Chinese-controlled input, and the government's 3 September notifications reflect precisely that distinction rather than caving on the whole schedule.

Reading the headline correctly. Multiple outlets reported this as the government "tightening" the rules, which is true only for the components that were still on the original schedule — for the magnet- and controller-dependent pieces, this is the third de facto postponement in the programme's history, just narrower and more targeted than the blanket delays of 2025 and March 2026.

The net read

This is not a story about a mandate failing to bind. Unlike a compliance mandate with a genuine feedstock alternative sitting unused — the pattern this blog has flagged elsewhere in Indian industrial policy — the constraint here is a single foreign country's export licensing on a mineral India does not yet refine or magnet-grade domestically at scale. A stick only works if the party it targets can actually comply; where compliance depends on an input controlled outside the country's own regulatory reach, even a government committed to holding a deadline has to build in the one exception that acknowledges that. The parts of the mandate that don't touch China's chokehold were not moved at all.

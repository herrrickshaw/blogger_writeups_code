Growth 7.1% against inflation 6.09%: the numbers before the RBI's 2-4 December meeting

Top line

  • 7.1% is the RBI’s October projection for 2026-27 real growth, raised 0.4 points (the first quarter came in at 7.8%), while its inflation projection is 5.2% and October’s reading is 6.09%.
  • 0.9 points is the gap between projected growth (6.9%) and inflation (6.0%) in October–December, down from 3.9 points in April–June.
  • −0.59 points is the repo rate of 5.50% less October’s inflation, so the policy rate is below inflation; a 25 basis point rise on 2–4 December would still leave it at −0.34.

What it means: The first quarter came in 0.8 points above the RBI’s own August expectation, and the October projection of 7.1% already assumes growth slows to 6.8% by January–March, so the RBI’s path already has a slowdown of one point built in.

What to do: The 30 November release of July–September growth, which the RBI projected at 7.2%, is the first data point to compare with that path before the committee meets.

What it means: After the October rise the repo rate of 5.50% is below October inflation of 6.09% and below the RBI’s own October–March path, and only a year-average inflation of 5.2% puts it above, which fits the committee’s statement that rate cuts are off the table.

What to do: The RBI’s December policy statement should state the real policy rate it is targeting and the inflation measure it is using, so the size of any rise can be read against that target.

Growth data on 30 November, the MPC on 2-4 December and CPI on 14 December frame the call

  2. 2–4 DecThe MPC meets. The repo rate is 5.5%, the standing deposit facility 5.25% and the marginal standing facility 5.75%; the October stance is calibrated tightening, adopted 4–2.

What to do: The December statement is the document to read for the committee’s vote split, its new quarterly projections and whether it changes the 7.1% and 5.2% figures set in October.

What would fix this

  1. Reserve Bank of India: publish the monthly path behind the quarterly inflation projection, so the 5.96% needed for November and December can be checked against the committee’s own numbers.
  2. Reserve Bank of India: state in each policy statement the real policy rate it is aiming at and the inflation measure used, since the same 5.50% repo is −0.59 against October and +0.30 against the year projection.
  3. MoSPI: publish the implicit GDP deflator alongside real and nominal growth in each release, so the gap between a 2.3% deflator and 6.09% consumer inflation can be read without computing it.
  4. Forecasters: state the base year and series of any growth forecast, since the first-quarter outcome and revisions moved to the new 2022-23 base.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

Method and definitions
  • Real growth is the year-on-year change in real GDP at constant prices. CPI inflation is the year-on-year change in the all-India combined index (base 2024=100), the series used in this blog’s October inflation post.
  • Quarterly inflation for April–September is the simple average of the three published monthly readings (3.93% and 4.94%). The RBI’s Q2 projection of 4.9% matches the second figure. A simple average of the four quarters gives about 5.1%, within 0.1 points of the RBI’s 5.2%, which is consistent with rounding and month weights, not an error in either.
  • The 5.96% for November–December is (6.0 × 3 − 6.09) / 2. The implicit deflator is (1.103 / 1.078 − 1) = 2.3%, from the reported nominal and real first-quarter growth.
  • The inflation target is 4% with a tolerance band of 2% either side. Under the RBI Act the target is treated as missed only if average inflation is above 6% (or below 2%) for three consecutive quarters; the RBI’s projections put October–December at 6.0% and January–March at 5.7%.
  • The RBI statement itself (rbi.org.in) could not be opened from this blog’s build environment. Its October figures were read from excerpts of the press release and from secondary reports, and the quarterly projections should be checked against the statement.
What this piece could not establish
  • The RBI’s quarterly tables from the primary document: the quarterly inflation figures (4.9%, 6.0%, 5.7%) and growth figures (7.2%, 6.9%, 6.8%) are as reported by secondary sources quoting the statement. The vote on the rate was reported as unanimous and the stance change as 4–2.
  • The IMF’s inflation forecast for India: outlets map its 5.0% and 4.4% to calendar and fiscal years differently, so none is used here.
  • Moody’s timing and base: reports date its move to 7% to September (from 6.0%) and to November (from 6.5%). Only the 7% level is used.
  • The GDP deflator is a computed approximation from reported rounded growth rates. The 30 November release date is as reported from MoSPI’s schedule; the release calendar itself was not opened.
  • The Reuters poll figure of 7.1% for April–June comes from one report and was not found in MoSPI’s release or other coverage.
  • The December decision. SBI Research and Nomura are forecasts reported by secondary outlets: SBI Research retained a 50 basis point call on 22 October and a later report has it at 25, and the dates of the later SBI report and of Nomura’s were not confirmed. Other forecasters differ.
  • Why October–December inflation is expected to ease: the statement lists drivers (monsoon, energy, food) but this piece did not test them.

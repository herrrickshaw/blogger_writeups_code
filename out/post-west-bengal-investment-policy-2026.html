West Bengal's Investment Promotion Policy, 2026, unveiled on 5 November 2026, caps total assistance at 100% to 120% of plant and machinery cost; this piece sets out what the ceilings and annual limits imply, the fund against the estimated backlog, the state's clocks, and where press accounts differ from the policy booklet.

West Bengal investment policy 2026: 100% to 120% ceilings, Rs 5,000 crore fund

Skip to article content
Text Size

Top line

  • 100% to 120% of plant and machinery cost is the ceiling on total assistance, drawn at no more than 15% to 20% a year, so the full ceiling takes at least 5.5 to 6.7 years.
  • 10 times as much as the Rs 5,000 crore set aside for 2026-27 is the estimated Rs 50,000 crore of earlier incentives, which the policy does not cover.
  • 2 reports read state the 120% ceiling as available more widely than the policy allows; the booklet gives it only to a thrust-sector unit in a priority area.
KindWhat this piece includes
DecidedThe West Bengal Investment Promotion Policy, 2026 applies to eligible investment made on or after 9 May 2026 and was unveiled by Chief Minister Suvendu Adhikari on Thursday 5 November 2026, 180 days later.
RulesA ceiling of 100%, 110% or 120% of plant and machinery cost, an annual limit, up to ten years from production, and a list of reimbursements for SGST, electricity duty, EPF, stamp duty and other costs.
TargetsNone found. No investment, jobs or number-of-units target was printed in the booklet or in the reports.
OutcomesNone. No Certificate of Intent, sanction or payment under the policy was reported.

The 120% ceiling needs both a thrust sector and a priority area

  1. 2 Apr 2025 (Wednesday)West Bengal revokes its earlier state industrial incentive schemes by the Revocation of West Bengal Incentive Schemes and Obligations in the Nature of Grants and Incentives Act, 2025, published in the Kolkata Gazette, Extraordinary, and retroactive to each scheme’s notification (the Act, official copy; Business Standard, 5 November 2026, calls it a retrospective withdrawal).
  2. 9 May 2026 (Saturday)The policy is deemed in force; the new government took office this day (Business Standard).
  3. June 2026The state Budget allocates Rs 5,000 crore for the current year to fund the policy (Business Standard, 12 November 2026).
  4. 4 Nov 2026 (Wednesday)Indian Express previews the policy; its EPF terms match the booklet.
  5. 5 Nov 2026 (Thursday)Chief Minister unveils the policy, calling it ‘a very small beginning’ (Business Standard).
  6. 12 Nov 2026 (Thursday)Business Standard notes that a separate land policy is in the offing and that delivery, especially on land, is the test.

What it means: The 120% ceiling is the top of a ladder that needs both a thrust sector and a priority area, and two of the reports read state it as available more widely.

What to do: The industries department should publish the ceiling for each type of unit in one table with its clause number, and repeat it in each release.

A unit can draw its full ceiling in 5.5 to 6.7 years, inside the 10-year window

What it means: A unit can reach its ceiling in 5.5 to 6.7 years, so the 10-year window is not the limit, and what limits assistance is how much SGST, electricity duty and EPF the unit actually pays.

What to do: The industries department should publish, for each type of unit, a worked example showing the tax and contributions paid and the assistance claimed in each year.

What it means: The fund is ten times smaller than the estimated backlog that the policy leaves out, and no report says whether any of the Rs 5,000 crore will go to old claims.

What to do: The state should publish the size, source and quarterly payments of the Assured Incentive Fund, and either a settlement schedule for the backlog or a statement that it is excluded, with a verified figure.

What it means: The state has bound itself to 105 days of approval and sanction time, with the terms locked for each investor at the Certificate of Intent.

What to do: The industries department should publish, every quarter, the number of Certificates of Intent and Eligibility issued, claims sanctioned and paid, and days taken against the clocks.

What would fix this

  1. West Bengal industries department: publish the Policy itself, the West Bengal Investment Promotion Scheme, 2026 and the list of priority-area sub-divisions with their index scores.
  2. West Bengal finance and industries departments: publish the size, source and quarterly payments of the Assured Incentive Fund, and state what part of the Rs 5,000 crore, if any, is for earlier claims.
  3. West Bengal government: publish a verified figure for the backlog of earlier incentives and a settlement schedule, or state plainly that it is excluded.
  4. West Bengal industries department: report every quarter the Certificates of Intent and Eligibility issued, investment and jobs recorded, claims sanctioned and paid, and days taken against each clock.
  5. West Bengal industries department: state the ceiling for each type of unit, with its clause, in every release, and state whether the 30-day claim clock includes payment to the unit.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

Method and definitions
  • The booklet of the West Bengal Industrial Development Corporation is the source for the rules; it summarises the Policy and says the Policy and the Scheme prevail where they differ. Clause numbers are as the booklet prints them.
  • A ceiling is a cap on total assistance as a share of Eligible FCI; an annual limit is a cap on any one year; neither is an amount the state pays automatically, since the assistance reimburses taxes and contributions the unit has paid.
  • Years to draw the ceiling, rupee examples, break-even loan and rent figures, the ratio of backlog to provision and the value-added multiples are computed here and each is checked by an assertion in the builder. The value-added multiples assume that net SGST is the SGST rate times value added with every input creditable; they are an illustration, not a forecast.
  • KPMG was read as page text; the Khaitan & Co note, the Act (official copy) and the Business Standard, The Hindu, BusinessLine, Economic Times, Indian Express, ETV Bharat and NDTV items were read as excerpts, mostly the opening of the article. News sites are secondary, and the Suvidha note is a consultant’s summary.
  • Weekdays were checked against the calendar.
What this piece could not establish
  • The text of the Policy and of the West Bengal Investment Promotion Scheme, 2026; only the booklet that summarises them was read, and only as extracted text.
  • Any investment, jobs or number-of-units target for the policy; none was printed.
  • The Rs 50,000 crore backlog beyond Business Standard’s estimate: no named document, and the Chief Minister’s statement on it is reported in different terms by Business Standard (the state may look at it when finances improve) and NDTV (gradually begin addressing).
  • Whether any of the Rs 5,000 crore is meant for earlier claims, and the size of the Assured Incentive Fund beyond that provision.
  • The list and number of priority-area sub-divisions, and the Policy’s own exclusion list (the list above is a law firm’s summary).
  • Whether the land the Finance Minister says is available for the next two to three years exists in the quantity stated; it is his statement in an interview.
  • Any Certificate of Intent, sanction or payment under the policy, and whether the 30-day claim clock includes payment after sanction.

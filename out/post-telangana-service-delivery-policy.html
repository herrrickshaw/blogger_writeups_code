Telangana service delivery: no general guarantee law found; industry has TG-iPASS

Skip to article content
Text Size

Top line

  • None found is the result of a search for a Telangana law guaranteeing citizen services within a time limit, as Karnataka has; a 2022 scoping study marks Telangana as having none, and the one statutory time-limit regime found is the TS-iPASS Act of 2014 for industrial clearances.
  • 94.34% of the 12,926 applications on the TG-iPASS dashboard between 1 January 2024 and 5 November 2026 are marked processed, leaving 732 unprocessed; the dashboard does not say how many were within the time limit.
  • 22.2% a year is the dollar growth needed from USD 0.201 trillion in 2026 to the USD 1 trillion target for 2034, against 8.9% a year in the Outlook’s own baseline path to USD 1.21 trillion by 2047.

What to do: Telangana’s General Administration Department should state publicly whether a general guarantee-of-services law exists or is planned and list which citizen services carry a statutory time limit.

What would fix this

  1. Telangana Industries and Commerce Department: add to the TG-iPASS dashboard the count decided inside the statutory time limit, the count deemed approved, and a definition of ‘processed’.
  2. Telangana General Administration Department: state whether a general guarantee-of-services law exists or is planned, and list the citizen services that carry a statutory time limit.
  3. Telangana Planning Department: state in the vision document and the Outlook whether the USD figures are nominal and which exchange rate is used.
  4. Telangana Revenue Department: publish time-to-decision for each Bhu Bharati service alongside the payment and slot counts.
  5. Telangana Electronic Service Delivery Department: publish the service level for each MeeSeva service, the share decided inside it, and the start date of each dashboard counter.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

Method and definitions
  • The dashboard rows were added by this blog: 28 departments, 12,926 received and 12,194 processed, and no department has more processed than received.
  • Implied growth is (end / start) raised to 1 over the years, in dollars, using USD 0.201 trillion for 2026. It is not the real GDP growth the Outlook lists, and the two should not be compared without a deflator and exchange rate.
  • The Act is titled TS-iPASS; the dashboard and district pages use TG-iPASS after the state’s abbreviation change. They are the same system.
  • Karnataka figures in the comparison are from this blog’s companion article on the Sakaala Services Act and carry the same caveats, including that the Sakala counters are undated.
What this piece could not establish
  • That no general guarantee-of-services law exists in Telangana. A Legal Data Hunter query returned only central Gazette notices, and a 2022 scoping study of right-to-public-service laws lists Telangana as ‘N’ in its annexure while also naming Telangana among the states it analyses. Neither is proof; a search of Telangana Gazette and Law Department listings was not done.
  • The text of TS-iPASS sections 12, 19 and 20 (time limits, penalty, right to clearances) and the notified time limits: only the Act index, sections 7 and 13, and a district portal summary were read. The penalty amount is unknown.
  • Whether TG-iPASS applications were processed within time: the dashboard shows received and processed only, with no definition of ‘processed’ and no approvals, rejections or delays.
  • The date of the USD 1 trillion milestone in one report that gives 2037: every Telangana source read (the vision document, the state press release of 9 December 2025, the Socio-Economic Outlook 2026 and Invest Telangana) says 2034, and the report giving 2037 was not found, so 2034 is used.
  • Whether the vision’s dollar figures are nominal or real, and the exchange rate behind USD 201 billion; the Outlook’s file date is 17 March 2026 in search metadata while its address shows August 2026.
  • The MeeSeva dashboard’s dates and definitions: the counters are undated, its category counts do not add to its own total (a gap of 586,716), 32.2% of requests fall in none of the four listed statuses, and the same page shows 27 active services, 38 departments and 9 centres in one block but 394 active services and 5,282 active centres in its October 2026 statistics. Only the October 2026 statistics and the status counts are used, with these caveats. Whether MeeSeva service levels have a statutory basis was not found.
  • Prajavani and Praja Palana outcomes: the Outlook describes them without counts. No other Telangana policy is included because no dated figure was found for it.
  • Any Telangana evaluation: the sources read are the state’s own documents, which state targets and counts, not independent outcomes.

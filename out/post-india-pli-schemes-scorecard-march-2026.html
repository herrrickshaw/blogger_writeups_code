India's PLI schemes: Rs 35,354 crore paid, 18.5% of outlay, jobs at 28% of target

Skip to article content
Text Size

Top line

  • Rs 35,354 crore of the Rs 1.91 lakh crore PLI outlay, 18.5%, had been paid by March 2026, with 58% of the combined scheme period gone; the report says payouts follow production.
  • 76% and 64% of the investment and production targets were met, against 28% of the jobs target: 8.49 lakh jobs of 30.19 lakh.
  • Rs 0 had been paid under the battery and solar module schemes; the battery scheme had built 1.4 GWh of a 50 GWh target and no commercial production.

What it means: Investment of Rs 2.40 lakh crore already exceeds the Rs 1.91 lakh crore outlay, which fits the report’s point that PLI pays only after firms invest and sell above a baseline, so a low payout share does not by itself mean weak uptake.

What to do: NITI Aayog should publish scheme-wise payout against the plan for each year with the reason for each gap, so that the 18.5% can be read against what was due.

What it means: The cumulative 28% of the jobs target conceals a yearly swing, and the report itself warns that a low cumulative figure during implementation cannot be read as failure to reach the final target.

What to do: NITI Aayog should publish jobs by scheme and by year with the definition of a PLI job, so that the swing between FY25 and FY26 can be traced to specific schemes.

What it means: Capacity can be built well ahead of payouts, as in solar modules, while the battery scheme shows the opposite case of capacity that is mostly unbuilt after technology gaps, imported machinery and project delays.

What to do: The ministries running the battery and solar schemes should publish a project-wise table of capacity commissioned against capacity committed, so that the 116 GW and the 1.4 GWh can be matched to named plants.

What would fix this

  1. NITI Aayog and the implementing ministries: publish payout against plan by scheme and year, with the reason for each gap.
  2. Ministry of Heavy Industries and the Ministry of New and Renewable Energy: publish capacity committed and commissioned by named plant for the battery and solar module schemes.
  3. NITI Aayog: define a PLI job and publish employment by scheme and year, so the FY25 and FY26 swing can be explained.
  4. Department of Expenditure: report the sunset date of each scheme next to its unspent outlay.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

Method and definitions
  • PLI pays a firm only after it invests and sells above a baseline, so payout share trails investment and production by design.
  • “Share of scheme period elapsed” is 578 of the 996 combined months in the 14 schemes, as reported.
  • Jobs, investment and production are as reported by participating firms to the implementing ministries; NITI Aayog and IIM Ahmedabad assessed them.
  • Full-tenure targets (Rs 3.15 lakh crore, Rs 35.37 lakh crore, 30.19 lakh jobs) are from press reports of the assessment; the percentages were recomputed here.
What this piece could not establish
  • Outlays of the three schemes at the top of the report’s table (large-scale electronics, bulk drugs, medical devices): the extract read does not print them; their combined outlay is Rs 44,553 crore by subtraction.
  • Whether the reported jobs are additional to what industry would have created anyway; the report says additionality is visible in several sectors, not all.
  • Export figures by scheme, which the assessment covers but this piece does not.
  • Battery and module capacity numbers come from a news report on the assessment, not from the PDF.

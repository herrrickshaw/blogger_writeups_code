Top line

  • 222 of 254 NELP blocks are relinquished or in that process (DGH, 1 April 2026), against 43 of 172 OALP blocks (25%). The tables count blocks, not area, but ONGC’s nomination area is 48,473 sq km in the 2024-25 annual report against 94,358 sq km on the ministry’s block-award page, a fall of 49% that no source labels relinquishment.
  • 431 ha vs 44 ha is Oil India’s planting under the Green Credit Programme against the area of its own drilling and operating sites restored in 2025-26 (32 sites, 440,291 sq m). The planting is on State forest land, not on the company’s own sites.
  • 5 years, 40% is what the August 2025 methodology requires before any green credit is issued: five years of restoration and a canopy density of 40%. The credits cannot be traded except between a holding company and its subsidiaries.

Giving land back: the DGH contract requires restoration, but the fund starts at production

  1. Giving area backThe DGH model revenue-sharing contract for OALP (20 December 2025) lets the contractor relinquish any part of the contract area at any time. Retained area must be whole polygons of a 1 arc-minute grid of the National Data Repository. Relinquishing does not end liabilities incurred before the date.
  2. The restoration dutyOn expiry, termination or relinquishment of any part, the contractor must decommission all equipment and installations and perform all site restoration under the contract and the Rules, and may use the site restoration fund.
  3. What restoration meansReturning a site to its state at the start, or making it fit for its intended after-use, including abandoning wells, removing equipment, structures and debris, establishing compatible contours and drainage, replacing top soil and filling excavations. The 2023 OALP model contract adds re-vegetation and slope stabilisation.
  4. The fundContributions start in the year oil is first produced and collected, by the unit-of-production method. The 2018 guidelines, amended in August 2021, allow a bank guarantee for the first three years after first commercial production, require third-party cost estimates every three years, and release money in phases against utilisation certificates.
  5. If it is not doneThe contractor is liable for damages equal to the Government’s estimate of the cost of restoration. Under the Petroleum and Natural Gas Rules 2025 the Centre can direct remedial work and, if the lessee fails, do it at the lessee’s cost. The national oil companies maintain a fund under the 1999 scheme.

What it means: A relinquished block does not end the operator’s duty to remove installations and restore the site, including replacing top soil and drainage, but the fund begins only with production, so on the contract’s wording a block relinquished without production has no accrued fund.

What to do: DGH should publish, for each relinquished block, whether site restoration was completed and certified, since the contract and rules assign the duty and no completion record was found.

Green Credit guidelines: forest-department land, five years, 40% canopy, no trading

  1. The rulesThe Green Credit Rules 2023 (12 October 2023) allow eight activities: tree plantation, water management, sustainable agriculture, waste management, air pollution reduction, mangrove restoration, Ecomark labels and sustainable buildings. A methodology must be notified for each. Only tree plantation and eco-restoration of degraded forest land is operational. ICFRE is the administrator.
  2. 1. LandDegraded forest land under a State Forest Department, uploaded to the portal by the Divisional Nodal Officer. A compact parcel of at least 5 ha, free of encumbrances. Wildlife sanctuaries, national parks and tiger reserves are excluded. The applicant gets no right, title or lease over the land.
  3. 2. Plan and costA detailed project report, approved by the State Nodal Officer. Planting uses a mixture of indigenous species suited to the site. Restoration can include soil and moisture conservation, fencing, assisted natural regeneration, invasive-species removal and fire prevention. The applicant bears every cost, including administration, verification and maintenance.
  4. 3. ReportingAnnual progress and expenditure reports by 30 April each year on the portal, with photographs, drone video and tree survival. Third-party verification and audits as the Administrator specifies.
  5. 4. The claimAfter at least five years of restoration and a canopy density of at least 40% (a moderately dense forest), the applicant files a claim and pays a verification fee. One green credit is awarded for each new tree older than five years, counted on survival and canopy change. The 2024 methodology’s minimum of 1,100 trees per hectare does not appear in the 2025 paragraphs read.
  6. 5. UseCredits from tree plantation are non-tradable and non-transferable, except between a holding company and its subsidiaries. They can be exchanged once for compensatory afforestation, CSR or other plantation obligations. For compensatory afforestation the user agency must have restored at least 25 ha, in parcels of 5 ha or more. A green credit is a separate instrument from a carbon credit.

What it means: The programme gives credit for restoring degraded forest land that belongs to a State Forest Department, after five years and 40% canopy, so land an oil company relinquishes or reclaims at its own sites does not earn a credit under the current methodology.

What to do: Oil companies should say in their reports which plantation counts toward Green Credits and which is site restoration, because the two sit under different rules and different land.

What would fix this

  1. DGH and the Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas: publish licence area held, area relinquished and area under mining lease by operator each year, with one definition of ‘acreage’ stated.
  2. ONGC and Oil India: report surface land held, released and restored in hectares, and say separately which plantation is under the Green Credit Programme.
  3. MoEFCC and ICFRE: publish a methodology for reclaimed industrial land if it is to earn credits, with a tonnes-of-carbon figure and a soil measure, and a way to count land the company itself restores.
  4. OTBL, ONGC and TERI: publish independently verified before-and-after hydrocarbon levels and hectares re-vegetated for the sites they clean.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

Method and definitions
  • Block counts and the relinquished shares come from the PPAC Ready Reckoner tables of 1 April 2026, which cite DGH. The NELP column is headed ‘relinquished / proposed for relinquishment / termination notice sent / operational issues / PEL awaited’, so it is broader than relinquishment. The OALP and DSF columns are headed ‘relinquished / proposed for relinquishment’. The table is saved with this piece.
  • The acreage measures are quoted as the sources state them. Exploration licences (PEL) and mining leases (PML) are different instruments. Cumulative awards include blocks later relinquished. None of the six figures is a sum of the others.
  • Area under licence and lease is taken from MoPNG Annual Report 2024-25 tables 2.7 to 2.11, which cite DGH, and summed by this blog. OALP licences, PSC-regime licences and nomination licences are added because they are separate instruments; leases are summed the same way. The 2019 figures are DGH’s own, as stated on its National Data Repository page.
  • A hectare is 10,000 sq m, so Oil India’s 440,291 sq m of restored sites is 44.0 ha. The comparison with the planting under the Green Credit Programme is of scale only: different years, different land, different purposes.
  • Green Credit rules are read from the Green Credit Rules 2023, the August 2025 methodology (S.O. 592(E)), the programme FAQ and PIB and Lok Sabha statements. The compensatory-afforestation conditions are from the Ministry’s guidelines of August 2025.
  • OTBL statements are from its own website (home and Oilzapper pages). The figures in the chart have different bases and are not summed.
What this piece could not establish
  • The total area ONGC or Oil India has relinquished. DGH and PPAC tables count blocks; the area appears only for individual cases found in Parliament answers and annual reports.
  • Whether the fall in ONGC’s nomination area (94,358 sq km on the ministry’s block-award page to 48,473 sq km in the 2024-25 annual report) is relinquishment, expiry, conversion of licences into leases, or a difference in date or definition. The block-award page is undated and no table breaks the change down.
  • Larger totals for acreage (5.66 lakh sq km for all companies and 4.3 lakh sq km for ONGC) circulate in other analyses. They were not found in any DGH or ministry table, and the licence and lease areas above sum to about 3.15 lakh sq km on 31 March 2025, so those totals are not used.
  • Whether the restoration obligations in the DGH model contract and the 2025 Rules have been met at any relinquished block, and what is held in the site restoration funds. The funds are not published. Only the model contract text was read, not individual contracts.
  • Surface land. How many hectares ONGC or Oil India actually hold under leases, rights of user and well pads, and how much has been released, was not found in any source read.
  • Whether ONGC takes part in the Green Credit Programme and on how many hectares. Oil India’s figures are in its reports; for ONGC none was found. Indian Oil’s 11,890 ha is a commitment reported in 2024.
  • Why Oil India reports 601 ha reserved in 2024-25 and 431 ha registered in 2025-26, and whether its two statements on when credits arrive (within two years of planting; in 2029) are both current.
  • Any tonnes of carbon dioxide sequestered or any soil-carbon measurement for Green Credit parcels. The methodology counts trees and canopy.
  • OTBL’s microbial enhanced oil recovery and wax-prevention pages, which returned a bot challenge and were not read; the ‘1.5 million tons’ on its site has no date, and no audit of it was found.
  • The ONGC Annual Report 2025-26 acreage pages were not opened. Section figures for ONGC come from the 2022-23 and 2026 reports as described in search excerpts and from the ministry.
  • Which land Oil India’s Green Credit parcels sit on: the reports name Assam and Odisha only, and the statement that they are State forest land is inferred from the programme’s rules.
  • Which of the Oil India or ONGC treatment figures were carried out by OTBL; only the TERI project for Oil India is stated to be TERI work.

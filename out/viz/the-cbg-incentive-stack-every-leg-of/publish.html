Updated 7 November 2026. The CBG blending obligation’s first year, FY2025-26, is reported as met: about 1.05% against the 1% target, per Swarajya, though PNGRB’s own 6 August 2026 sales figures (111.48 of 12,033 million standard cubic metres) work out to about 0.93% by this blog’s arithmetic. The obligation steps up to 3% for FY2026-27. Sources: Swarajya, PNGRB, 6 August 2026 (search summary only).

The fertiliser leg has at least 15 of the 70 circulars

What it means: The post's own count puts at least 15 of its 70 circulars on the fertiliser leg, the deepest trail it traces.

What to do: What to do: check the fertiliser leg's FCO and bulk-sale notices first, since they carry the most circulars.

Administered CBG price is ₹2,110 per MMBtu, against ₹1,478 before

What it means: The update reports the administered price rising from ₹1,478 to ₹2,110 per MMBtu, a figure the correction note says is confirmed by outlets but not by the PIB release.

What to do: What to do: confirm the price against the PIB release before relying on it, since the correction note could not open it.

The blending mandate rises from 1% to 5% of CNG by FY28-29

What it means: The obligation makes demand for CBG mandatory, rising from 1% in FY25-26 toward 5% by FY28-29.

What to do: What to do: recheck the pump-price effect of the 5% step, since the post's ~₹0.59/kg figure predates the new price.

What would fix this

  • The Petroleum Ministry should publish one consolidated, dated register of every central CBG incentive with the amount sanctioned and disbursed under each in the latest year, so that a stack built from some 70 circulars, half dated 2025–26, can be judged on money paid rather than schemes announced.
  • The Petroleum Ministry should publish CBG Blending Obligation compliance by city-gas entity each year — volume blended against the 1% rising to 5% obligation, so that ‘demand assurance by decree’ can be checked against what was actually bought.
  • MNRE should publish central financial assistance sanctioned and released, plant by plant, under the National Bioenergy Programme, so that capital support can be matched to plants that actually commission and produce.
  • NCDC should publish the split of its lending to cooperative CBG and biogas projects between term loans and working capital, so that the finding that the cooperative route is 96.5% working capital in practice is tracked and any shift towards project finance is visible.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

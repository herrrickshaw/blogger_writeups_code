India's food processing PLI scheme has created more jobs than it promised, ahead of its 2026-27 deadline. In the same sector, roughly 30% of the fruit and vegetables India grows spoils before reaching a consumer, because the cold storage to stop that from happening does not exist. Both things are true at once, and this piece prices both.

Food processing in India: PLI beats its job target, cold-chain gap remains

Skip to article content
Text Size

Top line

  • 3.39 lakh jobs by June 2026 under the food-processing PLI scheme, against the scheme's own 2.5 lakh target for 2026.
  • ₹915 crore for PMKSY, the scheme that funds cold chains directly, in the 2026-27 budget, below PLISFPI's ₹1,200 crore.
  • ~30% of India's fruit and vegetable output spoils before it reaches a consumer.

What it means: Processed food's share of agricultural exports rose from 13.7% to 20.4% over the decade, so India now exports more value-added product.

What to do: Track this share each year alongside raw rice exports, which still lead the export table.

What to do: Report these sales next to the ₹92,651 crore annual cold-chain loss so the scheme's growth is judged against the gap it sits on.

PMKSY, the cold-chain scheme, gets less than PLISFPI or PMFME in 2026-27

What it means: The 2026-27 allocation puts PMKSY, the one scheme that funds cold chains directly, below both PLISFPI and PMFME.

What to do: Compare PMKSY's ₹915 crore with the ₹92,651 crore annual loss before accepting that the budget addresses the gap.

Farm-export earnings fell 9.32% in April–October 2023-24, mostly from a wheat export ban

What it means: A single restriction, the wheat export ban, drove most of a 9.32% fall, so export earnings depend on domestic food policy.

What to do: Watch the cereal and rice categories for export restrictions whenever domestic food inflation rises, since they lead the export table.

What would fix this

  • The Ministry of Food Processing Industries should publish PLISFPI progress as a single quarterly snapshot with every metric dated to the same day — approvals, investment, capacity, jobs and incentive disbursed — so that the scheme is not assessed on figures spread between September 2025 and June 2026.
  • NABARD, with the Ministry of Food Processing Industries, should publish the study and methodology behind the ₹92,651 crore annual loss, the 30 per cent spoilage figure and the 3.5 million tonne cold-storage gap, or commission a fresh one, so that the sector's most-cited constraint rests on a traceable primary source.
  • The Ministry of Food Processing Industries should publish, for the 399 cold-chain projects approved under PMKSY, how many are operational and the storage capacity each has added in tonnes, so that progress can be measured directly against the 3.5 million tonne gap.
  • The Ministry of Food Processing Industries should report capacity utilisation for each of the 25 operational Mega Food Parks, and the status of the other 16 approved, so that the 28.57 lakh tonnes of preservation capacity claimed is shown to be in use.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

What this piece could not establish
What this article does not establish. This article's PLISFPI and Mega Food Park figures are compiled from secondary reporting of government data; PIB's original release could not be directly accessed (403 error) and was not independently cross-checked against the primary document. The ₹92,651 crore cold-chain loss figure and the 30% spoilage figure are widely cited but this article could not trace either to a single primary NABARD or MoFPI study with a stated methodology — they are reported here as commonly cited sector figures, not verified calculations. Whether the FY2026-27 budget allocations named above represent an increase or decrease on the prior year is not established here. And whether a wheat- or rice-export-restriction event is currently under consideration is not addressed — the 2023-24 episode is cited as a documented precedent, not a forecast.

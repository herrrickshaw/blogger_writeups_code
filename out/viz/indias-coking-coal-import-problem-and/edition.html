India imports roughly 90% of the coking coal its steel mills need — the single most expensive raw material in the steel-making process, and one the country cannot dig its way out of, because its own coal seams are largely the wrong grade. The government's real answer to that gap, Mission Coking Coal, is a domestic-mining and washery push, not a biomass one: India's actual biomass-pellet mandate exists for a completely different coal stream (thermal coal, in power plants), and the one real, India-specific attempt to put biomass into steel-making — a SAIL pilot at Rourkela — is a small-scale trial, not a deployed substitution programme. This piece keeps those three things separate, because coverage of "biomass replacing coking coal" routinely blurs them.

Top line

  • ~90% of the coking coal India's steel mills need is imported, the costliest raw material in steel-making.
  • 39% fall in the dollar value of coking coal imports from the FY2022-23 peak to FY2025-26, consistent with easing prices.
  • ₹61.85 crore of CAQM compensation imposed on six thermal plants that missed FY2024-25 biomass co-firing targets.
Updated 7 November 2026. Two things have moved since this piece. First, coking-coal imports rose again in 2025-26: about 63.7–66.3 million tonnes, up roughly 12%, out of total coal imports of about 246 million tonnes, while power-sector thermal coal imports fell to a four-year low of about 45 million tonnes (sxcoal, SMM, SteelOrbis, S&P Global). The gap in §1 between falling thermal imports and rising coking-coal imports has therefore widened. No full-year 2025-26 figure for domestic coking-coal output against the 83 million tonne target was found. Second, the 7% co-firing step for 2025-26 described in §3 was replaced by a November 2025 Ministry of Power policy: plants co-fire 5% biomass pellets from 2025-26, and plants in the National Capital Region 5% pellets plus 2% charcoal made from municipal waste, as this blog’s piece on ethanol plants’ coal boilers records; §3 is corrected. No extension of SAIL’s Rourkela biochar trial beyond Blast Furnace 1 was found.
Updated 26 October 2026. The December 2025 CAQM action on co-firing was a set of show-cause notices proposing ₹61.85 crore in compensation, not a final order; CAQM imposed the compensation on 8 April 2026, as this blog’s piece on ethanol plants’ coal boilers records from CAQM’s own release. The text below is corrected.

Updated 15 November 2026: rewritten as charts; figures unchanged.

Coking coal imports fell 39% in dollar value from the FY2022-23 peak

What it means: The decline tracks falling global coal prices rather than India needing less coking coal, since Ministry of Coal volume targets still rise.

What to do: DGCI&S should report coking coal imports by country of origin as well as consignment, so trade-hub figures can be checked.

Australia's US$5,110.92 million outweighs the USA, Singapore and Russia combined

What it means: Australia's supply is concentrated, while Singapore and Switzerland likely overstate their role as mine-of-origin suppliers.

What to do: DGCI&S should publish country-of-origin data so readers can see where Singapore's US$1,430.14 million coking coal actually comes from.

Six thermal plants face ₹61.85 crore compensation for missing biomass co-firing targets

What it means: Six plants in Punjab, Haryana and Uttar Pradesh were penalised for missing FY2024-25 co-firing targets under the Delhi-NCR track.

What to do: The Ministry of Power and CAQM should issue one joint notice stating the co-firing percentage that applied in FY2024-25, since the sources give 3% and 5%.

What would fix this

  • The Ministry of Power and CAQM should issue one joint notice stating the biomass co-firing percentage that applied in FY2024–25 to thermal plants within 300 km of Delhi, so plants penalised a combined ₹61.85 crore are judged against a single published figure rather than a 3% and a 5% schedule for the same year.
  • The Ministry of Coal should publish an annual Mission Coking Coal scorecard showing raw coking coal output against the 83 MT and 140 MT targets, washery capacity against 58 MT, and the status of each of the eight new CIL washeries and 16 auctioned blocks, so progress is visible against the ministry’s own milestones.
  • SAIL should publish the results of the Rourkela Blast Furnace #1 biochar injection trial and RDCIS’s coke-oven substitution trials, including share of PCI replaced and resulting coke quality, so whether the pilot has been extended or abandoned since August 2024 is on the record.
  • DGCI&S should report coking coal imports under HS 27011910 by country of origin as well as country of consignment, so the $1,430 million attributed to Singapore and $148 million to Switzerland in FY2025–26 can be traced to the mining countries that actually supply them.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

What this piece could not establish
What this article does not establish. Whether the Rourkela biochar trial has been extended beyond Blast Furnace #1, scaled up, or adopted at other SAIL or private steel plants since August 2024 — this piece found no update on that specifically. Nor could it find published results from RDCIS's coke-oven coking-coal-substitution trials, or any government policy (Ministry of Coal, Ministry of Steel, or otherwise) that mandates or incentivises biomass or biochar use in coke-making the way SAMARTH mandates biomass co-firing in power plants. If such a policy exists, it was not surfaced by the sources this piece checked.

This piece deliberately separates a real, well-documented government programme (Mission Coking Coal) from a real-but-different government programme (SAMARTH biomass co-firing) and a real-but-small industry pilot (SAIL Rourkela's biochar trial), because "biomass replacing coking coal in India" is not, on the sourcing this piece could find, an accurate description of current Indian government policy. Where a claim could not be verified — particularly the current status of the Rourkela trial and any coke-oven substitution results — that is stated directly rather than filled in.

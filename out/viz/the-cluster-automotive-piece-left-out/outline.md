## 1. MP isn't a greenfield pitch — it's an established cluster with an awareness problem

The honest starting point for this piece: Pithampur, 25–35km from Indore, already functions as a real automotive cluster by the same measures used to describe Chennai, NCR, Pune, Sanand and AURIC in the companion piece. It hosts 120 large industrial units and roughly 450 small and medium units, with combined investment of $11.3 billion and direct employment of about 25,000 people in the auto cluster alone. Six OEMs operate there (Force Motors, Eicher Motors, Avtec Motors, Kinetic Motors, Man Force, and L&T Case among the named operators), alongside Bridgestone's major tyre-manufacturing unit and roughly 60 auto-component and ancillary firms. State-wide, Madhya Pradesh hosts more than 10 OEMs, 2,770 engineering manufacturers and 200 dedicated auto-component manufacturers, employing around 200,000 technical workers.

An Indian OEM manufacturing plant — the kind of automotive base this piece argues Pithampur and Madhya Pradesh already host at scale. [Tata motors Pune plant](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Tata_motors_Pune_plant_1316.jpg), Rameshng, CC BY-SA 3.0, via Wikimedia Commons.

Why the omission matters. A cluster with $11.3bn in combined investment and 200,000 technical workers state-wide is not a rounding error next to Chennai or Pune — it's a genuine sixth data point that got left out of the mapping exercise because it doesn't carry the same brand recognition as "Detroit of India" or the NCR/Maruti story. The pitch case in this piece starts from correcting that visibility gap, not from arguing MP should become something it isn't yet.

## 2. The geography argument: MP sits on the corridor connecting the other five

Pithampur's position is not incidental to the clusters mapped in the companion piece — it sits directly on the Delhi-Mumbai Industrial Corridor (DMIC), the same trunk infrastructure spine connecting NCR (Gurugram-Manesar) at one end to Mumbai/Pune/Sanand-Gujarat at the other. National Highway 52 runs through Pithampur, and Indore has direct rail service to Mumbai, Delhi, Kolkata, Chennai, Ahmedabad, Pune, Jaipur, Lucknow, Bhopal and Nagpur — a genuinely central-India node rather than a peripheral one. Indian Railways has separately flagged Indore-Pune and Indore-Lucknow Vande Bharat services as planned additions, which would tighten the passenger (and indirectly, business-logistics-coordination) link to the Pune cluster specifically.

Every other cluster in the companion piece connects to at most two or three of the others directly. MP's position on the DMIC, plus its rail reach, puts it within a single logistics spine of nearly all of them.

This matters specifically for the just-in-time model documented in the companion piece: a supplier or Tier-1 facility based in Pithampur isn't just serving MP's own OEMs, it sits on a corridor that can plausibly feed NCR and Pune/Sanand without the multi-day transit times a more peripheral state would carry. That's a different, stronger claim than "MP has cheap land" — it's "MP has a structural position inside the same freight network the rest of India's auto industry already depends on."

Set against the JIT mechanics documented in the companion piece — Maruti's 76%-of-suppliers-within-100km model, the e-Nagare digital-flow system, dedicated freight lanes coordinated to shift schedules — the policy's specific bets (multimodal logistics parks, dry ports, 24×7 permissions) are aimed at exactly the kind of high-frequency, low-latency freight movement JIT and JIS depend on. A supplier deciding where to site a new Tier-1 facility to serve multiple OEM clusters is choosing, in effect, between paying for that speed via proximity to one cluster (the existing model) or via a logistics-subsidised central position that reaches several (the case this policy is trying to make attractive).

## 4. One asset none of the other five clusters have

MP's testing infrastructure is a genuine differentiator rather than a shared commodity. NATRAX (National Automotive Test Tracks), located in MP, spans 1,200 hectares with an 11.3km test track, built at a cost of roughly ₹1,653 crore (~$200 million). This is not duplicated at the scale of any of the five clusters mapped in the companion piece — it is a specific, capital-intensive piece of shared national testing infrastructure that happens to sit inside MP's own cluster geography, giving OEMs and Tier-1s based there a validation-and-testing advantage that proximity to NCR, Pune or Chennai alone doesn't confer.

## 5. The state's own investment-attraction track record

MP hosted the 8th Global Investors Summit in Bhopal (24–25 February 2025), where new industrial zones spanning thousands of acres were announced across Pithampur, Ratlam and Dewas. The single largest headline commitment from that summit was not automotive — a roughly ₹50,000 crore Bina Refinery Petrochemical Complex investment, aimed at making MP a petrochemicals hub — but it demonstrates the state can credibly attract and announce investment at a scale well above what a purely regional player would draw, which is itself part of the pitch case for a sector (automotive) where MP already has a real base to build from rather than starting cold.

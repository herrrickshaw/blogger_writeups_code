A companion piece on this site mapped India's automotive clusters — Chennai, NCR, Pune, Sanand, AURIC — and left one out: Pithampur, Madhya Pradesh, which already hosts 120 large industrial units, five OEMs and roughly $11.3 billion in combined investment. This piece makes the case that omission deserves correcting, using MP's own 2025 Logistics Policy and its position directly on the Delhi-Mumbai Industrial Corridor as the investment argument.

Top line

  • $11.3 billion of combined investment sits in Pithampur's auto cluster, which directly employs about 25,000 people.
  • 25–35km from Indore, Pithampur already meets the same cluster measures used for Chennai, NCR, Pune, Sanand and AURIC.
Updated 7 November 2026. Two developments since this piece was written. The state says investment proposals worth about ₹10 lakh crore from the February 2025 summit are now under implementation, roughly a third of the ₹30.77 lakh crore headline; independent reporting has questioned how much of the total is converting, and no project-level tracker has been published. And the summit’s automotive follow-through has partly gone beyond Pithampur: VE Commercial Vehicles, the Volvo–Eicher joint venture that already makes engines at Pithampur, announced a ₹544 crore greenfield plant for 12-speed automated manual transmissions at Vikram Udyogpuri near Ujjain (October 2025), with initial capacity of up to 40,000 units a year.

The logistics policy and the investor summit both came in February 2025

  1. February 2025Madhya Pradesh approves its Logistics Policy 2025, aimed at cutting logistics costs
  2. 24–25 February 20258th Global Investors Summit in Bhopal announces new industrial zones across Pithampur, Ratlam and Dewas

What it means: Two state moves in one month put Pithampur's industrial zones and the logistics policy on the investor agenda.

What to do: Track whether the zones announced at the summit receive logistics park approvals under the policy.

What would fix this

  • The Madhya Pradesh Industries Department should publish the full text of the Logistics Policy 2025 and its operational guidelines on Invest MP, including subsidy eligibility and disbursement rules, so the ₹50 crore and ₹75 crore logistics-park support can be checked against the primary document.
  • ACMA should publish auto-component turnover and employment by state, including Madhya Pradesh, so Pithampur’s weight can be compared with the five better-known clusters on the same basis rather than from cluster self-descriptions.
  • The National Industrial Corridor Development Corporation should publish typical road and rail freight transit times and costs between Pithampur and the NCR, Pune and Sanand clusters, so the claim that a central DMIC location can serve several OEM clusters on a just-in-time basis can be tested.
  • The Madhya Pradesh government should publish a tracker of investment commitments announced at the 2025 Global Investors Summit, with status by project and district, including the Pithampur, Ratlam and Dewas zones, so announcements can be followed through to implementation.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

What this piece does not establish. This is a pitch synthesis, not an independent cost-benefit or site-selection analysis: it does not compute actual freight cost or transit-time savings versus a supplier locating in an existing cluster instead, does not verify MP's logistics subsidy figures against the primary Madhya Pradesh Logistics Policy 2025 document itself (sourced here via secondary coverage), and does not have MP-specific ACMA cluster-turnover data comparable to the industry-wide $80.2bn figure cited in the companion piece — that state-level breakdown was not found in the sourcing gathered for either piece. A genuine investment-committee analysis would need primary-source freight-cost modelling this piece has not attempted.

In April 2025, a California renewable-diesel refinery that had burned through more than $2 billion filed for Chapter 11 — its senior lenders walked away with 100% of the common equity, and public shareholders got nothing. Four months later, a shipping line was writing a check to secure biogas supply, and a Korean refiner was buying a biodiesel-feedstock company for the third time in five years. Same industry, three completely different kinds of deal.

  • $34 million is the final second draw on Montana Renewables’ US Department of Energy loan, cut in September 2026 from up to $658 million; the SAF target fell from about 300 to about 200 million gallons a year.
  • $190 million in cash bought one 120 million-gallon-per-year ethanol plant from Green Plains: ethanol deals are single plants, not company mergers.
Updated 7 November 2026. Three deals in the table have moved on. XCF Global’s combination with DevvStream and Southern Energy Renewables closed on 16 October 2026; Montana Renewables’ US Department of Energy loan was amended in September 2026, cutting the second draw from up to $658 million to a final $34 million and the SAF target from about 300 to about 200 million gallons a year; and the Daekyung O&T purchase closed in late June 2026. World Energy’s Paramount refinery is still listed as an open case: unconfirmed aggregator reports say it has shut, but no primary source was found.

Why the post reads this as the renewable-diesel risk profile

A Bakersfield, California refinery converted to renewable diesel raised over $2 billion, was delayed more than three years, lost its offtake partner (ExxonMobil exited) and ended up handing 100% of its common equity to lenders.

What it means: A refinery that raised over $2 billion to convert to renewable diesel ended with lenders and its contractor holding all the new equity, which the post treats as the risk profile of the whole renewable-diesel build-out.

What to do: The California Air Resources Board should publish monthly renewable-diesel and sustainable-aviation-fuel output by facility, so that distress shows up in official data before it shows up as a restructuring.

Montana Renewables: second DOE draw cut from up to $658 million to a final $34 million

What it means: The segment’s largest capital event funded a first drawdown of approximately $782 million, but the amendment cut the SAF target to about 200 million gallons a year from about 300 and left the rest of the expansion to Montana Renewables’ own earnings.

What to do: The US Department of Energy’s Loan Programs Office should publish, for each loan guarantee, the amount committed, drawn to date and the date of each drawdown.

Biodiesel in the US, and the open distress case

Buffalo Biodiesel was acquired by HydroGenetics on 24 October 2025, alongside a separate $300 million growth-capital partnership with Verite Capital Partners to expand from 15 states to 25 across the eastern US.

World Energy’s SAF-producing Paramount, California refinery lost Air Products as funder of its SAF expansion (agreement terminated 24 February 2025) and had reportedly gone four months without producing SAF by late July 2025. No sale, bankruptcy filing or restructuring has been reported; the post calls it an open distress case and a possible second Global Clean Energy-style event.

What it means: Daekyung is a profitable seller of biodiesel feedstock rather than a distressed asset, which is part of why a strategic refiner paid up for feedstock security.

What to do: Watch World Energy’s Paramount refinery as the open distress case, since no sale, filing or restructuring has been reported for it.

What would fix this

  1. US Department of Energy, Loan Programs Office: publish the amount committed, drawn and the date of each drawdown for every loan guarantee.
  2. California Air Resources Board: publish monthly renewable-diesel and SAF output by facility from Low Carbon Fuel Standard reporting.
  3. Department of Food and Public Distribution: publish a distillery-wise register of India’s ethanol capacity, owner group and commissioning date.
  4. Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas: publish oil marketing companies’ ethanol allocations by supplier group for each ethanol supply year.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

What this piece does not establish. Two claims circulating in initial secondary-source research were checked and did not hold up, and are corrected here rather than repeated: "Shree Renuka Sugars acquired Sir Shadi Lal Enterprises" (it was Triveni Engineering, verified against Business Standard and ChiniMandi's contemporaneous reporting), and a GESS RNG Biogas USA acquisition initially assumed to be current-period activity (it dates to December 2021 and was dropped from this piece rather than misdated). The XCF Global/DevvStream/Southern Energy business-combination terms could not be independently verified beyond the fact of the agreement — the underlying SEC filing returned a 403 error on every access attempt, so deal valuation and equity split are not stated here because they were not confirmed. Gevo's sale price for the Agri-Energy plant to A.E. Innovation was not disclosed in any source found. Grapevine Energy's exact emergence date has a small discrepancy across sources (28 July vs. a 12 August announcement) and is stated here as "mid-August 2025" rather than a specific day. This piece covers deals surfaced through English-language trade press and SEC/press-release sourcing; it is not a comprehensive global M&A database and likely misses smaller deals, particularly outside the US, Europe, Korea and India. Archaea/NextGen is included as historical context (2022) for the RNG rollup pattern, not as current-period activity — it predates the window this piece otherwise covers.

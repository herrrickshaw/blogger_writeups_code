In 2021, Taiwan's worst drought since 1964 forced TSMC to run 8,000 water-tanker trucks a day just to keep its fabs running, while a million households had their taps cut twice a week. That's the real-world stress test for what "not enough water treatment capacity" means for a chip industry. Dholera's Tata Electronics fab has a dedicated 50 MLD treatment plant built for it — a number that looks adequate against the mature-node fab actually being built there, but small against what Taiwan's own science parks needed once they scaled up.

What it means: The 50 MLD plant has three to five times the 10 to 15 MLD one mature-node fab is estimated to need, so the margin is real for one fab.

What to do: Re-run this comparison if Dholera adds a second fab or moves to more advanced nodes.

TSMC alone uses about 60% of Taiwan's chip-industry water

What it means: TSMC's 150,000 tonnes a day is about 60% of the 250,000 tonnes the whole Taiwan semiconductor industry uses, so one company drives most of the demand.

What to do: Update this share as usage grows, since the 2019 share figure is now several years old.

Taiwan's answer is dedicated reclaimed water, rising to 36,000 m³ a day by 2026

What it means: Taiwan responded with dedicated reclaimed-water plants, with Hsinchu's planned supply rising from 10,000 to 36,000 m³ a day by 2026.

What to do: Build reclaimed-water supply and a recycling-rate target alongside any new fab, as TSMC's 90.3% recycling rate shows.

What would fix this

  • The India Semiconductor Mission should set a minimum process-water recycling rate as a condition of fab support and require each supported fab to report it annually, so India has a quantified commitment comparable to TSMC's reported 90.3 per cent rather than a framework mention of recycling plants.
  • The Dholera Special Investment Region Development Authority should state whether its 50 MLD water plant is installed, permitted or operating capacity, and publish monthly drawals by user, so the apparent three-to-five-fold headroom over a 10–15 MLD fab can be checked.
  • The Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change should require fabs in Dholera's blanket clearance zone to report water intake, ultrapure water produced, reject water and recycling rate in their compliance reports, so the fab's real water use is on record from the start.
  • Sardar Sarovar Narmada Nigam should publish the Narmada canal allocation to Dholera and the order in which industrial users would be cut during a drought, so the fab's supply risk in a dry year is known in advance, unlike Taiwan's 2021 rationing.
  • The Gujarat government should publish a plan for a dedicated reclaimed-water plant serving Dholera's fabs, with capacity and timeline, before a second fab is approved, so supply grows with demand as Taiwan's Hsinchu and Qiaotou plants were built to do.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

What this piece does not establish. The 10–15 MLD estimate for Dholera's fab and the 20–38 MLD range for "a single large modern fab" come from different, non-Taiwan-specific industry sourcing rather than one unified methodology, and this piece has not independently verified either against a primary engineering document for the Dholera project specifically. Taiwan's semiconductor-sector total (>250,000 t/day) and TSMC's Taiwan-wide figure (~150,000 t/day) are current as sourced, but the 3.4%-of-industrial-water figure for TSMC is dated to 2019 and almost certainly understates TSMC's current share given reported consumption growth since. No official Indian government document specifying a target water-recycling percentage for Dholera or other ISM-backed fabs was located; the ISM 2.0 "water recycling plants" reference is a policy-framework mention, not a quantified commitment comparable to TSMC's reported rate. Whether Dholera's 50 MLD figure is installed capacity, permitted capacity, or currently operational capacity was not stated precisely in the sourcing available to this piece.

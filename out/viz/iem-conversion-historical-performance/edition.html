Top line

  • 82.0% conversion in 2024, up from a 45.1% low in 2021; a same-year ratio, not a cohort match.
Updated 12 November 2026. These are still the latest IEM figures DPIIT has published, checked 12 November 2026. Its monthly IEM Statistics Report and company lists stopped after the September 2024 issue, which carries data to August 2024. Since 1 October 2025, IEM filings have been made on the National Single Window System, which publishes no IEM statistics. The annual totals here come from DPIIT’s Annual Report 2025-26, which runs to December 2025; DPIIT’s last two annual reports appeared in February. India’s Factory Intentions, 2001–2024 explains the move and what the public can no longer see.

What it means: Conversion held in the mid-50s through 2020, dipped to 45.1% in 2021, then reached 82.0% in 2024.

What to do: Pair each year's Part B with the Part A filings from earlier years before judging conversion in any single year.

Implementation reached Rs 4,93,765 cr in 2024, the highest year shown

What it means: Implemented value rose to Rs 4,93,765 cr in 2024 from Rs 2,76,925 cr in 2023, the largest year in the table.

What to do: Report implementation by state and sector next to the national total, since the 2024 jump is concentrated in a few states.

Part A filings peaked in 2021, so the 2024 base was smaller

What it means: Part A filings reached Rs 771,428 cr in 2021, the largest year shown, while the 2024 base was Rs 602,082 cr.

What to do: Read each year's conversion against the filing surge that preceded it, not against that year's own filings alone.

What would fix this

  • DPIIT should resume the monthly IEM Statistics Report, last issued for August 2024 data, and compile it from NSWS filings, so the Part A and Part B series that ran from 2001 is not broken by the move to the single window.
  • DPIIT should publish a formal erratum and corrected state table for 2019 IEM Part B data, replacing the Gujarat figure of ₹15,18,861 crore in Annual Report 2022-23 and the IEM count printed in the investment cell in 2021-22, so the series has no missing year.
  • DPIIT should publish the IEM Part A and Part B series as one downloadable, vintage-stamped time series with revision notes, so that users no longer have to difference and reconcile successive Annual Reports, and so that partial-year rows like “Up to Dec 2025” show how much they later revise.
  • DPIIT should publish a cohort table showing Part B implementations by year of original Part A filing, so that conversion can be measured as promises actually built rather than as a same-year flow ratio that can exceed 100%, as the provisional 109.9% does.
  • DPIIT should restate 2017–2024 IEM counts and values for projects above the new ₹125 crore threshold, so that counts before and after the April 2025 change from ₹50 crore are comparable on one basis.
  • DPIIT should add fields to the IEM Part B form for project type (greenfield or expansion) and whether state investment incentives were availed, so that the capital source behind years like 2024’s ₹4.94 lakh crore can be read rather than assumed.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

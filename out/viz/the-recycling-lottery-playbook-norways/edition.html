Top line

Updated 15 November 2026: rewritten as charts; figures unchanged.

Both systems return 92.4% or more of plastic bottles

What it means: Norway's 2025 rates of 93.1% for cans and 92.4% for plastic bottles sit close to Germany's 97.7%.

What to do: Ride existing infrastructure, as the post's first success factor says, rather than building new collection networks.

Jal Jeevan's 35% participation target sits below Norway's 39%

What it means: Norway's 39% is the share of adults who choose the lottery option, while 35% is a conservative design target for Jal Jeevan.

What to do: Report uptake quarterly against the 35% target, as the post's governance section calls for quarterly public reporting.

The white paper's revenue potential would exceed the Jal Jeevan budget

What it means: The post reads the white paper's $12bn as a yearly potential, which sits above the $8.4bn outlay.

What to do: Treat the $12bn as an extrapolation until the design includes the independent audit the post's success factors require.

What would fix this

  • State lottery departments should publish, in their annual budget documents, lottery ticket sales, prize payouts and net revenue to the state, so the low revenue transparency the Pahle India Foundation white paper criticises is addressed before any new lottery-funded scheme is considered.
  • State lottery departments should publish yearly records of prizes won, claimed and unclaimed, with the verification procedure used, so the unverified prize-claim processing the white paper flags can be checked state by state.
  • The Ministry of Environment, through the Central Pollution Control Board, should notify a deposit-return pilot for single-use PET beverage bottles in at least one city, with a fixed per-bottle deposit and a published return rate, so India tests the deposit-and-reverse-vending mechanic Germany’s Pfand system shows works at scale.
  • The Ministry of Jal Shakti should publish the state-wise funding requirement and allocated outlay for Jal Jeevan Mission each year, so any proposal for a new revenue instrument can be sized against a published funding gap rather than against total budget outlays.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

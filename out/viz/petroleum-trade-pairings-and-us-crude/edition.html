Top line

  • 30% of its 2017 peak: Cushing, the WTI delivery hub, held 21.0M bbl in July 2026.
  • US$90.16bn of US crude imports came from Canada in 2025, nearly ten times the next source.
Updated 7 November 2026. Two corrections and a storage refresh. The three OPEC series in Topic 06 do fall sharply in March 2026, but this page wrongly called that a change of definition: it is the Hormuz war, with OPEC crude output down 7.30 million barrels a day in March (Reuters survey) and EIA-estimated shut-ins of 10.9 million barrels a day at the May peak, 4.8 million in September. EIA weekly data for 23 October 2026 put the Strategic Petroleum Reserve at 282.6 million barrels (304.8 million in the tiles below), commercial crude at 410.6 million (407.0) and total crude including the SPR at 693.2 million (711.8); the US announced a 40-million-barrel SPR exchange on 29 September. The tiles and charts below still show 31 July 2026. Sources: Reuters, 31 March 2026; EIA Short-Term Energy Outlook, October 2026; EIA weekly stocks.

Updated 15 November 2026: rewritten as charts; figures unchanged.

Cushing and the SPR are far below their historical peaks

What it means: Cushing and the SPR are the two physical buffers behind the crude market, and both sit well below peak.

What to do: Refresh the Cushing figure, since the post's own update says stocks have risen to about 34% of peak.

US crude imports lean heavily on Canada, nearly ten times the next source

What it means: Pipeline-delivered heavy crude from Canada suits the Gulf Coast refineries that are built to run it.

What to do: Read the partner list as a grade story, not a supply-security story, when judging US crude dependence.

Gulf producers and the US are net petroleum sellers; India is the largest net buyer

What it means: The Gulf producers and the US export more petroleum than they import, while India is the largest net importer.

What to do: Read the Mexico position with care, because its gasoline import line does not reconcile with US export data.

What would fix this

  • DGCI&S should ensure India’s HS-6 petroleum import data reaches UN Comtrade and matches TRADESTAT totals, so India’s net petroleum position can be built on the same basis as the other reporters rather than patched in from a different source.
  • PPAC should publish India’s crude imports by source country with average API gravity and sulphur content, so the grade mix India buys, including US light crude, is reported directly rather than inferred from implied unit values per tonne.
  • DGCI&S should publish India’s petroleum trade by partner at HS-6 on a calendar-year basis alongside the fiscal-year series, so India can be compared with the US, Mexico and the GCC reporters, which report on calendar years, without mismatched periods.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

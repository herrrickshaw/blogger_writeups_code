#### Two different things called "reserves"

 Proved reserves (41,151 million bbl, above) are oil still in the ground — a geological
 and economic estimate revised annually. The SPR and Cushing are barrels already produced
 and sitting in tanks and caverns. They move on entirely different clocks, and only the
 second kind can be delivered against a futures contract next month.

### Why Cushing specifically

 Cushing is the physical delivery point for the WTI contract. When its tanks run
 toward operating minimum, the barrels available to settle that contract thin out, and WTI
 trades more on local tank economics than on global supply. At
 21.0M bbl — 30%
 of its 2017 peak — the hub is a small buffer under a very large benchmark.

## Topic 05What this data can and cannot tell you

 Findings from building the pipeline. Each one changes how the numbers above
 should be read.

 
 

### The two granularity levels fail in different countries

 Neither the chapter aggregate nor the HS-6 detail is reliable everywhere, and they
 break in opposite directions. India's chapter-27 imports reconcile closely with
 DGCI&S's figure ($209.1bn against $203.4bn — a 3% reconciliation) while its HS-6 import
 detail is almost entirely absent: crude at the six-digit level reads $0.28bn instead of
 roughly $140bn. Mexico and Oman are the mirror image — their HS-6 detail is sound but
 their chapter aggregates read $0.7bn and $2.5bn against HS-6 sums of $19.7bn and
 $44.8bn. Pick one level for a cross-country table and some countries will be wrong by
 an order of magnitude.

 

 
 

#### Qatar's LNG is missing at HS-6; Bahrain reports nothing

 Qatar's chapter-27 exports total $79.6bn, but its HS-6 detail accounts for only
 $15.0bn — all of it crude, with no LNG (HS 271111) recorded at all. Roughly $64bn of
 the region's most important gas trade is invisible at product level. Bahrain reports
 essentially no chapter-27 trade in either direction. Both need partner-declared
 (mirror) data for any product-level view, which is why they carry no pairing panel.

 

 
 

#### Partner names are not in the API response

 The preview endpoint returns partnerDesc: null on every row — only the
 numeric partner code is populated. Matching on the name field silently yields zero for
 every counterparty; the largest pairing in this dataset first appeared as
 "US → Mexico, $0.00bn". Names here are resolved from Comtrade's own
 partnerAreas reference file, and region aggregates are excluded so
 counterparties are not double-counted.

 

 
 

#### Diesel and base oil cannot be separated at HS-6

 HS 271019 bundles gasoil/diesel, kerosene, fuel oil and lubricating base oils
 into one line. No six-digit query can split lube-oil exports from diesel exports —
 that needs national tariff detail (India's 8-digit TRADESTAT, US HTS 10-digit).
 The $35.7bn "India 271019 exports" figure is mostly diesel, not lubricants.

 

 
 

#### UAE stops reporting after 2023; Qatar after 2024

 Verified with retries, not inferred from a single failed call. UAE 2024 and 2025 are
 genuinely absent, so UAE figures here are 2023 and are not comparable year-on-year with
 Saudi or US 2025 numbers. Kuwait's 2025 query hit the API row cap and was excluded.

 

 
 

#### Queries that hit the row ceiling are incomplete, not empty

 The public preview returns at most 500 rows per query. Any query that comes back at
 exactly the ceiling has had counterparties silently truncated — the response looks
 perfectly valid. Affected here: India Diesel, jet, fuel oil + base oils, India Lubricating preparations.
 Those partner lists are a floor, not a complete set.

 

 
 

#### The API silently drops data when throttled

 A first pass without retries showed 2023 missing for six of nine countries. With
 backoff and retry, every one of those years returned complete data — 4 throttled calls
 versus 5 genuinely empty ones. An unretried collector would have reported those years
 as zero trade.

 

 
 

#### How the reporting year was chosen — and where that rule is weak

 For each country the latest year was accepted only if its chapter-27 exports and
 imports both exceeded 55% of that country's own five-year peak, which correctly rejects
 trailing partial years (UAE 2024–25, Qatar 2025). The rule is only valid for trailing
 years: it also flagged Qatar 2021–23, but that is the 2022 gas-price spike moving the
 ratio, not missing data. And because it reads the chapter aggregate, it is itself
 unreliable for Mexico and Oman, whose aggregates are broken.

 

 
 

#### Paywalled and unavailable sources

 OilPriceAPI on this account returns HTTP 403 for OPEC production, storage and
 inventories, and requires a paid plan for any price history — it serves current spot only.
 An EIA API key now supplies OPEC production and the full storage series directly
 (Topic 06), so that gap is closed from a
 different source rather than from OilPriceAPI. Still missing: no S&P Global commodity
 feed is connected to this session — the finance connector available is MSCI Private Assets
 (private capital, real assets and index data), which carries no petroleum series, so
 Platts-style assessments remain out of reach. And EIA proved reserves end at 2021 by
 both routes.

 

 

## Topic 06OPEC production, and what the API key did not fix

 Now served by the EIA v2 API. The four storage series it returns match the
 public workbooks to the barrel — Cushing 20,955, SPR 304,809, commercial 406,987 — so the
 two routes independently corroborate each other. OPEC production is new; it also needs the
 most careful handling on this page.

 Proved reserves do not extend with the key. The API reports the same 2021 endpoint as the public workbook (2021), with dataset coverage 1899–2021. The gap is the dataset, not the access route — I expected the key to close it and it does not.

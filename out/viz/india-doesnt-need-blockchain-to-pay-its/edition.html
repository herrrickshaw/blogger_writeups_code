A small open-source project models farmer payments on a ledger. Tested against where Indian payments actually fail (PM-KISAN’s beneficiary list, sugar mills’ arrears, thin mandi auctions), most problems arise before any money moves. The one multi-party record worth a blockchain is land, which Andhra Pradesh is now piloting.

Blockchain for Indian farmers: land records are a stronger use case than payments

Skip to article content
Text Size

Top line

  • 9.34 crore farmers were paid in PM-KISAN's 24th instalment, down from 10.07 crore in early 2025.
  • ₹12,779 crore of 2025-26 cane dues were still unpaid by mills on 20 April 2026, despite a 14-day rule.
  • 11 crore Farmer IDs by 23 October 2026, up from 10.28 crore in July, five months ahead of target.

Updated 15 November 2026: rewritten as charts; figures unchanged.

Mills still owed farmers ₹12,779 crore in cane dues in April, despite a 14-day rule

What it means: Arrears persist because mills face a cash squeeze, with cane prices rising to ₹365 while the minimum sugar price has stayed at ₹31 a kilo since 2019.

What to do: Require a bank-guaranteed escrow for cane purchases and enforce interest on payments delayed beyond 14 days.

Tighter PM-KISAN lists cut paid farmers from 10.07 crore to 9.34 crore

What it means: Stricter checks removed ineligible names, but they also stopped payments to eligible farmers whose Aadhaar, e-KYC or land seeding was incomplete.

What to do: Publish, for each instalment, how many eligible farmers are held back and why, so the cut can be checked.

Farmer ID registries reached 11 crore in October, five months ahead of target

What it means: Each Farmer ID is matched to digitised land records, so the register is only as reliable as the land records behind it.

What to do: Extend registration to tenants and sharecroppers, of whom only about 28,680 had been registered in five states by October.

What would fix this

  • Publish PM-KISAN’s ineligible-payment ledger, state by state. The government publishes the amount recovered (₹416.75 crore) but not, in its recent releases, the total paid to people later found ineligible. Without that figure the recovery rate cannot be judged.
  • Publish stop-payment and failure counts with every instalment: how many farmers were held for e-KYC, bank seeding or land seeding, how many credits failed, and how many were later paid, so the fall from 10.07 crore to 9.34 crore can be split into cleaning and exclusion.
  • Seed the bank account at enrolment. The DBT Mission already asks schemes to check NPCI mapper status when Aadhaar is authenticated. Making that check part of Farmer ID creation would catch failed payments before they happen.
  • Price cane escrow before mandating it. A bank-guaranteed escrow for cane, backed by a mill’s sugar or ethanol receivables, could be tested in one state with high arrears. Enforcing the interest that the Sugarcane (Control) Order already allows on late payment would come first.
  • Put assaying labs, not ledgers, at the centre of e-NAM grade disputes, and log results so they cannot be edited afterwards; that log is where a hash chain is cheap and useful.
  • Measure Andhra Pradesh’s blockchain land records against its old system on mutation time, disputes and fraud before other states copy it.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

What this piece could not establish

The total amount paid to ineligible PM-KISAN beneficiaries nationally was not found in a recent official source; a figure of about ₹4,350 crore circulates but could not be traced to a Parliament answer or audit report, so it is not used. In 2021 the government was asked about recovering about ₹3,000 crore from 42 lakh ineligible beneficiaries, but that figure appears in the question, not in a confirmed answer. The count of beneficiaries stopped for pending land seeding in March 2026 was not read. The stop-payment categories may overlap, so the 57 lakh total is an upper bound. The February cane arrears and the comparison with earlier years come from press reports of a standing committee report; one report said arrears were up about ₹2,049 crore on the previous season, another said the previous figure was ₹497 crore, and the earlier-year comparison is therefore not used. The August cane figure is calculated from a rounded 97%. The escrow sizing assumes a 150–180 day crushing season and an even pace of purchases. No cost per payment, time-to-credit data or outcome study was found for any blockchain farmer-payment pilot, in India or abroad, and no results have been published for Andhra Pradesh’s land-record pilot.

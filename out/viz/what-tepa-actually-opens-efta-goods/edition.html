A companion piece on this site already flagged the India-EFTA TEPA's headline $100 billion investment commitment as a general framework, not a sector-specific plan. This piece goes one layer deeper: what does the actual tariff schedule cover, what does India's real trade with Switzerland and Norway look like today, and which categories are genuinely exposed to new import competition versus which are just noise around a number (gold) that TEPA doesn't actually touch.

India-EFTA TEPA: in force since 1 October 2025, with a $100 billion commitment

Skip to article content
Text Size

Top line

  • 89% of India's 2024 imports from Switzerland is gold, and TEPA's tariff schedule leaves gold untouched.
  • 99.6% of India's exports are covered by EFTA's tariff offer, against 95.3% of EFTA's exports under India's offer.
  • 260-to-1 is Norway's salmon sales to China against India this year, so tariff cuts alone will not build the market.
Updated 7 November 2026. TEPA’s first, partial year shows the same gold-dominated shape. India’s imports from Switzerland rose 11.4% to $24.27 billion in 2025-26, with Switzerland supplying about 40% of India’s record $71.98 billion of gold imports (PTI via The Week); India’s exports to Switzerland were reported above $1.2 billion (IANS, May 2026). In May 2026 the two sides set up a joint group to resolve implementation and non-tariff issues (NewsBytes, snippet only). No official tally of EFTA investment received against the $100 billion commitment was found. The 2024 and FY2024-25 tables below are unchanged.

Updated 15 November 2026: rewritten as charts; figures unchanged.

Gold is 89% of India's Swiss imports, and TEPA does not change its duty

What it means: Gold's effective duty does not change under TEPA, so the headline import figure is mostly not a TEPA exposure.

What to do: Strip gold out of gross trade figures before measuring what TEPA exposes India to.

What it means: Basmati and non-basmati rice get duty-free EFTA access with no reciprocal concession from India.

What to do: Actively market the duty-free rice access to exporters, as the post recommends.

Norway sells India about 200 tonnes of salmon, against 52,000 to China

What it means: Norway sold roughly 52,000 tonnes of salmon to China this year, against about 200 tonnes to India.

What to do: Pair the tariff cut with cold-chain and distribution investment before expecting salmon volumes to follow.

What would fix this

  • The Department of Commerce should publish an annual TEPA utilisation report showing trade with each EFTA country at preferential rates by HS chapter, with gold, pearls and precious stones shown separately, so the deal's real effect is not hidden inside a $22 billion import figure that is mostly untouched gold.
  • The Department of Commerce should publish yearly progress on TEPA's $100 billion investment commitment, by EFTA country and sector, so it can be checked whether money is reaching gaps such as precision machinery, medical instruments and specialty pharmaceuticals.
  • DGFT should publish India's TEPA tariff schedule as a searchable HS-line table giving each line's base rate, phase-out category and year it reaches zero, so exporters and importers can check concessions without working from the agreement's annexes.
  • APEDA should publish monthly basmati and non-basmati rice exports to Switzerland, Norway, Iceland and Liechtenstein from TEPA's entry into force, so uptake of the one-sided duty-free rice access can be tracked against earlier years.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

What this piece does not establish. This analysis works from secondary trade-data aggregators (Trading Economics, Eurostat-derived compilations, OEC) rather than a direct DGCI&S TradeStat pull, because both tradestat.commerce.gov.in and pib.gov.in returned access errors from this piece's research tools at the time of writing. The figures are corroborated across multiple independent sources and internally consistent (Swiss-specific and EFTA-wide numbers tell the same story), but a reader relying on this for a sourcing/compliance decision should verify the exact HS-line-level tariff schedule against the EFTA/India TEPA's own published Annexes 2.C–2.F rather than this summary.

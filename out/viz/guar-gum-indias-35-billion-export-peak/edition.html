India supplies roughly 80% of the world's guar gum, per APEDA's own data, and the crop it comes from is genuinely well-suited to the arid, low-fertility soils of Rajasthan, Haryana and Gujarat — drought-tolerant, nitrogen-fixing, and grown on land most other crops can't use. But the export number itself tells a boom-bust story, not a steady-growth one: guar gum exports peaked at $3.5 billion in 2012-13 on a US shale-fracking demand spike, crashed to $467 million within four years when that demand evaporated, and sat at $541.65 million in 2023-24 — a real, if partial, recovery, still nowhere near the peak. This piece separates the export-value story from the cultivation-opportunity story, because the two move on different timelines.

Top line

  • ₹21,287 crore was India’s guar gum export value in 2012-13 (about $3.5 billion), up from ₹121 crore in 2003-04. The 2024-25 figure of $550.12 million is roughly 84% below that peak.
  • Roughly 90% of guar gum exports still go to oil and shale-gas extraction, per APEDA, the same end use behind the 2014-15 crash.
  • 72% of guar production comes from Rajasthan on APEDA’s live data, which supersedes the earlier 70% to nearly 80% range.

  2. APEDA: publish the year-by-year end-use breakdown behind the 90% oil and shale-gas statement.

Who processes it: the major Indian suppliers
  • Hindustan Gums & Chemicals Ltd: joint venture of Solvay (USA) and the M.P. Birla Group (India); 60+ years in the industry.
  • Vikas WSP Ltd: listed on NSE/BSE since 1995; ~46,200 MT/year guar gum powder capacity plus a 100% export-oriented guar polymers unit (~39,900 TPA).
  • India Glycols Ltd, Rama Gum Industries (India) Ltd, Jai Bharat Gums & Chemicals Ltd, Lucid Group (Lucid Colloids), Neelkanth Polymers: guar processors and derivative makers.
  • Dabur India Ltd: FMCG major with a guar-gum-adjacent interest, not a pure-play processor.

This list is not exhaustive, and installed-capacity figures beyond Vikas WSP’s own were not independently verified. Most of these firms sit in or around Rajasthan, close to the growing regions.

Why guar fits low-fertility, arid land

Guar is a rainfed summer legume needing only 300-400mm of annual rainfall, with no supplemental irrigation in most seasons. It fixes nitrogen with Rhizobium bacteria, so a well-nodulated crop often needs no added nitrogen fertiliser and improves soil for a following wheat rotation. Intercropping guar, cowpea or pearl millet with peach, or turmeric with papaya, has been found viable for rehabilitating marginal land in Punjab and Himachal Pradesh’s Shiwalik hill region.

The National Wasteland Development Board (1985) and the Integrated Wasteland Development Programme prioritise nitrogen-fixing, drought-tolerant species. The post could not confirm that guar is enrolled by name under IWDP or a similar programme.

What this piece does not establish

Export-value figures across 2003-2024 are compiled from multiple APEDA-sourced and secondary reports spanning different years and currency-conversion conventions; treat the dollar figures as directionally accurate rather than exactly reconciled to one methodology. Rajasthan’s share has been cited as both “around 70%” and “nearly 80%”; APEDA’s live data resolves this at 72%. No dedicated central scheme incentivising guar cultivation on wasteland was found; the policy fit is an agronomic-criteria match, not confirmation of a guar-specific programme. The seed-price chart uses five documented points, not a continuous AGMARKNET or NCDEX series.

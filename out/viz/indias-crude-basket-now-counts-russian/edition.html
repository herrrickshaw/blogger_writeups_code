In March India changed its crude price formula so that Brent, the sweet benchmark, carries 61% of the weight. That reflects how its oil is priced, not what it is: in September about nine in ten imported barrels came from Russia and the Gulf, and sour crude costs refiners hydrogen.

India's crude basket: Brent weighted at 61%, while most imported barrels are sour

Skip to article content
Text Size

Top line

  • 61% weight of Dated Brent, a sweet marker, in the Indian Basket since March 2026, up from 21%.
  • about 89% of September's crude imports came from Russia and the Middle East, whose main grades are sour.
  • −87% fall in US crude arriving in India between October 2025 and August 2026.
Updated 7 November 2026. Provisional ship-tracking figures for October 2026 show the slate shifting after September. India imported a record of about 5.5–5.6 million barrels a day (S&P Global: 5.54 million). Russian arrivals were about 2 million barrels a day, roughly 35% of the total against about 56% in July, and Middle Eastern arrivals about 2.3 million against 2.7 million in September (Kpler data reported by ThePrint and PTI via The Week, search summaries). On those numbers Russia and the Middle East supplied about three-quarters of October’s crude, down from about 89% in September; the rest came from a wider spread of origins. No October average for the Indian Basket price was found; the September figure of $116.09 below remains the latest.

Updated 15 November 2026: rewritten as charts; figures unchanged.

The basket now gives sweet Brent 61% of its weight, up from 21%

What it means: The basket is a price formula, so a heavier Brent weight means India's crude is priced like sweet crude, not that more of it is sweet.

What to do: Read the basket price next to the grades actually loaded before treating its ratio as a sulphur mix.

Russian crude into India fell from 2,820 to 1,820 thousand barrels a day

What it means: Russian intake followed the discount, which had mostly disappeared by March as Urals sold above Dated Brent.

What to do: Track monthly tanker-tracking estimates against the sour grades refiners process before judging the sweet-sour mix.

US crude arriving in India fell 87% between October 2025 and August 2026

What it means: US crude, the sweetest grade in the mix, was cheap in October 2025 when the Brent–WTI spread was wide, and had shrunk to a fraction by August.

What to do: Check whether the trade talks with Washington show up in tanker arrivals in the months ahead.

What would fix this

  • PPAC should publish, alongside the basket, the monthly physical slate of crude processed by Indian refineries: volume by origin, API gravity band and sulphur band.
  • The basket notes should state plainly that the weights reflect pricing benchmarks, so that a Brent-heavy formula is not read as a shift to sweet crude.
  • Refiners should report hydrogen consumption per tonne of crude processed and its sulphur content each year, so the cost of the slate shows up in the green-hydrogen debate.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

What this piece could not establish

PPAC’s own basket notes and India’s customs data by grade could not be opened directly; the weights come from Business Standard’s report of the March change and the October ratio from a secondary summary that did not label its sides. Russian and Middle East volumes come from different tanker trackers (Kpler and Reuters), so the combined share is approximate; one weekly tracker reported much lower Russian arrivals in late September, which this piece could not reconcile. The US series comes from a single report of Kpler data. No published figure for Indian refiners’ gross margins in the July–September quarter was found, so this piece does not estimate the margin effect. Assay figures vary by cargo, and no reliable sulphur figure for Urals was found.

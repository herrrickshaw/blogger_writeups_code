India's insolvency regulator has published 9,531 liquidation auction notices since the IBC's early years. Read as a register rather than case by case, they show something case-by-case coverage rarely captures: exactly how much value a failed auction destroys, on a markdown schedule the regulations themselves set out in percentages.

  5. 2013Division bench flags an allegation that roughly ₹2,300 crore of properties were transferred

What it means: The 2013 winding-up order was followed by suspensions, an allegation of transfers and a 2026 judgment that left the key asset contested.

What to do: Verify the Dunlop chain against the court record, since the post relied on secondary sources it did not re-verify.

What would fix this

  • IBBI should record the outcome of every liquidation auction against its notice — sold or unsold, winning bid and buyer category, so that the register shows which attempts cleared and at what discount, not only that an auction was attempted.
  • IBBI should give each asset lot a persistent identifier that carries across re-listings, so that markdown sequences like Athena Chhattisgarh Power's can be followed automatically and bundled re-offers like Ind-Barath Thermal Power's are not mistaken for price changes.
  • IBBI should replace the free-text asset description with mandatory, standardised asset categories, and validate required fields before a notice is published, so that placeholder rows such as a ₹90,000 crore entry against a debtor named with a string of Qs cannot enter the official register.
  • The Ministry of Corporate Affairs should publish a list of companies still in court-ordered winding-up from before the IBC, with the order date, official liquidator and assets not yet realised, so that long-tail cases like Dunlop India, thirteen years on, are visible alongside the IBC register.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

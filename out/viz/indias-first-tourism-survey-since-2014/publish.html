MoSPI’s household survey of 2,46,113 households finds pilgrimage and treatment, not holidays, drive overnight travel. The Tourism Ministry’s count of domestic visits, built from state reports, rose 45.5% to 428.55 crore in 2025, and no published note reconciles the two.

Top line

  • 48% of overnight trips in the 365-day group were for pilgrimage, against 22% for holiday or leisure.
  • ₹29,454 average spend on a rural health trip, against ₹5,179 for a business trip in the 30-day group.

What would fix this

  • The Ministry of Statistics should publish the full survey tables by state, purpose and package or non-package spending, and a note comparing the design with the 2014-15 round (reference periods, trip definitions), so the reported rises in same-day travel can be checked.
  • The Ministry of Tourism should publish, for each state and union territory, how a “domestic tourist visit” is counted and which figures are estimated, in the annual statistics, so state counts can be compared with one another.
  • The Ministry of Tourism and the Ministry of Statistics should publish a joint note reconciling the administrative visit counts with the survey’s trip counts, with the number of trips per person and the share of day visitors in each.
  • The Ministry of Tourism should report its scheme spending by main trip purpose, pilgrimage, health, leisure and business, so spending can be compared with where trips are made.
  • The states with the largest reported visit counts, starting with Uttar Pradesh, should publish the source of each year’s count, including any event-driven jumps such as the 2025 Kumbh, separately from the baseline.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

Sources and caveats

Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation: “Press Note on Domestic Tourism Expenditure Survey July 2025 to June 2026” (23 September 2026, PIB 2313894); “Domestic Tourism Expenditure Survey” (17 December 2025, PIB 2205155, on the survey design); workshop of trainers for the survey and the National Household Travel Survey (29 May 2025, PIB 2132274); titles and dates from this blog’s PIB index. The press note itself is hosted on a site that could not be opened from this blog’s tools, and PIB pages could not be opened either, so the survey results are from press coverage of it (BusinessToday, The Tribune, Chartforest, Trav Talk, Retail Intel and others). Outlets differ on some figures, such as per-trip spending on shopping and health, and on which purpose “leads”, because they quote different reference periods; the figures used here are those that agree across at least two outlets or that are explicitly tied to a reference period. The descriptions of the 365-day and 30-day groups are from that coverage and not from the survey instrument. The MoSPI data portal’s NSS 80th-round dataset lists only telecom and education indicators, not tourism, so no table could be checked there. Ministry of Tourism visit counts (2,948 million in 2024; 428.55 crore in 2025; Uttar Pradesh 647 million and 155.74 crore; Tamil Nadu 307 million in 2024) are from press coverage of its India Tourism Data Compendium and a dataset derived from it; the 2025 figures were not seen in the original. The household count of 25 crore (the 2011 Census figure) is this blog’s assumption for the order-of-magnitude check, and all shares and ratios are this blog’s arithmetic. The survey’s state shares are given as reported, with the base unconfirmed. This article does not say that either count is wrong.

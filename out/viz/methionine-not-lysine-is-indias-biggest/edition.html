What it means: Methionine is made by chemical synthesis from petrochemicals, so it is not a sugar-fermentation business like lysine.

What to do: Check whether any domestic methionine plant or DGTR case exists before treating the gap as settled.

MSG imports are the one large amino-acid line growing fast

What it means: Monosodium glutamate is made by fermentation, yet no Indian producer or trade case turned up in the searches.

What to do: Look for Indian MSG producers and any trade complaint before deciding whether India should respond.

Glycine trade is nearly balanced, and India has opened a case

What it means: The near-zero deficit can hide exports of one glycine grade and imports of another, which the public data cannot separate.

What to do: Track the DGTR glycine anti-dumping investigation opened in September 2026 and the grade and price data it turns on.

What would fix this

  • The Department of Commerce should publish amino-acid imports by origin, tonnage and unit price at the eight-digit line, including methionine, threonine, tryptophan, valine and phenylalanine, so India’s dependence is measured.
  • The Directorate General of Trade Remedies should publish, in the glycine case, the non-confidential split of Indian exports and Chinese imports by grade, so it is clear what is being protected from what.
  • The Department of Chemicals and Petrochemicals should publish a costed route for backward integration into methionine, covering acrolein, methyl mercaptan and hydrogen cyanide, with the sulfur and cyanide supply that it would require.
  • The Petroleum and Explosives Safety Organisation and the Central Pollution Control Board should state the licensing and effluent norms for a plant handling hydrogen cyanide and methyl mercaptan, so the compliance cost is known before a plant is planned.
  • The Department of Animal Husbandry and Dairying should publish feed-additive use by amino acid, so demand for methionine, lysine and the next acids is a measured number and not a market-research estimate.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

What this piece could not establish

This piece sets out the whole set from the same trade registry, how each is made, and what that means for who could make it in India. The answer differs for each, and for two of them it is not what the sugar-mill pitch assumes.

The whole set, and how each is made

Eight amino-acid lines sit in the registry, which holds the 827 import lines that qualified on deficit and other tests. Together they imported $391 million in FY26 and ran a deficit of $354 million. Three of them make up 96% of the deficit: methionine, lysine and MSG. The colour in the chart is the route. Methionine and glycine are made by chemical synthesis, which gives a mixture of the D and L forms; that suits glycine, which has no mirror image, and DL-methionine, which poultry can use in either form. Lysine and the glutamates are made by fermentation. L-cystine comes from hydrolysing protein, and one regulatory review describes it being made from keratin, including human hair, with hydrochloric acid.

Sources and caveats

India trade: this blog’s chemical import-substitution registry (HS-8 lines 29304000, 29224100, 29224220, 29224910, 29224290, 29309014, 29225015, 29224210; FY26 imports, deficit, volume and price change from DGCI&S data; 827 qualifying lines). The $391 million, $354 million, 96% and 48% figures are sums and ratios of those rows. Methionine process: US patents 5990349, 5770769 and 12195422, a process-summary page and a cost-report listing (carbonate-hydantoin route, hydantoin from methylmercaptopropionaldehyde, ammonia, carbon dioxide and hydrogen cyanide; one-stage yield about 85%, hydrolysis up to 95%, temperatures to over 200°C, salty carbonate loop; origin at Degussa in the 1940s). Methionine market: a Feedstuffs report on Evonik’s Singapore expansion (40,000 t to about 340,000 t; a market-research claim of over 40% share), reports on Adisseo’s 150,000 t China plant and the ¥4.9 billion Quanzhou plant (2024 announcement; status not confirmed), an unverified outlook of 380,000 t of new Chinese capacity, and an India methionine market page quoting $2.68 a kilogram for Q1 2026 (unverified; the roughly 60,000 t is this blog’s arithmetic). Fermentation figures: a study extract on the microbial production of seven amino acids (B. divaricatum, 12% glucose, 30–35 h, 100 g/l, about 70% conversion) and Ajinomoto’s page on how amino acids are made (glutamate shifted to fermentation in the 1960s; chemical synthesis kept for glycine and DL-forms). MSG trade cases: US final determinations (8.3% China, 6.19% Indonesia), the EU measures of 2008 and a Vietnamese case opened in 2019; no Indian MSG producer or case was found. Glycine: a report of the DGTR initiation of 22 September 2026 (AD/OI/045/2026, complainant Avid Organics, period 2022–2025), the complainant’s own profile (4,200+ t a year, Vadodara; self-reported), a trade-data profile listing a Delhi address, and US Commission findings of June 2019 on glycine from India and Japan. Cystine: a US organic-standards review describing production from keratin with hydrochloric acid. Feed amino acids: a CCM report on China’s share (76% of lysine, 93% of threonine capacity in 2023), April 2025 Chinese threonine and tryptophan prices from trade press, and reports of the EU duties on Chinese lysine from 12 July 2025 (47.7% to 58.2%, the rates differing slightly between summaries), the 2026 re-opened inquiry and the 29 July 2026 registration of imports, and an aggregator’s July 2026 China FOB lysine price ($870 a tonne; indicative). The earlier DGTR glycine case and the 22–23 September 2026 initiation come from trade-press reports. Most sources were abstracts, patents, vendor pages or search summaries, not full texts. No Indian methionine, MSG or threonine plant was found, which is a limit of the search. No process or investment advice is given.

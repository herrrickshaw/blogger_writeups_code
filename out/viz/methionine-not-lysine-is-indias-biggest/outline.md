The first piece in this series put India’s lysine deficit at $102 million and concluded that lysine is a fermentation business won on scale. Lysine is not the biggest amino-acid gap, though. Methionine is, and it is made in a completely different way, from petrochemicals, with no sugar in it. Monosodium glutamate (MSG) is the third, and it is still growing. Glycine, the smallest, is the only one where India appears to make a lot, and it has just become the subject of an anti-dumping case.

## Methionine: the biggest gap is not a sugar story

India imported $171.3 million of methionine in FY26, a deficit of $170.8 million. Volume rose 6% and the unit price fell 7%, so it is a steady, large and slowly cheapening import. The reason it is not a sugar product is in the process. Industrial DL-methionine is made by the carbonate-hydantoin route, a descendant of a process developed at Degussa in the 1940s. Methylmercaptopropionaldehyde is turned into a hydantoin with ammonia, carbon dioxide and hydrogen cyanide, which is then hydrolysed with potassium carbonate and freed by carbonation. Patents describe one-stage versions from acrolein, methyl mercaptan, hydrogen cyanide and ammonium carbonate at about 85% yield, and hydrolysis at up to 95% on acrolein. The older conditions are harsh, up to over 200°C, and the carbonate loop moves a lot of salty liquor, which is why newer routes from methionine nitrile aim to be salt-free.

To make it, a plant has to be integrated back to acrolein, methyl mercaptan and hydrogen cyanide, which are the hazardous raw materials. That is petrochemistry and sulfur chemistry. A sugar mill brings nothing to it. The producers are few and large: Evonik’s Singapore complex was expanded by 40,000 tonnes to about 340,000 tonnes a year and is reported to hold over 40% of the market, on a single market-research claim. Adisseo built a 150,000-tonne powder plant in China and announced a ¥4.9 billion plant at Quanzhou, whose status was not confirmed. One outlook says two Chinese plants add 380,000 tonnes in 2025–26, which could not be checked. Sources disagree on who the top four are.

For scale, one report puts Indian methionine at $2.68 a kilogram in the first quarter of 2026. At that unverified price, $171 million is on the order of 60,000 tonnes, which would be about a fifth of the Singapore complex’s output. That is this blog’s arithmetic, since the FY26 price is not known. No domestic methionine plant and no DGTR case on methionine was found.

## MSG: the growing one nobody here seems to make

MSG is made by fermentation. The seven-amino-acid study extract gives the textbook figures: Brevibacterium divaricatum on 12% glucose, 30 to 35 hours, 100 g of glutamic acid per litre, and a glucose conversion of about 70%. Ajinomoto says glutamate was the first amino acid to move from extraction to fermentation, in the 1960s.

India imported $67.9 million of MSG in FY26. Volume grew 34% and value 17%, with the unit price down 12.8%. That is the one large amino-acid line that is accelerating. No Indian MSG producer, and no Indian trade case, turned up in the searches. MSG trade is contested elsewhere: the US imposed duties on Chinese and Indonesian MSG (8.3% and 6.19% in the final determination), the EU did so in 2008 after a complaint from its only producer, and Vietnam opened a case in 2019. India has not, as far as was found.

## Glycine: balanced trade and a live case

Glycine is the odd one. India imported $19.0 million in FY26 against a deficit of only $0.6 million, which means it exported almost as much, about $18 million. Volume fell 15% and the unit price 13.2%. The DGTR opened an anti-dumping investigation into glycine from China on 22 September 2026 (reported under case AD/OI/045/2026, a number seen in one notice), on a complaint from Avid Organics, covering 2022 to 2025. This is not the first such case: an earlier DGTR investigation, on an application from Paras Intermediates and Avid Organics covering April 2020 to March 2021, recommended duties, and the Finance Ministry decided not to accept the recommendation. The company describes itself as India’s largest glycine maker, with capacity above 4,200 tonnes a year at a zero-discharge plant in Vadodara; those figures are self-reported and were not independently confirmed.

Why would an exporter ask for protection? The registry cannot say. Glycine is sold in technical, food and pharmaceutical grades at different prices, and a line that nets to zero can hide Indian exports of one grade and Chinese imports of another. The US took a view on Indian glycine in 2019, when its Commission found that imports from India and Japan injured its industry and orders followed. The Indian case will turn on grade and price data that is not public.

## The ones nobody is counting

Threonine, tryptophan and valine are the feed amino acids after lysine and methionine, and China makes most of them: 76% of lysine and 93% of threonine capacity in 2023, by one industry report. Chinese threonine was about 10 yuan a kilogram in April 2025, about 10% below a year earlier, and tryptophan about 45 to 47 yuan, both on oversupply. The EU put definitive duties on Chinese lysine from 12 July 2025 for five years, 47.7% for Meihua and 58.2% for Eppen in one summary of the regulation, and in 2026 re-opened an inquiry into whether they have worked, registering Chinese imports from 29 July. Chinese exporters were reported to have shipped heavily to non-EU markets ahead of the duties, and China FOB lysine was quoted at $870 a tonne in July 2026. How much of it reaches India is not in the data found here.

## Where the data stop

- No import tonnage or origin. The registry gives value, volume change and price change, not tonnes or country. The 60,000-tonne methionine figure rests on one unverified price.

- Threonine, tryptophan, valine and phenylalanine are missing. They fall below the registry’s threshold.

- No Indian capacity figures for methionine or MSG, and the glycine capacity is the complainant’s own claim.

- Glycine grades. Nothing separates technical from food or pharmaceutical glycine in the trade data.

- Vendor and trade-press sources. The methionine producer shares, the Chinese capacity additions and the Indian price all come from market-research or trade summaries that could not be checked against primary sources.

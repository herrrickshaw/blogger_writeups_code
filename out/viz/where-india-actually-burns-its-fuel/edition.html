PPAC’s statewise sales series runs from FY2008-09 to FY2024-25, and it tells a sharper story than the national total: petrol demand grew more than three times faster than diesel, the diesel-to-petrol ratio collapsed from 4.6 to 2.3, the growth frontier moved east to Odisha and Chhattisgarh — and the slowest-growing fuel market in India is Delhi, which is exactly what its gas and metro build-out was meant to achieve.

What would fix this

  • PPAC should publish annual petrol and diesel sales for districts along state borders, so the cross-border buying driven by VAT differences, such as Haryana–Delhi and Puducherry–Tamil Nadu, can be measured rather than acknowledged and left unquantified.
  • PPAC should publish a back-cast of its statewise sales series splitting Andhra Pradesh and Telangana for the years before 2014, so the two states’ sixteen-year trends can be read separately instead of only as a combined figure.
  • PPAC should run and publish a periodic state-wise end-use survey of petrol and diesel by vehicle segment and sector, so the shift from 4.6 to 2.3 tonnes of diesel per tonne of petrol can be attributed to two-wheelers, cars, trucks or tractors with data.
  • PPAC should add a per-capita column to its statewise sales workbook using one stated set of official population projections, so readers can compare states per head without mixing census vintages across the 2014 bifurcation.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

What this piece could not establish
What this article does not establish. Per-capita consumption — that needs state population series, and mixing census vintages across a 2014 state bifurcation produces false precision, so shares and growth are presented instead. Causality for Delhi is characterised (CNG, metro, vehicle rules) but not decomposed into contributions. Sales are attributed to the state of sale, not of use — VAT-differential leakage across borders (Haryana–Delhi, Puducherry–Tamil Nadu) is real and unquantified here. FY2024-25 figures are provisional. Union territories below 2,000 TMT and the states created in 2000 are in the workbook but not in the exhibits.

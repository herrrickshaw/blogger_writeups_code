Two things stand out. Gujarat is India’s largest fuel market, ahead of the considerably more populous Maharashtra — industrial and freight intensity, not headcount. And the old giants grew slowest: Maharashtra and Tamil Nadu at +46% against a national +79%, while Uttar Pradesh doubled and Karnataka grew +128%.

PPAC statewise workbook, MS and HSD sheets. National petrol sales rose 11,258 → 40,004 TMT; diesel 51,710 → 91,408 TMT. The national diesel-to-petrol ratio halved from 4.6 to 2.3.

Sixteen years ago India sold 4.6 tonnes of diesel for every tonne of petrol; it now sells 2.3. The increment is two-wheelers and cars, not trucks and tractors — which is why the [ethanol-blending programme](https://blog.vertistratsolution.com/2026/08/state-by-state-who-actually-earns-from.html) (petrol-side) keeps gaining policy leverage while diesel-side interventions fade, and why the per-market playbook in the [diesel-to-CNG piece](https://blog.vertistratsolution.com/2026/08/diesel-doesnt-move-cpi-it-moves-40.html) matters most in the high-ratio states: Haryana at 3.0 and Rajasthan at 2.9 have the most diesel per litre of petrol left to convert.

## The frontier moved east; the plateau is scattered across the rest of the map

Odisha nearly tripled. Delhi barely grew — on purpose.

PPAC statewise workbook, all-products sheets. The fastest column is dominated by the eastern mineral belt — Odisha, Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand — plus Assam: late motorisation compounding on top of mining and industrial freight.

Delhi is the finding hiding in the slow column. The capital’s fuel sales grew 22% in sixteen years — a period in which its vehicle stock multiplied. The explanation is substitution, not stagnation: Delhi runs India’s densest gas transport network — 458 CNG station clusters and 19.6 lakh piped-gas connections on PNGRB’s May 2026 count — a metro carrying millions of trips, and the country’s most aggressive vehicle-age rules. Delhi is what the [CGD takeover of India’s gas demand](https://blog.vertistratsolution.com/2026/08/city-gas-just-became-indias-largest-gas.html) looks like from the petrol pump’s side of the ledger. Punjab’s +23% is the other kind of plateau — an agrarian, already-motorised economy whose diesel base stopped growing.

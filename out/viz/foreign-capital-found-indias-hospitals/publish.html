Hospitals have been open to 100 per cent automatic-route FDI since January 2000 — longer than almost any other sensitive sector. Twenty-five years on, foreign capital has arrived at scale, but overwhelmingly as private equity and sovereign-fund buyers of existing hospital chains, not as builders of new capacity. The bed shortage the money is supposedly solving is still there.

Foreign capital in India's hospitals: buying existing hospitals, not adding beds

Text Size

Top line

  • $8.89 billion of foreign equity went into hospitals and diagnostic centres from April 2000 to June 2023, while drugs and pharmaceuticals drew $21.6 billion.
  • 2.0% of all FDI in H1 2025 went to hospitals, down from a peak of 4.3% in calendar 2023.
Updated 7 November 2026. A recent deal cuts slightly against this piece’s headline. In September 2026 Advent International agreed to invest ₹3,150 crore of primary capital, new money for the company, in Yatharth Hospitals for a 24.9% stake, subject to closing conditions. Primary capital can fund new beds, unlike a purchase of existing shares. The DPIIT sector data below still run to June 2026; the next fact sheet is expected around the end of November. Source: BioSpectrum India.
Updated 23 October 2026. DPIIT’s fact sheet to June 2026 puts cumulative FDI equity into hospitals and diagnostic centres at $13.23 billion (April 2000 to June 2026), against $25.43 billion for drugs and pharmaceuticals. From January to June 2026, by difference from the December 2025 sheet, hospitals drew $499 million, 1.6% of all equity inflow, continuing the fall from 4.3% in 2023 described below. Over July 2023 to June 2026 as a whole, hospitals took about $4.3 billion (2.7%) and pharmaceuticals about $3.9 billion (2.4%), by subtraction of the June 2023 table (fact sheet, Annex B). What it means: Neither the bed ratio nor the shortfall figures have moved because of foreign capital, since the deals add no beds.

What to do: Pull a sourced, current bed-per-1,000 figure from the government before relying on the 1.4 or 3.5 numbers.

What would fix this

  • The Ministry of Health and Family Welfare's Central Bureau of Health Intelligence should publish an annual state-wise count of hospital beds per 1,000 population, split into government and private, so that the 1.4, 1.59 and 0.79 figures driving the investment debate are replaced by one sourced, current official series.
  • DPIIT should split FDI inflow into Hospital & Diagnostic Centres between acquisitions of existing hospitals and greenfield projects, so that whether foreign capital is adding beds or only buying them can be read from the official fact sheet rather than reconstructed deal by deal.
  • DPIIT should publish its sector-wise FDI tables on a financial-year basis alongside the calendar-year series, so that growth figures quoted in trade press can be checked against the official data instead of being flagged, as here, as a methodology mismatch.
  • The National Health Authority should publish, from the Health Facility Registry, bed counts by facility and ownership category, refreshed annually, so that changes in who owns India's hospital capacity, and whether total capacity is growing, can be tracked from a single public register.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

Sources and caveats

Pre-2017 baseline: DPIIT, Ministry of Commerce and Industry, “FDI Equity Inflows” sector tables on data.gov.in, annual 2000-01 to 2016-17 and cumulative 1 April 2000 to 30 June 2023; the April 2017 to June 2023 amount is this blog’s subtraction of the first from the second, and any restatement of earlier years in the later table would fall in it. The cumulative to June 2023 equals this table’s $8,890 million; the portal’s cumulative to 2016-17 ($4,340 million) is $105 million above the $4,235 million to December 2016 above, which one quarter could add (the 2017 calendar year drew $750 million).

FDI policy detail — hospitals on 100% automatic route since January 2000, greenfield pharma 100% automatic vs brownfield pharma capped at 74% automatic, health insurance under the general 74% automatic insurance cap — is from DPIIT's "Sectors under Automatic Route" annexure to the Consolidated FDI Policy. Sector-wise inflow figures for Hospital & Diagnostic Centres and Drugs & Pharmaceuticals, and hospitals' percentage share of total FDI equity inflow, are compiled directly from DPIIT's published fact-sheet tables (cumulative Jan2000–Dec2022, Jan2000–Jun2023, CY2023, H1 2024 and H1 2025); DPIIT publishes on a calendar-year basis and this article does not attempt to reconcile that against financial-year figures reported elsewhere. Deal-table entries are compiled from contemporaneous business and trade press (Business Standard, Bloomberg, Reuters via TradingView, DealStreetAsia, Trilegal's own deal announcement, BioSpectrum India, Mubadala's press release, Medical Buyer, BusinessToday); deal sizes described as "combined platform" or "combined entity" values are noted as such rather than presented as a single clean transaction price, and this article did not independently verify every stake percentage against a primary regulatory filing. The bed-per-1,000 figures were checked against the World Bank's own WHO-sourced series (indicator SH.MED.BEDS.ZS): India 1.59 (2021, latest published year, down from 2.13 in 2000), world average 3.29 (2021), OECD average 4.66 (2021). The commonly cited "1.4" (India, industry press) and "3.5" (the reference figure, often mislabelled a WHO minimum standard) both approximate these official series closely but are not identical to the latest published data point; the 0.79 government-only figure is a narrower trade-press citation not separately broken out in the World Bank series. No authoritative, source-attributed health-insurance-penetration percentage was found and none is asserted here. The roughly 30,000-bed, ₹35,000 crore capex pipeline and the "nearly half of leading chains now PE-owned" claim are as reported in trade press (DealFlowIQ's India Hospitals Report 2025, BusinessToday's August 2025 reporting on hospital consolidation) and are presented as industry reporting, not independently audited figures.

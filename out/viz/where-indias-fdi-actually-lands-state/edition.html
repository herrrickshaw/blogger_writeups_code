Twenty-four DPIIT quarterly newsletters, parsed and cross-checked against DPIIT's own printed sub-totals, now cover almost the whole run from October 2019 to March 2026. The picture that held up in the five-quarter version of this piece holds up even more strongly at full scale: four districts — Navi Mumbai, Bengaluru Urban, Delhi, Ahmedabad — have captured 72.6% of every rupee of FDI equity DPIIT has ever attributed to a specific district.

Top line

  • 72.6% of the ₹25,80,189 crore of FDI equity inflow recorded from October 2019 to March 2026 went to four districts: Navi Mumbai, Bengaluru Urban, Delhi and Ahmedabad.
  • 84.6% of Maharashtra’s own total is Navi Mumbai alone, and three districts make up 98.7% of the state, which is itself 31.7% of the national total.
  • 30.0% of the April–June 2026 quarter went to Tamil Nadu, its first quarter as the top state, against 7.0% of the total since October 2019.

How the figures were verified

The dataset was supplied directly, because dpiit.gov.in could not be reached, and was parsed from DPIIT’s own PDFs. Every figure checked from three quarters read directly matched exactly, and summing its quarters into fiscal years reproduced five independently sourced years: FY2021-22 $58.77bn, FY2022-23 $46.03bn, FY2023-24 $44.42bn, FY2024-25 $50.02bn and FY2025-26 $58.85bn.

This piece did not re-read all 24 source PDFs, and relies on the dataset’s own account of its parser checks, which put agreement with DPIIT’s printed totals at 0.00–0.03%.

What this does not establish
  • DPIIT attributes inflow to the recipient company’s registered office, so a holding company in Navi Mumbai or Bengaluru Urban can direct capital deployed anywhere; this dataset cannot separate that from local investment.
  • The January–June 2020 gap means no comparison across it is a clean three-month step.
  • The Ahmedabad July–September 2020 spike and the Parbhani and Kolhapur swings are reported as facts, not explained, and no transaction behind any of them is identified.
  • This is equity inflow only; total FDI, which also includes re-invested earnings and other capital, is larger and differently distributed, and is not broken down by state or district here.

Four fixes

  1. DPIIT: publish the state and district-wise FDI equity tables for January–March 2020 and April–June 2020.
  2. DPIIT: release the full state and district-wise series as a downloadable CSV file updated each quarter, instead of 3,320 rows locked in PDFs.
  3. DPIIT: add the state and district where the investment is deployed, alongside the recipient company’s registered office.
  4. DPIIT and the Reserve Bank of India: publish a state-wise breakdown of total FDI, including reinvested earnings and other capital, not only equity inflows.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

April–June 2026 by state is the difference between the two Annex C tables.

The full dataset behind every table on this page — all 3,320 quarter/state/district rows, plus the state and district cumulative totals — is a single JSON file, about 1 MB, served from herrrickshaw.github.io/masaladeutsch/data/dpiit_fdi_district.json. It is plain and freely downloadable; every table in this piece was built from it directly, so it carries no rounding or curation this page's own tables don't already reflect — take it if you want to slice the data yourself (e.g. a state or district this piece didn't have room to feature).

The consolidated 24-quarter, 3,320-row dataset this piece is built on (a CSV/Excel workbook and its accompanying methodology report) was supplied directly to this piece; it was not fetched from dpiit.gov.in by this session, which remains blocked from that domain across every route tried (see this piece's prior revision for that record). This piece independently re-derived every figure presented in §2 through §6 directly from the dataset's own row-level Raw Data sheet (3,320 rows), rather than copying its pre-built summary sheets verbatim, and cross-checked the results two ways: against three quarters of DPIIT PDFs this piece had itself read directly in an earlier pass (exact match on every figure checked), and against five fiscal years of national and state-level FDI figures this piece had separately compiled via web search in an earlier pass (matching to within rounding on every figure checked, several to the exact decimal — see §7 for the specific numbers). The dataset's own claimed internal validation — reconciliation against DPIIT's printed sub-totals and grand totals to 0.00–0.03% agreement across all 24 quarters, and its documented district-name-normalization decisions (e.g. Gurgaon→Gurugram, Bangalore Urban/Rural kept distinct, Dadra & Nagar Haveli/Daman & Diu's 2020 administrative merger reflected rather than force-corrected) — is reported as the dataset's own process and was not independently re-run against the raw PDFs by this piece. Nothing in this piece is investment advice; a reader relying on a specific figure for a real decision should consult DPIIT's own current Quarterly FDI Newsletter directly, at dpiit.gov.in, rather than this summary.

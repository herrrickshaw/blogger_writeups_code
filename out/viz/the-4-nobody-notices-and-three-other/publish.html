A bad monsoon, a pest outbreak, a family that migrates for work — none of these are new problems, and India has built four very different systems to sit between a shock like that and the people it would otherwise hit hardest. One quietly pools risk across every insurance policy in the country. One pays farmers directly when a crop fails. One tries to stop the failure from happening at all. One makes sure that even when everything else works, the food itself still reaches whoever needs it, wherever they've moved. None of the four is usually described as part of the same system. This is what they look like put next to each other.

Top line

  • 4% of every general insurance policy's business is ceded to GIC Re, the same rate for FY2026-27 as for FY2025-26.
  • 1.5-5% of the actuarial premium is paid by farmers under PMFBY; the Centre and states pay the rest.
  • 25-35% yield gains in NITI Aayog-documented drone pilots, against 10-15% in independent studies.
Updated 7 November 2026. The general insurance market GIC Re’s 4% cession is drawn from has grown: the General Insurance Council’s provisional data put non-life gross direct premium at about ₹3.36 lakh crore in 2025-26, up 9.3%, against the ₹2.90 lakh crore for 2023-24 cited below (Informist, CARE Ratings); the sentence has been updated. At 4%, that is roughly ₹13,400 crore of premium ceded to GIC Re a year, before exemptions.
Updated 27 October 2026. The Krishi Decision Support System was described as bringing “scheme-eligibility data” into one platform with satellite, weather, soil and groundwater data. Its layers cover crops, soil, weather, water resources and drought and flood monitoring; scheme eligibility is handled by the AgriStack Farmers’ Registry, a separate component of the Digital Agriculture Mission. The mission’s ₹2,817 crore outlay and the One Nation One Ration Card dates were confirmed.

The obligatory cession fell from roughly 20% to 4% and has held there since FY2022-23

What it means: The rate stepped down from roughly 20% to 4%, and IRDAI has confirmed 4% for FY2026-27 as well.

What to do: Do not read a change to the 4% rate as a change to what a PMFBY claimant receives.

What it means: PMFBY is structurally much closer to a government subsidy routed through insurers than to insurance as most people understand it.

What to do: Judge PMFBY's claim totals against the public premium that funded them, not against farmers' payments.

Precision-farming yield gains range from 10-15% to 25-35% by study type

What it means: Reported gains differ widely by who measured them, so no single yield figure is the expected gain from precision farming.

What to do: Ask for independent measurement before relying on the 25-35% pilot figures.

What would fix this

  • The Department of Food and Public Distribution should publish ONORC portable transactions split into inter-state and intra-state, by state and month, so that the 124 crore cumulative figure shows how much is actually migrants drawing rations across state lines.
  • The Ministry of Agriculture and Farmers Welfare should publish PMFBY statistics as downloadable tables of gross premium, farmer, state and Centre shares, and claims paid, by state and season, since the national portal’s statistics pages could not be queried for this post.
  • The Ministry of Agriculture and Farmers Welfare should publish, each season, the number of delayed PMFBY claims and the 12% interest penalty paid by each insurance company, so that the Kharif-2024 penalty’s effect on the gap between approved and received claims can be checked.
  • IRDAI should publish, alongside each obligatory-cession notification, the premium ceded to GIC Re and the commission paid back to insurers in the previous year, so that the dispute over whether the 4% cession is unfavourable can be judged on figures rather than lobbying.
  • NITI Aayog should commission an independent evaluation of Kisan Drone and Namo Drone Didi outcomes using one stated yield-measurement method, so that reported gains ranging from 10% to 35% can be replaced by a figure measured the same way across sites.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

Sources and caveats

This piece was researched entirely through web search rather than direct page fetches: the network this article was written from could not reach several primary and trade-press domains directly (a limitation consistent with other recent pieces on this blog), so every claim above rests on search-result summaries and cross-checking across multiple independent secondary outlets rather than a primary document read in full, except where a specific government explainer or PIB release is cited as the origin of a figure that release itself states directly.

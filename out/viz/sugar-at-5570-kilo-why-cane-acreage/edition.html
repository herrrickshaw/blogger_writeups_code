India's sugarcane acreage barely moved this season, sugar prices just jumped 10 per cent in a month to record levels, and the years-long story of sugar being diverted to ethanol is quietly running in reverse. Cane acreage is flat, mills are earning more from sugar than from ethanol for the first time in years, and one industry body is warning that juice- and B-heavy-molasses-based ethanol could stop altogether if prices stay where they are.

Updated 7 November 2026. The 2025-26 season has closed and 2026-27 has begun. ISMA put 2025-26 net sugar production (after diversion to ethanol) at about 279 lakh tonnes, against an initial gross estimate of 345 lakh tonnes cut to about 309 lakh tonnes, and the season ended with about 36 lakh tonnes of carryover stock. The sugar industry’s share of ethanol supply fell to about 25% in 2025-26, from over 80% in 2021-22 (Free Press Journal, 24 Aug 2026; Business Today, 26 Oct 2026). Prices rose again in October: ex-mill rates climbed by nearly ₹1,000 a quintal in ten days, and the Centre released 14 lakh tonnes for sale between 13 and 31 October (NewsBytes, 14 Oct 2026). The diversion question in Section 5 has now been settled by policy as well as price: the 2026-27 ethanol tender buys no ethanol from cane juice, sugar syrup or B-heavy molasses (see this blog’s piece on the tender). Uttar Pradesh had not announced its 2026-27 State Advised Price by early November; the 2025-26 rates of ₹400 and ₹390 a quintal still apply as the latest. The original text below is unchanged.
Updated 22 October 2026. The sugar import this piece describes as a possibility had already been decided. A DGFT notification of 20 August 2026 allowed duty-free imports of 10 lakh tonnes of raw sugar, only by companies with their own refining capacity and valid until 31 October 2026, according to Business Today and News on AIR. Press coverage describes these as the first such imports for domestic use in about a decade. ISMA's late-August projection of about 35 lakh tonnes of closing stock at end-September is in line with the 3–3.5 million tonne estimate quoted here. The original text below is unchanged.
Updated 12 October 2026. The August price spike described below has partly reversed. The government said in late August that ex-mill prices had fallen about 20% from their peak after stock limits and other supply measures, and trade reports put ex-factory prices at about ₹5,100 a quintal in Maharashtra and Karnataka on 27 August. The coverage uses a different price series from the ₹5,400 and ₹5,300 factory-gate figures quoted here, so the two are not directly comparable. Read “record levels” and “if prices stay where they are” as August-specific; the case that mills favour sugar over ethanol in 2026-27 is weaker, not removed. The exact current price has not been verified. Sources: Free Press Journal, Rural Voice, 27 Aug 2026. The original text below is unchanged.

What would fix this

  • The Department of Food and Public Distribution should publish fortnightly, state-wise sugar production, sugar diverted to ethanol and mill-held stocks during each crushing season as downloadable data, so figures such as Uttar Pradesh’s output are exact and need not reach the public through trade press.
  • The Department of Food and Public Distribution should publish a monthly official estimate of projected closing sugar stocks against domestic requirement, so a squeeze like the industry’s 3–3.5 million tonne estimate against roughly 5 million tonnes of need is visible before stock limits and import talk follow.
  • The Department of Food and Public Distribution should maintain one authoritative series of sugar diverted to ethanol by Ethanol Supply Year, with revisions noted, so the 2022-23 figure is not quoted as 43 lakh tonnes in one government statement and 45 in other summaries.
  • The Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas should publish each year the costing basis for ethanol procurement prices by feedstock route, set beside that season’s cane FRP, so the distillers’ claim of static ethanol prices against a 16 per cent rise in cane costs can be checked.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

What this piece does not establish. Acreage, production, and diversion figures throughout are drawn from ChiniMandi's own reporting on Ministry of Agriculture, ISMA, and government (Lok Sabha) statements, not from a direct pull of the underlying agmarknet.gov.in, ISMA member-portal, or DFPD dataset, and this piece did not independently re-verify any of them against those primary sources. Two different web searches conducted for this piece returned slightly different 2022-23 sugar-diversion figures (43 lakh tonnes from a directly quoted government statement versus 45 lakh tonnes from an aggregated secondary summary) — the government-statement figure (43 LMT) is used here as the more directly sourced number, but the discrepancy itself wasn't resolved. Uttar Pradesh's 2025-26 production figure is reported as "similar to last year" rather than an exact tonnage, because the source consulted for this piece did not give a precise same-date comparison the way it did for Maharashtra and Karnataka. The claim that firm sugar prices are causing lower ethanol diversion for ESY 2026-27 rests on industry-expert commentary and stated expectations as of August 2026, not a confirmed ESY 2026-27 diversion figure, which won't be known until the season plays out. This piece does not cover the separate SGST/state-revenue angle on ethanol blending, which this blog has reported on at length elsewhere (see related posts below); it focuses specifically on the cane-cultivation, sugar-price, and feedstock-diversion side of the story.

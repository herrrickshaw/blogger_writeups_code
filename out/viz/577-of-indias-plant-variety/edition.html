Top line

  • 57.7% of India's 10,885 plant-variety certificates are cereals, 6,280 in all, while farmers hold 49.3%.
  • 151 spice certificates, a dated 31 July 2026 snapshot, against 6,280 for cereals.

Spices hold 151 certificates against 6,280 for cereals

What it means: Spices appear only as a dated July snapshot of 151 certificates, while cereals stand at 6,280 in August.

What to do: Treat the spice IP gap as a named, quantifiable constraint and track it on a dated basis.

What would fix this

  • The PPV&FR Authority should restore the separate counts for Spices, Trees, Flowers, Cash crops and Medicinal & Aromatic plants on its homepage, so that the 151 spice certificates reported in July 2026 can still be tracked rather than disappearing into a single “Other” line of 242.
  • The PPV&FR Authority should publish a dated monthly archive of certificate counts by crop category and registration type, as a downloadable file with a methodology note defining each category, so readers are not relying on a live counter with no vintage archive that is intermittently unavailable.
  • The PPV&FR Authority should add a crop-wise table to its Annual Report listing certificates for each export spice crop, split by Farmer, New, Extant Notified, Extant VCK and EDV routes, so the spice gap can be read crop by crop and route by route.
  • ICAR should publish, institute by institute, how many of the spice varieties it has released have been filed for PPV&FR registration and how many certified, so the gap between varieties bred and varieties protected in a $4.45 billion export trade is measured rather than assumed.
  • The PPV&FR Authority should report each year how many applications sought Essentially Derived Variety status and how many were granted or refused, so readers can tell whether the 14 EDV certificates reflect little derived breeding or a doctrine rarely invoked.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

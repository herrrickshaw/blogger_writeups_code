India grows more coconuts than any other country, yet still imports copra and coconut oil-cake. Kerala holds the most land under coconut and the worst yield of the big four states. The Coconut Development Board just got its budget for replanting rewritten by a factor of eight. This is what the actual numbers say about where the opportunity is.

India's coconut sector: largest producer in the world by count, and an importer of copra

Skip to article content
Text Size

Top line

  • 9,664 nuts per hectare in Kerala, the lowest yield of the big four, on the largest coconut area of any state.
  • ₹56,000 per hectare: the area-expansion grant, raised from ₹6,500 in September 2025.
  • US$55.43mn of coconut oil-cake imports, India's largest coconut import line, while it exports refined oil.
Updated 7 November 2026. Two corrections and a wider export figure. The ₹6,500-to-₹56,000 per hectare planting grant was raised by the Coconut Development Board nationally in September 2025, not by Kerala’s government; the Budget 2026-27 Coconut Promotion Scheme builds on those rates, but its operational guidelines were still unpublished in August 2026 (Krishi Jagran; IMPRI). The shell-charcoal imports feed India’s coconut-shell activated-carbon exports, so they do not point to a capacity gap. On the Board’s wider basket, coconut-product exports rose 62% to about ₹7,038 crore (about US$795–825 million) in 2025-26, led by activated carbon (FreshPlaza; NewsBytes), far above the eleven codes in the table below. The government’s August 2026 factsheet names Andhra Pradesh as the most productive state, and 2,175 ha of new planting in 2025-26 (IANS).

Updated 15 November 2026: rewritten as charts; figures unchanged.

Tamil Nadu out-yields Kerala per hectare on less land

What it means: Kerala has the most coconut area of any state but the lowest yield among the big four producing states.

What to do: Treat the gap between the best and worst major state as the expansion target, not new acreage.

The Coconut Development Board raised its area-expansion grant eightfold

What it means: The Board moved money, not only advice, which signals that the productivity problem needs funding.

What to do: Watch whether the new rate moves replanting, since ageing palms may remain the binding constraint.

What to do: Check next year's trade data for whether oil-cake and copra imports shrink as new processing capacity comes online.

What would fix this

  • The Coconut Development Board should publish its state-wise area, production and productivity statistics as a downloadable table for a single stated reference year, including Andhra Pradesh and the smaller producing states, so the gap between Kerala’s ~9,664 and Tamil Nadu’s ~13,775 nuts per hectare can be read from primary data.
  • The Coconut Development Board and the Directorate of Economics and Statistics should publish one reconciled national production estimate with a revision note, so 2024-25 output is not quoted anywhere between 20,301 and 21,373 million nuts depending on the source.
  • The Coconut Development Board should report Replanting and Rejuvenation outcomes by state each year — hectares replanted against target, palms removed and yields before and after — so the 25,517 hectares and ₹130.46 crore spent over five years can be judged by what they did to productivity.
  • The Coconut Development Board should publish New Coconut Promotion Scheme guidelines with a list of processing units sanctioned, by product and state, so whether new capacity addresses the $56.8 million of oil-cake and $25.9 million of shell-charcoal imports can be checked against later trade data.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

Sources and caveats

National production/area figures and the India-vs-Indonesia-vs-Philippines nut-count-vs-tonnage distinction are sourced to PIB releases, a strong primary source. The New Coconut Promotion Scheme details (Union Budget 2026-27, the roughly eightfold area-subsidy increase, the ~sixfold seedling-subsidy increase, the ₹350 crore shared allocation) are sourced directly to PIB (PRID=2241416). The Coconut Palm Insurance Scheme premium-split and sum-insured figures are sourced to a Lok Sabha unstarred-question PDF (commerce.gov.in) and a parliamentary reply hosted on eparlib.sansad.in, both strong-to-moderate primary/near-primary sources, corroborated by a GKToday explainer. The Replanting & Rejuvenation Scheme's per-hectare and per-palm figures, the PICCS intercropping subsidy, the Producer Society/Federation/Company structure (drawn from CDB's own ProducerSocieties.aspx and an associated FPO concept note), the Neera tapping-licence counts, and the Cocomitra/Friends of Coconut Tree programme are sourced to a mix of CDB-adjacent pages and moderate-strength secondary reporting (IndiaFilings, IBEF, Deccan Herald, All India Radio/Newsonair) and were not independently cross-checked against primary CDB scheme-guideline documents in this research pass. The state-wise area/production/productivity table is compiled from multiple secondary sources (Vajiram & Ravi, IBEF, TNAU's agricultural extension pages) citing Coconut Development Board and Directorate of Economics & Statistics data; this article attempted to fetch CDB's own live Statistics.aspx page directly and reached only a search interface rather than the underlying dataset, so individual state-level cells in the table above should be treated as moderate confidence, not as this article's own primary-sourced figures, pending direct CDB verification. The ₹6,500 to ₹56,000/hectare area-expansion increase was announced by the Coconut Development Board on World Coconut Day, 2 September 2025 (corrected 7 November 2026 from an earlier attribution to Kerala’s government; Krishi Jagran). The coconut trade table (HS 0801, 1203, 1513, 2306, 4402 series) is this article's own analysis of the DGCI&S 8-digit trade dataset used elsewhere on this blog, FY2025-26. This article does not size an investment opportunity, recommend a cultivation or trading decision, or draw a policy conclusion beyond what the cited data supports; nothing here is agricultural, investment or trade advice.

India is the world's largest milk producer, and the government's own December 2025 answer to the Rajya Sabha proves it with a state-by-state livestock count. What it doesn't mention: that count is the 20th Livestock Census, taken in 2019. The 21st Census was launched in October 2024 with a target completion date of 31 March 2025 — and on that very date, in a separate written Lok Sabha reply, the ministry itself confirmed the enumeration was still ongoing, and fell back on 2019 numbers to answer the question in front of it. Laid against the government's own current-year production data, the stale headcount and the fresh output numbers tell two different stories about which states actually run India's dairy sector.

What would fix this

  • The Department of Animal Husbandry and Dairying should state publicly whether 21st Livestock Census enumeration is complete and publish a dated timetable for releasing its results, since the 31 March 2025 completion target passed with no published result set and Parliament is still being answered with 2019 figures.
  • DAHD should release provisional state-wise totals for cattle, buffalo, sheep and goats from the 21st Livestock Census ahead of the full reports, given that its mobile-app, GPS-tagged collection should allow faster tabulation than the paper-based 20th Census.
  • DAHD should publish 21st Livestock Census results at district level from the start, so district-specific Parliament questions, like the one on Sriganganagar and Hanumangarh, can be answered with current counts rather than six-year-old ones.
  • DAHD's Basic Animal Husbandry Statistics should report milk growth, yield per animal and per-capita availability for every state in every table, not a charted subset, so states such as Andhra Pradesh and Bihar are not missing from comparisons of scale against efficiency.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

What this piece could not establish
What this article does not establish. Whether the 21st Census's enumeration itself has since finished sometime after the 25 March 2025 “still ongoing” reply, with only publication pending, or whether fieldwork is still incomplete as of this article's research — no later government statement on enumeration status was located either way. Whether the population changes implied by six years of 12.77% CAGR production growth are evenly distributed across states or concentrated in a few — the 2019 census cannot answer this, and no interim state-wise population update was located. Why Andhra Pradesh's and Bihar's per-capita/growth figures are marked unavailable in Exhibit 2 — BAHS 2025's charted state subsets differ slightly across its milk-share, growth and per-capita graphs, and this article reports only what each specific chart covers rather than interpolating. Whether the 20th-Census population rankings (Exhibit 1) still hold in the same order today — plausible given the sector's growth has been broad-based per the report's own framing, but not verifiable without the 21st Census.

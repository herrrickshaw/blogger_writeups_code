On 30 April 2026, lemons were selling for ₹250/kg in the open market at Tamil Nadu's Puliyangudi market — a drought had cut the daily influx from 150-200 tonnes to 75. A year earlier, in the same state's Dindigul district, farmers were switching out of guava and tomato into lemon at a steady ₹90/kg, for a much duller reason: a fallen lemon survives three days on the ground, a fallen guava starts rotting immediately. Both stories are true, and they're the same crop.

What to do: Check borewell and dam supply before planting, since the post's drought case shows growers' wells failing alongside the market.

Agmarknet and eNAM put the national lemon average ₹5,500 a quintal apart

What it means: The two official platforms disagree on the national lemon average because they sample different mandis and calculate modal price differently.

What to do: Check both Agmarknet and eNAM, and which markets each samples, before quoting a national lemon price.

What would fix this

  • The Ministry of Agriculture and Farmers Welfare should publish, for Agmarknet and eNAM, the list of markets each covers for a commodity and how each computes its national modal price, with a reconciled lemon figure, so two official systems are not left nearly ₹5,500 a quintal apart on the same fruit.
  • The Ministry of Agriculture’s horticulture statistics division should report lime and lemon area and production by state separately from other citrus in its annual horticulture estimates, so national output is an official figure rather than an aggregator’s 3–3.5 million tonnes, and Andhra Pradesh’s 7.31 and 9.06 lakh tonne figures can be reconciled.
  • APEDA should publish citrus exports split into lime/lemon and other citrus, by destination and volume, in its export statistics, so the reported 99.45% concentration in Bangladesh and Nepal, and any growth from GI-tagged shipments such as the October 2025 UK consignment, can be checked against a primary register.
  • The National Horticulture Board should state in its MIDH cost norms the specific assistance available for establishing lime and lemon orchards, including drip irrigation, so growers exposed to drought-driven swings like Puliyangudi’s can see what support applies before they plant.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

What this piece does not establish. National production and area figures for lime/lemon specifically (as distinct from all citrus, a broader category that includes oranges and mandarins and would overstate the lime/lemon figure if conflated) come from market-data aggregators rather than a single verified government census release, and different sources gave different totals for Andhra Pradesh's output that were not reconciled. The ₹15 lakh/acre claim for a specific lemon variety, raised in this piece's source research, could not be verified and was dropped rather than repeated. The four grower case studies in Section 2 are real, sourced, individual accounts, not a representative sample of lemon-farming outcomes nationally — readers should not extrapolate typical returns from four examples spanning different states, varieties and years. Price figures in Section 3 come from two specific, dated local-market snapshots (Dindigul 2025, Tenkasi 2026) plus one-day national snapshots from Agmarknet and eNAM (18 August 2026 and early August 2026 respectively) — none of these is a full price history, and the Agmarknet/eNAM gap itself shows that even official sources don't agree on a single national number at a given moment. This piece did not access either platform's raw historical time series directly (both were read via their public dashboards/aggregator summaries), so seasonal or multi-year price trends are not established here, only point-in-time snapshots. The MIDH horticulture scheme is mentioned as context for the sector broadly; no lemon-specific MIDH subsidy figure was verified for this piece.

PNGRB’s own August 2026 pipeline report puts India’s gas grid at 28,157 km operating and 7,033 km of common-carrier line still to build. The North-East grid is the one big active build; four authorised lines have laid no pipe at all; and the pipelines PNGRB bid out ran at 5% of capacity. Gazette land notices show where the next pipe is going.

Top line

  • 28,157 km of 34,978 km authorised gas pipeline is operating, as of June 2026.
  • 5% of capacity on the lines PNGRB bid out was used in August 2026.
Updated 9 November 2026. New Gazette notices published between 30 October and 5 November 2026 have been added to the right-of-user table. They cover three lines. The North-East grid’s Siliguri–Gangtok line in West Bengal moved to its section 6(1) declaration (S.O. 5949(E), 30 October). IGGL’s Duliajan feeder line got a section 6(4) order letting it share 37.7 km of ONGC’s existing route in Sivasagar and Jorhat (S.O. 6003(E), 3 November). The Kandla–Gorakhpur LPG line had a batch of notices dated 30 October: section 3(1) and 6(1) in Gujarat, and section 3(1) in Prayagraj, Uttar Pradesh. A BPCL product line from Malkapur to Hyderabad airport in Telangana is also new (S.O. 6029(E), 3 November). Notices from late September and October that the table had missed are added too. GAIL has two last-mile gas lines: from the Mumbai–Nagpur–Jharsuguda line to JSW Steel at Raigarh (S.O. 5338(E)), and to Hindalco at Kansariguda in Rayagada (S.O. 5337(E)), both section 3(1) of 28 September. The Durgapur–Haldia section of GAIL’s Jagdishpur–Haldia–Bokaro–Dhamra line had a section 6(1) declaration in Howrah (S.O. 5618(E), 13 October). The Barmer–Bhogat crude line of Vedanta and ONGC had a section 6(1) declaration in Barmer and Balotra (S.O. 5637(E), 14 October). ONGC’s field and gathering lines remain left out, as before. The Duliajan line is a 175 km feeder line, authorised by PNGRB on 20 February 2026, to bring Oil India’s gas into the grid. The count of new trunk lines below does not include feeder lines. No PNGRB pipeline report newer than August 2026 was found, so the network figures are unchanged. Sources: the Gazette notices via Gazette Tracker, and ETEnergyWorld for the feeder line.

Updated 15 November 2026: rewritten as charts; figures unchanged.

Bid-out pipelines use 5% of capacity; older lines run at 50% or more

What it means: Pipelines PNGRB awarded by competitive bidding carried 15 MMSCMD against 315 MMSCMD of capacity in August 2026.

What to do: Match new trunk-line approvals to markets that already take gas before more capacity is authorised.

Four authorised lines have not lowered any pipe, covering 1,629 km

What it means: The four lines total 1,629 km, and none had lowered pipe as of June 2026.

What to do: Ask PNGRB to confirm whether these four authorisations are still live, since Ennore–Nellore was reported cancelled in 2019.

Two lines hold the largest unbuilt lengths, one with no pipe lowered

What it means: The North-East grid has 1,139 km of pipe lowered, the largest active build, while Mallavaram–Bhilwara has none.

What to do: Judge each line by pipe lowered rather than authorised length when deciding which projects are moving.

What would fix this

  • PNGRB should publish its monthly pipeline report as a machine-readable table, with every past target date for each line beside the current one, so that a target of February 2013 still printed in 2026 is visible as thirteen years of slippage.
  • PNGRB should decide the authorisations with no pipe laid — Kakinada–Vijayawada–Nellore, Kanai Chhata–Panitar and Ennore–Nellore — by cancelling or re-bidding them, so its committee’s alternate routes can be offered.
  • PNGRB should require bid-out pipelines to report contracted and used capacity separately, so the 5% utilisation of competitively bid lines can be traced to missing demand, missing connections or missing contracts.
  • The petroleum ministry should publish an index of its Right of User Act notices by pipeline, section and date, so that land acquisition can be tracked against construction.
  • PNGRB should report the outcome of the Ennore–Barshi and Jammu–Srinagar bids, which closed in May 2024.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

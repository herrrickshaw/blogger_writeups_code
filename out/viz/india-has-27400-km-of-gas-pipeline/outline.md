India’s natural gas grid is mostly built, slowly being finished, and barely being added to. PNGRB’s own monthly pipeline report for August 2026 puts 28,157 km in operation out of 34,978 km authorised, as of June 2026. Its common-carrier lines have 7,033 km still to build, spread over 15 pipelines; almost all of that length belongs to lines authorised between 2011 and 2020, and 4 of them, with 1,629 km between them, have not lowered a single kilometre of pipe. Only one new gas trunk line has been authorised since 2023, and two others bid out in 2023–24 have no award.

The map below shows where the work is. It combines the regulator’s line-by-line status, the lines PNGRB has authorised or bid out but nobody has started, the routes its 2025 expert committee proposes for 2040, and the Gazette notices by which the petroleum ministry acquires the right to lay pipe under private land.

On the North-East grid, 1,139 km of pipe has been lowered and 795 km hydrotested beyond the 392 km in operation — the largest active build in the country. GAIL is finishing the Kochi line to Bengaluru (288 km lowered of 429), Srikakulam–Angul and the Jagdishpur–Dhamra tails, and has lowered 152 of the 160 km of Gurdaspur–Jammu. The pipelines PNGRB has awarded by competitive bidding since 2008 carried 15 MMSCMD in August 2026 against 315 MMSCMD of capacity, about 5%. Line by line, Dahej–Uran–Panvel–Dabhol ran at 124% of its capacity, GSPL’s high-pressure Gujarat grid at 89% and the HVJ trunk at 70%; Mehsana–Bhatinda and Ennore–Tuticorin at 7%, Mumbai–Nagpur–Jharsuguda and the North-East grid at 4%, and Srikakulam–Angul at 0%. The newest lines were built to reach markets that do not yet take much gas, which is the same pattern this blog found in LNG terminals.

## City gas: the other pipe being laid

Most of the pipe going into the ground in India is not trunk line but city gas distribution. PNGRB’s city gas report for August 2026 shows its authorised networks with 3,30,815 inch-km of steel pipeline laid against a pro-rata target of 4,10,808 inch-km (81%) and a full work-programme target of 5,47,158 inch-km. They have built 9,115 CNG stations against a pro-rata target of 7,698, but connected 1.75 crore homes to piped gas against a pro-rata target of 4.62 crore (38%). The newest areas, authorised in 2024 across the North-East, Sikkim and Jammu and Kashmir, report almost nothing built yet, which matches the North-East trunk grid still being under construction.

## What the Gazette shows: land being acquired now

Before a pipeline can be laid across private land, the petroleum ministry publishes a notice under section 3(1) of the 1962 Right of User Act naming the villages and survey numbers on the route, then a section 6(1) declaration once objections are settled. This piece found notices from 2025–26 for at least six gas projects (counting the North-East grid’s sections once) and eleven crude, product, ethane or LPG lines.

## Bids and approvals: few new lines

The only new gas trunk line authorised since 2023 is IOCL’s 425 km Kochi–Kanyakumari–Thoothukudi line, dated 9 March 2026 in PNGRB’s report, which would connect Petronet’s under-used Kochi LNG terminal to IOCL’s Ennore–Tuticorin system; IOCL’s board approved ₹2,448.70 crore for it on 21 September 2026. PNGRB’s 2026 bidding round has been for LPG pipelines. From 1 January 2026 PNGRB also cut the unified gas transport tariff from three zones to two, about ₹54/MMBtu up to 300 km and ₹102.86 beyond, with city gas paying the first rate everywhere.

## What doesn’t follow from any of this

The map is schematic: each line is drawn through the towns its route names, not along its surveyed alignment, and the Gazette markers sit at district level. Line status and utilisation come from PNGRB’s August 2026 reports, which rest on data the pipeline companies submit; target dates are printed as the companies last gave them and many have passed. Lowered and hydrotested lengths may be defined differently by different companies. The Gazette entries were found through search summaries and then checked against the Gazette’s own text where the SansadSaar index holds the notice; three were not found there. A right-of-user notice shows land being acquired, not pipe being laid.

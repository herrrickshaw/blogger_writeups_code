Nano urea was sold on a substitution claim: replace half your conventional urea with a 500 ml bottle. In March 2026 the government cut the permitted nano dose from 50 per cent to 25. The independent trial that prompted it found 50 per cent substitution cut rice yield 13 per cent and wheat 17.2 per cent. The interesting part is that IFFCO’s own funded research says roughly the same thing.

Nano urea: a supplement, not a substitute, under the rules revised in March 2026

Skip to article content
Text Size

Top line

  • 16–19% yield penalty in IFFCO's own funded rice trial when nitrogen was cut by half and replaced with nano urea sprays.
  • 25% is the nano dose the Fertiliser Control Order now allows, cut from 50% on 9 March 2026.
  • -35% grain protein in the Punjab rice trial at 50% substitution, the least-tracked result.
Updated 7 November 2026. The sales question raised under “What to watch” now has a first answer. Department of Fertilizers figures, as reported by fertiliser economist Uttam Gupta (July 2026), put all-India nano urea sales at 18.6 million bottles in 2025-26, about 30% below IFFCO’s own sales alone in 2024-25, and cumulative sales from launch to 31 March 2026 at about 122 million bottles, less than half of one year’s installed capacity of 272 million bottles; the ministry’s own cumulative count is 1,219.27 lakh bottles. The ICAR bio-efficacy results have not been published; a Lok Sabha reply of 7 August 2026 says ICAR and the National Productivity Council are still assessing efficacy and urea replacement, and a second NPC study on the extent of replacement was signed on 14 November 2025. The 25% dose in the Fertiliser Control Order is unchanged.

The Fertiliser Control Order halved the nano dose to 25% in March 2026

  1. July 2025Agriculture Minister requests legal action against coercive nano-fertiliser sales
  2. 13 February 2026Ministry mandates fresh bio-efficacy trials across 12 major crops
  3. 9 March 2026Nano dose cut from 50% to 25% of nutrient requirement
  4. 13 March 2026No forced sale of nano fertilisers by tagging them to other fertilisers

What it means: The 9 March amendment moved the official protocol from the level both trials show fails to the level they show works.

What to do: Track sales volumes after the 13 March tagging ban to test whether demand for nano urea was ever real.

What would fix this

  • ICAR should add grain protein content as a reported outcome in the bio-efficacy trials ordered in February 2026, and publish crop-wise results with the underlying data, so the 35% protein fall seen in the Punjab rice trial is measured by the regulator’s own evaluation and not only by yield.
  • The Department of Fertilizers should publish monthly nano urea sales by state for the twelve months either side of the 13 March 2026 tagging ban, so the natural experiment on whether demand was real can be read from official data.
  • The Ministry of Agriculture should require any nano fertiliser seeking Fertiliser Control Order notification to submit independent multi-location trials at the exact substitution rate it will be marketed at, so a dose is not corrected five years after launch, as the 50% protocol was.
  • IFFCO should withdraw promotional and packaging material stating the 50% replacement protocol or the 85–90% nutrient-use-efficiency claim, and publish the date by which this is done, so farmers see the same 75% RDF plus 25% nano protocol the March 2026 amendment now sets.
  • State agriculture departments should publish the number of complaints received and actions taken under the 13 March 2026 Fertiliser Control Order amendment against tagged sales, so enforcement of the ban on forced bundling can be checked state by state.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

Sources and caveats

Update sources, 7 November 2026: Uttam Gupta, “Why has nano-urea failed to secure a foothold on India’s fertilizer landscape?” (29 July 2026), citing Department of Fertilizers data (search-result summary); DD News, cumulative nano-fertiliser sales of 1,593.37 lakh bottles, including 1,219.27 lakh of nano urea; The Policy Edge, summary of Lok Sabha Unstarred Question 3326 (7 August 2026); Rajya Sabha Unstarred Question 255 (20 July 2026), on the National Productivity Council studies.

The IFFCO protocol as launched in 2021 — 50 per cent conventional urea as basal dose, 50 per cent replaced by nano urea foliar spray from 500 ml bottles, with a claimed nutrient use efficiency of 85 to 90 per cent against about 25 per cent for conventional urea — and IFFCO’s testing claims of over 11,000 farm fields across 94 crops plus trials at more than 20 research institutes covering 43 crops, are as reported by Business Today (14 April 2026). The Punjab Agricultural University results — rice grain yield down 13 per cent, wheat down 17.2 per cent, grain protein down 35 per cent in rice and 24 per cent in wheat under two nano-urea sprays substituting 50 per cent of the recommended nitrogen dose, published December 2024, with the quoted summary from study lead Rajeev Sikka — are from the same Business Today report; the underlying paper was not retrieved directly for this piece. The multi-season rice trial is Frontiers in Sustainable Food Systems, published 29 April 2026: two locations (Varanasi and Guwahati), three kharif seasons 2022 to 2024, randomised block design with three replications, nitrogen at 100, 75, 66, 50 and 33 per cent of the recommended dose against zero to three nano-urea sprays, nano-urea at 4 per cent N in 2022-23 and 20 per cent N in 2024. Its findings that 66 to 75 per cent of RDN with two foliar applications produced yields and economic returns comparable to full dose, that 50 per cent caused 16 to 19 per cent yield penalties of 0.88 to 1.2 t/ha, and nitrogen savings of 27 to 40 kg/ha at Varanasi and 15 to 20 kg/ha at Guwahati, are from the paper. The paper states it was funded by the Indian Farmers Fertiliser Cooperative Limited; the authors declared no conflict of interest. That funding relationship is disclosed here because it strengthens rather than weakens the argument — a manufacturer-funded study finding against the manufacturer’s own marketed dose is unusually strong evidence. The Fertiliser Control Order amendments of 9 March 2026 (nano dose cut from 50 to 25 per cent, new protocol 75 per cent RDF with 25 per cent nano) and 13 March 2026 (prohibition on forced sale by tagging), the Agriculture Minister’s July 2025 letter to chief ministers, and the 13 February 2026 mandate for ICAR bio-efficacy trials across 12 major crops, are as reported by Business Today; the gazette notifications themselves were not retrieved for this piece and readers relying on the exact legal text should consult them directly. Both trial datasets discussed here are rice-and-wheat systems in northern and eastern India. Nano urea’s performance on other crops and in other agro-climatic zones is precisely what the February 2026 trial framework is designed to establish, and is not settled by anything cited above. Nothing in this piece is agronomic, investment or policy advice, and no reader should change a fertiliser regime on the basis of it.

Top line

  • 62% lower unit cost claimed for FCI-based rations, ₹71 down to ₹27 per unit.
  • 50% of the sourcing plan runs through FCI itself, the largest of five channels.
  • 53g daily protein target, against the 8-12g claimed for today's rations.

Updated 15 November 2026: rewritten as charts; figures unchanged.

FCI-based rations would cost 62% less per unit than private vendor rations

What it means: The deck's own table puts the proposed FCI route at ₹27 per unit against ₹71 for private vendors, a 62% cut.

What to do: Ask the proposers to show how the ₹71 to ₹27 unit cost is derived before the ₹2.85 Cr Year-1 setup budget is approved.

The FCI channel carries 50% of the sourcing plan, the largest of five routes

What it means: Half of the ₹300 Cr value runs through FCI, but the deck does not say whether ₹300 Cr is an annual or cumulative figure.

What to do: Direct the coordination across State Boards, APMC, NAFED and FPOs only once the ₹300 Cr total is shown year by year.

Soya mock meat matches fish curry's 16g protein per serving at ₹32 against ₹48

What it means: Soya mock meat at ₹32 a pouch gives the same 16g as fish curry at ₹48, which suggests the protein target need not rely on non-vegetarian rations.

What to do: Run the tasting and field campaigns the deck proposes with soya and animal-protein pouches side by side before full rollout.

What would fix this

  • The Ministry of Defence should publish current combat-ration unit costs, protein content per day and the share of supply held by the largest vendors, so that the pitch’s baselines — ₹71 a unit, 8–12 g of protein and about 70% from four companies — can be checked before any MOU is considered.
  • The Ministry of Defence should commission an independent costing that reconciles the ₹300 crore procurement figure, the ₹8.51 crore five-year saving and the ₹4.5 crore annual saving, since the deck’s own line items and implied scale-up arithmetic do not agree with each other.
  • The Food Corporation of India should publish an assessment of spare milling and storage capacity at Kanpur, Panipat and Ranipet before taking on a ration-production mandate, so that the pitch’s self-rated “LOW” capacity-strain risk is tested against FCI’s existing procurement and PDS load.
  • The Directorate General of Quality Assurance should publish batch-test and field-acceptance results from any 100,000-unit pilot, including measured protein per serving, so that the redesign’s nutrition and quality claims are judged on pilot evidence before full scale-up.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

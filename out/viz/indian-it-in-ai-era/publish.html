Top line

  • 64% of India's goods trade deficit is covered by net services alone, and 107% once remittances are added.
  • 135,000 net hires in FY2025-26 against about 450,000 in FY2021-22, while revenue kept growing.
  • 27% above their pre-AI trend: India's other business services exports, per the measured WTO series.

Updated 7 November 2026. Newer data. The RBI’s balance of payments for April–June 2026 (Q1 2026-27) shows net services receipts of $51.6 billion, up from $47.9 billion a year earlier, with growth in computer services, other business services and transport; remittances of $42.9 billion (from $33.2 billion); and a current account deficit of $4.2 billion, 0.5% of GDP, against a goods deficit of $86.1 billion (RBI, 1 September 2026). The services cushion described below is therefore still growing. On hiring, the five largest IT firms added a net 13,918 employees in July–September 2026, against 5,480 in the June quarter: HCLTech +6,450, Tech Mahindra +5,446, Infosys +4,480 and TCS +4,258, while Wipro cut 6,716 (Zee Business, Financial Express). Revenue guidance stayed weak: Zee Business reports Infosys narrowing its 2026-27 growth guidance to 1–2%. The FY2025-26 analysis below is unchanged.

Updated 15 November 2026: rewritten as charts; figures unchanged.

Net services and remittances together more than cover the goods deficit

What it means: Net services receipts of $216.6bn and remittances of $143.6bn together exceed the $337.3bn goods deficit.

What to do: Treat import substitution as balance-of-payments insurance while watching whether services receipts keep covering the gap.

AI has so far lifted India's business services exports, not cut them

What it means: Other business services exports reached $185bn in 2025, up from $121bn in 2022, and 27% above the pre-AI trend.

What to do: Do not claim displacement has happened, since no measured export series shows it yet.

Net hiring fell from about 450,000 in FY22 to an estimated 135,000 in FY26

What it means: Industry revenue is expected to pass $315bn in FY2026 while net additions fell from about 450,000 in FY2021-22 to 135,000.

What to do: Watch revenue per employee and price realisation rather than headcount, which companies manage.

TCS alone drove a large net fall in headcount among the big five

What it means: Infosys, HCLTech and Wipro added a combined 17,587 people in FY2026, while TCS's net fall of 23,460 drove the cohort's decline.

What to do: Watch the annual headcount figure rather than quarterly moves, since quarterly swings mislead.

What would fix this

  • The Reserve Bank of India should publish software and business services export receipts split between global capability centres and third-party service providers, so that the balance-of-payments effect of AI on captive centres and on listed Indian vendors — now roughly half the export base each — can be read separately.
  • The Reserve Bank of India should add contract type (headcount-based versus fixed-price or outcome-based) and average price realisation to its survey of computer services exports, so that the “AI deflation” executives describe shows up in an official series before it shows up in the current account.
  • The Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology should publish an official annual series of IT-BPM revenue, headcount, gross hiring and separations, with a documented revision history, so that the sector’s core numbers do not depend on restated industry-body estimates that cannot be compared across years.
  • The Ministry of Finance should constitute the AI Economic Council proposed in the Economic Survey 2025-26 with a published mandate and a fixed list of indicators it will report on each year, so that two Surveys reaching different conclusions twelve months apart are replaced by a consistent measured view.
  • The Ministry of Labour and Employment should run a recurring establishment survey of IT and BPO firms measuring roles eliminated, redeployed or added because of automation, so that claims of displacement rest on measurement rather than consultancy estimates such as the 20–25% entry-level figure.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

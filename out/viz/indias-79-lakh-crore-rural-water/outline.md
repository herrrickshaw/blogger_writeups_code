## How a rural water scheme actually gets planned

Jal Jeevan Mission (JJM), run by the Ministry of Jal Shakti's Department of Drinking Water and Sanitation, plans piped water supply through two scheme types: a Single Village Scheme (SVS) — one local source, storage, and distribution network serving one village — or a Multi Village Scheme (MVS), a shared source and trunk network feeding several villages at once. Neither is approved without going through a fixed chain: a village's Water and Sanitation Committee identifies the need, the District Water and Sanitation Mission (DWSM) vets it, and the State Level Scheme Sanctioning Committee (SLSSC) gives the actual cost approval. The Ministry's own "Format PM3" benchmark report puts the current scale of that pipeline at 6,31,340 schemes planned nationally (5,75,419 SVS + 55,921 MVS), covering 12.83 crore households, with a combined DWSM/SLSSC-approved cost of ₹8,01,653 crore.

A second live report, "Format B15," tracks the same universe from a completion-status angle instead: 6,41,995 total schemes, ₹8,29,181 crore total estimated cost, and ₹4,17,190 crore spent since JJM's FY2019-20 launch. The two totals don't quite match each other (6,31,340 vs 6,41,995) — a reminder that these are two separately-updated live dashboards built for different purposes (PM3 for planning and cost approval, B15 for tracking completion status), not one reconciled source, and neither claims to be final.

## "Not yet started" is nearly zero everywhere

Of the 5,32,205 schemes counted as "upcoming" nationally, only 3,445 — 0.6% — are still pre-construction, i.e. sanctioned with no work order issued. Almost the entire upcoming pipeline (5,28,760 schemes) is already "Ongoing." Two states hold most of the genuine not-yet-started backlog: Rajasthan (969) and Assam (2,382); everywhere else, the figure is 0, 1, or 2 schemes.

The ₹7.9 lakh crore remaining spend isn't waiting for a contractor to be sanctioned — it's already contracted and under construction, which is a different, generally lower execution risk than an unstarted pipeline of the same size would carry.

## Read the low "completed" numbers carefully

Several large states show startlingly low completed-scheme counts against their total: Uttar Pradesh (419 of 40,955, ~1%), Chhattisgarh (122 of 32,239, 0.4%), Kerala (43 of 1,730, 2.5%), Jharkhand (2,306 of 97,535, 2.4%). Read against the national household tap-connection rate of 81.6% (15.79 crore of 19.36 crore rural households, per a February 2026 Lok Sabha answer), these look contradictory — they aren't. "Scheme completed" in this dataset is a strict administrative and financial closure status: physical completion date, financial completion date, and the associated audit trail all have to be in place. A household can receive a working tap connection well before its parent scheme clears that full closure paperwork. The low completed-scheme counts do not mean most rural households lack water; they mean most schemes' financial books aren't formally closed yet, a normal, expected lag in an infrastructure programme moving this fast.

## What this data can't tell you: how many storage tanks

None of this — not PM3, not B15's district or scheme-level views — breaks a scheme down into physical components with a count. There is no national "number of overhead tanks" figure published anywhere in JJM's open reporting. The closest proxy is a per-scheme field called "Water distribution," which reads "Electric" or (presumably) "Gravity" — almost certainly a stand-in for whether the scheme pumps water to an elevated tank versus feeding it downhill from a higher source, but it's a distribution-mechanism flag, not a tank inventory. A genuine national OHT count would mean paging through all 6.4 lakh scheme records filtering on that field — a mechanical, doable pull, but a materially bigger job than the state-level status breakdown above.

There is also a formal JJM Web API ("Real time data integration") that likely holds this granularity, but it sits behind a username/password/captcha registration wall, not open access — a legitimate boundary this piece did not attempt to cross.

JJM's rural pipeline is only half of India's water-infrastructure spend. The urban counterpart, AMRUT 2.0, runs on the Ministry of Housing & Urban Affairs' own separate reporting — see [AMRUT's ₹1.97 Lakh Crore Urban Portfolio: Water Supply Is 41% of Projects, 60% of the Money](https://blog.vertistratsolution.com/2026/08/amruts-197-lakh-crore-urban-portfolio.html) for the state-wise breakdown there, and for why AMRUT's public data can't give the same Completed/Ongoing/Not-started split this piece just built for JJM.

This isn't the first time this blog has found a state's own headline water-infrastructure number doesn't match what the underlying reports actually say — see [Tamil Nadu's Water Infrastructure Runs on Three Separate Money Streams, and None of Them Match the Number You've Heard](https://blog.vertistratsolution.com/2026/08/tamil-nadus-water-infrastructure-runs.html) for the state-level version of the same pattern.

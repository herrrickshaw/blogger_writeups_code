"Upcoming" is doing a lot of work in Jal Jeevan Mission progress reports. Pull the actual scheme-status data behind the headline household-connection numbers, and a specific pattern falls out: almost none of what's "upcoming" is actually waiting to start. It's already under construction.

Top line

  • 5,28,760 of 5,32,205 upcoming JJM schemes are already ongoing, and only 0.6% await a work order.
  • 1,09,790 of 6,41,995 schemes in the B15 report are formally completed.
  • ₹4,17,190 crore spent so far, against a total estimated cost of ₹8,29,181 crore.
Updated 7 November 2026. The scheme counts below are the portal snapshot taken for this piece and could not be re-pulled (ejalshakti.gov.in is not reachable from the research machine). Two things have moved since: on 10 March 2026 the Union Cabinet extended Jal Jeevan Mission to December 2028 as “JJM 2.0”, with a revised outlay of ₹8.69 lakh crore including ₹3.59 lakh crore of central assistance, and a shift from building schemes to service delivery (ThePrint); and rural tap coverage reached 15.84 crore households, 81.87% of 19.35 crore, by May 2026, up from 81.59% in February (Deccan Chronicle, reporting a government document).

What to do: Confirm that these four states' schemes are ongoing, since the lower execution-risk reading depends on it.

What would fix this

  • The Department of Drinking Water and Sanitation should publish a note reconciling Format PM3's 6,31,340 schemes with Format B15's 6,41,995, explaining what each report counts and when each is updated, so the roughly 1.6 per cent gap between its own dashboards is explained, not left to users.
  • The Department of Drinking Water and Sanitation should report physical completion and financial closure as separate columns in Format B15, state by state, so a state like Uttar Pradesh showing 419 completed schemes of 40,955 can be read correctly against the national tap-connection rate of 81.6 per cent.
  • The Department of Drinking Water and Sanitation should extend the public scheme-level drill-down now available only for completed schemes to ongoing and work-order-not-issued schemes, so the 5,32,205 upcoming schemes can be checked by name and location, not only as aggregate counts.
  • The Department of Drinking Water and Sanitation should add a count and capacity of overhead tanks and other storage structures to each scheme record and publish state-wise totals, so a national storage-tank figure exists instead of being inferred from a “Water distribution: Electric” flag.
  • The Department of Drinking Water and Sanitation should offer open bulk downloads of the JJM scheme database without the registration and captcha wall on its Web API, so researchers can audit the full 6.4 lakh records directly rather than parsing report tables.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

What this piece does not establish. This piece does not reconcile PM3's 6,31,340-scheme planning total against B15's 6,41,995-scheme status total — both are live, separately-maintained dashboards, and the ~1.6% gap between them is reported rather than explained. It does not have scheme-name-level detail for "Ongoing" or "Work order not issued" schemes in any state — only the "Completed" column exposes a drill-down to named, geo-located scheme records in the public UI checked; the aggregate counts here are real and government-sourced, but not a named project list. It does not establish a national overhead-tank count, and explicitly does not treat the "Water distribution: Electric" field as a verified tank inventory. Sources and caveats

All scheme-count, cost, and status figures are drawn directly from the Ministry of Jal Shakti's own live reporting portal, ejalshakti.gov.in — specifically "Format PM3: JJM Benchmark, Status of Scheme Planning and Costs" and "Format B15: Piped Water Supply Schemes," both public, no-login report tiles under the JJM Reports home page, graded strong as primary government sources. The state-wise Completed/Ongoing/Work-order-not-issued breakdown was extracted from B15's full data table (each state row carries 46 numeric fields across a Total/SVS/MVS × Completed/Ongoing/Not-issued grid) and parsed programmatically rather than read by eye; the parse was verified by reproducing the portal's own published national total (6,41,995 schemes) exactly from the per-state sum, which is strong internal-consistency evidence the column mapping is correct. The household tap-connection rate (81.59%, 15.79 of 19.36 crore rural households) is drawn from a Lok Sabha Unstarred Question answered 05.02.2026 by the Minister of State for Jal Shakti, a primary parliamentary source, graded strong. Updated 7 November 2026: JJM 2.0 extension per ThePrint (PTI), 10 March 2026; May 2026 coverage per Deccan Chronicle and News Arena (search-snippet level). The existence of a credential-gated JJM Web API ("Real time data integration") was confirmed directly by navigating to it; this piece did not attempt registration or any access beyond viewing its public login page. The "Water distribution: Electric" field and its likely meaning as an elevated-tank proxy is this piece's own inference from the field name and general water-supply engineering logic, not a claim sourced to any JJM document, and is flagged as such.

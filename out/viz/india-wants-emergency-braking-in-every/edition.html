Automatic emergency braking in India: draft mandate for all cars from 2032

Skip to article content
Text Size

Top line

  • 8.4% of cars sold in 2025 had driver-assistance systems that include emergency braking, up from 6.4% in 2024.
  • ~43 lakh cars a year would need emergency braking added, at 2025-26 sales and today's fitment rate.
  • Oct 2032 when the draft rule would make AEB compulsory in every car built; Europe got there in 2024.

Updated 15 November 2026: rewritten as charts; figures unchanged.

Under one in ten new cars had emergency-braking driver aids in 2025

What it means: Most equipped cars are utility vehicles, and the cheapest car with full driver assistance is the top variant of the Maruti Baleno.

What to do: Carmakers should add emergency braking to entry-level variants now, rather than waiting for the 2030 and 2032 dates.

Europe made AEB compulsory in 2024; India's full mandate lands in 2032

  1. July 2022EU: new car types must have AEB
  2. July 2024EU: all new cars must have AEB
  3. September 2029US: FMVSS 127 applies to all light vehicles
  4. 1 October 2030India: every new car model must have AEB
  5. 1 October 2032India: every car built must have AEB

What it means: The draft gives carmakers four years to redesign new models and six to change models already in production.

What to do: Regulators should bring the India dates forward, or tie them to the Bharat NCAP 2.0 protocol expected from October 2027.

Crashes where a vehicle is hit from behind caused about a fifth of road deaths in 2023

What it means: AEB in cars addresses only part of this total, the crashes where a car is the one doing the hitting and those involving pedestrians.

What to do: The ministry should publish a breakdown of rear-end deaths by vehicle role, so the rule can be judged against it.

What would fix this

  • MoRTH should publish, with the final rule, its estimate of cost per car and lives saved, as the US regulator did for its rule, so the dates can be weighed against the benefit.
  • MoRTH’s annual road-accident report should split rear-end and pedestrian crashes by the type of the striking vehicle, so the share AEB in cars can address is known.
  • The rule should make electronic stability control compulsory in cars on the same timetable, since AEB depends on it.
  • Bharat NCAP 2.0 should make AEB tests compulsory for a five-star rating from 2029, so ratings reward it before the mandate arrives.
  • The government should track imports of automotive radar and camera modules under their own tariff lines, so localisation can be measured.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

What this piece could not establish

The draft notification and AIS-185 could not be opened directly; the dates come from news reports, which agree on 2030 for new models and 2032 for all cars but differ on wording. No reliable estimate of the cost of AEB per car in India was found; the per-airbag figure is a comparison, not a proxy. The ADAS share is a market-research estimate on calendar-year sales applied here to fiscal-year sales. The rear-end shares are as reported from the ministry’s 2023 report, and one outlet gives 23% of accidents instead of 21%. The radar import figure covers a whole customs line, not car radar alone. The status of the bus and truck mandate could not be confirmed.

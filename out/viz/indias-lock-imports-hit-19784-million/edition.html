India imports nearly $200 million of locks and padlocks a year, and the number is growing. Dindigul, Tamil Nadu — a Geographical Indication-tagged cluster with a genuine claim to being India's original lock town — is not the industry that import number describes. Its problem is older, more local, and harder to fix with a tariff.

India's lock imports: $197.84 million, with Dindigul facing other pressures

Skip to article content
Text Size

Top line

  • 38.8% of India's FY2024-25 lock imports under HS 8301 came from China, which grew 7.28%.
  • 5.79% growth in lock imports, to US$197.84 million in FY2024-25.
  • ~60 active Dindigul manufacturers in 2019, against about 2,000 in the 1990s.
Updated 7 November 2026. FY2025-26 data are now out. Summing the HS 8301 lines in this blog’s own copy of DGCI&S 8-digit trade data (lines above $1 million only, so a slight undercount) gives about $213 million of imports in 2025-26, up about 8% on 2024-25. Within that, “other locks” (8301 40 90) rose to $90.5 million and lock parts to $56.0 million, while padlocks fell to $4.8 million. The country table below is still FY2024-25 and has not been re-pulled. Data: India’s 8-digit trade book, searchable.

What it means: China's 38.8% share of India's lock imports is the largest single-supplier position in the trade data.

What to do: Track China's share in each year's HS 8301 data before linking it to Dindigul, since the post finds no measured effect on the cluster.

India's lock imports rose 5.79% to US$197.84 million in FY2024-25

What it means: Imports under this HS code are growing, yet the post puts them at a small share of India's US$721.2-billion import bill.

What to do: Re-check the HS 8301 total in the next DGCI&S release so the growth rate is tested against a third year.

Dindigul's manufacturer count fell from about 2,000 to about 60, by one count

What it means: The steep fall rests on one trade-press count, and the GI filing's 3,125 manufacturers and the 43-unit directory count cannot be reconciled with it.

What to do: Commission one agreed census of active Dindigul units before judging the GI premium or GST carve-out plans, since no official count exists.

What would fix this

  • The Tamil Nadu MSME Department, through the District Industries Centre in Dindigul, should publish a census of active lock units, workers and their age profile, and repeat it on a fixed cycle, so the cluster has one official baseline instead of counts ranging from about 43 units to 3,125 manufacturers.
  • The Geographical Indications Registry should publish, and keep current, the list of registered authorised users of the Dindigul lock GI by variety, so whether the 50 protected varieties are actually being made and sold under the tag can be checked.
  • The Department of Commerce, with CBIC, should create a separate national tariff line within HS 8301 for hand-made and GI-registered locks, so trade in Dindigul-type heritage locks can be measured apart from the mass-produced hardware that makes up the $197.84 million import bill.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

The Ethanol Blended Petrol Programme has already put ₹1.66 lakh crore directly into farmers' hands and cut CO₂ emissions by roughly 952 lakh tonnes. It has also built more distillation capacity than the E20 cap can absorb. Both facts are true at once, and the fix for the second one is not to slow down the first.

Top line

  • 20% national ethanol blending by 2025-26, five years ahead of the original 2030 target.
  • ₹71.86 a litre: maize's procurement price, the highest of the six feedstocks in ESY 2025-26.
  • 2,000 crore litres of ethanol capacity against about 1,100 crore litres of E20 demand.
Updated 7 November 2026. The 2026-27 ethanol tender changes the cane side of this case. It seeks about 1,100 crore litres, roughly 55% of capacity, and takes no ethanol made from cane juice, syrup or B-heavy molasses, as sugar prices rose; maize ethanol’s price was cut to ₹68.86 a litre. Cane growers’ income from ethanol in 2026-27 will therefore come only through C-heavy molasses, so the farmer-payment figures below, which are cumulative to July 2026, should not be read forward for cane. Sources: The Federal, Swarajya, and this blog’s post on the 2026-27 tender.
Updated 22 October 2026. This piece says FCI rice goes into ethanol at ₹2,320 a quintal and that the allocation runs only to 30 June 2026. Bioenergy Times and Outlook report that the price stays at ₹2,320 until 31 October 2026 and rises to ₹2,390 a quintal from 1 November 2026, for supplies reported to run from November 2026 to June 2027. Central-pool rice stocks were reported at 403.11 lakh tonnes on 1 July 2026, nearly three times the buffer norm, up from the ~386 lakh MT in April 2026 cited below. Reports differ on the size of the new rice allocation and the previous year's figure, so the allocation numbers below have not been updated. Sources: Bioenergy Times, Outlook. What it means: Blending held near 1.5% for most of the decade, then reached 20% by 2025-26.

What to do: Move the demand-side fixes (E25, E85 and flex-fuel vehicles) faster, as the post argues, rather than cut farmer prices.

What it means: Maize's price rose faster than any other feedstock, reflecting the push to diversify beyond sugarcane.

What to do: Raise maize yields, which the post puts at about 3.3 tonnes a hectare against roughly 11 in the US.

Ethanol capacity of about 2,000 crore litres outruns E20 demand of about 1,100

What it means: CareEdge says only about 60% of the ethanol OMCs offer for allocation is being absorbed at the current 20% blend.

What to do: Build the E25 and flex-fuel vehicle demand that the post says must arrive faster to absorb the surplus.

What would fix this

  • The Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas should publish the ledger behind its ₹1.66 lakh crore farmer-payment figure, broken down by ethanol supply year, feedstock and state, so that the headline scorecard can be re-derived from OMC purchase data rather than accepted as a cumulative total.
  • The oil marketing companies should publish, for every ethanol supply year, the volume offered, allocated and actually lifted, by feedstock and by producing state, so that the roughly 60 per cent absorption rate and the Maharashtra surplus and Tamil Nadu deficit can be tracked from official data instead of rating-agency estimates.
  • The Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas should publish the methodology behind its 952 lakh MT CO₂ avoided figure — base year, emission factors and scope — so that it can be reconciled with CareEdge's 8.69 crore tonne estimate rather than treated as merely the same order of magnitude.
  • The Department of Agriculture and Farmers Welfare should commission a published test of tractor and pump-set fuel economy on CNG and CBG against diesel, so that the farm-equipment saving this post could only approximate from a passenger-car model becomes a measured figure a farmer could budget on.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

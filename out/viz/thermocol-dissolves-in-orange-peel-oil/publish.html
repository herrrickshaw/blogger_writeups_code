Top line

  • 212 g of polystyrene dissolve per 100 g of p-cymene at 50°C, against 182 to 197 g for limonene.
  • 3,000 tonnes a year is the initial capacity of the Terneuzen solvent plant, restarted in 2023 after insolvency.
  • 30,000 to 70,000 tonnes a year is the range of estimates for world technical-grade limonene supply.
Updated 12 October 2026. This piece said the European CreaSolv demonstration “did not reach its target of 3,000 tonnes a year”. That was not supported and is corrected below: the Terneuzen plant was built for that capacity, its operator went insolvent in March 2022, a German group of foam makers bought it, and it was reported in regular operation in June 2023. A search for 2026 cost, life-cycle or pilot studies of limonene or p-cymene foam recycling found none, so the main finding stands.

Updated 15 November 2026: rewritten as charts; figures unchanged.

Limonene is one of several solvents, and p-cymene dissolved more at 50°C

What it means: Limonene is a workable solvent but not the strongest one in the published record.

What to do: Test p-cymene and the other named solvents on the same foam and temperature before choosing one for a plant.

The one named industrial solvent plant was bought out of insolvency and restarted

  1. March 2022Operator files for insolvency; German EPS Converters later buys the plant
  2. June 2023In regular operation at 3,000 tonnes a year initial capacity, 8,000 target; current throughput not found

What it means: The plant that was built to handle 3,000 tonnes a year of foam waste has run, but its current output is unknown.

What to do: Publish the Terneuzen throughput since 2023 before treating the plant as proof that the process pays.

Estimates of world limonene supply differ by more than twofold

What it means: The solvent supply that a foam-recycling industry would compete for is not reliably known.

What to do: Obtain trade and peel-oil production data for citrus oils before sizing any limonene foam plant.

What would fix this

  • The Department of Science and Technology should fund an independent pilot of a limonene or peel-oil foam loop with a published mass balance: polystyrene recovered, solvent lost per cycle, residual solvent in the polymer, and solvent quality after ten cycles.
  • The Central Pollution Control Board should publish expanded polystyrene collection and recycling tonnage separately from the EPR filings, so the size of the problem, and the share handled by compaction recyclers, can be seen.
  • The Bureau of Indian Standards should set a specification for recycled polystyrene from solvent routes, with a limit for residual solvent and a statement of which uses, such as food contact, are excluded.
  • The Ministry of Food Processing Industries and APEDA should publish citrus peel oil output by processor and region, and the Directorate General of Commercial Intelligence and Statistics should report citrus oils at a finer tariff line, so supply can be judged.
  • The Petroleum and Explosives Safety Organisation should say how a plant that distils limonene would be licensed, given its flash point of 45–48°C, so that safety requirements are known before a pilot is built.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

Sources and caveats

Noguchi, Miyashita, Inagaki and Watanabe, “A new recycling system for expanded polystyrene using a natural solvent”, Packaging Technology and Science 11, 1998 (part 1, abstract; part 2 not seen; part 3 is a life-cycle assessment by the same authors, not seen, so the “independent” study count above excludes it); a review chapter, “Recycling of expanded polystyrene using natural solvents” (InTech, doi 10.5772/59156, read in full), for the semi-industrial scale of limonene recycling, solubility tables and steam-distillation recovery; the “reused at least 10 times” statement was not found in that chapter on re-reading, so its source is unconfirmed; US patents 5629352 and 5863002 for limonene’s odour, flash point and instability; García et al., Waste Management 29, 2009 (abstract and a summary; the 0.26 g/mL figure is from a summary); Hattori et al., Journal of Wood Science 2010 (full text, Kitami Institute of Technology repository, Table 1, for p-cymene, 2-p-cymenol, limonene and its isomers at 50°C; an earlier study by the group with a different polystyrene film gives d-limonene 127 g per 100 g, InTech chapter Table 2); Shin and Chase, Polymer Bulletin 2005 (repository record and a patent citing it); a Colombian study of EPS dissolution in natural solvents (regional journal); Ozeki, Ida and Osa, Polymer Journal 2024 and 2025 (abstract read on nature.com: d-limonene is a medium solvent for atactic polystyrene, between toluene, a good solvent, and cyclohexane, a poor one); a 2024 preprint and paper on the solubility limit of the polystyrene, limonene and alcohol system; Yadav and Sharma, Polymer Bulletin 2018 and Journal of Applied Polymer Science 2019, a DST release and the India Science and Technology portal for the IIT Hyderabad work and its ₹35.7 lakh grant. CreaSolv and PolyStyreneLoop: the EU LIFE project page and Fraunhofer IVV (HBCD removal above 99.7%); Plasteurope and EUWID reports (insolvency, the German EPS Converters takeover, restart in 2022, regular operation in June 2023 at 3,000 t a year, €17 million invested; the insolvency year is inferred from the dates of those reports). Other solvents: US patents 7728047 and 7745503 (dimethyl carbonate), US 12162991 (isoamyl acetate, dioxolane) and a Polystyvert patent (p-cymene and heptane); a 2025 Springer review of green solvents (ionic liquids); student theses on ethyl acetate and acetone, a repository paper on essential oils. All are abstracts or search summaries. A 2025 gate-to-gate life-cycle and techno-economic study of densified polystyrene (Polymers) and a ScienceDirect study of chemical recycling of foam to styrene. Safety: the EU risk assessment committee opinion, safety data sheets and the Japanese NITE classification (flash point 45–48°C; air-oxidised limonene is a skin sensitiser). Supply: an IARC monograph (about 45,000 tonnes a year, early 1990s), a 2014 RSC paper (above 70,000 tonnes) and a supplier statement (about 30,000 tonnes of technical grade); these disagree and are of different grades and years. Indian policy: PIB and CPCB texts of the single-use plastics ban and the Maharashtra notification of 23 March 2018, as described in secondary guidance; the 2026 EPR timing was not confirmed. Indian recycling: a Business India profile and a trade directory listing. The 91.9% limonene in Nagpur peel oil is from a Research Square preprint. All figures were seen in abstracts, patents or search summaries, not in the papers themselves. OpenAlex was later reachable for metadata checks, but no citation counts or systematic search were run. The 1/4 polymer-by-weight figure assumes limonene density of 0.84 g/mL. No safety, process or investment advice is given.

Since 1 April 2024, any company that sells base oil or lubrication oil in India has had a legal recycling target — a Rule 27 obligation under the Hazardous and Other Wastes (Management and Transboundary Movement) Second Amendment Rules, 2023, ramping from 5% this year to 50% by 2030-31. CPCB's own live portal shows the target for 2024-25 alone: 68,946 tonnes of used oil to be recycled, and 119 producers granted registration against 252 applications received. A BIS standard for the recycled product — Re-refined Base Oil (RRBO) — followed the rules by about ten months. What's still missing, according to the people actually running collection centres and shaping the standard, is the price floor that would make any of this pay for itself.

Used-oil EPR in India: 50% recycling target by 2030-31, 119 producers registered

Top line

  • 119 of 252 producer applications granted on the CPCB portal by 10 August 2026.
  • 3% of the 2024-25 used-oil target is covered by EPR certificates, per Parliament's answer of 5 March 2026.
  • 5% → 50% the used-oil recycling target ramp, from 2024-25 to 2030-31 onward.
Updated 12 November 2026. The first national figures on what the scheme has actually recycled are now out. In a Parliament answer, the environment ministry gave used-oil EPR’s status as on 5 March 2026: 103 registered recyclers, about 19,000 tonnes of used oil recycled and about 2,000 tonnes of EPR certificates generated, against a 2024-25 target of 68,946 tonnes. The deadline for producers’ and recyclers’ annual returns for 2025-26 has since been extended from 30 June to 30 November 2026. A new section sets this out. The registration counts below are still those of 10 August 2026: the portal’s dashboard could not be re-read for this update.

Updated 15 November 2026: rewritten as charts; figures unchanged.

Fewer than half of producer applications have cleared registration

What it means: Fewer than half of the 252 producer applications had been granted by 10 August 2026, more than two years into the obligation period.

What to do: Producers with incomplete applications should finish them on the portal, since 94 are marked that way.

EPR certificates cover about 3% of the 2024-25 target

What it means: About 2,000 tonnes of certificates had been issued by 5 March 2026, against a first-year obligation of 68,946 tonnes.

What to do: Recyclers should reconcile recycled tonnage with certificates, since the Parliament answer does not explain the gap.

Lube oil imports rise each year while exports stay small

What it means: Imports ran from 2,412 to 3,177 thousand tonnes a year across 2023-24 to 2025-26, while exports stayed between 11 and 17 thousand tonnes.

What to do: Check the provisional 2025-26 PPAC figures against final data before drawing a trade conclusion.

The recycling target climbs in steps to 50% from 2030-31

What it means: The obligation starts at 5% in 2024-25 and reaches 50% from 2030-31 onward, in steps set by Rule 27(2).

What to do: Producers and importers should plan purchases of EPR certificates against these steps, since each year's target rises.

What would fix this

  • CPCB should set a published time limit for deciding used-oil EPR registration applications and report monthly how many are granted, under process and incomplete for producers, importers, recyclers and collection agents, so the 94 incomplete and 39 pending producer applications behind only 119 grants become a tracked backlog.
  • CPCB should publish, for each obligation year, the national EPR target, certificates generated by recyclers, certificates bought by producers and importers, and the shortfall charged Environmental Compensation, so compliance against the 68,946-tonne 2024–25 target can be checked rather than assumed. The only official figure so far, about 2,000 tonnes of certificates by March 2026, came in a Parliament answer, not from the portal.
  • CPCB should publish the conversion factors it assigns by recycling technology and the volume-weighted price at which EPR certificates change hands within the 30–100% band, so the market’s actual price signal to re-refiners is visible.
  • PPAC should split its LOBS/Lube oil import and export line into base oil, finished lubricants and greases, and name the HSN codes it aggregates, so imports of 3,177 thousand tonnes in 2025–26 can be set against what re-refined base oil could actually replace.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

Maruti Suzuki gets over 76% of its 246 suppliers within 100km of its assembly lines, and its "e-Nagare" system cut the order-to-shopfloor cycle from 30 hours to 2. That single number is the clearest evidence of how India's automotive clusters actually work today — not as five separate manufacturing hubs, but as increasingly tight, just-in-time-organised ecosystems where component suppliers sit inside the same radius as the OEM. This piece maps the clusters, the mechanics of the JIT shift, and the one real crisis — the 2021 semiconductor shortage — that showed exactly where that model breaks.

India's auto component clusters: ₹7.59 lakh crore turnover in FY2025-26, up 12.7%

Skip to article content
Text Size

Top line

  • 2 hours to push a production plan to the shopfloor under Maruti's e-Nagare, down from about 30 hours.
  • 80% of NCR's auto-component firms are Tier-2/Tier-3 MSMEs, against about 20% large Tier-1 manufacturers.
  • ₹44,326 crore of investment attracted by PLI-Auto by March 2026, against a ₹25,938 crore outlay.
Updated 7 November 2026. ACMA’s figures for 2025-26 are now out: industry turnover was ₹7.59 lakh crore (about $85.9 billion), up 12.7% from ₹6.73 lakh crore in 2024-25, with supplies to vehicle makers up 16.3% to ₹6.63 lakh crore. Exports rose 5% to about $24 billion while component imports reached $25.4 billion, so the industry ran a small trade deficit; ACMA puts growth at a 17% compound annual rate from 2020-21 to 2025-26. The cluster analysis below is unchanged. Sources: Autocar Professional, Zee Business.

What it means: Just-in-time only works when the production sequence reaches suppliers quickly, and e-Nagare does that digitally.

What to do: Any new cluster should build this digital plan link before adding more suppliers, as the Maruti case suggests.

NCR's component base is mostly small MSMEs, unlike Pune and Chennai

What it means: A cluster can be close to its OEM yet still too small to absorb the working-capital demands of just-in-time supply.

What to do: Policy support for NCR suppliers should target that capital and technology gap, the post's central concern for this cluster.

PLI-Auto has drawn more investment than its outlay so far

What it means: The 50% domestic value addition floor pushes more of the supply chain inside the radius where just-in-time already works.

What to do: Track the 50% domestic value addition target against the 18 applicants with DVA certificates, as the post suggests.

What would fix this

  • ACMA should publish auto-component turnover and exports by cluster — Chennai, NCR, Pune, Gujarat and the East India belt — every year, so that the industry’s ₹7.59 lakh crore turnover can be placed on the map rather than described only nationally.
  • The Ministry of Heavy Industries should publish PLI-Auto DVA certificates by product and by state of manufacture, so that it is visible whether the 155 certified products are deepening existing clusters or building supply inside the short radius just-in-time delivery needs.
  • The Ministry of Heavy Industries should publish the list of 28 components in the localisation roadmap with a yearly status for each, so that progress on imported, far-upstream inputs like the microcontrollers behind the 2021 output cuts can be checked.
  • MoSPI should publish Annual Survey of Industries output for auto components by district, so that cluster geography — including the Tier-2/3-heavy NCR belt and the Chennai–Bengaluru–Hosur corridor — can be mapped from official data rather than from consultancy and social-media profiles.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

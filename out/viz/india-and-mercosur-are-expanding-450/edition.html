Top line

  • $4.9 bn India's deficit with the MERCOSUR bloc in 2025-26, against about $1.2 billion a year earlier.
  • 81% of that deficit is with Argentina, which sold India $5.0 billion and bought $1.0 billion.

Argentina accounts for 81% of India's deficit with the bloc

What it means: Argentina's sales to India of about $5.0 billion far exceed its purchases of about $1.0 billion.

What to do: Report the composition of Argentina's sales to India, which the post could not find in official data, before the preferences are widened.

Expansion talks launched in September 2026 on a 2009 pact with no product list

  1. 1 Jun 2009Agreement enters into force with about 450 Indian and 452 MERCOSUR tariff lines
  2. 2013Request lists to widen coverage exchanged, still under review years later
  3. 16 Oct 2025India–Brazil declaration; next step a technical dialogue
  4. 14 Sep 2026Negotiations to expand launched; electronic certificate of origin protocol signed
  5. About Sep 2027End of the one-year aim cited by Brazil

What it means: The launch statements do not say which products will be added, so the scope of the expansion is still open.

What to do: Publish the terms of reference and a utilisation figure for the existing preferences before the scope is fixed.

What would fix this

  • The Department of Commerce, with Customs, should publish each year how much of India’s trade with MERCOSUR claimed the preference: shipments, value and duty foregone, by line, for each direction, so the existing 450 lines can be judged before more are added.
  • The Directorate General of Commercial Intelligence and Statistics should publish India–MERCOSUR trade at the eight-digit line, flagged as covered or not covered by the pact, so the share of trade the agreement reaches is visible.
  • The Department of Commerce should publish the terms of reference and a summary of the request lists by sector once agreed, and run a public consultation, especially with edible oil, pulses, sugar and ethanol producers and the chemicals, auto and pharma exporters who would gain.
  • The Department of Agriculture and the Department of Food and Public Distribution should publish an impact note on domestic oilseed and edible oil prices before any preference on vegetable oils is offered.
  • The Department of Commerce should publish the negotiating calendar against the one-year aim reported by Brazil, with a note after each round on what was discussed.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

Sources and caveats

Commerce ministry releases, titles and dates from this blog’s PIB index: launch of negotiations (PIB 2310166) and first additional protocol on electronic certificates of origin (PIB 2310083), both announced 14 September 2026 and indexed 15 September; India–Brazil joint declaration for deepening the MERCOSUR–India agreement (16 October 2025, PIB 2180058); India–Brazil Trade Monitoring Mechanism (7 October 2025, PIB 2176041; 31 August 2026, PIB 2304804); Brazil’s President’s visit (21 February 2026, PIB 2231335). PIB pages could not be opened from this blog’s tools, so the statements are described from press coverage (Gulf News, ANI, India Briefing, DD India, Drishti IAS and others) and from the Brazilian foreign ministry’s account. 2025-26 trade for Brazil and Argentina is from the Commerce ministry’s Trade Intelligence and Analytics portal and TRADESTAT data in this blog’s data repository (captured 18 July 2026); Uruguay and Paraguay are from a trade-briefing table, unchecked. 2024-25 totals are from press reports of official data, and the $4.9 billion deficit and 81% share are this blog’s arithmetic on those figures. The year-on-year comparison joins two sources and should be treated as approximate. Argentine edible oil figures are from the Argentine ambassador and trade press (Ukragroconsult) and Brazilian crude and soybean oil from calendar 2024 trade-data summaries; none is an official Indian figure, and the periods differ. The 1 October completion of the terms of reference is from a single outlet. Sources disagree on the split of preference margins, which is not used. The Joint Administration Committee, which the 2025 declaration named as the next step, was not found in coverage of the 2026 launch. This article takes no position on whether the agreement should be expanded, or on which products.

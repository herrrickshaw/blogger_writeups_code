India's naphtha exports to China doubled to $2.4bn in FY2025-26, the same fiscal year ethanol blending freed up the refinery capacity to make it. Benzene exports to the same buyer have risen four-and-a-half-fold since FY2022-23. Both are the entry-level building blocks for paracetamol and ibuprofen's own synthesis routes — molecules where India still imports 91% and 80.73% of its finished drug, by value, from the same country these raw materials are shipped to.

  • +101% rise in India's naphtha exports to China in FY2025-26, to $2,425.0mn.
  • 4.5× rise in benzene exports to China since FY2022-23.
  • 13.6% of India's exports to China in FY2025-26 is basic petrochemical feedstock.
Updated 7 November 2026. The benzene trend continued into FY2026-27. China’s benzene imports in April 2026 were about 440,600 tonnes, above forecasts, and market reports attribute the surprise mainly to Indian cargoes, which rose by nearly 50,000 tonnes from March and made India China’s second-largest benzene supplier, with close to 19% of the month’s imports. No April–September 2026 naphtha figures for China were found; the tables below remain full fiscal-year DGCI&S data to FY2025-26.

Updated 15 November 2026: rewritten as charts; figures unchanged.

India's naphtha exports to China rose 101% in FY2025-26

What it means: Naphtha exports to China reached $2,425.0mn in FY2025-26, up from $1,207.1mn the year before.

What to do: What to do: name paracetamol and ibuprofen route KSMs as targets in an import-substitution scheme, as the post proposes.

Benzene exports to China rose 4.5× since FY2022-23

What it means: Benzene exports reached $220.6mn in FY2025-26, up from $49.5mn in FY2022-23.

What to do: What to do: design market rules that route naphtha and benzene to domestic KSM plants before they reach a paying foreign buyer.

Isobutylbenzene exports to China fell every year, down 82.6% since FY2022-23

What it means: Isobutylbenzene exports to China fell from $22.4mn in FY2022-23 to $3.9mn in FY2025-26.

What to do: What to do: keep ibuprofen's starting material at home and track whether the 80.73% China import dependence keeps falling.

What would fix this

  • The Petroleum Planning and Analysis Cell should publish refinery-wise disposal of naphtha and benzene each year — domestic petrochemical use, other domestic use and export, so that whether the barrels freed by E20 are the same barrels going to China can be tested rather than inferred from a coincidence of timing.
  • The Petroleum Planning and Analysis Cell should report domestic naphtha production split between cracker-grade and fuel-grade, so that the claim that Indian naphtha commonly is not refined to cracker purity — one reason it is exported rather than used at home — can be checked against an official figure.
  • The Department of Chemicals and Petrochemicals should publish an annual demand–supply balance for benzene, nitrobenzene, para-aminophenol and isobutylbenzene, showing production, imports, exports and domestic consumption, so that the paracetamol and ibuprofen chains can be followed molecule by molecule in one place.
  • The Department of Pharmaceuticals should publish annual China import dependence, by value, for each key starting material and intermediate on the paracetamol and ibuprofen routes, so that improvements like ibuprofen's fall from 99.34 to 80.73 per cent are tracked officially and attributed to what actually caused them.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

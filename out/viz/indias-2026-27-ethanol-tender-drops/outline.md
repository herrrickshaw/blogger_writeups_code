India’s oil marketing companies opened bidding on 16 October for about 1,100 crore litres of ethanol for the year from November 2026 ([BPCL e-tender 26985](https://bpcltenders.eproc.in/Product/LiveDoc/BPCLDocs/TenderDocuments/26985.pdf)). Set beside last year’s tender ([tender 22376](https://www.chinimandi.com/esy-2025-26-omcs-invite-bids-for-around-1050-crore-litres-of-ethanol/)), three things change. Ethanol made from cane juice, sugar syrup and B-heavy molasses is not being bought at all, because the government has told the industry to divert no sugar to ethanol this year. Maize, the feedstock that carried the blending programme last year, gets Rs 3 a litre less. And the order of allocation flips: last year, after cooperative sugar mills and plants with long-term contracts, sugar-based ethanol was taken first and everything else after it; this year the rest goes to the cheapest feedstock first, which puts maize last in line. Every award is also provisional, pending Supreme Court cases over how ethanol is allocated.

How much to trust it. Both years’ terms are read directly from the two tender documents. Last year’s outcome (offers and allocation by feedstock) and the court cases come from press reports, and the FCI rice price for this year from the DFPD OMSS(D) 2026-27 policy order as reported by Rural Voice and Outlook India; the BPCL tender portal and government sites could not be opened from here. Where this piece calculates something, it says so.

The basic rates are unchanged for every feedstock still bought. The only price change is the maize incentive, cut from Rs 5.79 to Rs 2.79 a litre, which brings maize ethanol from Rs 71.86 to Rs 68.86. That is still the highest price in the tender, Rs 4.86 above damaged grain and Rs 8.54 above FCI rice. The All India Distillers’ Association has asked for the cut to be reviewed, citing higher raw-material, energy and logistics costs, according to ChiniMandi.

The saving to the oil companies depends on how much maize they end up buying. At last year’s maize volume of about 479 crore litres, Rs 3 a litre is about Rs 1,440 crore a year, by this blog’s arithmetic; the actual figure will be known only after allocation.

Last year cane juice, sugar syrup and B-heavy molasses together supplied about 26.4% of the roughly 1,048 crore litres allocated, about 276 crore litres. None of that is being sought this year. The government’s reason, as reported, is a smaller sugar crop in Maharashtra and Karnataka and lower opening sugar stocks. Only C-heavy molasses, the final residue of sugar-making, which supplied about 1.2% last year, remains on the cane side. Grain will have to supply almost all of the 1,100 crore litres.

One cost moves against distillers even where the ethanol price does not. Last year’s tender stated FCI’s rice price for distilleries, Rs 2,320 a quintal; this year’s does not, and the price rises to Rs 2,390 a quintal for November 2026 to June 2027, according to the Department of Food and Public Distribution’s OMSS(D) 2026-27 policy order of 2 July 2026 ([Rural Voice](https://eng.ruralvoice.in/government-to-sell-fci-rice-for-ethanol-at-rs-2320-per-qtl-below-paddy-msp-omss-policy-2026-27-announced); also [Outlook India](https://www.outlookindia.com/national/fci-sold-rice-to-ethanol-distilleries-at-nearly-40-below-acquisition-cost)), while the tender keeps rice ethanol at Rs 60.32 a litre.

The supply should be there. Last year distillers offered about 1,305 crore litres from grain alone, more than this year’s whole requirement. The question is which grain.

## Who gets allocated first: sugar-first becomes cheapest-first

Last year’s tender divided the country into zones. Zones short of local supply took every offer from their own distilleries. In zones with surplus offers, cooperative sugar mills and the contracted quantity of long-term-contract plants came first, and the rest was filled in this order: sugar, syrup and cane juice; then B-heavy and C-heavy molasses; then “all other feedstocks”, meaning grain, with maize, FCI rice and damaged grain treated alike. Surplus offers then topped up short zones pro rata.

This year’s tender drops the zones and the sugar-first order. When offers exceed the requirement in a quarter, it allocates in this order:

- Cooperative sugar mills registered with the national federation, for their eligible offers.

- Dedicated ethanol plants with long-term offtake agreements, up to the annual quantity in their contracts, cheapest feedstock first.

- Everyone else, cheapest feedstock first: C-heavy molasses and other feedstocks at Rs 57.97, then FCI rice at Rs 60.32 (capped at 108 crore litres a quarter), then damaged grain at Rs 64.00, then maize at Rs 68.86. Within the cheapest category still open, plants from the oil companies’ first long-term tender (2021) get preference up to their design capacity on a “best endeavour” basis, and the rest is shared pro rata.

Uttar Pradesh is allocated separately because of state excise rules, and a linear-programming optimiser then assigns depots, with at least three suppliers per depot cluster and no supplier above 40% of a cluster.

Last year maize competed on equal terms with other grain once sugar was placed. This year, because maize is the dearest feedstock, it is allocated only after cheaper offers run out, unless the plant has a long-term contract or is a cooperative. FCI rice is capped, and none at all can be offered for August to October, when the requirement is 250 crore litres. ChiniMandi’s analysis argues that damaged grain, mostly broken rice, at Rs 64 could take share from maize. How much depends on offers this blog cannot see.

## The court cases

Both the opening disclaimer and the allocation annex make every allocation, award and purchase order provisional. They are subject to a writ petition of 2025, special leave petitions in the Supreme Court led by SLP 22411 of 2026, cases from the Bombay High Court’s Nagpur bench and the Patna High Court transferred to the Supreme Court on 4 August 2026, and an interim arrangement approved that day. Those cases challenge the oil companies’ allocation method, including last year’s tender 22376. Last year’s cooperative-first priority was contested by dedicated grain plants: one, VINP Distilleries, won a Karnataka High Court order in June 2026 for a larger allocation under its long-term contract, and the Supreme Court ordered status quo on BPCL’s appeal. On 4 August 2026 the Supreme Court allowed the oil companies to award an extra 149 crore litres for August to October 2026, taking last year’s total to about 1,197 crore litres. Bidders must accept that allocations can be cut, moved or cancelled without compensation. The tender’s long “best endeavour” clause, which says the preference for long-term plants is “not a guarantee of offtake”, reads as written with those cases in mind.

## What is not known

- Offers. How much each feedstock will offer, which decides how much maize is bought. Bids close on 29 October.

- B-heavy and juice plants. What happens to distilleries built around cane juice and B-heavy molasses, and to the sugar mills that relied on ethanol sales to pay cane farmers.

- Court outcome. The Supreme Court could change the allocation method after awards are made.

- Later cycles. The tender is cycle 1. Whether cane juice returns in a later cycle depends on the sugar crop.

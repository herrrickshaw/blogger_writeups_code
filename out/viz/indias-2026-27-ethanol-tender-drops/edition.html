The oil companies want 1,100 crore litres for November 2026 to October 2027. Unlike last year, they will buy no ethanol from cane juice or B-heavy molasses, pay Rs 68.86 instead of Rs 71.86 for maize, cap FCI rice at 108 crore litres a quarter instead of requiring 40% of grain offers from it, and allocate the cheapest feedstock first where last year put sugar first. Every award is subject to Supreme Court cases on allocation.

India's 2026-27 ethanol tender: no cane juice, and maize cut by Rs 3 a litre

Skip to article content
Text Size

Top line

  • Rs 3 a litre off maize ethanol, which falls from Rs 71.86 to Rs 68.86 but stays the tender's dearest feedstock.
  • 26.4% of last year's allocation, about 276 crore litres, came from cane juice, sugar syrup and B-heavy molasses, which this tender does not buy.
  • 1,100 crore litres sought for November 2026 to October 2027, with grain expected to supply almost all of it.
Updated 7 November 2026. The oil companies pushed the bid deadline back from 29 October to 5 November 2026, with bids opened the same evening, according to a corrigendum reported by ChiniMandi and QCIntel. No offer totals or allocations had been reported by 7 November, so last year’s outcome (about 1,776 crore litres offered, about 1,048 crore litres allocated) remains the only comparison. The All India Distillers’ Association has also asked for higher FCI-rice ethanol prices as well as a review of the maize cut (Business Today, 17 October).

Updated 15 November 2026: rewritten as charts; figures unchanged.

Maize ethanol falls Rs 3 a litre but stays the dearest feedstock

What it means: The basic rates are unchanged, so the only price cut is the maize incentive, and maize still costs the most of any feedstock still bought.

What to do: Distillers should press for the maize incentive cut to be reviewed, as the All India Distillers' Association has asked, before the bids close on 29 October.

Cane juice, sugar and B-heavy supply, 26.4% last year, is not sought

What it means: Cane-based ethanol supplied about a quarter of last year's allocation, and this tender removes it entirely, leaving grain to carry the programme.

What to do: Watch the offers from distilleries built on cane juice and B-heavy molasses, since the post says it cannot see whether those plants will keep running.

Allocation flips from sugar-first to cheapest-first, putting maize last

  1. ESY 2025-26Zones short of supply take all offers; surplus zones fill sugar, syrup and cane juice before molasses and grain
  2. ESY 2026-27No zones; cooperative sugar mills and long-term-contract plants come first, then the cheapest feedstock first
  3. ESY 2026-27Maize comes last, after C-heavy molasses, other feedstocks, FCI rice and damaged grain

What it means: Because maize is the dearest feedstock, it is allocated only after cheaper offers run out, unless the plant has a long-term contract or is a cooperative.

What to do: Read the allocation outcome by feedstock once awards are made, since every award is provisional and can be cut under the pending Supreme Court cases.

What would fix this

  • The oil companies should explain the switch from sugar-first to cheapest-first in the tender itself, with the expected cost saving, since it changes which plants get orders.
  • The oil companies should publish the allocation by feedstock, state and supplier type for each cycle, as ChiniMandi’s reporting of last year’s figures shows is possible, so that the effect of the maize cut can be seen.
  • Tie the FCI rice cap to live central-pool stocks, not a figure fixed for the year, so that rice for ethanol tapers if stocks fall.
  • Announce the sugar-diversion rule before the cane crushing season and before distillers commit feedstock, with a rule for when cane juice comes back, instead of deciding each year.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

Corrected 25 October 2026. The FCI rice price for the 2026-27 supply year is attributed to the DFPD’s OMSS(D) 2026-27 policy order (Rural Voice, Outlook India), not to a Business Standard report of a Rajya Sabha reply: Rs 2,320 a quintal applies only until 31 October 2026 and Rs 2,390 from 1 November 2026. The tender text itself (quarter split, 72 LMT, 108 crore litre cap, cheapest-first order) could not be re-checked against the BPCL document, which is not available online.

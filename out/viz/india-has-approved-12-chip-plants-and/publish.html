India's chip plants: about Rs 5,619 crore of Rs 76,000 crore spent by March 2026

Skip to article content
Text Size

Top line

  • 5 of 12 approved chip projects are in commercial production, all doing assembly, test or packaging.
  • 20% of the Rs 1.64 lakh crore approved investment is in plants already producing.
  • 7% of the Rs 76,000 crore first-round outlay is spent (Rs 5,619 crore, at the revised estimate).

What it means: Five units are producing, all assembly, test or packaging, and none makes wafers.

What to do: Watch whether the Dholera fab and the Jewar plant meet their 2028 targets.

Central spending is about 7% of the outlay, because support is paid at milestones

What it means: Support is paid in instalments as projects reach milestones, so spending lags approvals of Rs 1.64 lakh crore.

What to do: Measure whether projects reach milestones faster, since that sets how quickly the Rs 127,500 crore of Semicon 2.0 is spent.

Semicon 2.0 pays fabs a smaller share of cost than the first round did

What it means: Semicon 2.0 is larger at Rs 127,500 crore and adds equipment, materials and chip design support.

What to do: Watch whether the lower fab rates change which projects apply within the three-year application window.

What would fix this

  • MeitY should publish a project-wise table for every approved project, updated quarterly: approved investment, central support approved, support released, investment made and date of commercial production.
  • The Union Budget should show approved commitments under the semiconductor programmes alongside yearly spending, so that the gap between money promised and money paid is visible.
  • Semicon 2.0 should set and publish milestones for each project at approval, with a review if a project misses two in a row.
  • Production figures for the five operating units, in chips or wafers a month, should be published so “commercial production” can be measured.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

What this piece could not establish

No official project-wise status or disbursement table was found; statuses come from news reports and ministers’ remarks, and the status of SiCSem, 3D Glass Solutions and ASIP could not be confirmed. Spending figures come from a parliamentary committee report and PRS’s budget analysis, read through search-engine text; the 2023-24 figure may be labelled differently from later years, and spending before 2023-24 was not found. The Semicon 2.0 support rates are from news coverage of the notification, and the rate for assembly and packaging units was not found. Sources disagree on whether the outlay was Rs 1.25 or Rs 1.27 lakh crore; Rs 1.25 lakh crore was the Finance Ministry’s clearance in June and Rs 1.27 lakh crore the Cabinet’s approval in July.

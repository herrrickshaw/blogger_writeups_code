India runs a genuine refined-fuel surplus — US$53.0 billion of petroleum products out against US$9.3 billion in. But the biggest buyer is the Netherlands, not the neighbourhood, and Nepal and Bhutan are already supplied almost entirely by Indian public-sector refiners. On ethanol the premise reverses completely: India is a net importer.

For April–September 2026, Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas data reported by Kotak Neo put petroleum-product export earnings at about ₹2.59 lakh crore, roughly 50% more than a year earlier in rupee terms, while export volumes fell 16% (diesel volumes 19%): the fall in value described below has reversed on higher prices and a weaker rupee, and the volume decline this piece could not measure is now visible. Kpler data reported by ThePrint put October 2026 diesel exports at about 700,000 barrels a day, a 13-month high.

What it means: Customs data show India as a buyer of alcohol, so idle distillery capacity is unused plant rather than exportable stock.

What to do: Do not build an export plan on ethanol until the trade balance, not installed capacity, turns positive.

What would fix this

  • DGCI&S should populate the quantity fields in TRADESTAT for HS 2710 and its sub-headings 271012 and 271019, so a 15.3 per cent fall in refined-fuel export value can be split between lower prices and fewer barrels instead of being read only in dollars.
  • CBIC and DGCI&S should split HS 2207 into separate tariff lines for fuel-grade, industrial and potable ethyl alcohol, so the US$352.9 million net import position can be traced to the grade that matters for the blending programme rather than all alcohol together.
  • DGCI&S should report the “Unspecified” destination for refined-fuel exports by type — bunker sales, ship-to-ship transfers and other undeclared cargoes, so the US$2,418 million booked without a destination in FY2025–26 can be accounted for.
  • PPAC should show sales to Nepal by IOCL and to Nepal and Bhutan by BPCL as separate lines in its import-export tables, rather than only noting that they are counted in total exports, so captive neighbourhood supply can be distinguished from competitive export business.
  • The Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas should publish an official annual figure for installed ethanol distillation capacity and its utilisation, so policy on idle distilleries rests on government statistics rather than a ratings agency's estimates of 2,000 crore litres and 60 per cent utilisation.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

What this piece could not establish
What this article does not establish. Physical volumes: TRADESTAT returned no quantity figures for these queries, so every comparison here is in US dollars and a fall in value may be a fall in price rather than in barrels. Refining capacity and throughput are not examined. Nor is the delivered economics of any particular route — margins, freight and duty would decide whether a given cargo is worth moving, and none of that is in trade statistics. The ethanol figures cover HS 2207 in full and cannot isolate fuel-grade ethanol.

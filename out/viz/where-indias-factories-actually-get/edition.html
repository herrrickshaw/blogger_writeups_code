Factory investment in India: 63.3% in Gujarat, Odisha and Maharashtra, 2021-2025

Top line

  • 63.3% of implemented industrial investment, 2021 to December 2025, sits in Gujarat, Odisha and Maharashtra.
  • 22% of national FDI goes to Karnataka, against 2.2% of implemented manufacturing investment.
Updated 12 November 2026. These are still the latest IEM figures DPIIT has published, checked 12 November 2026. Its monthly IEM Statistics Report and company lists stopped after the September 2024 issue, which carries data to August 2024. Since 1 October 2025, IEM filings have been made on the National Single Window System, which publishes no IEM statistics. The annual totals here come from DPIIT’s Annual Report 2025-26, which runs to December 2025; DPIIT’s last two annual reports appeared in February. India’s Factory Intentions, 2001–2024 explains the move and what the public can no longer see.

What it means: Gujarat, Odisha and Maharashtra took 63.3% of all implemented industrial investment in the five years to December 2025.

What to do: DPIIT should resume the monthly IEM Statistics Report, last issued for August 2024 data.

Karnataka has 2.2% of implemented investment and 22% of national FDI

What it means: Karnataka is 2.2% of national implemented investment but 22% of national FDI.

What to do: DPIIT should add a field to IEM filings recording whether the capital is domestic or foreign.

Maharashtra has the most filed intent not yet built, Rs 454,589 crore

What it means: Maharashtra has the largest unbuilt intent, while Gujarat built more than it filed in the window.

What to do: DPIIT should tag each Part B implementation with the year of its original Part A filing and publish conversion rates by filing cohort.

What would fix this

  • DPIIT should resume the monthly IEM Statistics Report, last issued for August 2024 data, and compile it from NSWS filings, so the Part A and Part B series that ran from 2001 is not broken by the move to the single window.
  • DPIIT should publish the investment value of Part A filings by sector, not only their count, so intent and implementation can be compared in rupees for each industry rather than only for states.
  • DPIIT should add a field to IEM filings recording whether the capital is domestic or foreign, so a Tata plant and a Daikin plant no longer file identically and states like Odisha and Karnataka can be read without setting IEM against separate FDI data.
  • DPIIT should publish a concordance from the 1951 Act’s industry categories to current NIC codes, with electronics shown as its own line, so electronics investment is not split across Electricals Equipment, Comm-Office Equipment and Telecommunications.
  • DPIIT should tag each Part B implementation with the year of its original Part A filing and publish conversion rates by filing cohort, so figures like Punjab’s 356% and Gujarat’s 178% are not pipeline artefacts of same-period ratios.
  • DPIIT should list, in each Annual Report, the individual implemented IEMs above a stated size threshold, and reconcile the period labels of Appendices V and VI, so single-year spikes such as Glass in 2024 or Fuels in 2025 can be traced to specific projects.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

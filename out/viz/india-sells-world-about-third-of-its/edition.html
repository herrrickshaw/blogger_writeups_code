An oleoresin is a spice with the fibre taken out — the colour, heat and aroma of paprika, pepper or turmeric concentrated into a thick extract that food makers can dose by the gram. India exported US$535.9 million of spice oils and oleoresins in 2024–25, about a third of a global market put at US$1.6 billion, at roughly ten times the value per kilo of its spice exports as a whole. In August the United States found that India's paprika oleoresin was both dumped and subsidised, and on 18 September its trade commission cleared the way for duties of about 23% to 31% on the single biggest product in the market.

Top line

  • US$535.9m of spice oils and oleoresins exported in 2024–25, from 20,940 tonnes: about a third of the world market on the post's estimate.
  • US$25.60 a kilo, against US$2.62 for India's spice exports overall.
  • 23–31% combined US anti-dumping and anti-subsidy rates on Indian paprika oleoresin, final in August 2026.

What to do: Keep the import and export pepper figures apart when quoting them, since the post says the periods and sources differ.

What would fix this

  • The Department of Commerce's trade-defence wing should publish a plain summary of which Indian programmes the US found countervailable in the paprika case and at what rate, so exporters of other products that use the same schemes can see their exposure before the next petition rather than after it.
  • The Spices Board should split its "spice oils and oleoresins" export line by product and destination — paprika and capsicum, pepper, turmeric, ginger and others — using the ITC-HS 3301.90 sub-lines it already defines, so a US$536 million export can be analysed product by product.
  • DGFT should publish annually how much spice is imported under advance authorisation for oils and oleoresins, and the value of the exports made from it, so India can measure how much of the oleoresin trade is domestic value added.
  • The Spices Board should use its SPICED scheme's "Clean and Safe Spices" mission to fund routine ethylene oxide and solvent-residue testing to EU and US limits at its spices parks, open to smaller extractors that cannot run their own accredited laboratories, to protect the EU access India has just regained.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

Sources and caveats

Export quantities and values for 2020–21 to 2024–25 are from the Spices Board of India's "Item-wise export of spices from India" table (source DGCI&S, Kolkata); 2024–25 is final and 2023–24 revised final. The 2025–26 share (12% of US$4,430.9 million) is from the Spices Board's annual export release as reported; the dollar figure derived from it is this blog's. Global market sizes, regional and product shares are market-research estimates from Fortune Business Insights ("Oleoresin Market", 2026 edition), IMARC and Mordor Intelligence, read via search extracts; their methods are undisclosed. India's share of European supply and the "more than 30%" market share are from CBI (Netherlands) market information on oleoresins, using 2021 data. Synthite's domestic and global shares are from CRISIL's rating rationale of 31 December 2024; its FY25 revenue is from a company-filings aggregator. Plant Lipids' revenue and import share are from Acuité's rating of 17 July 2025; Mane Kancor's figures from CARE Ratings (2023); Akay's from CRISIL (January 2025); AVT's FY26 results as reported by Business Standard (29 May 2026). Extraction yields are from trade and technical literature and are indicative. The advance-authorisation value-addition rule is DGFT Public Notice 08/2024-25 (3 June 2024) as summarised by tax and trade publications. Pepper import volumes are UN Comtrade data as compiled by a trade-data provider and were not checked against DGCI&S; the Spices Board's own import table could not be opened. The US case is from Commerce Department and US International Trade Commission notices in the Federal Register (initiation 22 July 2025; preliminary determinations 6 February and 2 April 2026; final determinations 21 August 2026) and the USITC's 18 September 2026 news release; the petition-era US import value is from Commerce's initiation data; the 1979 and 2001 cases are USITC Publications 989 and 3415. Preliminary rates are not given because sources conflicted. The EU pepper and capsicum change is Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/194; the ethylene oxide limit is Regulation (EU) 2022/1396; the alert study is in the Journal of Food Safety (May 2024); solvent limits are 21 CFR Part 173 and Regulation (EU) 231/2012. The SPICED scheme is from a PIB release of September 2024. Most figures were read through search extracts because the sources were not directly reachable while this was compiled, and should be opened before being relied on. What this piece does not cover: supercritical CO₂ extraction and the ethanol-plant CO₂ opportunity, which this blog covered separately, and essential oils such as mint.

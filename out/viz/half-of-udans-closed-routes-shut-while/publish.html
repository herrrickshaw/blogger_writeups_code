Modified UDAN puts Rs 28,840 crore into regional flying over ten years and stretches each route’s subsidy from three years to five, on a taper. Of 331 routes that had closed by July, 165 shut before their subsidy ran out, so a longer taper addresses only part of why routes fail.

UDAN regional routes: 165 of 331 closed before their subsidy ran out

Top line

  • 331 of 679 UDAN routes that started had closed by July 2026; 348 were flying.
  • 165 of the closed routes shut while their subsidy was still being paid, about half of all closures.
  • Rs 2,960 of airline subsidy per UDAN passenger over ten years.

Updated 15 November 2026: rewritten as charts; figures unchanged.

What it means: Only 7% of the routes awarded were still flying once their subsidy had ended.

What to do: Report awarded, started and surviving routes on the same basis in every round so later counts can be compared.

Modified UDAN extends full support by half a year, then tapers it

What it means: The new terms give about 17% more full support over a route's life but cut year-three support from 100% to 75%.

What to do: Judge the five-year taper against the 165 routes that closed under full subsidy, since a longer taper does not help them.

What would fix this

  • The Ministry of Civil Aviation should publish a route-level register: award date, start date, end date, aircraft, seats offered, load factor and VGF paid each year, so that survival can be measured by round, region and aircraft type rather than reconstructed from parliamentary answers.
  • Routes that close while still fully subsidised should trigger a review of the bid before the airport or the route is offered again, since a longer taper does not address them.
  • States should extend the jet-fuel VAT concession for UDAN routes to match the five-year support, and the Ministry should report which states have done so.
  • For each of the 100 new airports, the plan should say how fuel will be supplied and at what cost per kilolitre, alongside the runway and terminal.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

What this piece could not establish

The Cabinet release, the PIB notes and the Rajya Sabha reply could not be opened directly; figures come from their text as indexed by search engines and from news and exam-preparation summaries. A Civil Aviation Ministry factsheet reported by IANS on 20 October gave 1.49 crore passengers and 625 routes, figures that match the scheme’s April 2025 totals; this piece uses the October 2026 PIB figures (1.72 crore and 687). The route counts differ slightly between sources (679 in July, 687 in October). How many of the 348 flying routes are still within their subsidy period, and how much of the Rs 5,091 crore went to routes that later closed, is not published. The Rs 400 crore not itemised in the outlay table is the difference between the total and the four components the release lists.

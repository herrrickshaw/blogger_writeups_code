West Bengal has sanctioned 99.99% of its PMAY-G rural-housing target since FY2021-22 — and completed 12% of it. Nationally, the completion rate over the same window is 56.6%. The gap isn't a construction problem: Parliament's own answer says the Centre withheld West Bengal's funds over irregularity complaints starting FY2022-23, and the state's own corrective report is still "under examination" four years later.

PMAY-G in West Bengal: 12.6 lakh houses sanctioned, 1.5 lakh built, funds frozen 4 years

Top line

  • 12.1% of West Bengal's five-year PMAY-G target is complete, against 56.6% nationally.
  • ₹687.84 crore of central share released to West Bengal over five years, against ₹24,232.39 crore for Assam.
Updated 18 October 2026. Press reports from June and July 2026 say the Centre has approved fresh PMAY-G houses for West Bengal after the change of government in the state, which this piece does not mention. A PTI report carried by ThePrint says one lakh rural houses were pre-approved before the beneficiary survey was complete; a UNI report gives 10 lakh houses as part of a wider rural package; and NBM&CW reports one lakh houses alongside rural-road funds. The reports disagree on the numbers. None of them says whether the central share withheld from FY2022-23 has been released, and the 11 August 2026 Lok Sabha answer quoted below still described the state’s Action Taken Report as under examination. So the old freeze may formally stand while new sanctions have resumed. These are search-result summaries; no Ministry of Rural Development release was found. The original text below is unchanged.
Updated 7 November 2026. The state’s own replacement scheme has kept paying while the central share was frozen. Banglar Bari Gramin, renamed Paschim Banga Awas, paid a second and final instalment of ₹60,000 each to 10,20,379 beneficiaries in August 2026 (The Week); these are state-funded houses and are not counted in the PMAY-G completions below. No Ministry of Rural Development release ending the PMAY-G freeze was found as of 7 November.

What it means: Odisha's freeze ended after a satisfactory Action Taken Report, but West Bengal's has no resolution date.

What to do: Ask for a decision date on West Bengal's Action Taken Report, as Odisha's case shows a freeze can be lifted.

What would fix this

  • The Ministry of Rural Development should set and publish a deadline for completing its examination of West Bengal’s PMAY-G Action Taken Report, so that a central-share freeze running since FY2022-23 has a stated end point rather than remaining “under examination” indefinitely.
  • The Ministry of Rural Development should publish a written protocol for withholding and resuming PMAY-G central share, with the criteria an Action Taken Report must meet and review timelines, so that cases like Odisha, where funds resumed, and West Bengal, where they have not, are judged on the same published test.
  • The Ministry of Rural Development should publish the findings of the National Level Monitoring and Senior Officers Teams on West Bengal’s PMAY-G implementation, so that the irregularities behind withholding funds for houses already sanctioned are on the public record, not only summarised in a parliamentary answer.
  • The West Bengal government should publish its Action Taken Report and a status list of the roughly 11 lakh houses sanctioned since FY2021-22 but not completed, so that households waiting on 12,60,941 sanctions with only 1,52,014 completions can see where their case stands.
  • The Ministry of Housing and Urban Affairs should publish a state-wise PMAY-U table of target, sanctioned, completed and central funds released, in the same form as the PMAY-G annexures, so that state-level delivery gaps in the urban scheme can be found as this one was.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

The Bihar and Rajasthan sanction figures are drawn from Ministry of Rural Development press notes on the Minister's state-level events, posted 20 August 2026 and 17 August 2026 respectively — these are announcement releases, not written parliamentary answers, and carry the ministry's own framing language alongside the hard figures reported here. The PMAY-U milestone figures are drawn from a Ministry of Housing and Urban Affairs press release on the 8th CSMC meeting, posted 9 August 2026. All are primary government sources, graded strong; none of the figures in this piece were independently re-derived or cross-checked against a non-government source.

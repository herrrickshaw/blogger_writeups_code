What would fix this

  • The Office of the Economic Adviser, DPIIT should publish calendar-year average WPI inflation alongside its April–March figures, so cross-country producer comparisons do not carry gaps like the 4.1-point difference for India in 2023 that this post traces mostly to April-anchoring against calendar-year averages.
  • The Office of the Economic Adviser should publish an experimental producer price index for India alongside the WPI, with a documented concordance between the two, since most economies compared here publish a PPI and India's headline wholesale basket has no direct international counterpart.
  • The Labour Bureau should publish an annual national real-wage index, deflating its wage-rate series by CPI on a stated base year, so the question this post could not answer — whether Indian wages kept pace with four years of 5–6.7% inflation — has an official series behind it.
  • The World Bank should review and annotate Italy's producer price values in its Global Database of Inflation, where readings of 56.1% for 2020 and 42.8% for 2022 could not be reconciled with any national source and forced Italy's exclusion from this comparison.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

What this piece could not establish

For India, China, Brazil, South Africa, Indonesia and Saudi Arabia, this article cannot pass the same verdict — and says so rather than improvising one. None are OECD members, so the wage bulletin above does not cover them, and no comparable harmonised real-wage series was located in this research pass. What can be said, honestly, from the price side alone: China’s essentially flat five-year price level (3.5% total) is unambiguously easier on a fixed or slow-growing income than Brazil’s 35.7%, whatever wages did in either country, because the arithmetic of purchasing-power erosion runs through the price side regardless. India’s middle-class family lived through four consecutive years (2021–24) of inflation between 5% and 6.7% — the “long plateau” described earlier — which is a longer sustained squeeze than any single-year spike, and whether Indian wages kept pace over that plateau is not established by anything in this article and would need a dedicated piece using India’s own wage-rate or earnings data.

Land under power lines: 30–60% of market value paid, against 10% for pipelines

Top line

  • 30% to 60% of market value is the corridor payment for power line land, against 10% for a pipeline.
  • 200% of market value for the land under each tower base.
  • ₹44.34 lakh more per kilometre of 400 kV line when market value applies.
Updated 7 November 2026. Two intermediate letters this piece could not find are now described in secondary sources, and they move some of the “what changed” below to 2025. A law-firm summary of the 21 March 2025 supplementary guidelines says they already set the 30/45/60% corridor rates, created the district Market Rate Committee with independent valuers, let work proceed once the committee’s rate is paid, and allowed CERC pass-through. A January 2026 report of an amendment (presumably the 15 December 2025 letter) describes a lottery between valuers’ reports. Against those, what the 6 October 2026 letter itself adds is the three-valuer, ±20%-of-median clipping rule and the state Appellate Authority. The per-hectare comparison with pipelines is unaffected. Neither letter was read in the original. Sources: Mondaq (7 May 2025), Power Line (20 January 2026).

Updated 15 November 2026: rewritten as charts; figures unchanged.

Power line corridors pay 30% to 60% of market value, a pipeline 10%

What it means: The 6 October 2026 letter sets corridor payments at 30%, 45% or 60% of market value, while the 1962 pipeline Act pays 10%, once.

What to do: Landowners below a corridor should check whether their state has notified a Market Rate Committee and Appellate Authority, since states may still adopt their own rules.

What it means: On a hectare valued at ₹70 lakh, a tower base pays ₹140.00 lakh at 200%, against ₹21.00 lakh at 30% for a rural corridor.

What to do: Owners of tower-base land should ask for the Market Rate Committee's notified rate, since the 200% is applied to that market value.

What it means: Corridor and tower-base payments for one kilometre total ₹103.46 lakh at market value and ₹59.12 lakh at circle rate, a gap of ₹44.34 lakh.

What to do: Developers and regulators should track the CERC Change in Law pass-through, since extra compensation can be recovered from tariffs paid by consumers.

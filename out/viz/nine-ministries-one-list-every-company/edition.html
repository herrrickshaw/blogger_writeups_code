India runs 14 Production Linked Incentive schemes and a handful of major competitive-auction programmes, and almost every one of them names its winners in public — in Press Information Bureau releases and, less consistently, on ministry websites, but never on one aggregated portal. This piece compiles all 14 PLI schemes plus four auction categories, reading the actual release and portal text rather than trusting a headline. It also asks the harder question — how many companies actually competed, not just who won — and reports plainly, scheme by scheme, what is and isn't publicly known. No names or numbers are invented where none exist.

Top line

  • 145 e-bids from 40 companies for 23 Discovered Small Field (DSF) Round-II areas; 14 companies were awarded areas.
  • 239 applications for 50 approvals across the first two rounds of the bulk drug scheme.
  • 44 bids for 67 mines in the 10th round of commercial coal mine auctions.
Updated 7 November 2026. Both auction programmes have moved on since this list was compiled. The Coal Ministry’s transaction adviser put the commercial coal auction total at 145 blocks across 15 tranches in October 2026 (the table below stops at the 13th round), and a 16th round of 29 mines opened on 17 September 2026. In critical minerals, 64 of the 100 unique blocks offered in eight tranches had been auctioned by November 2026 (the table below stops at Tranche V); Tranche IX, with 20 blocks, opened on 5 November 2026 under amended rules that need only two technically qualified bidders in the second round. Winner names for the later rounds have not been added here. The 58 companies found eligible under the Critical Mineral Recycling Incentive Scheme have still not been named.

Updated 15 November 2026: rewritten as charts; figures unchanged.

Updated 16 November 2026: The Ministry of Heavy Industries received 22 bids for the 10 GWh grid-scale storage tranche. The bid deadline was extended from 13 October to 10 November, and technical bids were opened on 11 November. Bidders include Reliance Industries, JSW Neo Energy, Amara Raja Advanced Cell Technologies, Exide Energy Solutions, Waaree Energy Storage Solutions and Vikram Solar. No winners have been named, and the ministry has not said when financial bids open. Source: PIB, 12 November 2026.

Oil and gas: 14 of 40 companies that bid won areas in DSF Round-II

What it means: Of the 40 companies that bid in DSF Round-II, 14 were awarded contract areas.

What to do: Publish the full bidder list for each round alongside the winners, as the post asks of the government.

Bulk drugs: 50 approvals from 239 applications in the first two rounds

What it means: Combined, the two rounds received 239 applications and approved 50 of them.

What to do: Name the roughly 189 unsuccessful applicants in the scheme's release, as the approved ones are named.

Coal mine auctions: 44 bids for 67 mines in the 10th round

What it means: The 10th round received 44 physical bids for 67 coal mines on offer.

What to do: Publish the number of bidders per block for every round, not only for the winners.

What would fix this

  • DPIIT should publish one consolidated, downloadable register of approved beneficiaries across all 14 PLI schemes, listing each company, committed investment, actual investment and incentive disbursed, so that the cross-ministry list this post had to assemble release by release exists in one official place.
  • MeitY should publish the full roster of the 32 companies approved under the mobile and electronic-components PLI, with disbursement by company and period, rather than naming beneficiaries only in occasional Empowered Committee releases such as those for Padget Electronics and Foxconn.
  • The Ministry of Mines should publish, with every critical-mineral tranche result, the number of technical bidders per block and their names, matching the “List of Bidders” PDFs the Ministry of Coal already posts on coal.nic.in, so that premiums such as 752.05% for Ponchi can be read against competition.
  • The Ministry of Mines should publish the names of the 58 companies declared eligible under the ₹1,500 crore Critical Mineral Recycling Incentive Scheme, with each one’s pledged capacity, rather than only the count and aggregate figures released in April 2026.
  • MSTC should publish its mineral-block event results as static, downloadable documents with stable links, so that the auction results held in its dynamically loaded “Event Results” section can be cited and checked independently.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

Sources and caveats

Update sources, 7 November 2026: Ministry of Coal, pre-bid presentation by the transaction adviser for the 16th round (7 October 2026); Rajya Sabha Unstarred Question 2419 (10 August 2026): 141 blocks auctioned across 14 rounds as of 30 June 2026; IANS, “Centre launches 9th tranche of e-auction for critical mineral blocks” (5 November 2026); PSU Watch on the Mineral (Auction) Third Amendment Rules, 2026.

Every company name in this piece was transcribed from the body text of a specific government release cited beside it — a PIB press release (via its PRID, Press Release ID) or a document/page hosted directly on a ministry's own website (texmin.gov.in, mofpi.gov.in, mnre.gov.in, coal.nic.in, mines.gov.in) — never inferred from a title, URL, or search-engine snippet. Where information came only from a search snippet describing a page this piece could not itself open (originally the Specialty Steel round figures, now resolved via PIB PRID 2225496 as noted in an update; the Kavach vendor names, still unresolved), that is explicitly flagged as unverified or secondary-sourced rather than presented as a confirmed government finding. Ministry websites are, on the evidence gathered here, wildly inconsistent in accessibility: some (Textiles, Food Processing, Mines, Coal, MNRE) serve plain, readable data; others (Steel, DoT, Civil Aviation) actively blocked automated access during this research. This piece does not verify that any contract or approval was subsequently completed, cancelled, or modified after its cited release date — the Nayakkarpatti Tungsten annulment noted in Part 1 is the one exception found in passing, and even that was not independently confirmed. Very long applicant rosters (Textiles, ~170; Food Processing, 119; Auto Components, ~75) are given as representative samples with the source URL for the full list, not reproduced in full, to keep this piece readable. This article does not recommend any investment, business, or policy decision; nothing here is investment or policy advice.

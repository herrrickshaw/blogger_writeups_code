India's oil ministry has its own scoreboard for how seriously the world's oil and gas majors are taking decarbonisation — and it isn't flattering. Buried in the Energy Transition Advisory Committee's 2023 report "The Green Shift" is a company-by-company table of who has pledged what, and a companion chart showing oil and gas has the weakest net-zero commitment rate of any hard-to-abate sector benchmarked. The same report is also the clearest public trail connecting that global scorecard to what ONGC, IOCL and OIL are actually building at home — carbon injection wells and biogas-fertiliser plants, not press-release pledges.

Oil ministry's Green Shift report: scope 1+2 emissions at about 4% of global CO₂

Top line

  • 8,400 Mt of CO₂ a year comes from customers burning the fuel, against about 1,600 Mt from the industry's own sites.
  • 10 of 20 of the largest oil and gas producers have a 2050 net-zero commitment, the lowest rate of the four sectors checked.
Updated 7 November 2026. The pledge board below is the report’s February 2023 snapshot, and two of its rows have since moved. Shell cut its 2030 net-carbon-intensity target to a 15–20% reduction and dropped its 2035 target (March 2024); bp’s February 2025 strategy reset retired key emission targets and raised its oil-and-gas output goal. In India, the 2026-27 Budget set aside ₹20,000 crore over five years for carbon capture, but the country still has no large-scale capture plant operating; the Koyali–Gandhar scheme described below remains a plan, and ONGC’s smaller Gandhar pilot uses CO₂ from Dahej and Hazira instead. Sources: Offshore Engineer, AFP via TerraDaily, Argus.

Updated 15 November 2026: rewritten as charts; figures unchanged.

Scope 3 product combustion is more than five times the industry's own emissions

What it means: Most of the industry's climate footprint sits in the fuel customers burn, not in the company's own operations.

What to do: Read every net-zero pledge against the scope it actually covers before accepting it as a headline number.

Oil and gas has the weakest net-zero commitment rate of any sector ETAC checked

What it means: Ten of the twenty largest oil and gas producers have committed to net zero, against thirteen in steel and in airlines.

What to do: Ask oil and gas producers for the same 2050 net-zero commitment that steel and airline producers have already made.

IOCL commits to net zero in 2046, ahead of the international majors bar Reliance

What it means: The baselines come from different fiscal years, so the absolute figures are not directly comparable across rows.

What to do: Ask each Indian producer to restate its baseline on one common year so the net-zero paths can be compared like for like.

What would fix this

  • The Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas should require its oil and gas PSUs to report scope 1, 2 and 3 emissions each year against one common baseline year, in a single published table, so IOCL, ONGC, BPCL, HPCL, GAIL and Oil India targets can be compared without six different baselines.
  • The Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas should publish an annual progress update to “The Green Shift”, showing each PSU’s actual emissions against its stated net-zero pathway, so the February 2023 pledge board does not remain the latest official reference.
  • ONGC and Oil India should publish firm net-zero target dates and the studies behind them, so ONGC’s “aspirational” 2050 and Oil India’s “to be firmed up” become commitments that can be tracked like IOCL’s 2046 and BPCL’s FY2039-40.
  • ONGC and IOCL should publish a construction and injection status report for the Koyali–Gandhar carbon capture project, including tonnes of CO₂ injected to date, so progress towards the 5–6 million tonne 2040 estimate can be checked.
  • IOCL should publish, every quarter, the number of SATAT compressed-biogas plants commissioned against Letters of Intent issued, and the volume of fermented organic manure sold, so the 18-of-2,247 figure is replaced by a current count.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

What this piece does not establish. This piece summarises company-level decarbonisation targets exactly as tabulated in ETAC's Exhibit 1.7 and does not independently verify each company's current target against its own most recent sustainability disclosure — targets in this space are revised periodically, and the report is dated February 2023. It does not reconcile scope coverage across companies into one comparable metric; several 2050 figures shown are scope 1+2 only, and the report itself does not always specify whether a company's absence from the scope-3 column reflects no target or simply no disclosure found. It does not establish IOCL's current 2026 CBG commissioning count beyond the report's own 18-of-2,247 figure, which is now roughly three years old. Sources and caveats

All company-level decarbonisation targets, the scope 1/2/3 emissions breakdown, and the Exhibit 1.7 and Exhibit 1.8 figures are drawn directly from "The Green Shift," the Final Report of the Energy Transition Advisory Committee, Ministry of Petroleum & Natural Gas, Government of India (published February 2023, sourced from mopng.gov.in) — a primary government document, graded strong; both exhibits cite company reports and the Science Based Targets initiative as their own underlying sources, which this piece has not independently re-verified against each company's current disclosures. The ONGC–IOC Koyali–Gandhar carbon capture project (CO₂ compression, pipeline routing to Gandhar, EOR objective, 5–6 million tonne 2040 sequestration estimate, 10% oil-gain projection) and the ONGC–Equinor MoU are drawn from the same report's company-profile chapter, graded strong as a primary-source project description, though this piece did not find a public 2025–2026 status update on the Koyali project's construction progress. Update, 7 November 2026: Shell’s 2024 target revision per Offshore Engineer; bp’s February 2025 reset per AFP; India’s ₹20,000 crore CCUS allocation and the absence of operating large-scale CCUS per Argus. Update, 27 September 2026: ONGC has since launched a related but distinct CCS pilot at Gandhar itself — repurposing two abandoned onshore wells to inject roughly 100 tonnes/day of CO₂ sourced from Dahej-region industrial units and ONGC's own Hazira plant, described as the company's first fully integrated capture-transport-storage pilot. Separately, on the Koyali refinery side specifically, Dastur Energy, Air Liquide Global E&C Solutions and the Bureau of Economic Geology have been engaged to design and assess feasibility for a much larger (5,000+ tonnes/day potential) capture project — still at the design/feasibility stage, not yet under construction, per iamrenew.com, carbonherald.com, chemindigest.com and Indian Chemical News coverage from 2026. The IOCL CBG figures (2,247 LOIs, 5.5 MMTPA capacity, 18 plants commissioned, the Jaipur and Gorakhpur plant details, and the manure-marketing business vertical) are drawn from the same report's IOCL profile and biofuels chapter, graded strong as a primary-source company disclosure, though these figures reflect the report's February 2023 publication date and have not been independently updated against IOCL's current SATAT scheme numbers.

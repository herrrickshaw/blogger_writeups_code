Since 1984, Tamil Nadu Newsprint and Papers Limited has made paper without cutting down a pulpwood plantation to do it — running its mills mostly on bagasse, the fibrous stalk left over after a sugar mill crushes cane. It is the clearest working example in India of an agro-residue substituting for wood at real industrial scale. What's less clear is whether the industry's own trade body is actually asking for more of it, or asking for something else entirely.

Updated 15 November 2026: rewritten as charts; figures unchanged.

Hardwood, not bagasse, is the largest of TNPL's three pulping lines

What it means: Bagasse is a real but second-largest line at Karur, with hardwood carrying more tonnes.

What to do: Recheck the claim that bagasse is TNPL's primary furnish against the 730 tonnes a day of hardwood.

Agro-residue is the smallest fibre stream in India's paper industry

What it means: Agro-residue is not a growing share of national fibre, and the trade body's public asks do not name it.

What to do: Search IPMA's own press archive for any bagasse capacity ask before treating it as an industry priority.

Domestic wood supply falls short of the paper industry's demand

What it means: The wood gap, which the post projects toward 15 million tonnes, is the supply problem IPMA publicly names.

What to do: Track the projected widening of the wood gap before treating bagasse as a substitute for pulpwood.

What would fix this

  • The Department of Food and Public Distribution should require sugar mills to report annual bagasse generated and its disposal — captive cogeneration, sale to paper mills, other uses — and publish the totals by state, so that the 5 to 24 million tonne range for paper’s share can be settled.
  • The Central Pulp & Paper Research Institute should publish an annual mill-wise census of fibre furnish — wood, recycled fibre and agro-residue including bagasse, so that the industry mix rests on an official count rather than the trade association’s own estimate of 6–8% agro-residue.
  • CPPRI should publish its findings on silica removal and recovery-boiler fouling in bagasse soda pulping, with measured costs, so that mills weighing a TNPL-style furnish can see the size of the handicap this post could describe but not quantify.
  • The Ministry of New & Renewable Energy should report bagasse cogeneration capacity separately from non-bagasse biomass and waste-to-energy in its state-wise table, so that the 11,747.57 MW combined figure shows how much bagasse is committed to power.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

Sources and caveats

This piece was researched through web search rather than direct page fetches: this session's network could not reach several of the primary and trade-press domains cited below directly, so claims rest on search-result summaries and cross-checking across independent outlets rather than a primary document read in full, except where a company's or association's own site is cited as the direct source of a figure it states itself. Seshasayee's and Century Pulp's bagasse-use figures (Section 4) are from Papermart's company profile and Century Pulp's own marketing materials as reported in trade coverage respectively; Rama Newsprint was checked and excluded as primarily recycled-fibre-based.

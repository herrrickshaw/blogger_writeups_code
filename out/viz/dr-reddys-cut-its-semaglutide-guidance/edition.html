A scale-up batch of Dr. Reddy's own synthetic semaglutide API tested out of specification in July 2026 — not a regulator's finding, the company's own quality check — forcing a self-disclosed supply halt that cut FY27 pen guidance in half, cost ₹240 crore in write-downs, and pushed India's biggest GLP-1 generic launch back by months. It is a live case study in exactly the kind of process risk that makes domestic API manufacturing hard, distinct from the price competition this blog has covered before.

Dr Reddy's semaglutide: FY27 pen guidance halved after a self-detected impurity

Top line

  • 45% cut to FY27 semaglutide pen guidance, from 12 million to 6–7 million.
  • ₹240 crore one-time provision; Q1 FY27 net profit fell 69% year on year.
Updated 7 November 2026. Dr. Reddy’s Q2 FY27 results (23 October 2026) showed revenue of ₹8,671 crore, down 1.5% year on year, and net profit down about 36% (reported at ₹913–925 crore). The company said it remained on track to resolve the out-of-specification semaglutide API batches, with relaunch targeted for November 2026 subject to validation, and kept the FY27 target of 6–7 million pens. Whether commercial supply has actually resumed had not been reported at the time of this update.

What would fix this

  • The Central Drugs Standard Control Organisation should issue published guidance on impurity profiling and process validation when synthetic peptide APIs move from pilot to commercial scale, so that the scale-specific risk this episode exposed is addressed by a written regulatory expectation rather than left to each manufacturer's own quality system.
  • The Department of Pharmaceuticals should fund a shared scale-up and validation facility for complex peptide APIs at a NIPER campus, open to domestic manufacturers, so that process-engineering capacity — which this post argues neither a plant subsidy nor a PLI scheme supplies — is built as public infrastructure.
  • The Central Drugs Standard Control Organisation should publish a list of approved semaglutide formulations showing each one's API source and whether that API is made synthetically or imported, so that the concentration of supply behind more than five launched generics can be judged from official data.
  • SEBI should add a standard disclosure format for voluntary halts of commercial drug or API supply by listed companies — affected batches, scale, patient-safety status and expected resumption date — so that disclosures like this one are comparable across companies rather than depending on each firm's choice of an investor call.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

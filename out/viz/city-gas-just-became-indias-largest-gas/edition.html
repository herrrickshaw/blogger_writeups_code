For as long as PPAC’s sectoral series has run, fertiliser plants were India’s biggest burners of natural gas. In the first quarter of FY2026-27 that ended: city gas distribution — the CNG in vehicles and PNG piped into kitchens — consumed 4,964 million standard cubic metres against fertiliser’s 4,703, leading in May and June individually. The crossover happened inside a shrinking pool, on a gas supply that is now majority imported.

Top line

  • 4,964 MMSCM of city gas in Q1 FY2026-27 beat fertiliser's 4,703, the first sustained period with city gas on top.
  • 23.9% of India's gas now goes to city gas, up from 11.4% in FY2015-16.
  • 1,446 MMSCM of June 2026 fertiliser gas was imported RLNG, against 850 for city gas.

What it means: City gas's share rose from 11.4% to 23.9% over the decade, while power's share fell from 22.8% to 11.5%.

What to do: Publish the rule that allocates domestic gas between kitchens, vehicles and fertiliser plants, since city gas is now the largest lever on urban fuel bills.

Fertiliser burned more imported gas than city gas in June 2026, 1,446 against 850 MMSCM

What it means: Fertiliser's LNG exposure means every dollar on the LNG price flows through urea production cost into the fertiliser subsidy.

What to do: Budget the fertiliser subsidy against the LNG price, not only against the domestic gas allocation.

What would fix this

  • PPAC should publish a reconciliation note bridging the sectoral workbook total (69,048 MMSCM) and the Snapshot consumption series (68,753 MMSCM) for FY2025-26, so that users of two provisional series from the same publisher know which to cite and why they differ.
  • PPAC should publish a back-cast of sectoral consumption on the post-FY2020-21 classification for the years before the break, so that the growth of “Other/Miscellaneous” from 9.0% to 14.1% of the pool can be compared across the whole decade without a seam.
  • The Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas should publish monthly domestic-gas allocations by sector and priority category alongside PPAC’s consumption data, so that the administrative lever this piece calls the biggest influence on urban fuel bills can be tracked as CGD demand grows.
  • PPAC should add sector-wise average landed gas prices, split by domestic gas and RLNG, to its sectoral consumption workbooks, so that price exposure for fertiliser plants and CGD entities can be read directly rather than inferred from RLNG volume shares.
  • The Central Electricity Authority should publish gas-based installed capacity and plant-level utilisation in the same annual table as PPAC’s power-sector gas burn, so that the stranded-asset question for a fleet whose burn fell 27% in a decade can be measured, not characterised.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

What this piece could not establish
What this article does not establish. Prices: nothing here says what CGD entities or fertiliser plants paid per MMBtu; RLNG share is a volume proxy for price exposure, not a landed-cost calculation. Gas-power installed capacity and plant-level utilisation are not quantified here; the fleet’s size relative to its burn is characterised, not measured. FY2025-26 and all FY2026-27 figures are provisional and the sectoral workbook’s FY2025-26 total (69,048 MMSCM) differs slightly from the Snapshot’s consumption series (68,753) — different compilation vintages of the same provisional data; both are shown with their sources rather than reconciled by force. PPAC’s sector reclassification at FY2020-21 limits “Other/Misc” comparability across that break.

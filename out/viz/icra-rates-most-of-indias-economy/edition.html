Line up ICRA's most recent outlook notes across fourteen Indian sectors and a pattern falls out that no single sector report would show on its own: eleven sectors are Stable or improving, largely on the back of one thing — government infrastructure capex. The three that got downgraded to Negative in 2026 — aviation, fertilisers, and integrated oil refining and marketing — were all cut for the same reason, within weeks of each other, and it wasn't anything sector-specific.

ICRA outlook for India: most sectors stable, 3 downgraded by one shared cause

Top line

  • ₹36,000-38,000 crore projected aviation net loss for FY2027, widening from the FY2026 estimate.
  • Three of the fourteen sectors ICRA reviewed were downgraded to Negative in 2026, for one shared cause.
Updated 7 November 2026. ICRA’s aviation half-yearly update of 27 August 2026 keeps the sector’s outlook Negative and its FY2027 loss projection at ₹36,000-38,000 crore, but lowers the FY2026 loss estimate to ₹28,000-30,000 crore from the ₹32,000-34,000 crore quoted below, and cuts FY2027 domestic passenger growth to 3-6%. The aviation chart still shows the earlier FY2026 figure. No change to the fertiliser or oil refining and marketing outlooks after May 2026 was found.

Updated 15 November 2026: rewritten as charts; figures unchanged.

Aviation's projected net loss widens to ₹36,000-38,000 crore in FY2027

What it means: ICRA says airlines are unlikely to pass higher fuel, lease and operating costs fully through fares, so the pressure is expected to persist.

What to do: Track whether airlines recover costs through fares and whether the FY2027 domestic passenger growth guidance of 3-6% holds.

Banks: a deliberate credit slowdown, with the banking outlook kept Stable

What it means: ICRA frames the slowdown as deliberate and keeps banking Stable, citing comfortable capitalisation and manageable asset quality.

What to do: Watch net interest margins, which ICRA expects to face pressure from elevated funding costs before an easing later in FY2027.

Renewables: 40-45 GW of the pipeline still lacks a signed power purchase agreement

What it means: Demand for renewable capacity is large, and ICRA names land, transmission, PPA-signing delays and discom health as the execution risks.

What to do: Check that a project's power purchase agreement is signed before financial close, since that contract drives its bankability.

What would fix this

  • The Ministry of New and Renewable Energy should publish a quarterly list of renewable capacity awarded but without a signed power purchase agreement, by implementing agency and project, so that the 40–45 GW gap that rating agencies flag is tracked from official data, not inferred from research notes.
  • The Department of Fertilizers should publish the formula and review dates by which subsidy rates are revised when sulphur, ammonia and phosphoric acid prices move, so that the subsidy-adequacy gap ICRA names as the driver of the sector’s Negative outlook can be checked against a stated rule.
  • The Directorate General of Civil Aviation should publish a quarterly aggregate of scheduled airlines’ fuel costs, lease costs and foreign-exchange losses, so that industry-wide loss estimates of ₹32,000–34,000 crore rest on an official series rather than on rating-agency and press estimates alone.
  • SEBI should require credit rating agencies to publish a machine-readable history of every sector outlook change, with date, previous and new outlook and the stated reason, so that patterns like three unrelated sectors downgraded for one shared cause can be seen without assembling individual notes.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

Sources and caveats

This piece's single largest sourcing limitation is stated plainly rather than smoothed over: icra.in and www.icra.in were both blocked by the network egress proxy in this research environment on every attempt, confirmed across multiple distinct URLs (the research report index, the root domain, and the non-www variant). Every ICRA-attributed figure in this piece therefore rests on how ICRA's own report was summarised in a search-engine result — visible title, date and snippet text from the indexed icra.in page or PDF — or on business-press coverage (Autocar Professional, Business Standard, Indian Cement Review, Tribune India, Business Standard again, Hotelier India, Fibre2Fashion, Informist Media, Outlook Business, Indian Aviation News) that explicitly names ICRA as its source, rather than on a primary ICRA document read in full. This is a materially lower-confidence sourcing basis than this blog's usual practice, and it applies uniformly across every sector in this piece rather than being flagged selectively. Section 1's cross-sector pattern (three Negative downgrades sharing one cause) is this piece's own synthesis of individually-sourced sector notes, explicitly identified as such rather than a single ICRA report making that comparison itself. Two sectors that could plausibly carry a real, dated ICRA outlook — shipping/ports and chemicals/pharmaceuticals — were not covered in this piece's research pass for reasons of scope, not because no ICRA material exists for them; a reader interested in either should treat their absence here as incomplete coverage, not as evidence ICRA has nothing to say about them. Nothing in this piece is investment, credit, or financial advice; a reader relying on any specific figure for a real decision should verify it directly against ICRA's own current, primary published research, not this summary.

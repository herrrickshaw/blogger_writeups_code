In December 2021, India offered up to ₹12,000 crore to whichever of at most two companies built a large-panel display factory. Two real bidders showed up, proposing a combined $6.7 billion — and neither was ever approved. Four years, one scheme reopening and one Foxconn withdrawal later, the government's own tally of chip-and-display-fab wins now includes a "display fab" — but it is a ₹3,200 crore gallium-nitride micro-LED project approved under a completely different, much smaller category than the one Vedanta and Elest actually applied for.

India's display fab scheme: 2 billion-dollar bids, neither approved, then redefined

Skip to article content
Text Size

Top line

  • 0 large-panel display fabs have been Cabinet-approved under the 2021 scheme's terms.
  • 35% is now the top fiscal support for a display fab, down from the uniform 50% of 2021.
  • ₹3,200 crore is the one display-fab approval, a micro-LED project under the smaller tier.
Updated 28 October 2026. The Semicon 2.0 support rates and display-fab thresholds in this piece, earlier described as secondary reporting, have been checked against the scheme’s gazette notification of 31 August 2026 (CG-DL-E-31082026-275860). They match: 40% for silicon fabs, 35% for compound semiconductor and display fabs and for advanced packaging, 25% for legacy packaging, and for Micro LED display fabs a ₹1,500 crore investment and ₹600 crore revenue floor.

Updated 15 November 2026: rewritten as charts; figures unchanged.

Semicon 2.0 cut display-fab support to 35% of project cost, from 50%

What it means: Support is now tiered by fab type, so a display fab gets 35% of project cost where the 2021 scheme gave 50%.

What to do: Read any new display-fab bid against the 35% tier and the ₹10,000 crore investment floor, not the 2021 terms.

The display-fab revenue floor fell from ₹7,500 crore to ₹600 crore for Micro LED

What it means: A Micro LED fab now needs ₹600 crore of revenue, while the 2021 large-panel category required ₹7,500 crore.

What to do: Check Crystal Matrix's delivery against its balance sheet before counting it as evidence of display-fab progress.

The 2021 scheme drew two bids by its deadline, and neither was approved

  1. 21 December 2021MeitY notifies the Display Fab Scheme
  2. February 2022Vedanta and Elest bids reach the deadline
  3. 1 June 2023Scheme reopens under the Modified Semicon India Programme
  4. 10-11 July 2023Foxconn withdraws from its roughly $19.5 billion joint venture with Vedanta
  5. Early May 2026Crystal Matrix approved, roughly ₹3,200 crore, for micro-LED wafers and panels
  6. 15 July 2026Cabinet approves Semicon 2.0, ₹1,27,500 crore
  7. 31 August 2026Semicon 2.0 guidelines notified by MeitY

What it means: Two bids reached the February 2022 deadline and neither was approved; the public record on Elest goes quiet after mid-2023.

What to do: Ask the India Semiconductor Mission for Elest's application status, since the public record shows none.

What would fix this

  • MeitY and PIB should report the India Semiconductor Mission's project tally with explicit sub-category labels, so a ₹3,200 crore Micro-LED project and a ₹10,000 crore-plus large-panel fab don't both simply read as "1 display fab" in a headline count.
  • The government should publish a current status for Elest's application — pending, withdrawn, or lapsed — rather than leaving a scheme applicant's fate untraceable in the public record three years on.
  • MeitY should explain, in its own words, why display-fab support fell from 50 per cent to 35 per cent while the revenue threshold also fell — whether that reflects a genuine reassessment of what's achievable at India's current stage, or simply an attempt to broaden eligibility after four years without a large-panel winner.
  • Any future evaluation of the Dixon-HKC joint venture, if it becomes a formal ISM applicant, should be conducted against clear, published criteria given the scheme's history of applicants stalling indefinitely in negotiation rather than being approved or publicly declined.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

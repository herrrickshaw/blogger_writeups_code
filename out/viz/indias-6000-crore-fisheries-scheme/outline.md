## Three schemes, one department, very different money

India's fisheries programming runs through three overlapping instruments, all administered by the Department of Fisheries under the Ministry of Fisheries, Animal Husbandry and Dairying. Pradhan Mantri Matsya Sampada Yojana (PMMSY) is the parent scheme, running 2020–21 to 2026–27 at a total outlay of ₹20,750 crore, funding fishing harbours, cold chains, hatcheries and landing centres. The Fisheries and Aquaculture Infrastructure Development Fund (FIDF), running since 2018–19, is a ₹7,522.48 crore concessional-finance window for the same kind of infrastructure. And Pradhan Mantri Matsya Kisan Samridhi Sah-Yojana (PM-MKSSY), a sub-scheme launched in 2023–24 at an estimated outlay of ₹6,000 crore, is different in kind: instead of funding harbours and cold storages, it is meant to put money directly into individual fishers' and fish farmers' hands — an aquaculture-insurance subsidy, credit-linkage incentives, and performance grants for small fisheries enterprises — and it runs on a digital backbone, the National Fisheries Digital Platform (NFDP), launched 11 September 2024.

India's coastal fishing fleet is the constituency PM-MKSSY's direct-to-fisher payments are meant to reach. [Fishing boats at Chellanam fishing harbour](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Fishing_boats_at_Chellanam_fishing_harbour.jpg), Vis M, CC BY-SA 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons.

Parliament has been asked about all three this month. The infrastructure-money answers and the direct-benefit-money answers tell two very different stories.

PM-MKSSY is where the story changes. Two separate Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha answers, both posted 11–12 August 2026, give the scheme's actual beneficiary numbers against its digital-registration count.

A separate Ministry answer on digital transformation, posted a day after the "Status of PM-MKSSY" answer, put the NFDP registration count at 35.85 lakh rather than 35.92 lakh — a small discrepancy between two Parliament answers from the same Department within 24 hours, not reconciled in either text. The live counter's higher 36.88 lakh figure, 24 days later, is consistent with ordinary registration growth (it shows 3,183 new sign-ups in the 24 hours before this piece checked it) rather than a further discrepancy.

Registering a digital identity on NFDP is a first step, not an entitlement — a fisher isn't owed an insurance payout or a performance grant simply for signing up. But three years into a four-year, ₹6,000 crore scheme, the total confirmed direct-incentive money that has reached individual beneficiaries — ₹40.03 lakh in insurance incentives plus ₹4.33 crore in performance grants — adds up to about ₹4.73 crore. Against a ₹6,000 crore outlay, that is roughly 0.08%.

The ₹28.04 crore in sanctioned bank loans sits in a different category — that's credit facilitated through the scheme's bank tie-ups, not central government money spent, and it's the one line in this table where PM-MKSSY looks like it's actually moving activity: 535 loans is a real number of transactions, even if small next to 36.88 lakh registrations. The cooperative-strengthening line (3,829 cooperatives, ₹72.75 crore) is described in the source answer as "approved," not confirmed as released, so it isn't counted in the ₹4.73 crore direct-incentive total above.

## A state-wise table that doesn't add up to the national figure — and NFDP's own live data confirm why

The Rajya Sabha answer on PM-MKSSY's status also carries a state-wise annexure, titled "Support Provided to Small and Micro Fisheries Enterprises," with a national total of 20,09,341. That figure is roughly 56% of the 35.92 lakh national registration count quoted in the same answer's main text — and the per-state numbers in the annexure (2,04,967 in Andhra Pradesh; 2,15,000 in Maharashtra; 2,35,665 in Telangana) are far too large to plausibly be counts of "enterprises" in the ordinary sense of the word.

NFDP's own live public dashboard settles which side of that guess is right. It breaks registrations into two categories: 36,65,661 Individual Registrations and just 21,998 Organisation Registrations nationally — the latter split further into 21,998 tagged Enterprises, 11,392 Cooperatives and 2,604 FFPOs (Fish Farmer Producer Organisations; these sub-categories overlap rather than sum, per the portal's own presentation). Genuine "enterprises," in other words, number in the low tens of thousands nationwide — nowhere close to 20,09,341, and not close in a single state either. The Rajya Sabha annexure's title is confirmed mislabelled: whatever the table's 20,09,341 figure is actually counting, it is not "Small and Micro Fisheries Enterprises" in the sense NFDP itself uses that term. The most likely explanation, unconfirmed, is that the annexure reused a partial individual-registration count under the wrong header.

## The EEZ Access Pass: a new entitlement, and a sharp coastline mismatch

A separate, newer instrument sits alongside PMMSY and PM-MKSSY: the Access Pass for fishing in India's Exclusive Economic Zone (EEZ), a free digital pass issued via the ReALCRaft Portal under the Sustainable Harnessing of Fisheries in the EEZ Rules, 2025 (notified 4 November 2025). It's required for mechanised vessels and large motorised boats over 24 metres fishing beyond 12 nautical miles; traditional non-motorised and small artisanal craft are exempt. As of 5 August 2026, a Rajya Sabha answer put the national total at 9,650 passes issued.

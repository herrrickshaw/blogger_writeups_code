The case for moving Indian fleets off diesel is usually made on inflation: diesel drives prices, so switching fuel eases them. Fifteen years of official data do not show that link. The real case is different and stronger — a ₹40-per-litre gap between how the same fuel is sold to two sets of buyers, a stationary segment that is trivial to convert, and a fuel that is far harder to siphon out of a tanker.

Top line

  • +0.11 correlation between diesel wholesale growth and CPI headline growth, FY2012–FY2026: no measurable pass-through at annual frequency.
  • ₹40 per litre gap between direct-sales and retail diesel is pulling bulk buyers to the pump.
Updated 7 November 2026. The direct-sales/retail gap this piece is built on was still about ₹40 a litre in late September 2026. Bulk and industrial buyers moved to retail pumps, and Jio-bp and Nayara capped diesel sales per customer at their outlets (Jio-bp at 50 litres a day), according to a PTI report of 28 September (ThePrint; PSU Watch). The stat tiles below remain May 2026 figures; the correlation findings are unchanged.

Every freight indicator grew faster than diesel demand's 1.6% in May 2026

What it means: Freight proxies ran well ahead of diesel consumption, a gap the post leaves as an open question rather than explaining away.

What to do: Establish what else is carrying freight before attributing the gap to rail or efficiency.

CNG costs 26% less per km than diesel in a sedan and 25% less in an SUV

What it means: On the modelled four-wheeler basis CNG is cheaper per kilometre than diesel, but the figures are for passenger cars only.

What to do: Build a commercial-fleet cost model with bulk CNG pricing and real duty-cycle data before extending the switching case to trucks and buses.

What would fix this

  • PPAC should commission and publish a fresh economy-wide diesel end-use survey, so that the sectoral shares used for policy — commercial vehicles, buses, agriculture, gensets, towers — no longer rest on fieldwork from January 2012 to June 2013 that predates BS-VI and diesel deregulation.
  • PPAC should publish the monthly retail and direct-sales diesel price series side by side in its Industry Consumption Report, so that the more-than-₹40-per-litre gap driving channel shift and private-marketer losses can be tracked as a number rather than a single statement.
  • The CAG should audit diesel consumption per kilometre at state transport undertakings and publish depot-level variances, so that the scale of siphoning shown in cases like the 60,000 litres seized in Bengaluru can be measured instead of inferred from press seizures.
  • PESO should require licensed doorstep-diesel operators to report whether each sale is priced off the retail or the direct-sales rate, so that it is known whether this channel formalises the ₹40 arbitrage or merely adds delivery on top of the pump price.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

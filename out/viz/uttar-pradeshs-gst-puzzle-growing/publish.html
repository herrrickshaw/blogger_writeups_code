Uttar Pradesh's own Economic Survey projects 10–12% GSDP growth and a ₹50 lakh crore investment pipeline. Its state budget assumed a 41% jump in own-tax revenue for FY2025-26. And yet the state's actual domestic GST collection fell 11.0% that same year, in a year national collection fell — among the sharpest state-level declines this piece has data for, though not independently verified against every major state's own filing, and this piece has not separately confirmed how many of the prior five years also saw a national decline. Those two facts look contradictory until you look inside the year rather than just at its total: UP's GST was roughly flat as late as December 2025, the decline was concentrated in the first quarter of calendar 2026, and by April 2026 — the first month of the new fiscal year — collection was back to positive growth alongside a striking 19.6% jump in registered taxpayers. This piece walks through how GST actually gets divided between Centre and states, why that mechanism matters for reading any state's numbers correctly, and what UP's month-by-month data says about whether its GST dip was a real slowdown or a transition effect from September 2025's GST 2.0 rate cuts working through the system.

What it means: Once IGST settlement is added to SGST, UP's effective total of ₹87,107 crore is ahead of Gujarat's ₹83,096 crore.

What to do: Read state GST figures on effective take, meaning SGST plus settled IGST, rather than raw collection.

UP's settled IGST is larger than the SGST it collects directly

What it means: UP's settled IGST of ₹51,422 crore is more than the ₹35,685 crore of SGST it collects directly, the sign of a state that consumes more than it taxes at the point of sale.

What to do: Keep IGST settlement and Finance Commission devolution separate when judging state revenue.

What it means: UP's GST was flat to growing in December 2025, so the 11.0% full-year fall was driven mostly by the final quarter, January to March 2026.

What to do: Watch the construction-sector slowdown that Amar Ujala reported in several UP GST zones, since it is not visible in the state aggregate.

What would fix this

  • GSTN should publish a monthly state-wise table that sets SGST collected, IGST settled and the resulting effective GST revenue side by side, so a consuming state like Uttar Pradesh, which received ₹51,422 crore in settlement against ₹35,685 crore of SGST, is not judged on collection alone.
  • The GST Council secretariat should publish a state-by-state assessment of the revenue effect of the September 2025 rate changes, separating rate effects from refunds and input-credit transitions, so a quarter like Uttar Pradesh’s January–March 2026 drop can be attributed rather than inferred from timing.
  • Uttar Pradesh’s Commercial Tax Department should publish zone-wise monthly GST collections with year-on-year comparisons, so the localised declines reported in Prayagraj and other zones can be checked against official figures rather than press reports.
  • Uttar Pradesh’s Finance Department should publish provisional actual own-tax revenue for FY2025-26 against the budgeted 41 per cent growth, so the gap between that target and the 11 per cent fall in GST collection is stated officially.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

The Jan Aushadhi scheme now sells 2,439 products — 2,125 medicines and 314 surgical items — at Kendras across India, priced 50–80% below branded equivalents. Cross-referencing its most common molecules against independently verified China-API-dependency figures shows real exposure on several of them — but no public dataset answers the sharper question of which specific Jan Aushadhi batch actually contains a Chinese-sourced ingredient, because that data simply isn't published anywhere.

Ibuprofen's China dependence fell sharply with no PLI scheme behind it

What it means: Ibuprofen is the one checked molecule where dependence fell by a large margin without a Bulk Drugs PLI project.

What to do: Press BPPI for API country-of-origin disclosure, the gap the post says no current public dataset closes.

What would fix this

  • The Pharmaceuticals & Medical Devices Bureau of India should add a status field to the PMBI product list stating whether each item is priced, actively procured or approved only, so that the 344 products carrying a ₹0 or blank MRP can be read without guessing.
  • PMBI should publish a dated change log of products added to or removed from the Jan Aushadhi basket, with reasons, so that a withdrawal such as the rifampicin and isoniazid tablet, drug code 379, is explained rather than discovered by comparing old and new lists.
  • The Department of Pharmaceuticals should publish annual import dependence by country for every API on the National List of Essential Medicines, so that cefixime, ceftriaxone, metronidazole, azithromycin and ampicillin — five of the fifteen molecules checked here — no longer have no public figure.
  • CDSCO should record the API manufacturer and its country for each approved formulation in its manufacturer registry, so that its WHO-GMP data, which now shows only where a formulation is made, can also show where its active ingredient was synthesised.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

A companion piece to this blog's earlier survey of AI-in-the-field claims for India's sugar and ethanol sector looked at a much larger, previously untapped research notebook — 83 sources deep, the biggest single collection behind any article on this site. What it actually contains is not more AI hype: it's the unglamorous enterprise software layer — ERP, maintenance management, lab systems, compliance platforms — that a biofuel plant runs on day to day, plus two genuinely new India-specific finds: a July 2025 marine biofuel-bunkering circular, and a seventeen-year-old magazine story that this "2026 digital transformation" collection had filed as current.

Top line

  • $145,000 per day is the production loss a single critical pump failure risked at one named Indian plant, per a vendor's own case study.
  • 83 sources in the notebook behind this piece, the largest collection behind any article on this blog.
  • 22 Jul 2025 is the date of India's first dedicated regulatory circular on marine biofuel bunkering.
Updated 7 November 2026. On the marine-biofuel circular discussed below: trade press reports it as the final guidelines, issued on 22 July 2025 after a draft that was open for comment until 18 July, with suppliers needing DGS approval and a six-month onboarding period. In August 2026 the Directorate General of Maritime Administration (the renamed Directorate General of Shipping) went further and proposed that coastal vessels use marine fuel with at least 10% biofuel by volume from 1 January 2029, after a voluntary 2028; comments closed on 31 August. India's marine biofuel rulebook is new; the RIN software integration is from 2008

  1. 2008John Deere adds RIN management to its AGRIS software, per a 2008 trade article
  2. 22 Jul 2025DGS Circular 32/2025 sets India's first dedicated biofuel bunkering rules
  3. 28-30 Oct 202528th Energy Technology Meet, Hyderabad, convened by CHT with BPCL as co-host

What it means: The circular says biofuel bunkering in India is at a nascent stage, with no dedicated procedures before it.

What to do: Expect LCFS- and CORSIA-style documentation software to eventually be needed for marine bunkering too.

What would fix this

  • The Directorate General of Shipping should mark DGS (Engineering) Circular No. 32 of 2025 as final, amended or proposed in its public circular register, so that ports and suppliers know whether India's first biofuel-bunkering rulebook is actually in force.
  • The Directorate General of Shipping should publish annual volumes of biofuel bunkered at Indian ports, by port and by blend up to B30, so that whether marine biofuel bunkering moves beyond the nascent stage the circular describes can be tracked.
  • The Centre for High Technology should publish the papers and proceedings of each Energy Technology Meet openly online, so that the technical debate on refining, biomass and AI within India's main government-industry forum can be read, not only its brochure.
  • The Department of Food and Public Distribution should survey and publish which digital systems — ERP, LIMS, SCADA, condition monitoring — are in use at distilleries supplying ethanol to oil marketing companies, so that adoption is measured directly instead of inferred from vendor marketing pages.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

What this piece could not establish
What this article does not establish. Beyond the IFFCO/Nanoprecise case study, none of the ERP, CMMS, LIMS, SCADA, or compliance-software vendors named in Exhibit 1 are confirmed to be deployed at any specific named Indian ethanol, biodiesel, or biogas plant — these are vendor marketing pages describing products aimed at the sector, and inclusion here is not evidence of adoption. The China green-power-traceability blockchain paper is included for pattern comparison with the biofuel-specific proposals covered elsewhere on this blog and should not be read as India-relevant or biofuel-specific research. Whether the DGS biofuel-bunkering circular has since been finalised, amended, or superseded was not established by this research; the source consulted describes itself in places as a "proposed" circular even while carrying an issued file number and date, and its current regulatory status should be verified against DG Shipping's own current circular register before being relied upon. The 28th Energy Technology Meet's proceedings and papers themselves (as opposed to the event brochure) were not reviewed for this piece.

Petrol pump dealer margins: October 2024 brought the first raise in 7 years

Top line

  • Rs 4.77 lakh gross dealer commission a month at the average outlet, on about 137 kilolitres sold.
  • +Rs 0.52 a litre on petrol from 30 Oct 2024: the first dealer commission rise in seven years, and a litigation settlement.
  • Rs 6 to 12 a litre: analyst estimate of the oil companies' petrol margin in Apr-Jun 2026, against about +Rs 10 a year earlier.
Updated 7 November 2026. No dealer-commission revision after October 2024 was found. The oil companies’ quarterly results show the swing this piece describes: the three public-sector marketers reported a combined standalone loss of about ₹18,150 crore in April–June 2026 and a combined net profit of about ₹30,106 crore in July–September, helped by gross refining margins of about $32 a barrel (Channel IAM, Finology on HPCL). The reports seen give no per-litre petrol or diesel marketing margin for the quarter. Ethanol prices for the 2026-27 supply year run from ₹57.97 a litre (C-heavy molasses) to ₹68.86 (maize), and the tender buys no cane-juice ethanol; see this blog’s tender piece. The ₹49.41–71.86 range in the table below is for 2025-26.
Updated 17 October 2026. This piece now uses PPAC’s own dealer-commission table, posted on 3 December 2024, and corrects two things. First, the October 2024 revision was the first increase since 1 August 2017, not since October 2016: PPAC lists four increases after October 2016 and a small cut in February 2019. The headline said “one dealer raise in eight years”; it now says seven. Second, PPAC puts the 2024 increase in the fixed part at Rs 0.52 a litre on petrol and Rs 0.34 on diesel, against the Rs 0.65 and Rs 0.44 reported in the press. The press figures match the change in Delhi price-sheet averages. A new section adds the PPAC table, a 1 December 2024 formula re-set and the inflation-adjusted value of the commission. The conclusion on E20 is unchanged. Also corrected 18 October 2026: the UPI merchant fee, described in the first version as in effect from 15 October, was deferred by NPCI on 13 October until after Diwali (Outlook Business).

Updated 15 November 2026: rewritten as charts; figures unchanged.

Delhi dealer commission on petrol rose to Rs 4.39 a litre after the 2024 revision

What it means: The October 2024 revision lifted Delhi's average petrol commission by about Rs 0.62 a litre, with no change in pump prices.

What to do: PPAC should keep its dealer-commission table current past December 2024, so a later revision cannot be missed.

Fixed petrol commission, in real terms, is still below its August 2017 level

What it means: The October 2024 revision restored about 40% of the real loss in the fixed petrol commission since August 2017, on the blog's own calculation.

What to do: PPAC should publish the slab-based incentives paid by credit note since August 2017, which its table mentions but does not quantify.

Oil companies' petrol and diesel margin: analyst estimates turned negative in 2026

What it means: Analyst estimates put the April-June 2026 marketing loss at Rs 6 to 12 a litre on petrol, and no company states a per-litre figure.

What to do: Oil companies should report auto-fuel marketing margins per litre in their quarterly decks on a consistent basis.

What would fix this

  • PPAC should keep its dealer-commission table current past December 2024, add CNG, and publish the slab-based incentives paid by credit note since August 2017, which the table mentions but does not quantify.
  • The Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas should commission and publish a survey of retail-outlet costs and net income by outlet type, so dealer profit is a measured figure, not a blog estimate.
  • The oil companies should publish the number of outlets suspended for E20 contamination, the test results, and any compensation to dealers, so the 2,573 figure is official or withdrawn.
  • The oil companies should report auto-fuel marketing margins per litre in their quarterly decks on a consistent basis, so analysts do not have to estimate them.
  • The Ministry should publish the refinery-gate price of petrol and the handling charge on ethanol next to the ethanol price, so the cost comparison behind E20 can be checked.
  • The Department of Financial Services should state the final UPI fee for fuel and whether dealers are exempt, since it falls on a margin of about 4%.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

E20: Bar and Bench on the Supreme Court order, Autocar India on the February 2026 notification, Autocar India on the 2,573 outlets, OMCs on contamination claims, TaxGuru on the E22 to E30 exemption. OMC margins and ethanol: Moneylife on the March 2026 excise cut, Business Today on CNG prices. Outlets and private players: PPAC outlet counts as reported by Outlook Business and Storyboard18 in December 2025; Nayara and Jio-bp outlet counts from company and press reports of June to October 2026. Company results: the standalone and consolidated figures for Indian Oil, BPCL, HPCL and Reliance are as relayed by the press, brokerages and aggregators for results announced on 23 to 31 July 2026; the stock-exchange filings themselves could not be opened. Figures from analyst notes (Nomura, Crisil, Emkay, ICICI Securities, Nuvama, Jefferies, PL India) are as relayed by the press. The gross-commission arithmetic, the implied pre-revision figures and the mileage upper bound are this blog’s own, from the inputs stated in the text; they are not published estimates.

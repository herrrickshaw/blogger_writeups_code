Gross foreign direct investment is at a record, but most of it leaves again: foreign investors cashing out through IPOs and private-equity exits, and Indian companies investing abroad. Net FDI, which helps pay for the current-account deficit, has fallen from about $43 billion a year to single digits.

India's net FDI: $7.7 billion stayed of a record $94.5 billion last year

Skip to article content
Text Size

Top line

  • $94.5 bn of gross FDI into India in 2025-26, a record, but net FDI was only $7.65 bn.
  • 57% of that gross inflow left again as repatriation by foreign investors.
  • 2.6 times the 2019-20 level of money invested abroad by Indian firms, $33.3 bn in 2025-26.
Updated 7 November 2026. The current year is running well ahead of 2025-26. RBI data for April–August 2026-27 put net FDI at about $13.8 billion ($13.79 billion), up from $9.6 billion in the same months a year earlier, on gross inflows of $54.93 billion against $45.55 billion (Angel One; NewsBytes, both citing RBI). Five months of 2026-27 have already produced almost twice the net FDI of the whole of 2025-26. Reports disagree on July (about $7.3 billion in some, $4.1 billion in another), so the monthly split is not used here.

What it means: Repatriation, not a fall in new investment, is now the main reason gross inflows fail to become net inflows.

What to do: Report net FDI next to the gross record in every headline figure, since the gross number alone overstates new foreign capital.

What to do: Treat the deficit financing gap as a planning risk, since portfolio flows and borrowing can reverse quickly.

What it means: Outward investment by Indian companies now absorbs a large share of gross inflows and is a second reason net FDI is low.

What to do: Track outward FDI alongside inward FDI so that the balance-of-payments effect of both is visible.

What would fix this

  • The RBI should publish repatriation each quarter by route (IPO offer for sale, secondary sale, buyback, liquidation) and by broad investor type (PE/VC fund, foreign parent company, other), so that exits by funds can be told apart from parents reducing their stakes.
  • DPIIT’s FDI factsheet should show RBI’s repatriation and net FDI figures next to its gross equity numbers, with the difference explained.
  • Outward FDI data should report the ultimate destination of investment routed through Singapore, Mauritius and similar holding locations, as far as the reporting forms already allow.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

What this piece could not establish

The RBI’s own pages could not be opened directly; figures for 2023-24 to 2025-26 come from news reports of its bulletins and from figures tabled in Parliament, and they are revised in later releases, so the most recent years may change. The 2023-24 outward figure here is a residual, not a published number. No breakdown of repatriation by sector, route or investor type was found, so the drivers above are drawn from IPO and private-equity data that overlap with, but are not the same as, the RBI’s repatriation figures. The LG Electronics dollar value uses an approximate exchange rate of Rs 88.8 to the dollar.

In 2022 the government promised a new law to turn export zones into development hubs that could also sell in India. The Bill stalled over tax breaks and never reached Parliament. Four years on, parts of it are arriving by notification, 95 of 372 notified zones sit idle, and an “SEZ 2.0” report has yet to be published.

India's SEZ law: DESH Bill stalled, 95 of 372 notified zones idle

Skip to article content
Text Size

Top line

  • 26% of notified SEZs were not operating on 31 July 2026: 95 of 372 zones.
  • 4 years since the 2022 Budget promised a new law to replace the SEZ Act; no bill has reached Parliament.

Updated 15 November 2026: rewritten as charts; figures unchanged.

95 of 372 notified SEZs were not operating on 31 July 2026

What it means: Of 372 notified zones, 95 were not operating on 31 July 2026, according to the ministry's reply.

What to do: Publish the SEZ 2.0 report, which has not appeared, so the idle zones can be addressed under a stated policy.

Haryana has the most idle zones, 14, followed by Telangana with 12

What it means: Haryana led the idle zones with 14, ahead of Telangana with 12, Karnataka with 11, and Tamil Nadu and Uttar Pradesh with 9 each.

What to do: State governments with idle zones should report the approval delays the ministry cited, since slow statutory approvals were one of its reasons.

SEZ exports rose to Rs 16.4 lakh crore in 2025-26 from Rs 14.63 lakh crore

What it means: Full-year SEZ exports rose from Rs 14.63 lakh crore in 2024-25 to Rs 16.40 lakh crore in 2025-26.

What to do: Compare full-year export figures from the same source before drawing conclusions, since the April–December releases measure nine months.

What would fix this

  • The Department of Commerce should publish the SEZ 2.0 committee’s report and state whether the government intends a new Act, amendments to the 2005 Act or rule changes only.
  • Before deciding whether to extend the domestic-sales window beyond March 2027, the CBIC and the Department of Commerce should publish how many units used it, the value of goods cleared and the duty forgone.
  • The SEZ statistics on sezindia.gov.in, the fact sheets and replies to Parliament should use one export series, labelled goods, services or both, so that full-year and part-year figures can be compared.
  • For each of the 95 notified but idle zones, the Department of Commerce should publish the date of notification, the developer and the stated reason for delay, as it does in aggregate in replies to Parliament.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

What this piece could not establish

The government pages behind these figures, including the SEZ India portal, PIB and Drishti IAS’s explainer, could not be opened directly for this piece; figures come from the text of those pages as indexed by search engines and from news reports, as listed below. The DESH draft is described as reported in 2022, not from the draft text. Whether the SEZ 2.0 committee has submitted its report, and what it recommends, is not known.

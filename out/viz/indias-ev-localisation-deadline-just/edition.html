Three postponements into India's push to force domestic manufacturing of e-bus and e-truck traction motors, the government's 3 September 2026 notifications finally split the deadline in two — holding the line on most components while quietly giving ground on exactly the one input China controls.

Top line

  • ₹4,391cr of PM E-DRIVE's ₹10,900cr original outlay goes to e-bus procurement, the largest of its three budget lines.
  • 1 Apr 2027 is the new deadline for e-bus magnet and shaft fitment, while the base traction-motor build stays due 1 Sep 2026.
Updated 9 November 2026. No further amendment to either PMP has been reported since the 3 September notifications, so the 1 April 2027 magnet deadline stands. The domestic magnet capacity it implicitly relies on has not started. The ₹7,280 crore rare-earth magnet scheme drew 20 bids, whose technical bids were opened on 13 August, but no beneficiary had been named by 7 November (Autocar Professional; see the 7 November note on this blog’s magnet-scheme piece). China’s April 2025 licensing of seven heavy rare earths, including dysprosium, still applies. Its one-year pause on the wider October 2025 controls is due to lapse on 10 November 2026, and no extension had been announced (Mining Technology).
Updated 28 October 2026. Checked against the Gazette of India. PM E-DRIVE’s extension to 31 March 2028 was S.O. 3626(E) of 7 August 2025, not April 2026, and S.O. 4424(E) of 10 August 2026 raised the scheme’s outlay from ₹10,900 crore to ₹11,900 crore, so the ₹10,900 crore in the chart and table is the original figure. The amendment table is also corrected: the second amendment to both PMPs is dated 13 March 2026 (S.O. 1330(E) and 1331(E)), not 3 March; later notifications’ recitals read “3 March” in the OCR, but the notifications themselves carry 13 March. The e-bus PMP was first notified on 3 March 2025 as S.O. 1078(E).
Updated 12 October 2026. PM E-DRIVE’s original window ran from 1 October 2024 to 31 March 2026. Press reports say the Ministry of Heavy Industries has since extended it to 31 March 2028 (or until the ₹10,900 crore is exhausted) for electric buses, e-trucks and e-ambulances; the gazette shows the extension was S.O. 3626(E) of 7 August 2025. The e-bus and e-truck lines this article is about therefore run past the “Oct 2024–Mar 2026” window shown in the chart and table. Separately, the e-two-wheeler incentive was extended to 31 March 2028 by an order of 10 August 2026. Both are now checked against the gazette; see the 28 October note. Sources: Outlook Business, Energetica India. The original text below is unchanged.

What to do: Watch for a named beneficiary under the magnet scheme as the first sign that the April 2027 magnet deadline can be met.

What would fix this

  • The Ministry of Heavy Industries should publish a consolidated, current Phased Manufacturing Programme schedule for e-buses and e-trucks after every amendment, so manufacturers and readers do not have to reconstruct the rules from three bus and five truck notifications and OCR’d gazette scans.
  • The Ministry of Heavy Industries should publish PM E-DRIVE’s budget by component, including administrative costs, adding up to the ₹10,900 crore outlay, so the scheme’s allocation can be checked from one official table rather than from trade coverage that does not sum.
  • The Ministry of Heavy Industries should publish the industry representations it relies on when deferring a PMP deadline, such as SIAM’s reported July 2026 letter, with a note of its reasons, so the case for moving magnet fitment to 1 April 2027 is on the record.
  • The Ministry of Heavy Industries should publish, model by model, which e-buses have been certified by the testing agencies as meeting the 1 September 2026 traction-motor requirement, so it is checkable whether the deadline that held is being met in practice.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

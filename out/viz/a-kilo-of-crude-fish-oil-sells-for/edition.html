Updated 15 November 2026: rewritten as charts; figures unchanged.

Refined into capsules, a kilo of fish oil is worth many times its crude price

What it means: Crude fish oil sells for Rs 247-646 a kilogram, while the same oil in 1,000 mg capsules retails for Rs 7,550-13,320.

What to do: Landing-centre groups should sell crude oil under contract to licensed refiners, as the post proposes.

A tonne of raw oily fish earns most as refined capsules, not as fishmeal

What it means: On the post's illustrative arithmetic, a tonne of oily fish is worth Rs 9,240-23,100 as fishmeal and Rs 369,950-652,680 as capsules.

What to do: Landing-centre cooperatives and women's groups should own the low-cost first stage rather than sell juvenile fish to plants.

What it means: A study counted 35 fishmeal plants, 23 of them in Karnataka, and the post finds no official national figures exist.

What to do: Plants should take processing waste rather than whole fish, which the post calls the better raw material for meal and oil.

India sells fish body oil raw and buys back some refined oil

What it means: India exported Rs 354 crore of fish body oil in 2024-25 and imported Rs 204 crore of fish lipid, fish body and cod liver oil.

What to do: Support should go to shared testing and refining standards that small plants can meet, since a small plant cannot pass FSSAI tests alone.

What would fix this

  • The Department of Fisheries should publish annual statistics on fishmeal and fish-oil production, plant by plant, with the share of raw material that is processing waste rather than whole fish.
  • PMMSY should fund waste-collection, chilling and by-product units at landing centres as a named component, owned by fishermen’s cooperatives or women’s groups, and report them separately.
  • Coastal states should require fishmeal plants to record the species and size of the fish they buy, so that minimum-legal-size rules can be enforced at the plant gate, not only at sea.
  • ICAR-CIFT and FSSAI should publish a simple standard and testing route for small producers of fish amino acid and crude fish oil, so that cooperative products can be sold to refiners and farmers with known quality.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

What this piece could not establish

There are no official national figures for fishmeal and fish-oil production or for the domestic omega-3 capsule market, and capsule imports cannot be separated in the trade data. The fishmeal plant count and output come from a single older study covering a few states. Indian fishmeal and sardine landing prices were found only as sellers’ asking prices, so the value chain uses world fish-oil prices and is illustrative. Sources disagree on the 2025 oil sardine catch (1.68 or 2.53 lakh tonnes) and on the volume of fish waste (0.3 or 4–6 million tonnes). The shelf-life figures come from separate studies of different species and products, some decades old. No current figures were found for Matsyafed’s by-product output or for incomes of women’s dry-fish groups.

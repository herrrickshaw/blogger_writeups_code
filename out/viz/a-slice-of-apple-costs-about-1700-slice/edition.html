Indian investing apps already let you buy a $10 slice of a $780,000 Berkshire Hathaway share. That's a real, working piece of financial inclusion, built entirely on foreign-market plumbing. The much harder version — a fractional slice of an Indian stock like MRF — is still against the law, and the regulatory path to changing that only opened in mid-2025.

Fractional investing in India: available for US stocks, not yet for Indian stocks

Top line

  • 90.2% of listed stocks trade under ₹1,000 a share, but they hold 41.8% of market value.
  • 0.93 is the median beta of both the cheapest band and the ₹5,000–10,000 band, yet their one-year returns differed by 26 points.
Updated 7 November 2026. Demat accounts reached about 23.44 crore in July 2026 (CDSL 18.83 crore, NSDL 4.62 crore), up from the 23.15 crore of June used below. The bill referred to in the 22 October note is the Corporate Laws (Amendment) Bill, 2026; its Joint Parliamentary Committee reported on 3 August 2026, and the bill was not taken up in the Monsoon Session. No report found says the committee added fractional shares, so the Companies Act barrier described below still stands. Sources: Asianet Newsable, PRS JPC report summary.
Updated 22 October 2026. This piece says the Companies Act change needed for fractional shares has not been made. That is still true, but there is now a bill to watch. Press reports say a Companies Amendment Bill introduced in the Lok Sabha on 23 March 2026 left fractional shares out, reportedly over concerns for minority shareholders, since such shares would carry no voting rights. In August 2026 SEBI asked the Ministry of Corporate Affairs to add them, as reported by 5paisa and CA Alley. We found no report that the bill has passed or that fractional shares have been added to it. The original text below is unchanged.

What would fix this

  • The Ministry of Corporate Affairs should publish a draft amendment, with a consultation deadline, for the fractional-share change the Company Law Committee recommended in 2022, so that the status of the one legal step this post identifies as the real barrier can be checked rather than left open-ended.
  • SEBI should publish the outcome of the Xaults innovation-sandbox test of depository-level fractional custody admitted in July 2025, including whether it graduated and with which depository, so that investors can rely on SEBI’s own record rather than unverified crypto-press claims such as tokenised Reliance shares.
  • SEBI should maintain a public page listing every fractional-ownership product for Indian-listed securities it has authorised or is testing, so that any claim of a launched product can be checked against one official list, the verification this post could not complete for the tokenised-shares reports.
  • SEBI should consult on smaller trading lots or unit denominations for REIT units quoted at ₹10–11 lakh each, such as the three Propshare units in this post’s ₹10,000-plus table, since REIT units are not bound by the Companies Act whole-share rule that blocks fractional equity.
  • The RBI should publish Liberalised Remittance Scheme outflows for overseas equity purchases by remittance-size band, so that the scale of the small-ticket fractional US-stock investing this post describes, starting at $5–10, can be measured against the US$250,000 annual ceiling.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

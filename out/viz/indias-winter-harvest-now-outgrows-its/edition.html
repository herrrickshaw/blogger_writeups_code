India's rabi and summer crops: 53% of foodgrains, up from 36% in 1966-67

Skip to article content
Text Size

Top line

  • 53% of India's foodgrains came from rabi and summer crops in 2025-26, up from 36% in 1966-67.
  • 3,013 kg a hectare for rabi and summer foodgrains, against 2,323 kg for kharif, in 2025-26.
  • +32% growth in rabi and summer foodgrain area over twenty years, while kharif area stayed flat.

Updated 15 November 2026: rewritten as charts; figures unchanged.

Rabi and summer crops now supply more than half of India's foodgrains

What it means: Rabi and summer crops grew from 36% to 53% of India's foodgrains between 1966-67 and 2025-26.

What to do: Plan procurement, storage and warehouse credit around the rabi harvest months, when arrivals concentrate.

Rabi and summer land yields more foodgrain per hectare than kharif

What it means: Rabi and summer land also gives more grain per hectare, mainly because most rabi crops are irrigated.

What to do: Track kharif yields closely, since kharif output grew mainly through yield rather than land.

Rabi and summer foodgrain area grew 32% while kharif area stayed flat

What it means: Kharif land has not grown, while rabi and summer foodgrain area rose 32% over the same twenty years.

What to do: Watch groundwater and canal supply for the summer season, since these crops depend on them in the hottest months.

What would fix this

  • The Department of Agriculture should publish summer-season estimates back to at least 2010-11 by separating them from the existing rabi series, so that the growth of zaid crops can be tracked over more than four years.
  • Agmarknet should publish a coverage index, the number of reporting markets by state and month, alongside its arrivals data, so that a rise in arrivals can be separated from a rise in the number of markets reporting.
  • Procurement agencies (FCI, NAFED, NCCF and state agencies) should report their purchases by state and month in the same format as mandi arrivals, so that the share of a crop sold through any formal channel can be measured, not only the share sold in regulated mandis.
  • State agriculture departments should publish groundwater use for summer rice, maize and moong, the three crops driving zaid growth, before extending input subsidies or support prices to the season.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

Sources and caveats

Area, production and yield: Department of Agriculture and Farmers Welfare, Unified Portal for Agricultural Statistics (UPAG), all-India estimates by season and crop, 1966-67 to 2025-26 (2025-26: third advance estimates), and state estimates for 2025-26 (summer and rabi leaders). Mandi arrivals: Agmarknet (Directorate of Marketing and Inspection), state-wise monthly arrivals, January 2019 to October 2026. Both are as extracted in the open repository herrrickshaw/india-mandi-arrivals (files data/upag/apy_all_india.csv.gz, data/upag/apy_state.csv.gz and data/agmarknet/state_monthly.csv.gz), which documents the endpoints, the November 2025 coverage change and other caveats. Season definitions follow the ministry’s usage; summer (zaid) is reported separately from 2022-23 and is added to rabi here for comparisons over time. Cotton production is in bales and its mandi arrivals are seed cotton, so the two are not comparable; paddy arrivals are unmilled grain, compared here with milled rice production only in direction. Three-year averages, shares, volatility and arrival calendars are this blog’s arithmetic. Research notes: out/research/seasons/ in this blog’s archive.

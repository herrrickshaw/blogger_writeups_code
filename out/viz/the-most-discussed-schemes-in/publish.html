What it means: Parliament asks about UDAN / RCS more than about any other incentive scheme in the register.

What to do: Read the linked answer PDFs for UDAN before judging progress, since question counts do not show whether the scheme is working.

The Rajya Sabha asks more about UDAN than the Lok Sabha does

What it means: UDAN is the one scheme where the upper House asks more often than the lower House.

What to do: Compare the two House registers for UDAN answers before assuming the gap reflects a difference in the subject lines.

Ten announced schemes have drawn no question in either House

What it means: Zero questions mostly means not yet, because Parliament has not had a full session cycle since most of these launched.

What to do: Check PLI White Goods under generic PLI subject lines before reading its zero count as neglect.

What would fix this

  • The Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha Secretariats should index the full text of answers in their question search, not only the subject line, so a scheme like PLI White Goods can be checked for scrutiny that may sit under generic “PLI Scheme” subjects.
  • The Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha Secretariats should adopt a common list of scheme identifiers and tag every question with it, so the same scheme is counted once across both houses and keyword artefacts such as the duplicate PLI Specialty Steel and PLI-ACC rows do not arise.
  • PIB should tag every release with the scheme or schemes it concerns and publish a searchable scheme index, so comparisons of government publicity with parliamentary attention — PM-KUSUM against the Semiconductor Mission, for example — rest on official tags rather than keyword matching.
  • DPIIT should table an annual progress statement on PLI White Goods in both houses, since a scheme running since 2021 has not drawn a single subject-titled question in five years and its results otherwise reach Parliament only incidentally.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

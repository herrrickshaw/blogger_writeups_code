The 2027-28 wheat MSP is up 1.0%, against 5.6% to 10.3% for oilseeds and lentils. With 480 lakh tonnes of wheat in the central pool, more than twice the buffer norm, the government has spent the past year lifting stock limits, reopening exports and failing to sell surplus wheat at auction.

Top line

  • 1.0% rise in wheat's support price for 2027-28, the smallest of the six rabi crops.
  • 106% wheat's margin over its cost of production, still the widest of the six.
  • 2.3x wheat stock in the central pool against the 1 October buffer norm.

What it means: The central pool held 479.92 lakh tonnes of wheat on 1 September 2026, against a buffer norm of 205.2 lakh tonnes.

What to do: Hold any large support price rise until wheat stocks fall back towards the buffer norm, since a big rise would add to stocks the government is already trying to sell.

What would fix this

  • The Commission for Agricultural Costs and Prices should publish its rabi price-policy report before the Cabinet decides, and the Cabinet release should say where it departed from the recommendation, so a 1% rise reads as a stated stock-management choice rather than something inferred from the granary figures.
  • The Department of Food and Public Distribution should review the buffer norms set in January 2015, which still define “surplus” a decade later, against current offtake under the free-grain scheme, and publish the method.
  • The Directorate General of Foreign Trade and the food department should publish a stock-linked rule for wheat exports, such as opening exports when central pool stocks exceed a stated multiple of the buffer norm, so traders and farmers are not working from a four-year ban followed by a sudden reopening.
  • The Food Corporation of India should publish quantities offered and sold at every open-market sale auction, with the reserve price, so the gap between stocks and what the market will buy is on record.
  • The Department of Agriculture should publish procurement as a share of production by state for each crop, so it is clear how much of the crop the support price actually reaches outside the main procuring states.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

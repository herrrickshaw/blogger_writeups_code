Ten BRICS members and partners supplied $321.8 billion of India's imports in FY2025-26 against total merchandise imports of $774.98 billion. India sold them $95.8 billion. The gap — $226.1 billion — is more than two-thirds of India's entire merchandise trade deficit of $333.19 billion.

Rupee trade with China: China would hold $112.2 billion of rupees a year

Skip to article content
Text Size

Top line

  • $228.5 billion a year of India's BRICS imports would have to be held as rupee claims by sellers, because self-financing trade covers only $93.4 billion.
  • 49% of that residual sits with China, which sells India machinery and electronics, only 8.0% of it commodities.
  • 17.3% gold growth in Russia's reserves over eleven months, while its foreign-exchange component fell 3.0%.

Updated 15 November 2026: rewritten as charts; figures unchanged.

China alone would hold 49% of the $228.5 billion rupee residual

What it means: The residual is imports minus exports, and China's $131.6 billion of imports against $19.5 billion of exports makes it dominate the total.

What to do: Treat China separately, through import substitution and supply-chain diversification rather than currency policy.

Russia's reserves rose 4.5% in eleven months, all of it in gold

What it means: Gold now sits at 41.5% of Russian reserves, up from 37.0% in August 2025.

What to do: Publish quarterly rupee balances held in Special Rupee Vostro Accounts, so any build-up is an official figure.

The UAE trades with India at near parity with China; other partners do not

What it means: The UAE is the only one of these four partners that has signed a local-currency settlement arrangement with India, in July 2023.

What to do: Scale rupee settlement with the UAE by Indian exports to it, not by diplomacy alone.

China, the UAE, Saudi Arabia and Indonesia would earn a pickup on Indian bonds

What it means: A Chinese holder of a ten-year Indian bond earns 513 basis points more than a Chinese government bond pays, a pickup that is compensation for expected depreciation.

What to do: India should say plainly that the carry is a real price for deficit financing, not free money.

What would fix this

  • The RBI should publish quarterly aggregate balances held in Special Rupee Vostro Accounts, by the counterparty bank's country, so the build-up or run-down of rupee claims — such as Russia's peak near $8 billion and fall to about $3.5 billion — is an official figure rather than a press estimate.
  • The RBI should publish the share of India's imports and exports settled in rupees, by partner country, each year, so progress on local-currency settlement with BRICS members can be measured against the $93.4 billion self-financing volume and the $228.5 billion residual.
  • The RBI should publish foreign holdings of Fully Accessible Route government securities by the investor's country of domicile, so it can be seen whether low-yield creditors such as China, the UAE and Saudi Arabia are actually taking up rupee paper.
  • The RBI should publish the total cost it bears for absorbing hedging costs under the June 2026 FCNR(B) and overseas-borrowing swap facility, alongside the inflows mobilised, so the price of the $143.5 billion raised is on the record.
  • The Department of Commerce should publish annual gold and silver imports from the UAE under the CEPA tariff-rate quota, with quota utilisation, so the share of the $24.8 billion precious-metals line that rests on the duty differential can be assessed.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

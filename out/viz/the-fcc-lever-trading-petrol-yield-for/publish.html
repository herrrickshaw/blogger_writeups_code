India's ethanol suppliers offered refiners 17.76 billion litres for ESY2025-26 against a stated requirement of just 10.5 billion — a gap covered in an earlier piece on this site. That surplus opens a narrow but real opportunity: the petrol a refinery no longer needs to blend can, with real (not cosmetic) modifications to the FCC unit's gas-recovery train, come out as LPG instead — directly addressing India's import-heavy cooking-gas supply. This piece works through what that modification actually involves, how it differs mechanically from the naphtha-cracker petrochemical pivot covered separately on this site, and the parallel, independently-verified push to expand India's underground crude storage that gives refiners more room to absorb the supply-side volatility this shift is a response to.

Updated 7 November 2026. Three things have moved since this piece was written. The oil companies’ tender for November 2026 to October 2027 seeks about 1,100 crore litres of ethanol (up from about 1,050) and buys none from cane juice, sugar syrup or B-heavy molasses, so the surplus in Section 1 now sits almost entirely with grain distillers. India consumed 33.2 million tonnes of LPG in 2025-26 against 13.1 million tonnes of domestic production, so about 60% was imported (PPAC). On strategic storage, a parliamentary committee report (August 2026) says Bikaner’s feasibility study is complete and Bina’s pre-feasibility study is done, with a detailed feasibility report next; both still need Cabinet approval, and reported capacities for Bina vary from 1.5 to 5 million tonnes, so the table below is unchanged. The Union Budget chemical-parks scheme in Section 6 was approved by the Cabinet in July as BHAVYA Rasayan (₹3,030 crore).

Updated 15 November 2026: rewritten as charts; figures unchanged.

Total ethanol offers run about 1.7 times the blending requirement

What it means: Grain-based producers alone offered 13,040 million litres against a stated OMC requirement of about 10,500 million litres.

What to do: Track the ARAI engine-life and mileage study on E25 before counting this surplus as a blending outlet.

Four named fixes each cut 1 to 5 psi of pressure drop

What it means: Pressure-drop cuts of this size free wet gas compressor capacity without a full replacement, which the paper says to avoid.

What to do: Start with pressure surveys across piping segments, since field measurements are more accurate than calculations alone.

Bikaner's proposed reserve, at about 5 MMT, is the largest of three storage projects

What it means: The three projects sit at different stages, from a feasibility study to board approval and a pre-feasibility study.

What to do: Confirm the Bikaner feasibility study result before counting it toward the buffer for crude diversification.

The Andhra Pradesh greenfield, at about ₹91,000 crore, is the largest of seven projects

What it means: The seven figures are separately sourced and may overlap, so each is an individual data point rather than a reconciled total.

What to do: Reconcile the seven investment figures against one source before adding them into a single total.

What would fix this

  • PPAC should publish refinery-wise monthly LPG and propylene output, showing how much FCC-derived C3/C4 goes to LPG cylinders and how much to petrochemical feedstock, so it can be checked whether any FCC yield shift actually cuts LPG imports rather than feeding polypropylene plants.
  • The Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas should publish ARAI's study of E25 on engine life and mileage in the existing fleet, with its completion date, so the gate between notified E22–E30 standards and any blending rollout is visible to refiners planning yield changes.
  • ISPRL should publish a status table for its proposed Bikaner, Bina and Mangalore extension reserves, giving study stage, approvals, capacity and target completion dates, so progress towards roughly 15 MMT of strategic storage can be tracked against the existing 5.33 MMT.
  • PNGRB should publish the authorised capacity and actual throughput of each dedicated LPG pipeline in its registry, so it is clear whether the network from Kandla–Gorakhpur to Vizag–Secunderabad has room to carry any increase in domestic LPG output.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

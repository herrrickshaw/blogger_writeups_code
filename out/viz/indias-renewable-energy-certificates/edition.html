A Renewable Energy Certificate traded at ₹2,300 in early 2022, ₹115 by August 2024, and back near ₹380 in mid-2026 — a twenty-fold swing in a market meant to put a stable price on being green. That volatility is the headline here, and REC is only one of at least six live certificate-based compliance markets now running in India, with two more about to start.

Top line

  • ₹115 was the August 2024 REC low, against ₹2,300 in early 2022 (press-reported session prices).
  • ₹380 was the mid-2026 level, so the price kept moving inside the unbanded market that CERC created in May 2022.
  • 1 is the certificate multiplier for solar, the same as on-shore wind and the lowest tier, while biomass gets 2.5.

REC prices have eased since the mid-2026 figures in this piece: IEX sessions cleared at ₹370 and ₹350 in August 2026 and at ₹338 and ₹330 on 14 and 28 October, when 12.22 lakh certificates traded, up 97.5% from October 2025 (two rows added to the price table). REC prices fell from ₹2,300 in 2022 to ₹115 in 2024, then recovered

What it means: Press-reported session prices show REC falling sharply after the band was removed, then partly recovering.

What to do: Watch whether CERC or NLDC publishes a current unsold-inventory figure for RECs.

Solar sits in the lowest certificate tier, with a multiplier of 1 like wind

What it means: Solar gets the lowest multiplier, so one MWh of solar generation yields one certificate, the same as on-shore wind.

What to do: Check the multiplier table before assuming a hydro or biomass plant earns the same certificates as solar.

In 2015, far more RECs were available than were traded

What it means: The 2015 gap between available and traded certificates is an early sign of the oversupply that has continued since.

What to do: Confirm whether unsold inventory has been published for 2023 to 2026, since this post could not find it.

What would fix this

  • Grid-India, as the REC Central Agency, should publish a monthly statement of certificates issued, redeemed and traded, and the closing stock of unsold certificates, so oversupply can be measured directly instead of inferred from a ₹115 low and a 56.3% fall in volume, with the latest precise inventory figure dating from 2015.
  • CERC should publish a single downloadable time series of every REC trading session’s clearing price and volume on IEX and PXIL since the May 2022 regulations, so the twenty-fold price swing can be read from one official source rather than stitched together from individual press reports.
  • The Bureau of Energy Efficiency should publish the Detailed Procedure for Compliance Mechanism under CCTS, including any rules and timeline for moving the nine PAT sectors and their ESCerts into carbon credit certificates, since CERC’s trading regulation is silent on the migration.
  • CPCB should publish monthly transaction prices and volumes for certificates traded on its EPR portals, starting with used oil, plastic, battery, e-waste and tyre waste, so the newer certificate markets can be watched for REC-style volatility from their first trades.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

Sources and caveats

The REC mechanism description (NLDC/Grid-India as Central Agency, 1 MWh per certificate, RPO linkage, ~1,095-day validity, FIFO redemption) is sourced to recregistryindia.nic.in's own published FAQ and issuance-process pages, both strong primary sources. The 2022 CERC Regulations text (No. RA-14026(11)/1/2022-CERC, dated 9 May 2022) was independently retrieved and read directly for this article — the 9 May 2022 date, the removal of the floor/forbearance band (Regulation 13, pricing "as discovered in the Power Exchange(s)"), the single unified Certificate definition (Regulation 12), the Certificate Multiplier table, and the repeal clause confirming the prior existence of separate Solar and Non-Solar Certificates (Regulation 19(2)(b)) are all sourced directly to that primary document, not to a secondary summary. The price table is stitched together from individual IEX session reports and trade-press coverage (Mercom India, SolarQuarter, Business Standard) of specific trading dates rather than one consolidated official time series — no single CERC or IEX publication covering the full 2022-2026 window in one place was found; each price point carries the confidence of its individual press source, while the overall volatility trend is corroborated across multiple independent reports and is the reliable finding. The 2015 unsold-REC-inventory figure (1.22 crore certificates) traces to Eco-Business/PTI reporting citing REC Registry data from that period; no comparably precise current-year figure was found, and this article explicitly does not extrapolate the old figure forward. The APTEL ruling on RPO enforcement (20 April 2015, O.P. No. 1 of 2013, directions under Section 121 of the Electricity Act to all State Commissions) is corroborated across multiple independent legal-citation sources that reference it as precedent in later judgments, but the tribunal's own original order PDF could not be retrieved directly during this research (access errors on aptel.gov.in and a state-regulator mirror), so it is reported at moderate, not full, confidence; it is also explicitly distinguished here from the REC certificate-trading mechanism itself, since the 2015 ruling addresses RPO compliance enforcement, not certificate pricing or the multiplier system. The certificate-market inventory table (used oil, plastic/battery, e-waste and tyre EPR, PSLC, PAT/ESCert, CCTS) draws on CPCB's own e-governance portal listings, BEE and PIB publications on the PAT scheme, ICAP's tracking of India's Carbon Credit Trading Scheme, RBI and legal/policy summaries for PSLC, and MoEFCC's own Green Credit Programme FAQ for the explicit exclusion of that scheme from the tradable-certificate list; individual dates and volumes within that table (e.g. the PAT III ~1.8 lakh certificate figure, the October 2026 CCTS trading-start expectation) are sourced to trade press (Carbon Pulse, Business Standard) rather than primary regulator publications and should be treated as moderate confidence. CERC's own draft CCC trading regulation (No. RA-14026(13)/1/2024-CERC) was independently retrieved and read directly for this article, confirming CCTS's Ministry of Power parent notification (S.O. 2825(E), 28 June 2023), the Grid Controller of India/Bureau of Energy Efficiency roles, and the Compliance/Offset market structure — but that regulation does not itself address PAT-to-CCTS migration or ESCert conversion, so the claimed migration timeline and conversion mechanism remain sourced to trade press only and are flagged as such rather than presented as confirmed by the primary trading regulation. This article does not size any of these markets as an investment opportunity, recommend a trading position, or draw a policy conclusion; nothing here is investment, trading or compliance advice.

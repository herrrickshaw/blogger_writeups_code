A nine-judge Supreme Court bench ruled that states may tax mineral rights and mineral land. The MMDR Amendment Act 2026 now bars such levies except on the Centre’s terms and cancels uncollected arrears. Jharkhand loses a cess worth a fifth of its own revenue; Odisha loses the arrears it claimed.

Mining taxes: MMDR Amendment Act 2026 cancels up to Rs 2 lakh crore of state dues

Skip to article content
Text Size

Top line

  • Rs 1.5–2 lakh crore of state mineral-tax arrears since 2005, as estimated, is cancelled unless already collected.
  • 38% of Odisha's own revenue comes from mining, which the Act leaves untouched.
  • 22% of Jharkhand's own revenue is budgeted from its mineral-land cess, now barred.
Updated 9 November 2026. Three developments. First, the Centre already has a curative petition pending against the 2024 nine-judge ruling, filed in September 2025. In May 2026 the Court put off hearing the related appeals because of it. Second, on 4 November 2026 a seven-judge bench ruling on state sales-tax surcharges (Arjun Flour Mills v. State of Odisha, 2026 INSC 1192) noted both that curative petition and the new section 9D, and said neither affected its question. Its reasoning has been reported only in a secondary summary. Third, Jharkhand has called a special Assembly session on 25–26 November to pass a resolution against the Act. No case number, hearing or interim order on the states’ own challenges has been found. Sources: LiveLaw and Business Standard (November 2025), ETEnergyWorld (May 2026), CaseMine summary of the 4 November judgment, New Indian Express (27 October 2026).

What it means: The Act reverses the 2024 ruling by ordinary law, not constitutional amendment, and took effect within days of assent.

What to do: Watch the Centre's curative petition, pending since September 2025, which could still change the 2024 ruling.

What would fix this

  • The Centre should publish the rules under the new Section 13, setting out on what terms a state may levy, with a cap per tonne by mineral, so that states know what they can raise and industry knows its maximum cost.
  • The Ministry of Mines should publish, state by state, the arrears that the Act cancels and how much was collected before it commenced, with the share owed by central public-sector companies.
  • If the Centre’s aim is uniform mineral costs, a shared levy collected centrally and passed to producing states by output would meet it without removing the states’ revenue.
  • Laws that reverse a Constitution Bench ruling should go to a standing committee before passage.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

What this piece could not establish

The Act (No. 20 of 2026, CG-DL-E-17082026-275519) and its commencement notification (S.O. 4642(E) of 22 August 2026, CG-DL-E-22082026-275676) were read in the Gazette of India’s own text, through the SansadSaar index of the archive.org Gazette of India collection; the provisions and dates above match it. The arrears figures are claims and estimates, not audited amounts, and they overlap. Jharkhand’s cess collections (Rs 1,380 crore in 2024-25 and Rs 7,487.91 crore in 2025-26) come from a party leader, not the state’s accounts. For Karnataka only the mining share of own non-tax revenue was found (54% in 2021-22, 43% in 2022-23); mining receipts for Rajasthan and Madhya Pradesh were not found on a comparable basis and are left out. Karnataka, Kerala and Telangana announced a joint challenge on 20 August, and a LiveLaw analysis says it has been filed; Jharkhand decided on 17 September to challenge the Act. No case number, hearing date or interim order was found by 28 October 2026. This is not legal advice.

Table: What the Act does
ProvisionWhat it does
Mineral-bearing land brought under the ActSection 2 now covers “mineral bearing lands”, defined by parameters the Centre prescribes, so they fall under central regulation
New Section 9DNo state tax, cess or levy on mineral rights or mineral-bearing land, whether computed on quantity, value, royalty or any other basis, except under conditions the Centre prescribes
Rule-making (Section 13)The Centre sets the conditions and parameters for any future state levy; PRS notes the Act gives no guiding principles for them
Past leviesAny such levy not deposited with or recovered by the state before the Act commences is deemed invalid; amounts already collected are not refunded
ScopeMajor minerals such as coal, iron ore, bauxite, manganese and copper; minor minerals such as sand stay with states

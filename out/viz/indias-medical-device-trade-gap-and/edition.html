A $6.2 billion deficit, growing at 22% a year, with exports flat — against a policy landscape (PLI, SMDI, four device parks) that has demonstrably moved the needle in imaging and implants but never touched the two largest gaps: lab instruments and IVD reagents. This piece sets the scenario; a companion piece maps it against what PLI actually covers.

What to do: Climb the value ladder, starting with IVD reagents and X-ray/CT cores, as the post's bottom line proposes.

What it means: Lab and analytical instruments alone run a $1,984 million deficit at 10% coverage, the largest single gap in the basket.

What to do: Start localisation with lab and analytical instruments, where quality-control demand is policy-captive, as the post argues.

Dressings and surgical consumables are net exports; lab instruments cover 10%

What it means: Dressings export 264% of what they import and surgical consumables 157%, while lab and analytical instruments cover only 10%.

What to do: Point the parts-and-accessories programmes at the ~$1.7bn component layer, since assembly-only PLI leaves those imports in place.

India has about 3 MRI units per million people, under half the global average of 7

What it means: India has roughly 3 MRI units per million people against a global average of 7, with only about 2,500-3,000 scanners nationally.

What to do: Fund MRI localisation down to magnets and gradient coils, not stopping at scanner assembly, as the post recommends.

What would fix this

  • The Department of Pharmaceuticals should report a device-only trade series, built on its own HSN mapping guidebook, separately from pharma-side lines in parliamentary answers, so that the 31% coverage it tables and the 37% device-basket figure are not confused.
  • The Department of Pharmaceuticals should publish SMDI component sub-scheme approvals by HS line and component, so that it can be checked whether support reaches the roughly $1.7 billion ‘parts and accessories’ layer rather than moving imports one line down.
  • The Department of Pharmaceuticals should publish, for each PLI-approved imaging product, which core assemblies are made in India, so that MRI localisation can be judged on magnets and gradient coils, and X-ray on tubes and detectors, not on final assembly alone.
  • The Health Ministry should publish a district-wise count of installed MRI and CT units, public and private, every year, so that the roughly 3 MRI units per million and the 14% of blocks reporting local access are tracked from official data.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

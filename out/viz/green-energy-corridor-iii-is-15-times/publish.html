The Cabinet has approved ₹1.86 lakh crore for lines inside states and 50 GWh of batteries, to carry up to 135 GW of renewable power by 2032-33. The Centre pays 29%. The two earlier phases, a fraction of the size, have connected 26 of their 44 GW, and part of the new money pays their old bills.

What it means: The Cabinet approved the largest transmission scheme yet backed for renewable energy, with the first intra-state phase to pay for batteries.

What to do: Track progress against the roughly 7,300 circuit kilometres a year the scheme needs from 2026-27 to 2032-33.

Only 26 GW of the 44 GW meant for the two earlier phases is connected

What it means: Two earlier phases were meant to connect 44 GW, and by March 2026 only 26 GW had been connected.

What to do: Measure each new phase by connected gigawatts, not by money approved.

What it means: Support per MWh has fallen with each round, as battery prices have, which leaves less subsidy for each megawatt-hour of storage.

What to do: Set out how each battery's discharge duration and cost split between transmission charges and generators will be decided.

What would fix this

  • The Ministry of New and Renewable Energy should publish GEC progress by state and by phase every quarter: circuit kilometres, MVA and capacity connected against target, and the central money released and still owed, covering Phases I, II and III together.
  • The Ministry of New and Renewable Energy should release Phase III money in tranches tied to commissioning, and publish which states have received what, so that slow states are visible.
  • Grid Controller of India should publish monthly renewable curtailment by state, by cause and by inter-state or intra-state network, so it is clear which part of the grid is holding power back and whether Phase III is reducing it.
  • The Forum of Regulators should agree a common approach to recovering Phase III costs in state tariffs, including how battery costs are shared between transmission users and the generators they serve, before states start bidding.
  • State governments should adopt the Ministry of Power’s right-of-way compensation guidelines for transmission lines, the bottleneck named in every Parliament answer on the earlier phases.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

India has built nine separate technology relationships with Europe since 2010 — one with the European Union as a bloc, eight with individual European countries (not all of them EU member states: Norway and Switzerland are among them). This is the full paper trail, verified against India's own Press Information Bureau one release at a time, with two widely repeated claims about a February 2026 France visit removed because the government's own record does not back them.

Updated 7 November 2026. The third India–EU Trade and Technology Council meeting is now confirmed: held in Brussels on 14–16 July 2026, it agreed deeper cooperation on AI, semiconductors, clean technologies, high-performance computing, quantum and 6G, and launched talks on India’s association to Horizon Europe, aimed at concluding before the end of 2026 (The Tribune; Austria’s ERA portal). Separately, India and the Netherlands set out a technology roadmap in May 2026 including a semiconductor “brain bridge” between Indian institutes and Dutch partners. Coverage in early November reports the two sides still differing on how AI, EVs, semiconductors and telecom should be worded in TTC versus FTA texts. These are from press reports; the PIB records were not checked, so they are not added to the tables below.

What would fix this

  • The Ministry of External Affairs should maintain a single, downloadable register of technology agreements with the EU and each European partner, listing date, signatories, domain, instrument type and current status, so sixteen years of cooperation across nine counterparties no longer has to be rebuilt release by release.
  • PIB should tag every release by partner country and let its archive be searched by country, ministry and date range across all years, so an agreement such as the Indo-German Centres of Excellence or the India-Switzerland climate MoU can be found on PIB if it was ever released there.
  • The Ministry of External Affairs should label each item in a visit’s list of outcomes by instrument type — MoU, letter of intent, roadmap theme or working group, so a “Trusted AI” pillar is not reported as a named India-France AI Working Group that the text does not create.
  • The Department of Science and Technology should publish an annual activity report for each bilateral centre and programme it runs with European partners, such as IGSTC and the DST–VINNOVA calls, giving projects funded and outputs, so the record shows what got done, not only what got documented.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

Sources and caveats

Every PIB URL in this article was independently fetched via HTTP request and keyword-matched against its claimed subject before inclusion (e.g. the TTC releases were checked for "Trade and Technology Council," the Denmark releases for "Denmark," the Switzerland rail MoU for "Switzerland," and so on) — this is a verification step applied specifically because government press-release URLs referencing a numeric ID are easy to state incorrectly, and a wrong ID is worse than an honest gap. Three items are explicitly marked "not confirmed on PIB" because a genuine search effort could not locate a pib.gov.in release for them within this research pass: the Indo-German Centres of Excellence on Innovation (primary source is India's Ministry of External Affairs bilateral-documents page) and the India-Switzerland Climate Change & Environment Technical Cooperation MoU (primary source is pmindia.gov.in's Cabinet-approval release). This does not mean no PIB release exists for these — PIB's archive is large and not fully searchable by a single query — only that this research pass did not find one, and a fabricated PRID would be worse than that honest gap. Two claims previously in circulation about the February 2026 France visit — a named India-France AI Working Group and an ISRO-CNES letter of intent on microgravity/human spaceflight research — were checked directly against PIB's Joint Statement (PRID=2229412) and List of Outcomes (PRID=2229255) for that visit and found in neither; they are reported here as unconfirmed rather than as fact. This article does not attempt to value, rank, or draw investment conclusions from any agreement listed; nothing here is investment, trade or diplomatic advice.

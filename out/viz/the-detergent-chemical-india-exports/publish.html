Two petrochemical intermediates sit behind the same supermarket aisle: one turns into the gel that keeps a diaper dry, the other into the surfactant that makes a detergent foam. India makes plenty of the second one and sells the surplus abroad. Until very recently, it made none of the first — every gram was imported.

Detergent and diaper chemicals in India: LAB exported, SAP mostly imported

Skip to article content
Text Size

Top line

  • 162,000 tonnes a year: Indian Oil's LAB unit, the largest reported, after its 2022 revamp.
  • ₹3,686 crore of alkylbenzene (LAB) imports in FY2024-25, against ₹37 crore exported.
  • 200 tonnes a year: BPCL's SAP demo plant, against a national market of about 50,000 tonnes.
Updated 7 November 2026. FY2025-26 trade data are now available. On the same DGCI&S 8-digit lines, in US dollars: finished detergents and surfactants (HS 3402) exported $641 million against $492 million of imports, so the surplus narrowed from about $197 million in FY2024-25 to about $150 million as imports grew 10%. The SAP-bearing acrylic-polymer line (39069090) imported $601 million against $243 million exported, a deficit of about $358 million (up from $323 million). Alkylbenzene imports (HS 3817, including LAB) rose to $468 million, with exports under $4 million. Diapers and pads (HS 9619) moved close to balance, with imports falling to $47 million against $42 million of exports. The table below is still FY2024-25 in rupees. No commissioning of BPCL’s planned 50,000-tonne SAP plant has been reported; a 2023 market report had expected it by FY2026.

Updated 15 November 2026: rewritten as charts; figures unchanged.

India already makes LAB, and Indian Oil's unit is the largest reported

What it means: Two domestic producers have reported capacity, and TPL says its LAB sits in roughly 40% of India's fabric detergents.

What to do: Track whether Reliance and ONGC Petro publish capacity figures, since none were found for them.

Detergent makers run a small surplus, while LAB imports far outweigh exports

What it means: Finished detergents are in surplus, but the chemicals that feed them are imported in large amounts.

What to do: Check whether FY2025-26 alkylbenzene imports keep rising, as the post's updated trade figures suggest.

Domestic SAP output is a sliver of a market of about 50,000 tonnes

What it means: India can now make SAP at small scale, but nearly all of its demand is still met by imports.

What to do: Watch for the commissioning of BPCL's planned 50,000-tonne commercial SAP plant, which has no confirmed date.

What would fix this

  • CBIC and DGFT should create separate ITC-HS 8-digit lines for detergent-grade linear alkylbenzene and for sodium polyacrylate super absorbent polymer, so that India's actual LAB and SAP trade balances can be read directly instead of through broad headings like HS 3817 that do not reconcile with producers' own export claims.
  • The Department of Chemicals and Petrochemicals should publish annual LAB and SAP capacity and production by producer, including Reliance and ONGC Petro, so that the domestic supply side of both chemicals is on the official record rather than pieced together from company announcements.
  • BPCL should publish a dated project schedule for its planned 50,000-tonne-a-year SAP plant, and annual output from the Kochi demonstration unit, so that whether India's only petrochemical-route SAP programme is moving beyond 200 tonnes can be tracked.
  • The Department of Chemicals and Petrochemicals should state whether any central incentive scheme covers SAP manufacturing, so that market-research claims of an unnamed chemicals PLI accelerating SAP investment can be confirmed or retired.
  • The Karnataka State Pollution Control Board should publish regular surfactant and phosphate measurements for Bellandur and Varthur lakes and their inflows, so that the dispute over how much of the foaming comes from detergent rather than sewage can be settled with monitoring data.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

Sources and caveats

This piece was originally researched entirely through web search rather than direct page fetches, because the network it was written from could not reach several primary and trade-press domains directly. Update, 1 September 2026: a follow-up pass has since fetched several of those sources directly — IOCL's own investor PDF, Indian Chemical News, BPCL's own site, busy.in's TradeStat-sourced tables, theweek.in, thecore.in, and IISc's own lake-foaming explainer. That confirmed IOCL's 162,000-tonne LAB capacity exactly; found TPL's own site (tnpetro.com) returning an unrelated, apparently hijacked page rather than usable content; corrected BPCL's SAP demonstration-plant date from November 2024 to its actual 11 January 2022 inauguration, a substantive fix since the plant has existed nearly three years longer than this piece originally said; filled in the previously-missing HS 39069090 export figure (₹2,127 crore, a ₹2,725 crore deficit for that code) from the same TradeStat table; corrected IOCL's Petrochemical Intensity Index target from a previously-stated "6.5% to 16%" to 15% after tracing "16%" to a likely rounding/transcription variant in a single secondary report, corroborated instead by IOCL's own investor-call transcript and independent S&P Global reporting, while confirming the underlying tonnage target; corrected the Bellandur Lake 17.8 ppm figure's source paper to 2024, not 2026; and, on Swara Baby Products, found the Alok Birla/Kimberly-Clark biographical detail is secondary-sourced only (not in the original press release), while a dated 2026 IPO-coverage source independently confirmed the FirstCry-promoter connection this piece had earlier excluded as unconfirmed. LAB producer and capacity figures (Section 2) are corroborated across IOCL's own investor materials, Indian Chemical News, and industry market-report summaries for Reliance and ONGC Petro's producer status. SAP market-size and zero-domestic-production figures (Section 3) are from ChemAnalyst's and BlueWeave's India superabsorbent polymer market research summaries. BPCL's SAP demonstration plant is now dated to BPCL's own site; EF Polymer's figures are from trade coverage of the company; BASF's Mumbai lab is from BASF's own press release. The March 2026 LAB price-surge figure (Section 5) remains from search-result summaries, not directly re-fetched on this pass. Factory and OEM details (Section 4b) are corroborated across The Week, The Print, Business Standard, Nonwovens Industry, Technical Textiles Today, IndiaMART, AFAQS, and each company's own corporate pages, with source-by-source detail given at the end of that section. No figure in this piece should be read as a current, live production or import statistic without checking the primary source cited, given the pace at which both BPCL's commercial SAP plant and ongoing LAB capacity additions are reported to be moving. Update of 7 November 2026: FY2025-26 figures are from DGCI&S TradeStat 8-digit data as compiled for this blog’s searchable 8-digit trade book (US$ million, summed across the 8-digit lines under each heading); BPCL SAP plant status per ICIS (2021 plan) and Research and Markets (2023 forecast of FY2026 commissioning); no later commissioning report found.

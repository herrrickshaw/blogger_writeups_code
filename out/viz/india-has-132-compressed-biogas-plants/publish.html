SATAT set out in 2018 to build 5,000 compressed biogas plants by 2023-24. India has 207 functional plants (217 commissioned on the GOBARdhan portal by 6 August 2026). Of the 207, the regulator's own August 2026 count says only 24 are actually connected to a city gas network by dedicated pipeline — meaning most of the plants that exist still cannot inject their gas into the grid the blending mandate assumes they will feed.

Top line

  • 217 plants were commissioned by 6 August 2026, 4.3% of the original 5,000-plant SATAT target.
Updated 7 November 2026. The blending obligation’s first year, FY2025-26, is reported as met: about 1.05% of CNG and piped-gas sales against the 1% target (Swarajya). PNGRB’s own 6 August 2026 figures, 111.48 of 12,033 million standard cubic metres, work out to about 0.93% by this blog’s arithmetic (PNGRB, search summary only). With only 24 of 207 plants connected, this blog infers that most of that gas reached city gas networks by truck rather than pipe; the test this piece describes comes as the obligation steps up to 3% in FY2026-27.
Updated 13 October 2026. The 132 commissioned plants (January 2026) and the 17 pipeline-connected plants quoted from the regulator below may reflect older counts. A PNGRB October 2025 deck lists 17 plants connected, while a March 2026 status report lists 24, and a PNGRB FY2025-26 connectivity report (August 2026) is summarised as 24 of 207 functional plants connected. A Gobardhan-portal summary puts commissioned CBG plants at about 210 as of June 2026. These figures were first drawn from search summaries; the PNGRB reports have since been opened and the text below updated to 24 of 207 (see the 25 October 2026 note near the end). The blending obligation rises from 1% to 5% of CNG and piped-gas supply

What it means: FY2025-26 was reported at about 1.05% of sales, against a 1% requirement.

What to do: Check the step to 3% in FY2026-27 against the 24 pipeline-connected plants that can feed city networks.

What would fix this

  • Sequence connectivity funding ahead of new plant approvals, not behind them. A CBG plant sanctioned without an assessed, funded pipeline route to the nearest CGD network is being set up to rely on trucking indefinitely; PNGRB's own "proximity and feasibility" mapping exercise could become a pre-sanction gate rather than a post-hoc audit.
  • Publish the CBO's actual blend achieved against the mandated ramp, by CGD entity. A 1%-this-year, 5%-eventually mandate is only checkable if there is a public number for what share entities are actually managing to blend; the ATR does not publish one.
  • Report the 15 "in progress" connectivity projects' expected completion dates. Naming a date lets a reader distinguish a genuinely near-term fix from an open-ended one.
  • Give the 148 functional plants with no connectivity plan a public reason. Some are likely too far from any CGD network to justify a dedicated line; some may simply not have been assessed yet. Those are different problems with different fixes, and the ATR does not distinguish between them.
  • Publish district- and constituency-level CBG connectivity data, the way ODOP publishes a product per district. A public map of which districts, Lok Sabha and Assembly constituencies have a commissioned or pipeline-connected CBG plant — and which have neither — would let each constituency's own MP or MLA see, and be seen to act on, a concrete local gap rather than a national percentage.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

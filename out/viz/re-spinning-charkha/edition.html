Updated 12 November 2026. Recomputed from TradeStat (DGCI&S) chapter-level data, India’s combined Chapter 29 + 39 trade deficit was $19.6 billion in FY2025-26 and $20.4 billion in FY2024-25. This piece previously tied the $20.4 billion figure to FY2025-26 and described $19.6 billion as TradeStat’s “running total”; in fact $20.4 billion is the full FY2024-25 deficit and $19.6 billion the full FY2025-26 deficit. The year labels here now match the companion post From Fuel to Fibre. Between the two years the organic-chemicals (Chapter 29) deficit narrowed from $6.5 billion to $5.0 billion while the plastics (Chapter 39) deficit widened from $14.0 billion to $14.6 billion. For April–August 2026, the latest month TradeStat has published, the combined deficit is $9.5 billion, against $9.3 billion in April–August 2025. The rounded “$20bn” used elsewhere in this piece holds for both years.

Reliance spent 33 years building the chain ethanol blending now aims to free

  1. 1966First manufacturing plant: woven fabric at Naroda (Vimal brand)
  2. 1982Polyester filament yarn at Patalganga; stops importing its core yarn
  3. 1991–92Naphtha cracker and fibre intermediates at Hazira
  4. 199927 MMTPA Jamnagar refinery, the crude-to-naphtha layer

What it means: Reliance integrated backward one link at a time, financing each plant itself.

What to do: Check whether the post's proposed naphtha-to-fibre route can be followed by several companies in parallel rather than one.

What would fix this

  • DGCI&S should publish a standing country-wise import table for the main fibre feedstocks — PTA, MEG, polyethylene, polypropylene, acrylonitrile and caprolactam — at the 8-digit level, so supplier shares for these monomers come from government data rather than industry trade analytics.
  • The Department of Chemicals and Petrochemicals and the Ministry of Textiles should publish a joint annual fibre-feedstock balance showing domestic production, imports and consumption of each major monomer, so a chain split across two ministries has one scorecard for the $20bn deficit it is meant to close.
  • PPAC should publish each year the volume of petrol-range naphtha and reformate freed by ethanol blending and how much of it went to domestic petrochemical units rather than export, so the ethanol-to-fibre pathway this post describes is measured rather than inferred.
  • The Ministry of Textiles should publish a dated status table for each of the seven PM MITRA parks, listing construction stage, allotted tenants and committed investment, so progress on the distributed version of backward integration can be checked park by park.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

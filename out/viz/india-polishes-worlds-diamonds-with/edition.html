Every diamond polished in Surat is polished with diamond — a powder of crushed synthetic crystals worked into an iron wheel with oil. India imported ₹239.5 crore of that powder in 2024–25 and exported about a ninth as much, and most of it comes from China, which put fine diamond powder on an export-licence list last October and has only paused it until 10 November 2026. India now runs thousands of lab-grown diamond reactors and has just approved ₹9,817.5 crore more in Odisha. Grinding those stones into powder is the obvious-sounding answer. The carats and the prices say it isn't one.

Diamond polishing powder: India imported ₹239.5 crore in 2024–25, mostly from China

Skip to article content
Text Size

Top line

  • ₹239.5 cr of diamond dust and powder imported in 2024–25, against ₹27.5 crore of exports.
  • ~1% of India's powder imports by carats is what the whole lab-grown output would cover.
  • 10 Nov 2026 is when China's paused export-licensing rule on fine synthetic diamond powder is due to return.
Updated 7 November 2026. Full-year 2025-26 trade data are now out (DGCI&S 8-digit, in US dollars). Imports of diamond dust and powder (HS 710510) rose to about $30.7 million from $28.3 million in 2024-25, but by volume they jumped about 37%, to roughly 1,794 million carats from 1,308 million — the powder got cheaper per carat as China’s oversupply continued. Exports stayed at about $3.4 million. Against that larger volume, all of India’s lab-grown output would now cover nearer 0.7–0.8% of the powder imported than 1%. Diamond grinding-wheel exports (HS 680421) slipped about 8%, to $26.9 million, after 2024-25’s jump. China’s pause on licensing fine diamond powder exports still runs to 10 November 2026; no extension had been announced by 7 November (CIRS on Announcement No. 70 of 2025).

What it means: Net of exports, India's diamond powder bill is a deficit of about ₹212 crore in one year.

What to do: Track the powder deficit as a supply-security line, not as a sign of diamond-industry strength.

India's lab-grown output would cover about 1% of its powder imports by carats

What it means: Grinding up every lab-grown stone the industry grows would cover only about 1% of the powder bill by weight.

What to do: Build HPHT grit capacity rather than rely on lab-grown gem growing to replace imported powder.

China's licence on fine diamond powder is suspended only until 10 November 2026

  1. 9 Oct 2025Announcement No. 55 puts synthetic diamond micropowder under export licensing, due to take effect 8 November
  2. 7 NovAnnouncement No. 70 suspends the licensing rule with other export controls
  3. 10 Nov 2026Suspension ends unless extended; a licence is not a ban

What it means: A Chinese export licence on the powder behind most of India's diamond polishing is on a timer.

What to do: Plan for India to have no domestic fallback at scale if the suspension lapses.

What would fix this

  • The Directorate General of Commercial Intelligence and Statistics and CBIC should split HS 71051000 into fine micron powder and coarser grit at the 8-digit level, mirroring the size-based line China's export control uses, so India can measure how much of its import bill would actually fall under a Chinese licence.
  • IIT Madras's India Centre for Lab-grown Diamond, and the Department of Science and Technology that funds it, should add HPHT industrial grit synthesis and micron-powder classification to its mandate, with a pilot press-and-grading line. The country's exposure is to industrial HPHT grit, not to gem growing or quantum-grade CVD.
  • GJEPC and the Surat Diamond Association should organise pooled procurement or a three-to-six-month buffer of polishing powder for small units before 10 November 2026, since most polishing workshops buy powder in small lots and have the least ability to absorb a licensing delay.
  • The Bureau of Indian Standards should publish a standard for diamond micron-powder grading, drawing on ISO 6106 and the FEPA and ANSI systems importers already quote, so that any domestic producer — including a lab-grown grower selling reject material as powder — can certify its grades against imports.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

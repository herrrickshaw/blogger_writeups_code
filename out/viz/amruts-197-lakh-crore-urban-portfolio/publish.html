AMRUT 2.0, the urban counterpart to Jal Jeevan Mission's rural water push, has approved ₹1.97 lakh crore across 8,777 projects nationally. Water supply alone is 41% of that project count but 60% of the money — the single most capital-intensive line item in India's urban infrastructure programme. What the public data can't tell you, unlike its rural counterpart, is how much of that money has actually been spent.

What it means: Five states together account for over half of the national water-supply total of ₹1,18,953 crore.

What to do: Check the state figures against the national total before quoting the five-state share, because the top five in the table differ from the five named in the post.

Only 104 of 594 sewerage and septage projects were completed

What it means: Only 17.5% of the sewerage and septage projects the Standing Committee checked were complete.

What to do: Publish sewerage and septage completion status alongside the water supply figures, so the 36% cost share can be judged against delivery.

What would fix this

  • The Ministry of Housing and Urban Affairs should publish a state-wise rollup of AMRUT 2.0 project status — completed, ongoing, contract awarded, not yet tendered — on the AMRUT Universe portal, matching what Jal Jeevan Mission’s Format B15 already provides for rural water.
  • MoHUA should release the full list of 8,777 AMRUT 2.0 projects as a downloadable dataset with stable project IDs, city, component, sanctioned cost and milestone, so that status can be counted without drilling state → city → project through encrypted, non-sequential IDs.
  • MoHUA should add funds released and expenditure incurred alongside sanctioned cost in the State Wise List of Approved Projects, so that the ₹1,97,201.55 crore portfolio can be read as money spent, not only money approved.
  • MoHUA should date-stamp the State Wise List page and archive a snapshot each quarter, so that cross-checks against parliamentary answers — such as the ₹1,18,968.38 crore versus ₹1,18,952.82 crore water-supply figures here — can be traced to a fixed version.
  • The urban development departments of Uttar Pradesh, Maharashtra, Karnataka, Gujarat and Tamil Nadu should each publish project-wise AMRUT water-supply completion status, since together they hold ₹62,014 crore, over half of the national water-supply capital, and no state rollup exists.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

What this piece does not establish. This piece does not have a state-wise completion-status breakdown for AMRUT the way the companion JJM piece does for rural water — only national totals appear in parliamentary answers, and the state-aggregated form does not appear to exist in MoHUA's public reporting, and this piece did not attempt to reconstruct one by manually drilling city-by-city through thousands of individual projects. It does not verify whether MoHUA publishes milestone-stage-specific PDFs beyond the "Contract Awarded" one found (e.g. a "Completed" or "Work in Progress" equivalent) — only the public links visible on the AMRUT Universe login page were checked. Sources and caveats

All state-wise project counts and costs are drawn directly from the Ministry of Housing & Urban Affairs' AMRUT Universe portal, "State Wise List of Approved Projects" (amrut.mohua.gov.in/approvedProjects/state), a live, public, no-login page, graded strong as a primary government source. The cross-check against the Lok Sabha figure (3,572 projects / ₹1,18,968.38 crore) is drawn from a Ministry of Housing and Urban Affairs written reply in the Lok Sabha, answered 06.08.2026, a primary parliamentary source, graded strong. The existence of a per-project "Milestone" status field, reachable via state → city → project drill-down with encrypted (non-sequential) IDs, was confirmed directly by navigating the portal; this piece did not attempt to enumerate all cities or projects given the scale involved. The "Status of Water Supply Projects with Water Treatment Plants" PDF (910 rows, dated as on 02.03.2026) was downloaded and checked directly; the finding that essentially all rows share the "Contract Awarded" status is this piece's own text-extraction and pattern count, graded strong for what was checked, though the PDF's dense, wrapped multi-line table formatting means a small number of rows could have been missed or misparsed. National award and completion figures as on 2 March 2026, and the extension to March 2027, are from Rajya Sabha unstarred question 3418 (23 March 2026); the 9 July 2026 figures are from a Lok Sabha reply as reported by Kashmir Life (7 August 2026); the sewerage completion count is from the Standing Committee report as reported by Construction World (10 August 2026).

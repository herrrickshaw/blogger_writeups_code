## Four components, one portfolio

Atal Mission for Rejuvenation and Urban Transformation (AMRUT) 2.0, run by the Ministry of Housing and Urban Affairs, funds four categories of urban infrastructure through its state governments and Urban Local Bodies: Water Supply, Sewerage & Septage Management, Water Body Rejuvenation, and Parks & Green Space Development. The Ministry's own public, no-login "State Wise List of Approved Projects" page puts the current national total at 8,777 projects worth ₹1,97,201.55 crore. Every state and UT total below is pulled directly from that live page.

Water supply is the single most capital-intensive line item in AMRUT 2.0's portfolio — 41% of projects but 60% of the money. ## Water supply carries the money, not the project count

Water Supply is 41% of AMRUT's project count nationally but 60% of its total cost — by a wide margin the most capital-intensive of the four components. Sewerage & Septage Management, despite being only 7% of project count, still accounts for 36% of the total AMRUT cost — both water supply and sewerage are infrastructure-heavy, capital-intensive categories, unlike Water Body Rejuvenation and Parks & Green Space, which together are 26% of project count but just 3.6% of total spend.

The national figure cross-checks cleanly against an August 2026 Lok Sabha answer on AMRUT 2.0 (previously covered on this blog in the JJM rural-water piece): that answer put water supply at 3,572 projects worth ₹1,18,968.38 crore — within 0.01% of this page's 3,572 projects and ₹1,18,952.82 crore. Two independently-maintained government sources agreeing this closely is itself a useful confirmation that the underlying project count is stable, not still being actively revised.

Uttar Pradesh, Maharashtra, Karnataka, Gujarat, and Tamil Nadu together account for over half of India's AMRUT water-supply capital — ₹62,014 crore of the ₹1,18,953 crore national total.

## What this table can't tell you

This is a count-and-cost table, not a progress table. Unlike Jal Jeevan Mission's rural-water reporting — covered in [India's ₹7.9 Lakh Crore Rural Water Pipeline: What "Upcoming" Actually Means, State by State](https://blog.vertistratsolution.com/2026/08/indias-79-lakh-crore-rural-water.html), which has a clean, pre-aggregated Completed/Ongoing/Work-order-not-issued split for every state — AMRUT's public reporting has no equivalent state-wise status summary. Individual AMRUT projects do carry a status field (labelled "Milestone" in the portal's own project-level views, e.g. "Contract Awarded"), reachable by drilling state → city → project through the AMRUT Universe portal, but there is no single published table that rolls that status up by state the way JJM's "Format B15" does. That's not a gap in this piece's research — it's a gap in what MoHUA has chosen to pre-aggregate and publish; the Ministry of Jal Shakti built and published that specific rollup for JJM, and MoHUA has not built the equivalent for AMRUT.

Parliamentary answers do give a national roll-up, without the state split. On 2 March 2026, 7,080 projects with a capital cost of ₹1,26,357.51 crore had been awarded and works worth ₹54,429.73 crore physically completed, and the mission had been extended to March 2027 (Rajya Sabha, unstarred question 3418, 23 March 2026). By 9 July 2026 the awarded figures had reached 7,687 projects and ₹1.37 lakh crore, with ₹66,472 crore of works completed (Lok Sabha reply, as reported by Kashmir Life). Parliament’s Standing Committee on Housing and Urban Affairs found only 104 of 594 sewerage and septage projects, 17.5%, completed.

One MoHUA-published PDF looked like it might fill the gap — "Status of Water Supply Projects with Water Treatment Plants" — but checking all 910 project rows in it found essentially every one carries the identical status, "Contract Awarded." That's not a completion breakdown; it's a snapshot scoped to one single milestone stage, not a status distribution across stages.

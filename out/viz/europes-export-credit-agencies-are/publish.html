Twenty national export credit agencies sit behind the EU's €300 billion Global Gateway pledge. In India, their money has gone almost entirely into urban water, transit, and green power — not into the technology-transfer manufacturing deals the "de-risking from China" narrative implies.

European export credit agencies in India: infrastructure more than factories

Skip to article content
Text Size

Top line

  • 20 of 27 EU member states ran a national export credit agency in the Commission's 2023 review.
  • €2.2bn of the EIB's €3.8bn India lending since 1993 went to urban rail.
Updated 7 November 2026. The Commission’s next annual review, COM(2025) 737 final, covers 2024: 21 member states provided officially supported export credits that year, against the 20 listed below for 2023 (Council document ST 16423/2025, text read from the Council register). The EIB’s newest India financing fits the same pattern this piece describes: in October 2025 EIB Global announced more than ₹5,200 crore, made up of €289.5 million for the Nagpur and Pune metros, $191 million for water supply and sanitation in Uttarakhand, and up to $60 million for the India Energy Transition Fund. None of it is manufacturing or technology transfer.

What would fix this

  • The Department of Economic Affairs should publish an annual consolidated table of all European bilateral and multilateral loans and EU grants to India — AFD, KfW, EIB and the EU budget — by programme, sector, state, sanctioned and disbursed amount, so that the pattern this post reconstructed from two EU documents is available from an Indian source.
  • The European Commission should add a country-by-sector breakdown for India to its annual review of member states' export credit activity, so that the split between infrastructure and manufacturing exposure of the 20 national ECAs can be read directly rather than inferred from individual deal announcements.
  • The EU Delegation to India and the Ministry of External Affairs should publish, for the Science & Technology pillar of the EU-India Connectivity Partnership, a list of financed projects with amounts — or state that none exist — so that the gap between that pillar and the urban, water and energy pillars is on the record.
  • The Ministry of External Affairs and the EU Delegation should tag every Global Gateway announcement concerning India by instrument (concessional loan, grant, ECA guarantee, equity) and by sector, so that readers can tell a transit or water loan from a manufacturing technology-transfer deal without reconstructing the financing structure themselves.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

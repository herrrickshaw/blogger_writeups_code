MoSPI moved the Index of Industrial Production to a 2022-23 base in June 2026. For the three years both series cover, the new index shows faster industrial growth (5.7% rather than 4.0% in 2024-25), and in 14 industry-years the two disagree on whether output rose or fell, so IIP figures quoted before and after the switch cannot be read against each other.

Industrial production index: electronics down 11% in 2023-24 on old base, up 20% on new

Skip to article content
Text Size

Top line

  • 14 of 69 industry-years change sign between the two series; in 13 of them the old base showed a fall and the new base shows growth.
  • +31.2 points: the gap between the two bases for computer, electronic and optical products in 2023-24.
  • 5.7% all-industry growth in 2024-25 on the new base, against 4.0% on the old.

Updated 15 November 2026: rewritten as charts; figures unchanged.

Electronics went from an 11.3% fall to 19.9% growth, depending on the base

What it means: Electronics is the sharpest case: whether its output grew or shrank in 2023-24 depends on which base is used.

What to do: Quote the base year alongside any IIP growth figure, especially for runs of years that cross April 2023.

All-industry growth in 2024-25 was 5.7% on the new base, against 4.0% on the old

What it means: The gap for all industry is largest in 2024-25 and almost closes in 2025-26, when the two bases show 4.1% and 4.3%.

What to do: Read the 2023-24 to 2025-26 figures from both bases side by side rather than from one series alone.

Fourteen of 69 industry-years flip sign between the two series

What it means: Most of the largest gaps are in industries whose product mix changed most, though this blog could not confirm the cause for any single industry.

What to do: Say which base a growth rate uses before comparing it with a figure published before June 2026.

What would fix this

  • MoSPI should publish a linked back series on the 2022-23 base from 2012-13 at the two-digit industry and use-based level, not only a linking factor for the general and sectoral indices, so a growth series can run across the change without being spliced by each user.
  • MoSPI should publish a bridge table for 2023-24 to 2025-26 that splits each industry’s old-versus-new difference into basket change, weights and price deflation, starting with the industries where the series disagree on direction.
  • MoSPI should put the item basket and weights in eSankhyiki, the 1,042 products with their weights and data sources, in machine-readable form; the API currently stops at the two-digit industry.
  • Ministries running production-linked incentive schemes (MeitY, DPIIT, the Department of Pharmaceuticals) should restate any output claims that rest on IIP growth on the new base, and say which base a figure uses.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

Sources and caveats

Index values and growth rates: MoSPI, Index of Industrial Production, annual and monthly indices, 2011-12 and 2022-23 bases, through the eSankhyiki API (retrieved 9 October 2026); the industry rows are two-digit NIC divisions as MoSPI publishes them. August 2026: MoSPI quick estimates released 28 September 2026 (PIB). Basket size, sectors and linking method: MoSPI’s first release of the new series (June 2026) as summarised by Drishti IAS, Vision IAS and Policy Circle. 2011-12 weights: MoSPI press release of 12 May 2017. New-series weights are from secondary summaries and should be checked against MoSPI’s technical note. Annual figures from the API differ slightly from some press summaries (for example 5.7% rather than 6.4% for 2024-25 on the new base); this piece uses the API. Explanations for individual industry gaps are this blog’s reading, not MoSPI’s.

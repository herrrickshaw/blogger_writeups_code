MoPSW's guidelines for the ₹25,000 crore Maritime Development Fund split the money into a ₹20,000 crore equity vehicle and a ₹5,000 crore interest subsidy — a blended-finance answer to one specific number: Indian shipyards borrow at roughly double what Chinese and South Korean yards pay.

Top line

  • ₹20,000 crore of the ₹25,000 crore goes into equity in ships and ports; ₹5,000 crore rebates shipyard loans.
  • 10–10.5% is what Indian yards report paying on ship construction loans, against 4–8% for Chinese yards.
  • 0.06% is India's share of global shipbuilding by gross tonnage, while China, South Korea and Japan together hold above 95%.
Updated 7 November 2026. Added where the fund stands: the Governing Council has been constituted and SBI Ventures was appointed Fund Manager of the ₹20,000 crore Maritime Investment Fund in May 2026, per the Directorate General of Maritime Administration. This blog found no report of a SEBI registration or first close by early November.

Most of the ₹25,000 crore is equity, with ₹5,000 crore for loan rebates

What it means: The equity vehicle is the large one, so most of the fund is a stake in companies and projects rather than a loan subsidy.

What to do: Read the Implementing Agency's SOPs for the IIF once published, since the subvention rate is not yet fixed.

Indian shipyards pay more to borrow for ships than their Chinese or Korean rivals

What it means: The rate gap is the financing cost the Interest Incentivization Fund is designed to reduce.

What to do: Check the reported loan rates against published bank or leasing terms before relying on the 15-20% cost-gap estimate.

What would fix this

The Guidelines answer the "how is the money structured" question. They leave the "will it actually get deployed and to what effect" question to instruments — SOPs, the Fund Manager's own reporting — that don't yet exist in public form.

  • Publish the IIF subvention rate against the published borrowing-cost gap, not as a flat number. If Indian yards borrow at 10–10.5% against China's 4–8% and Korea's 5–6%, an IIF that closes a fixed percentage-point gap year to year would be checkable against outcomes; an undisclosed subvention formula is not.
  • Report MIF's first-close and final-close progress against the 12-month and 36-month deadlines the Guidelines themselves set. The fund's own quarterly reporting (Section 2.9.3) goes to investors, not the public; a simple public dashboard of committed-vs-target corpus would let the fund's own timeline be checked without a Parliament question.
  • Publish the conflict-of-interest protocol for SMFCL's overlapping roles once the Fund Manager is selected. Section 2.10 requires Chinese-wall mechanisms in general terms; naming how SMFCL's fiduciary, IA, and selection-committee-chair roles are specifically separated would close the one structural question this piece could not resolve from the Guidelines alone.
  • Track GT capacity and Indian-flagged cargo share annually against the 2047 target, not just fund disbursement. The MDF's stated purpose is a 20%-by-2047 cargo-share goal; disbursed corpus is an input metric, not the outcome the fund exists to move.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

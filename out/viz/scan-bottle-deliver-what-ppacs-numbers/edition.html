Two oil marketing companies have piloted QR codes on domestic LPG cylinders since 2022 — a quality and anti-pilferage check, not a completed national rollout. Meanwhile PPAC's own numbers show two things that don't obviously square: active domestic LPG connections have grown from 14.9 crore in 2015 to 33.14 crore this July, a genuine, government-verified expansion — and PSU oil companies' domestic LPG sales in April-June 2026 actually fell 15.6% year-on-year. Both figures are real. This piece works through what each is measuring, and maps where India's 214 LPG bottling plants actually sit.

Top line

  • 33.14 crore active domestic LPG connections on 1 July 2026, up from 14.9 crore in April 2015.
  • 15.6% fall in PSU LPG sales in April-June 2026, a quarter PPAC gives no cause for.
  • 214 bottling plants across five regions, with none in several small states and UTs.
Updated 7 November 2026. The fall in LPG volumes below was not a one-quarter dip. PPAC data, as reported, show national LPG consumption down 14.93% in April–September 2026, at 13,797 thousand tonnes against 16,218 a year earlier, with September down 9.15% and October down about 12% (Construction World; ThePrint). Reports link the decline to West Asia supply disruptions, fuel switching and affordability stress (Down To Earth). These are total LPG consumption figures, a wider measure than the PSU oil companies' domestic sales quoted below, and India imported about 60% of its LPG in 2025-26.
Updated 22 October 2026. This post treats the 15.6% fall in April–June 2026 domestic LPG sales as possibly a one-quarter dip, and notes that PPAC gives no cause. The fall has continued. PPAC's preliminary monthly data, as reported in the press, show India's LPG consumption down 14.93% year on year in April–September 2026, and down 9.15% in September alone. Press coverage links the fall to supply disruption through the Strait of Hormuz and to a government-backed shift to piped gas. Those causes come from the press, not from PPAC. These are national consumption figures, a different series from the oil companies' sales in the LPG Profile Report, so the two are not strictly comparable. Sources: UNI, Construction World, ThePrint. What to do: Read connection counts next to actual gas sold, since the two diverged in April-June 2026.

Estimated LPG coverage of households rose from 56.2% in 2015 to about 95.6%

What it means: The figure divides active connections by projected households, so it is an estimate, not a count of homes served.

What to do: Report coverage on one method across years, since PPAC does not reconcile its two reports.

What would fix this

  • PESO should issue a circular stating whether the bar-code requirement in Rule 6(2)(c)(i) of the amended Gas Cylinders Rules applies to domestic 14.2 kg LPG cylinders, and if so from what date filling of unscannable cylinders will be refused, so a household’s cylinder is not left in a legal grey zone.
  • PPAC should add to each LPG Profile Report a breakdown of PSU OMC sales by domestic, PMUY and non-domestic segments, with a short note on the drivers of any year-on-year change, so a 15.6% fall in April–June 2026 sales beside a record 33.14 crore connections is explained, not just stated.
  • PPAC should publish LPG coverage for at least one common date on both its old and revised household methods, so the 99.8% reported for 2021 and the roughly 95.6% for July 2026 can be compared on a single basis.
  • Indian Oil and BPCL should publish the current reach of their QR-coded cylinder programmes — cylinders tagged, distributors and cities covered — and a target date for national coverage, so progress beyond the 23,827 cylinders of the 2022 Delhi pilot can be tracked.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

What this piece does not establish. Whether IOCL's 2022 Delhi pilot or BPCL's "Pure for Sure" has since expanded to a specific number of cities, distributors or a national completion date could not be confirmed from the sourcing gathered here; both are described in their own announcements as initiatives rather than completed nationwide rollouts, and no follow-up figures beyond the original 2022 pilot numbers were found. Whether Rule 6(2)(c)(i)'s general marking requirement is being actively read by PESO to cover domestic 14.2 kg cylinders — separately from Rule 6(2)(5)'s narrower, named-fuel enforcement clause — is an open legal question per independent regulatory analysis (Newtral), not something this piece resolves. PPAC's Q1 FY2026-27 report does not state a cause for the 15.6% year-on-year de-growth in April-June 2026 domestic LPG sales, and this piece does not speculate on one.

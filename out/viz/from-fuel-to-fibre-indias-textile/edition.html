Top line

  • $19.6 billion combined deficit in plastics and organic chemicals in FY2025-26, down from $20.4 billion.
  • $396M net deficit in specialty fibres and composites in FY2025-26, the largest of any segment.
  • $325M Indutech deficit in FY2025-26, up from $112M the year before.
Updated 12 November 2026. Recomputed from TradeStat (DGCI&S) chapter-level data, India’s combined Chapter 29 + 39 trade deficit was $19.6 billion in FY2025-26 (exports $28.0 billion, imports $47.6 billion), down from $20.4 billion in FY2024-25. The FY2024-25 figures in the original table check out against TradeStat; what changed is the year and the mix. The organic-chemicals (Chapter 29) deficit narrowed from $6.5 billion to $5.0 billion as imports fell 4.4%, while the plastics (Chapter 39) deficit widened from $14.0 billion to $14.6 billion as exports fell 6.9%. The lead figure and the table below now carry FY2025-26. For April–August 2026, the latest month TradeStat has published, the combined deficit is $9.5 billion, against $9.3 billion in April–August 2025: Chapter 39 is wider ($6.7 billion against $6.3 billion), Chapter 29 slightly narrower ($2.7 billion against $3.0 billion).
Updated 7 November 2026. Provisional FY2025-26 TradeStat figures put Chapter 39 (plastics) imports at $22.2 billion and Chapter 29 (organic chemicals) at $25.4 billion, a combined $47.6 billion against $48.7 billion in FY2024-25, as compiled in this blog’s india-trade-data-analysis dataset. Recomputed from TradeStat on 12 November 2026, the FY2025-26 deficit is $19.6 billion; the $20.4 billion figure is FY2024-25.

Updated 15 November 2026: rewritten as charts; figures unchanged.

Plastics carries most of the $19.6 billion feedstock deficit

What it means: The Chapter 39 plastics deficit widened from $14.0 billion to $14.6 billion while the Chapter 29 chemicals deficit narrowed to $5.0 billion.

What to do: Keep the chapter split visible in future trade tracking, since the combined headline hides the widening plastics gap.

Specialty fibres, Indutech and Mobiltech show the largest FY2025-26 deficits

What it means: Specialty fibres and composites has the largest net deficit at $396M, ahead of Indutech at $325M and Mobiltech at $310M.

What to do: Prioritise these three segments for feedstock and capacity support, as the post's segment table recommends.

Indutech's deficit nearly tripled in one year, to $325 million

What it means: Indutech moved from a $112 million deficit in FY25 to a $325 million deficit in FY26, and the post links the segment to LLDPE and HDPE dependence.

What to do: Lock multi-year feedstock offtake contracts before new LLDPE and polyester capacity comes online.

What would fix this

  • The Ministry of Textiles should resume publishing its official 207-code technical-textiles trade table every year, starting with the missing years to FY2025-26, each with a stated data cut-off date, so the series does not stop at FY2021-22 and surplus figures such as $384 million and $532 million can be reconciled.
  • The Ministry of Textiles should publish one downloadable HSN-to-segment concordance for technical textiles, marking which codes belong to which of the 12 official segments, so the older 18-label list and wider code baskets are not merged in working spreadsheets.
  • The Department of Chemicals and Petrochemicals and the Ministry of Textiles should issue a joint annual statement on imports of fibre feedstocks — MEG, acrylonitrile, caprolactam, BDO and fibre-grade polymers — since about 1,111 of the 1,115 relevant codes sit with Chemicals and only four with Textiles.
  • The Ministry of Textiles should publish a quarterly park-by-park PM MITRA dashboard of acres allotted, investment grounded, units operating and central infrastructure spending, so progress against the ₹2,160.17 crore approved for external infrastructure, of which ₹564.72 crore was spent by February 2026, can be tracked without waiting for press releases.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

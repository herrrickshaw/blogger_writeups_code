E20 blending now removes a structural fifth of the petrol pool from every litre refiners sell, and they are redirecting the freed capacity into petrochemicals at real scale — but the aromatics and olefins reaching the ground today are chasing polymers and LPG, not the paracetamol- and ibuprofen-precursor chemistry that could cut into a pharma import bill still 43% dependent on China. Bilateral trade data adds a sharper edge to that gap: India's naphtha exports to China doubled the same fiscal year E20 took hold nationwide — freed capacity is reaching China's crackers before it reaches India's own.

Refiners are routing more of their output into petrochemicals

What it means: The capacity that ethanol blending frees is going into petrochemicals rather than pharma feedstock.

What to do: Track whether the national petrochemical intensity keeps rising toward the individual refiner targets.

Naphtha exports to China doubled in FY2025-26 after two flat years

What it means: A rising share of the freed naphtha is being sold as raw feedstock to China's own crackers.

What to do: Check whether FY2026-27 exports keep climbing, and whether any of that naphtha is routed to a domestic KSM plant instead.

What it means: Most fermentation-route molecules remain almost wholly dependent on China, and diclofenac is the most open synthetic-route target.

What to do: Direct any new KSM scheme at diclofenac sodium first, since it has no PLI project and no market-driven improvement yet.

What would fix this

  • PPAC should publish refinery-wise petrol and naphtha yields for the years before and after E20, alongside naphtha exports by destination, so the link between ethanol blending and the 101% jump in naphtha exports to China can be tested rather than read as a dated coincidence.
  • PPAC should split domestic naphtha output into chemical-grade cracker feed and fuel use, and report chemical-grade naphtha imports separately, so the specification constraint that keeps freed naphtha out of crackers is measured each year instead of described.
  • The Department of Pharmaceuticals should publish China import dependency for key starting materials such as para-aminophenol and isobutylbenzene, and for aspirin, at the same granularity as its API figures, so the synthetic-route chain is tracked molecule by molecule, not only at the finished-drug stage.
  • The Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas should require PSU refiners to state, in each petrochemical project’s public disclosure, the product slate and the downstream industries it is designed to supply, so it is checkable whether any of the capacity at Paradip, Bina or Barmer is aimed at pharma intermediates.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

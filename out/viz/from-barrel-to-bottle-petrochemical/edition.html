India has roughly 50 crore LPG cylinders in circulation, almost all of them steel, each weighing around 15.3 kg empty before a single kilogram of gas goes in. A newer alternative — a composite cylinder built from HDPE and fibreglass rather than steel — weighs about half that. This piece works through what that cylinder is actually made of, the real government reply that describes it, and why the same refinery investment already covered on this site in the petrochemical-pivot lever supplies exactly the polymer feedstock this substitution would need.

Composite LPG cylinders: about half the weight of steel ones at around 15.3 kg

Skip to article content

Top line

  • Two-thirds lighter: a comparable composite cylinder weighs 5.2 kg empty, against roughly 15.3 kg for steel.
  • 650 KTA of combined HDPE/LDPE capacity at IOCL across two lines, 300 KTA of it dedicated to HDPE.
  • 83.1% fall in bulk LPG consumption in May 2026, from West Asia supply restrictions, while household cylinder deliveries stayed large.
Updated 13 October 2026. The LPG supply strain described below reflects PPAC’s May 2026 data. On 25 June 2026 the Petroleum Ministry reportedly removed the sectoral restrictions on non-domestic packed LPG, with bulk supply relaxed to 50% of pre-crisis levels. PPAC’s provisional January–June 2026 consumption was about 8% lower than a year earlier (June down nearly 17%); one report says August consumption reached a six-month high after the rules were eased (single source, unverified). The Swiggy Instamart–HPCL delivery pilot of 15 July 2026 is confirmed by several outlets. The term “Control Order” itself did not turn up in current coverage, so check that wording. Sources: The Week, Business Today. The original text below is unchanged.
Updated 7 November 2026. All three public-sector oil companies now sell a 10 kg composite cylinder. HPCL launched HP Navya on 17 July 2026, BPCL launched Bharatgas Lite ZIP in Mumbai on 18 July and says it reached 100 cities by mid-August, and IOCL launched Indane XTRALITE NOW in Chennai in late July; IOCL’s product page lists its equipment cost at ₹2,700 plus GST (October 2026), a different charge from the ₹3,000 security deposit quoted below for the older Xtralite connection. A trade report says HPCL ordered 1.4 lakh composite cylinders from Time Technoplast for ₹38.14 crore. No company has published a national count of composite cylinders in use. Sources: BPCL press release, 17 August 2026, IOCL, Indane XTRALITE NOW, PSU Connect (search summary).

What it means: Composite cylinders are lighter, rust-free and translucent, which makes them easier to handle and to check for remaining gas.

What to do: Scale composite cylinder output from domestic HDPE, since feedstock cost is what keeps them in limited circulation.

Refiner IOCL already makes polyethylene at scale for its own cylinder

What it means: The same refiner that sells the HDPE-bodied Xtralite is also investing in the capacity that could supply it.

What to do: Track whether the reported 200 KTA project is commissioned, since it would add to the HDPE supply a composite cylinder needs.

Industrial LPG is squeezed while household cylinder deliveries stay large

What it means: A 70% cap on commercial and industrial LPG demand protects household supply while bulk supply is restricted.

What to do: Watch the household channel while the cap is in place, since it is the supply the composite cylinder would serve.

What would fix this

  • IOCL should publish the exact tare weight and the kilograms of HDPE and fibreglass composite in each Xtralite size, so the “about half” claim can be compared with Time Technoplast's published 5.2 kg and the polymer demand from converting steel cylinders can be sized rather than left unquantified.
  • The Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas should report each year how many composite cylinders the public-sector OMCs have in circulation, by company and size, against the 50 crore steel cylinder base, so “limited circulation” becomes a number that can be tracked.
  • PPAC should add composite-cylinder connections and refill deliveries as separate lines in its monthly Industry Consumption Report, so the uptake of Xtralite and HP Navya, including through channels such as the HPCL–Swiggy pilot, can be seen alongside the 13.04 crore monthly cylinder deliveries.
  • The Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas should publish the security deposit and refill price of composite and steel cylinders side by side for domestic and PMUY consumers, so the cost gap its own Rajya Sabha reply names as the barrier can be measured over time.
  • The Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas should commission and publish a household survey that isolates cylinder weight, separately from refill cost and distributor distance, as a reason women need help changing cylinders, filling the evidence gap this post identifies.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

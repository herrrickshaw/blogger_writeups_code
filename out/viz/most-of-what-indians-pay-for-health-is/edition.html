Out-of-pocket health spending in India: 43.4% in 2022-23, mostly outpatient care

Skip to article content
Text Size

Top line

  • 43.4% of India's health spending was paid out of pocket in 2022-23, up from 39.4% a year earlier.
  • ~70% of health spending goes on outpatient care, which PM-JAY does not cover.
  • Rs 0.5–1.1 lakh crore a year: this blog's rough cost of outpatient cover for PM-JAY's 55 crore eligible people.

What it means: Households paid 43.4% of health spending directly in 2022-23, up from 39.4% a year earlier.

What to do: Put government health spending on the committee's time-bound path to 2.5% of GDP.

Households pay more per hospital stay than PM-JAY pays per admission

What it means: Households paid an average of Rs 34,064 out of pocket per hospital stay in 2025, against about Rs 15,300 paid per PM-JAY admission since 2018.

What to do: Extend PM-JAY to outpatient consultations, diagnostics and medicines, as the committee proposes.

Covering outpatient care would cost 2.1 to 4.2 times PM-JAY's hospital claims

What it means: Covering PM-JAY's eligible people for outpatient care would cost Rs 0.5 to 1.1 lakh crore a year, 2.1 to 4.2 times what PM-JAY pays for hospital care.

What to do: Deliver outpatient care through free medicines and tests at public facilities such as Ayushman Arogya Mandirs, rather than a claim for each visit.

What would fix this

  • The National Health Authority should publish an annual split of out-of-pocket spending by beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries of PM-JAY, by outpatient care, medicines, tests and hospital stays, so the scheme’s effect on what families pay is measured rather than assumed.
  • Before any outpatient extension, the Centre should pilot two models in comparable districts, insurance reimbursement and free medicines and diagnostics at public facilities, and publish the cost per person and the change in out-of-pocket spending for each.
  • The National Health Accounts should be published within two years of the year they cover, not three, and should report outpatient and inpatient spending separately.
  • NITI Aayog should update its 2021 “missing middle” estimate now that PM-JAY covers everyone over 70, so the size of the uncovered group is known.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

What this piece could not establish

The committee report, the National Health Accounts and the NSS report could not be opened directly; figures come from the PRS summary, news reports and summaries of those documents. The 70% outpatient share is the committee’s figure as cited in a parliamentary question; the health accounts’ own outpatient and inpatient split was not found. PM-JAY’s eligible population is reported as about 55 crore for the central scheme and 60 crore or more with state extensions; this piece uses 55 crore. The outpatient cost is in 2022-23 prices, ignores any rise in use once care is free, and assumes the poorer half spends between half and all of the national average per person. The average PM-JAY admission and the yearly average are this blog’s division of cumulative figures over eight years.

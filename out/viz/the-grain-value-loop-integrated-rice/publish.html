Updated 15 November 2026: rewritten as charts; figures unchanged.

Edible-oil imports cost ₹1,68,000 cr a year; the food subsidy costs ₹2,05,000 cr

What it means: Edible-oil imports are a large recurring cost, and the post treats domestic processing as the way to recover part of it.

What to do: Redirect the oil spend to domestic processing and jobs, as the post proposes.

Processing could replace ₹21,087 cr of imports a year; ₹11,000 cr is capturable now

What it means: The capturable part comes from rice bran oil, cottonseed oil and soy and sunflower crush, per the post's stream-by-stream estimate.

What to do: Ring-fence the commercial arm and contract the offtake, as the post recommends.

Edible-oil imports (168 LMT) exceed domestic edible oil (122 LMT)

What it means: Byproduct oil competes with imports rather than with food grain, so the post argues it does not compete with people's food supply.

What to do: Build the byproduct oil routes first, as the post's sequencing proposes.

What would fix this

  • The Department of Food and Public Distribution should publish each year the tonnage of bran and husk generated from custom-milled government paddy and what millers are charged for it, so that the ₹7,650 crore byproduct transfer becomes an official figure rather than an estimate.
  • FCI should publish annually the carrying cost of rice and wheat stocks held above buffer norms, so that the roughly ₹18,000 crore cost of excess stock, and the 2–2.4 times buffer-norm rice carry-over, are reported by the holder of the stock.
  • The civil supplies departments of Karnataka, Gujarat, Andhra Pradesh, Maharashtra, Telangana and Kerala should publish per-commodity spending on edible oil and pulses in their PDS, so that the state oil subsidy can be sized beyond Tamil Nadu’s ₹3,800 crore.
  • The Department of Agriculture & Farmers Welfare should publish a single state-wise registry of FPOs formed under all implementing agencies, not SFAC alone, so that siting decisions that match feedstock to farmer aggregation rest on complete counts.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

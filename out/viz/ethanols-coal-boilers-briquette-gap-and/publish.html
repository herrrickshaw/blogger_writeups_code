Not all Indian ethanol distilleries burn fossil fuels — but the ones that do are exactly the ones the sugar-mill story leaves out: standalone grain distilleries with no bagasse to fall back on. India already runs a coal-to-biomass mandate that's the direct precedent for fixing that — and it's currently short by 12–17 million tonnes of biomass pellets a year, penalties and all. Layered on top is a carbon-credit opportunity distilleries can claim today, one they mostly aren't. And America's own ethanol-CCS record, checked against the Global CCS Institute's own numbers, is a useful reality check on how far "credit-eligible" is from "actually built."

What would fix this

  • The Department of Food and Public Distribution should publish a register of ethanol distilleries by feedstock (molasses, cane or grain) and principal boiler fuel, so that how many of India’s ~380 distilleries burn coal or furnace oil is an official figure rather than something no authoritative current count could settle.
  • The Bureau of Energy Efficiency should issue methodologies for fermentation CO₂ capture and for 2G ethanol from crop residue under the Indian carbon market, so that the cheapest industrial capture point and avoided field burning become creditable rather than stranded for lack of a document.
  • The Ministry of New and Renewable Energy should publish how many ethanol distilleries have claimed National Bioenergy Programme assistance for non-bagasse biomass cogeneration, with MW installed and amounts paid, so that uptake of the ₹40 lakh-per-MW offer can be judged against the coal still in use.
  • The Ministry of Power should publish plant-wise biomass co-firing achieved and pellets procured each quarter through the SAMARTH Mission, so that the national shortfall — 2.5 million tonnes produced against 15–20 million tonnes needed — is tracked from official data rather than press reporting on CAQM penalties.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

What this piece does not establish. The exact split of India's ~380 distilleries between molasses/cane-based and standalone grain-based, and what share of grain distilleries specifically use coal versus rice husk or purchased biomass, could not be pinned to one authoritative current count from the sourcing gathered here. The BM IN02.001 methodology reference, the 50 vs 70 gCO₂e/MJ carbon-intensity figures for Indian ethanol specifically, and the claim that no Phase II CCUS methodology has been issued for fermentation-CO₂ capture were not independently retrieved and verified against a primary methodology document by this piece; they are presented as reflecting current sourcing on the topic, not confirmed facts. The $52/tCO₂e capture cost and 58% carbon-intensity reduction figure come from a US, corn-ethanol-specific peer-reviewed study and have not been shown to transfer directly to Indian grain- or molasses-based ethanol production. The Global CCS Institute's public reporting does not itself break out an ethanol-specific project count separate from the broader "biofuel and bioenergy" category, so the ethanol share of the 7 newly-under-construction bioenergy CCS projects is not stated by the source and is not estimated here.

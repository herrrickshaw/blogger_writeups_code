Five official government models were run on the same corn ethanol scenario in 2023 and produced land-use-change emissions estimates ranging from −0.95 to roughly 41 grams of CO₂e per megajoule — a number that, in the real world, decides whether a fuel batch clears a regulatory threshold or not. GCAM was one of the five. But GCAM is not the model that actually issues a RIN, clears a RED III threshold, or prices a CBIO on Brazil's B3 exchange — none of the world's three big biofuel carbon-credit systems run GCAM as their compliance engine. This piece works out what GCAM's real job is in this space, and how the three real systems differ from each other and from it.

Top line

  • −0.95 to ~41 gCO₂e/MJ: the land-use-change range across five models run on the same corn-ethanol scenario.
  • 19% to 32% swing in the land-use-change estimate from land-protection assumptions alone, same model.
  • 50% to 65% GHG savings a transport biofuel must clear under EU RED II/RED III, set by plant start date.
Updated 7 November 2026. Corrected: an earlier version said a biofuel must save 70–80% of greenhouse gases under the EU’s RED III. Those thresholds apply to biomass used for electricity, heating and cooling. For transport biofuels, Article 29(10) of the Renewable Energy Directive requires savings of at least 50% (plants operating by 5 October 2015), 60% (started 6 October 2015 to 2020) or 65% (started from 1 January 2021), and the 2023 revision (RED III) did not change them. The default values are in Annex V of the directive, not Annex IV. Sources: 2BSvs, “RED III and updated specifications” (May 2025); 2BSvs GHG methodology v9, quoting Article 29(10).

What it means: ADAGE's run implies land-use change was a net carbon sink at −0.95 gCO₂e/MJ, while the high end across the five frameworks reached roughly 41 gCO₂e/MJ.

What to do: Check any single land-use-change figure against the full model range before relying on it.

Land-protection choices alone shift the GCAM-T result by 7 to 11 gCO₂e/MJ

What it means: Changing only how much non-commercial land is protected moved the estimate by 7 gCO₂e/MJ (19%) and by 11 gCO₂e/MJ (32%).

What to do: Report the land-protection assumption alongside any GCAM-based carbon-intensity figure.

EU transport biofuels must clear a GHG-savings bar that depends on plant start date

What it means: A transport biofuel either clears the threshold for its plant's start date or it does not, with no traded credit involved.

What to do: Confirm a plant's start date before assuming which GHG-savings threshold applies.

What would fix this

  • The Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas should publish a lifecycle greenhouse-gas methodology for ethanol under the blending programme, stating carbon intensity separately for each feedstock pathway, with land-use change shown as its own component, so the molasses versus sugarcane-juice differences the MAgPIE study found are visible in policy.
  • NITI Aayog should commission an Indian model comparison on the template of EPA's 2023 exercise, running the same ethanol scenarios through GCAM-CEEW, MAgPIE and SAFARI and publishing the spread of land-use-change results, so India knows how much its answer depends on the choice of model.
  • The Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas should require every ethanol land-use estimate it cites or commissions to state its land-availability assumption — how much non-commercial land is protected from conversion — alongside the result, since this post shows that assumption alone moved one model's estimate by 19% to 32%.
  • The Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas should publish the ethanol supplied for blending each year split by feedstock, separating molasses-based from sugarcane-juice-based supply, so the mix that determines whether blending adds or removes land-use emissions, in the MAgPIE study's findings, can be tracked over time.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

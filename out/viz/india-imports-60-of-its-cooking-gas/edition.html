India imports 60 to 67 per cent of the roughly 31 to 33 million tonnes of LPG it burns in kitchens every year, and about 90 per cent of that import volume transits the Strait of Hormuz. That single fact is funding four separate attempts to redesign what comes out of the stove's burner: dimethyl ether blended into the cylinder, hydrogen blended into it or generated inside the appliance itself, and ethanol or methanol gel burned in a dedicated stove. The Bureau of Indian Standards has already reversed course once on how much of one of these fuels a cylinder can legally carry. This piece separates what is actually for sale from what is still a laboratory result, and names the body — LERC, the oil marketing companies' own equipment-research centre — whose single 2025 study did the reversing.

LPG in India: about 60% imported in 2025-26, 1 of 4 redesigned stoves on sale

Skip to article content
Text Size

Top line

  • ~60% of India's 33.2 million tonnes of LPG in 2025-26 was imported, close to 90% of it through the Strait of Hormuz.
  • 8% is now the most DME that BIS allows in an LPG cylinder, cut from 20% by mass.
  • ₹1.05 lakh is the price of the one hydrogen cooktop on sale in India, for a single burner.
Updated 7 November 2026. Consumption and import share now use PPAC’s full-year figures: 33.2 million tonnes consumed in 2025-26 and 13.1 million tonnes produced, so about 60% imported (earlier: a 31–33 million tonne, 60–67% range from secondary sources). Supply disruptions in West Asia cut consumption to 14.74 million tonnes in January–June 2026, from 15.95 million a year earlier (PPAC via Business Today). The ethanol-cookstove section now notes the petroleum ministry’s reported policy framework (July 2026); no scheme has been notified.

Updated 15 November 2026: rewritten as charts; figures unchanged.

About 60% of India's LPG is imported, most of it through Hormuz

What it means: India consumed 33.2 million tonnes of LPG and its refineries made 13.1 million, so about 60 per cent was imported.

What to do: Judge each LPG substitute by how much of the 20.1 million tonne import gap it can actually displace.

India's DME-in-LPG blend ceiling was cut from 20% to 8% in one revision

What it means: BIS notified a 20 per cent DME blend in 2024, then cut it to 8 per cent by mass from 15 May 2026 after a 2025 LERC study found existing seals and hoses not reliably compatible.

What to do: Ask whether any named oil marketing company sells an 8 per cent DME cylinder at retail before counting this as supply.

The only hydrogen cooktop on sale in India costs ₹1.05 lakh for one burner

What it means: The electrolyser sits inside the stove, and the price is set for commercial and institutional buyers rather than mass household use.

What to do: Do not count hydrogen stoves toward LPG replacement until a household price or subsidy is announced.

SIMI's gel stove claims 67.3% efficiency against a 36% industry average

What it means: SIMI says its patented design reaches 67.3 per cent efficiency against a 36 per cent industry average, a figure this piece could not verify.

What to do: Wait for a third-party or BIS-equivalent test before comparing gel stoves with LPG on efficiency.

What would fix this

  • BIS and LERC should publish the 2025 elastomer-compatibility study behind the 20%-to-8% DME cap in full, not just its conclusion. A safety standard that halves its own permitted blend level on one study deserves a public methodology, not a cited-but-unpublished finding circulating through trade press.
  • MoPNG or the OMCs should name a specific city, timeline and cylinder count for the first commercial DME-blended LPG pilot. An 8 per cent standard with no announced rollout leaves "DME is coming" indistinguishable from a standard that may sit unused for years, the way several BIS fuel-blend standards have before.
  • Coverage and government communication should stop using "hydrogen cooking" as a single category. Piped-gas blending, cylinder blending and on-site electrolysis are different technologies at different readiness levels; a reader cannot evaluate any one of them while they are reported as interchangeable.
  • A BIS-equivalent efficiency and safety certification should exist for ethanol/methanol gel cookstoves before they are recommended at any policy level, so a company's own efficiency claim (SIMI's 67.3 per cent, in this piece) is not the only figure a buyer or a policymaker has to go on.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

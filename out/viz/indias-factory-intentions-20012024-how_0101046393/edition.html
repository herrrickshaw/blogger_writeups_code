For 23 years DPIIT published, every month, the name of every large company that told the government it planned a new factory, and of every one that said production had started. Read together, the two lists show that only 15% of the filings made from 2001 to 2018 are cited by a later production report, and 23% if reports from the same company are counted too. Since October 2025 the filings sit on the National Single Window System, and the monthly company lists, last posted for August 2024, have not come back.

Factory plans filed in India, 2001 to 2018: 15% cited by a later production report

Skip to article content
Text Size

Top line

  • 15% of 2001–2018 factory plans filed with DPIIT are cited by a later production report.
  • 45% of plants proposed in Assam reached production, against 10% in Jammu and Kashmir.
  • 36% of filings made in 2016–2018 reached production, up from 19% for 2001–2006 (wider count).

Updated 15 November 2026: rewritten as charts; figures unchanged.

Most factory plans filed with DPIIT never appear as started production

What it means: Filing a plan with DPIIT is not a commitment to build, so most filings leave no production record.

What to do: DPIIT should give every Part A filing a status such as production started, still planned, withdrawn or lapsed.

Filings from later years were more likely to be followed by production

What it means: Later filings show a higher share, but filings from 2020 onwards are understated because the Part B lists stop in August 2024.

What to do: DPIIT should resume the monthly IEM Statistics Report so the series from 2001 is not broken by the move to NSWS.

Plants proposed in Assam were followed through far more often than in Tamil Nadu

What it means: The share that reached production varies widely by the state where the plant was proposed.

What to do: MoSPI should link IEM acknowledgement numbers to the Annual Survey of Industries through GSTIN or CIN, so production can be checked from survey and tax records.

What would fix this

  • DPIIT should publish the NSWS IEM register as an open monthly dataset on data.gov.in, in a machine-readable file: company, plant district and state, NIC code, item, capacity, proposed investment, filing date and acknowledgement number for Part A, and the cited Part A number for each Part B. The PDFs it posted until 2024 already made this information public; NSWS makes it easy to export.
  • DPIIT should give every Part A a status (production started, still planned, withdrawn or lapsed), using the expected commencement date that NSWS now collects, and ask companies to update it once that date passes. Companies can already ask DPIIT to cancel an acknowledgement under its January 2024 procedure, but cancellations are not published, so today a filing that never became a plant looks exactly like one whose owner forgot to file Part B.
  • The National Statistics Office (MoSPI) should link IEM acknowledgement numbers to the Annual Survey of Industries frame through GSTIN or CIN, so that whether an intended plant actually started producing can be checked from survey and tax records instead of from voluntary Part B returns.
  • DPIIT should resume the monthly IEM Statistics Report that stopped after September 2024, with Part A and Part B counts, intended and actual investment by state and industry, so the series that ran from 2001 is not broken by the move to NSWS.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

Sources and caveats

IEM company lists: DPIIT, SIA Statistics, “List of IEM Filed” and “List of IEM Implemented”, January 2001 to August 2024, catalogued from dpiit.gov.in on 7 October 2026. Totals for 2022–2024: DPIIT, IEM Statistics Report, September 2024 issue (data to August 2024). Legal basis: notification S.O. 477(E), 25 July 1991, under the Industries (Development and Regulation) Act, 1951, and DPIIT’s schedule of industries under compulsory licensing. Nature of the acknowledgement and Part B requirement: DPIIT, Frequently Asked Questions on IEM services. Part A contents: NSWS, IEM details page. Thresholds: DPIIT Press Note 1 (2025 Series), 1 April 2025. Cancellation procedure: DPIIT office memorandum of 2 January 2024, as reported by Outlook Business and Fox Mandal. NSWS migration: PIB release 2173538, 1 October 2025, and DPIIT’s closure notice for the G2B portal. Revised thresholds: PIB release 2118292, 3 April 2025, citing Ministry of MSME notification S.O. 1364(E) of 21 March 2025. The register was built by reading every PDF with PyMuPDF (word positions for the fixed-column reports, table cells for the later ruled tables, plain text as a cross-check); the code is tools/iem_records.py in this blog’s source repository. Matching uses the Part A number and year printed in Part B; name-only matches are weaker and are shown separately. A filing with no Part B found may still have been built. Company status and CIN (added 9 October 2026): public company pages on QuickCompany, which republish Ministry of Corporate Affairs company master data, read 7–9 October 2026; the Ministry’s open Company Master Data on data.gov.in (data up to 3 November 2023) was used as a reference for the format. Current company records for filings from 2021 (status, last AGM, last balance sheet): retrieved 10 October 2026 through the Apify actor factden/mca-company-director-scraper, which reads the Ministry’s company master data; it returned between a fifth and a half of the CINs requested per pass, depending on batch size, so coverage is partial. Company identity was also checked against the GLEIF Legal Entity Identifier register, pulled 10 October 2026. A company listed Active may not have built the plant, and one not found may exist under another name.

Top line

  • 298 votes for the 131st Amendment against 352 needed, so the bill fell in the Lok Sabha on 17 April 2026.
  • 50 years since seat allocation was frozen in 1976, and Census 2027 ends that freeze.
  • 3 constitutional amendment bills moved since December 2024, and none has passed.
Updated 7 November 2026. Three open points are now settled. The One Nation One Election committee has not reported: on 29 July 2026 the Lok Sabha extended its deadline to the last week of the 2026 Winter Session (Deccan Herald). The challenge to the 2023 Election Commission appointments law produced a split verdict on 23 September 2026 on whether a larger bench should hear it, and the matter went to the Chief Justice; there is no ruling on the Act itself, and a recall plea is pending (Vision IAS summary of The Indian Express; LiveLaw). The Lok Sabha Deputy Speaker post is still vacant; opposition MPs raised it again in March 2026 (India Today NE). The text and scorecard have been updated.

What it means: The bill would have enlarged the Lok Sabha to 850 seats and brought women's reservation forward, but it was defeated.

What to do: Parliament must agree a seat formula before Census 2027 unfreezes allocation, so the next bill has to win the support the 131st lacked.

Governors: courts dropped their own timelines, so only Parliament can set them

  1. 1988Sarkaria Commission asks for time limits on Governors' assent to bills
  2. April 2025Supreme Court sets timelines in the Tamil Nadu case
  3. 20 November 2025Presidential Reference opinion: no judicially imposed timelines, no deemed assent

What it means: Governors' assent has been recommended for reform since 1988, and the November 2025 opinion left no court-set deadline.

What to do: A fixed timeline for assent under Article 200 needs a constitutional amendment, so Parliament must draft one.

Two bills in committee have had their deadlines extended into the Winter Session

  1. December 2024One Nation One Election bill (129th) introduced by 269 votes to 196 and sent to a JPC
  2. July 2026JPC on the 130th Amendment defers adopting its draft report
  3. 29 July 2026Lok Sabha extends the 129th Bill's deadline to the first day of the last week of Winter Session
  4. 31 July 2026Lok Sabha extends the 130th Bill's deadline to the last week of Winter Session

What it means: The One Nation One Election and 130th Amendment bills are still with joint committees, and neither has reported.

What to do: Both committees need to table reports by the Winter Session deadline, since a third extension would leave the bills unresolved.

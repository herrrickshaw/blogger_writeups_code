Top line

  • ~43% of India's ~US$37 bn textile and apparel exports is apparel (HS Ch 61 and 62), the single largest category.
  • ~33% of the Apr–Oct FY25 export basket was cotton textiles, against ~15% for man-made textiles.
Updated 7 November 2026. FY2025-26 totals are now in. Textile exports including handicrafts rose 2.1% to ₹3,16,334.9 crore (Ministry of Textiles, via All India Radio). Apparel has weakened this year: in April–August 2026 apparel exports fell 9.10% in dollar terms while textile exports rose 6.94%, leaving the combined basket 0.24% lower (CITI, September 2026). Technical textiles ran a ₹3,420.20 crore trade surplus in FY2025-26 (exports ₹29,217.60 crore, imports ₹25,797.40 crore). The FY2024-25 chapter figures below are unchanged.

What it means: Apparel is the single largest textile and apparel category, and it is built on cotton-skewed woven and knitted lines.

What to do: Track the US and EU export shares, which the post puts at 28.5% and 19.6%, as the main tariff exposure for FY26.

Cotton textiles hold a larger export share than man-made textiles, about 33% to 15%

What it means: Cotton textiles were ~33% of the Apr–Oct FY25 export basket against ~15% for man-made textiles, even though man-made fibres are about 70% of fibre consumption.

What to do: Track the synthetic-fibre lines the post lists, such as 610323, 610333 and 610343, as the place where India's cotton skew shows up.

Technical textiles are 12–15% of India's value chain, against ~50% in advanced economies

What it means: India holds only 4–5% of the global technical-textiles market, and the post ties the gap to specialty fibres and technology it must import.

What to do: Judge the NTTM and PLI schemes by whether they close the specialty-fibre import gap in the high-value segments.

What would fix this

  • The Ministry of Textiles should publish annual exports and imports for each of the 12 technical-textile segments, built from the 207 notified HSN codes and the 32 added later, so that segment-level surplus and deficit is reported officially rather than compiled from export councils.
  • DGCI&S should publish apparel exports from Chapters 61 and 62 split by fibre — cotton, man-made and other — as a standard annual table, so that India’s cotton skew against a world where man-made fibre is about 70% of consumption is measured directly.
  • The Ministry of Textiles should publish import volumes and prices for the 13 synthetic knitted-fabric codes under the Minimum Import Price before and after its introduction, so that the curb’s effect on upstream MMF fabric dependence can be judged.
  • The Ministry of Textiles should report domestic production capacity for aramid, carbon, high-tenacity and technical nylon fibres each year under the National Technical Textiles Mission, so that the specialty-fibre constraint behind the Meditech, Protech and Geotech deficits is tracked.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

Top line

  • 119 plants are authorised to sell bio-manure in bulk, built up over seven Gazette notifications in 27 months.
  • 13% was the national utilisation of rated CBG capacity in FY2024-25, against the 70-85% the situation report calls sound.
  • ₹1,500 per MT is the MDA subsidy on FOM/LFOM sold, and the post argues plant economics rest on that leg.
Updated 2 November 2026. The SATAT ex-plant price of ₹54 a kilogram used below, and its comparison with spot RLNG at about ₹45, could not be traced to an official source; the last official per-kilogram figure this blog found is ₹77.4, including ₹8 for compression, for 2025. Since September 2026 the GOBARdhan scheme sets an administered price of ₹2,110 per MMBtu, excluding taxes and compression, held to 31 March 2036 with a yearly review; that is about ₹98 a kilogram at 95% methane (the August announcement converted it to ₹105). It replaced ₹1,478 per MMBtu. So the price comparison below is out of date, though the point that fertiliser and carbon-credit revenue matter to plant viability still stands. Updated 28 October 2026. The 13 October note below has been checked against the Gazette. The Fertiliser (Inorganic, Organic or Mixed) (Control) Seventh Amendment Order, 2026 (S.O. 3482(E), dated 29 June 2026; Gazette of India Extraordinary CG-DL-E-03072026-274068) does what was reported: makers of Fermented Organic Manure and Liquid Fermented Organic Manure need no clause 8(3) authorisation letter for ten years from the Order’s publication, and “and Bio Gas plants” is added after “Compressed Bio Gas plants” in the definitions and in the Schedule heading for organic carbon enhancer. No Eighth Amendment Order affecting this waiver was found; the Ninth Amendment Order of 22 July 2026 (S.O. 4045(E), CG-DL-E-24072026-274783) changes only the dealer provisions of clause 8(4). A new, eighth clause 22(c) list was found: S.O. 4123(E) of 27 July 2026 (CG-DL-E-29072026-274927) authorises 8 more plants to sell in bulk for three years, in Karnataka (Kodagu; Bengaluru Urban), Rajasthan (Hanumangarh), Gujarat (Tapi; Surat), Punjab (Fazilka) and Uttar Pradesh (Mathura; Mirzapur), taking the total to 127. The S.O. numbers for notifications V, VI and VII below match the Gazette. These were read in the Gazette of India’s own text via the SansadSaar index of the archive.org Gazette of India collection.
Updated 13 October 2026. The three-year waiver of the clause 8(3) authorisation letter for FOM/LFOM manufacturers, described below, has reportedly been replaced. A Seventh Amendment Order of 29 June 2026 (S.O. 3482(E)) is reported to exempt them from the letter for ten years from publication, and to add plain biogas plants alongside compressed biogas plants in the definitions; the three-year waiver would otherwise have run out around 17 May 2026. The Gazette text has since been read; see the 28 October note above. The “before November 2026” renewal question for the 27 named plants may also be affected. Sources: TeamLease RegTech, Simpliance. What it means: Plant siting follows agricultural and dairy feedstock, which leaves the bio-fertiliser base concentrated in a few states.

What to do: Route the bulk-sale programme toward the states that have the plants and the degraded land, not only the largest feedstock pools.

Only 13% of rated CBG capacity was used, so running plants is the bottleneck

What it means: The report puts the gap down to execution and utilisation, not demand, since the CBG blending obligation guarantees a buyer.

What to do: Fund the plant-running gap first, since the post's own sources say offtake is not the bottleneck.

What would fix this

  • The Department of Agriculture & Farmers Welfare should publish, before November 2026, whether the 27 plants named in Notification I will be renewed for bulk FOM/LFOM sale, so that the first three-year authorisations do not lapse silently plant by plant.
  • ICFRE should publish on the Green Credit registry the methodology that converts a registered plantation activity into a number of green credits, so that participants and observers can check how credits are issued rather than relying on a legal framework with no published formula.
  • The Department of Fertilizers should publish Market Development Assistance claims paid, claims rejected after State sample checks, and average payment time, plant by plant, each quarter, so that delays in the ₹1,500/MT payment that underpins CBG plant viability are visible.
  • The Bureau of Energy Efficiency should state in a published methodology whether CBG projects qualify under the CCTS Offset Mechanism, and under which Phase I category, so that the carbon-credit leg of plant economics rests on a rule rather than an assumption.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

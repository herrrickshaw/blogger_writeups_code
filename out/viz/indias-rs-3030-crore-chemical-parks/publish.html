Top line

  • 93% of units in India's three petrochemical investment regions are in Dahej.
  • 6% of the bulk-drug parks scheme's Rs 3,000 crore had been released after nearly six years.
  • $22.8 bn of plastic raw-material imports in 2025-26, while chemicals as a whole run a small surplus.
Updated 7 November 2026. The Department of Chemicals and Petrochemicals has invited all states to apply under the scheme guidelines, with park proposals due by 30 November 2026 (Indian Chemical News; IMPRI). No state has yet been reported as applying, and the official guidelines document was not read for this update.

Updated 15 November 2026: rewritten as charts; figures unchanged.

The largest single import lines are polymers and their petrochemical inputs

What it means: Plastics are where India's chemical trade runs a deficit, while chemicals as a whole are roughly in balance.

What to do: Judge the new scheme by whether its shared utilities reach these polymer and intermediate lines, not by its park count.

Dahej holds about 93% of the units across India's three petrochemical regions

What it means: The 2007 regions mostly did not fill, with Dahej, beside an existing chemical belt, carrying almost all the units.

What to do: Before committing new parks, the government should report unit and investment counts by region, so the gap between plan and outcome is visible.

Only Rs 180 crore of the bulk-drug parks' Rs 3,000 crore had been released

What it means: The scheme that BHAVYA Rasayan copies spent only a small share of its money in its first five years.

What to do: Publish release and utilisation figures for the bulk-drug parks before the new Rs 3,030 crore scheme is approved park by park.

What would fix this

  • The Department of Chemicals and Petrochemicals should publish, for each BHAVYA Rasayan park, a quarterly report of land handed over, utilities commissioned, plots allotted, investment committed and units producing, starting with the challenge-round scores of every state that applied.
  • The challenge round should give weight to sites inside or next to existing chemical clusters and notified PCPIR land, where Dahej’s record shows demand already exists, over greenfield sites.
  • Release of central money should be tied to anchor tenants signed, not only to construction milestones.
  • The scheme should name the HS lines it expects the parks to serve, so that their effect on imports and exports can be tracked against DGCI&S data.
  • The government should report realised, not committed, investment for the PCPIRs, region by region, since it told Parliament there is no monitoring dashboard.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

What this piece could not establish

The Cabinet release, the PCPIR status table and the parliamentary replies could not be opened directly; figures come from their text as indexed by search engines and from news reports (ANI, PTI, DeshGujarat). The split of PCPIR investment, units and jobs between Visakhapatnam-Kakinada and Paradeep was not published; the combined figures are this blog’s subtraction of Dahej from the total. The 2007 targets come from later government statements, not the original policy text. The plastics comparison mixes a 2025-26 import figure for raw materials with a 2024-25 export figure that includes finished products, so it shows the size of the gap, not its exact value. The challenge round’s deadlines, criteria and applicants had not been published by 27 October 2026.

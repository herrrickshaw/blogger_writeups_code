Customer support, application maintenance and labour-billed back-office work are being hit first; airline back office and AI data work are still arriving. The common pattern is fewer fresher hires and lower prices, not layoffs, with India taking a larger share of a smaller pool. Meanwhile courts, claims and grievance systems at home carry backlogs of the same work.

AI and India's offshore services: fewer fresher hires and lower prices rather than layoffs

Skip to article content
Text Size

Top line

  • +0.4% growth in India's BPO exports in FY26, against +11% for computer services.
  • 9,000 to 5,000 Salesforce's own support staff after its AI agents took on about half of conversations.
Updated 7 November 2026. Results reported since this piece was compiled point the same way. EXL’s third-quarter 2026 revenue rose 19.7% to $634.0 million while its Digital Operations line fell another 1.7%; management says the work is moving into its Data and AI-led line, which grew 36% and is now almost two-thirds of revenue, and iMerit added $15.3 million in its first two months (EXL release, 27 October 2026). Genpact’s third-quarter revenue rose 7.3% to $1.385 billion: its Advanced Technology Solutions grew 30.3% while Core Business Services, the larger part, was flat (Genpact release); on its second-quarter call it said revenue not priced per person had passed 50% for the first time, up from the 48% used below (Nasdaq call summary). In July–September the five largest Indian IT firms added about 13,900 people net: HCLTech 6,450, Tech Mahindra 5,446, Infosys 4,480 and TCS 4,258, while Wipro shed 6,716; TCS onboarded about 10,000 freshers and Infosys over 8,000, and Infosys kept its 20,000 target (People Matters, from company results). Hiring has not stopped; the slowdown described below is in its pace and mix.

Updated 15 November 2026: rewritten as charts; figures unchanged.

Clients are cutting their own support staff, and Salesforce's fell from 9,000 to 5,000

What it means: Clients are cutting their own support staff, while Concentrix and TTEC show vendor revenue shrinking too.

What to do: Track Indian contact-centre headcount against vendor revenue, since the post's evidence shows the two decoupling.

Computer services grew 11.1% in FY26 while BPO was almost flat at 0.4%

What it means: The pattern fits AI pressure on voice and back-office work, though currency and pricing could explain part of it.

What to do: Compare BPO growth with computer services in the next RBI survey before reading the gap as an AI effect.

The Philippines cut its 2028 outsourcing jobs target, the only hub to do so

What it means: IBPAP cut the target and says AI weakens the link between revenue and entry-level jobs, though the sector still added about 80,000 jobs in 2025.

What to do: India should publish and revise its own export-workforce forecast, as IBPAP did, since NASSCOM has no equivalent yet.

What would fix this

  • MeitY and NASSCOM should publish an annual headcount split by service line (customer support, finance and accounting, testing, infrastructure, data labelling), so that the effect of AI can be measured instead of guessed.
  • The labour ministry should add an occupation-and-age table for IT and business services to the Periodic Labour Force Survey, so that India can run the kind of analysis that found the youth decline in Korea and the United States.
  • The Centre and states should tender court translation, claims review, land-record reconciliation and grievance handling as outcome-priced managed services in Indian languages, with human review, the way foreign banks and insurers buy them from Indian firms, and the way the tax department bought its processing centre.
  • A larger share of the e-Courts budget should go to translation and transcription with human post-editing, starting with High Court judgments.
  • Link these contracts to apprenticeships, so that public work replaces some of the fresher hiring that the export firms are cutting.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

A Japanese startup ran a rocket engine on liquefied cattle manure in 2023 — not a stunt, but 99%-pure methane that matched rocket-grade propellant. India spent early 2026 testing its own methane engines, from ISRO's national programme down to a private Gujarat startup that built one on biomethane specifically. The two threads haven't been tied together yet. This piece lays out why India's existing, underperforming biogas scheme is the raw material for exactly that.

Top line

  • 99%+ purity of liquid biomethane that Japan's IST fired in a rocket engine in December 2023.
  • 200+ CBG plants commissioned against India's 5,000-plant SATAT target.
  • ₹2,110 per MMBTU administered CBG price that GOBARdhan guarantees for a minimum 10 years.
Updated 7 November 2026. Two of the programmes here have moved on. Interstellar Technologies ran a 60-second static fire of its ZERO engine in April 2026 (Nikkei, 28 May 2026); ZERO has still not flown, its Hokkaido launch complex is due for completion in the first quarter of 2027, and in November 2026 JAXA’s Space Strategic Fund selected it for up to ¥12 billion to fund flights from the second one on. ISRO says its LM20 LOX-methane technology demonstrator engine has completed one cold-flow and five hot tests, the first full-scale gas-generator ignition for its larger LME1100 engine followed on 9 October 2026, and the integrated engine is targeted for mid-2027. ISRO’s programme still plans to run on LNG-derived methane; OMSPACE remains the only Indian biomethane engine found.

Updated 15 November 2026: rewritten as charts; figures unchanged.

India has commissioned 200+ of the 5,000 CBG plants it targeted

What it means: Over 200 CBG plants were commissioned by 6 August 2026, against a target of 5,000.

What to do: Planners should treat the scheme's guaranteed price floor as the basis for new plant investment.

Rocket-grade liquid methane is 99% pure, against 90% under IS 16087

What it means: The methane purity that rocket fuel needs is above the minimum set for city-gas and vehicle biomethane.

What to do: Any plant aiming at rocket-grade output should plan an extra liquefaction and purification step beyond CBG grade.

The CNG/PNG blending obligation rises from 3% to 5% by FY2028-29

What it means: The offtake obligation grows each year, giving CBG producers a rising market for vehicle fuel.

What to do: Producers should match plant output to the blending schedule before committing to new capacity.

What would fix this

  • The Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas should state in the GOBARdhan operational guidelines whether capital assistance of up to ₹2 crore per TPD can fund purification and liquefaction to liquid biomethane sold outside the vehicle-fuel and city-gas market, so eligibility for an aerospace-grade step is settled in writing rather than inferred.
  • The Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas should publish a quarterly, plant-wise register of commissioned CBG plants under GOBARdhan, with capacity, feedstock and output grade, so progress from the 200-plus plants commissioned towards the 5,000-plant target, and any plant producing above IS 16087 grade, can be tracked.
  • The Bureau of Indian Standards should publish a free summary of the limits changed in the 2025 revision of IS 16087 against the 2013 Table 1, so producers and buyers can see the current methane, CO2, moisture and H2S tolerances without buying the standard.
  • The Bureau of Indian Standards, with ISRO, should open a work item for a liquefied-biomethane grade at 99% methane or above, with separate limits for CO2, moisture and H2S, since no internationally agreed propellant-grade methane standard exists and each launch programme now sets its own tolerance.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

What this piece could not establish
What this article does not establish. Where OMSPACE currently sources the biomethane for its engine, and whether it comes from any SATAT/GOBARdhan-registered CBG plant — not found in this research. When IST's ZERO will make its first orbital flight — as of November 2026 it had not flown, after a 60-second engine static fire in April 2026; the launch site is due for completion in early 2027. Whether GOBARdhan's capital-assistance or Challenge Fund components have ever been used, or are intended, for aerospace-grade purification specifically — not stated anywhere in the scheme announcement; the aerospace angle is this article's own connection of two otherwise unrelated government/industry facts. Whether the purification cost of taking CBG-grade biogas to rocket-grade liquid biomethane has been costed anywhere publicly for an Indian plant — not located; this article treats it as a real but unquantified gap. Whether Air Water's Obihiro plant specifically uses a membrane stage ahead of its cryogenic liquefaction step, as this article's Exhibit 2 note describes as the general industry pattern, or a different upgrading technology — not confirmed for that specific plant; the two-step membrane-then-flash sequence described here is this article's synthesis of standard biogas-upgrading industry practice, not a verified description of IST/Air Water's actual process flow. The exact 2025-revision figures in IS 16087 — this article verified the 2013 edition's Table 1 directly and could not confirm whether the 2025 revision changed any individual limit.

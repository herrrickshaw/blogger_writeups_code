Delhi's air spent 87 of the last 151 days in the "very poor" category, and the interventions getting attention right now are a smog tower an official committee called a "costly experiment with limited utility," four brand-new street purifiers in Jaipur that face the same physics problem, an untested algae bioreactor from a Kerala startup, and a wave of Army-linked tree-plantation photo-ops. This piece checks each of those against what independent assessment actually found, and separates two very different things both marketed under "the Army is greening Delhi": a one-day plantation ceremony, and a 44-year-old ecological battalion with a tracked, decades-long survival rate. Several sources behind the Central Ridge and CERCA sections could not be fetched directly during research; that is flagged where it matters.

Delhi's smog towers and purifiers: almost no measured effect at city scale

Skip to article content
Text Size

Top line

  • 105 of 151 winter days in Delhi, 2025–26, had severe or very poor air.
  • 48–56% efficiency measured near the Connaught Place smog tower, at most 17% within 100 metres.
  • 163µg/m³ average PM2.5 across the season, against India's annual standard of 40µg/m³.
Updated 7 November 2026. The winter 2025–26 figures below remain this piece’s baseline. The new season began better: Delhi’s average AQI for October 2026 was 166, the lowest October in eight years (223 in October 2025), and the city logged 220 “good to moderate” days from January to October, per the Commission for Air Quality Management; CREA attributes part of that to an unusually wet October. Air quality then turned “very poor” in early November ahead of Diwali, with AQI above 400 at more than 35 of 59 stations on 6 November. Delhi’s civic “Ek Ped Maa Ke Naam” drive is now reported at 71 lakh saplings against its 70-lakh target (updated in the table below); survival counts are still not published.

Updated 15 November 2026: rewritten as charts; figures unchanged.

Delhi's 2025–26 winter: severe or very poor air on 105 of 151 days

What it means: The season ran for five months, far longer than the few bad days around Diwali.

What to do: Judge every intervention against a full season of severe days, not a handful of bad ones.

Connaught Place smog tower: 48–56% efficiency near it, at most 17% by 100 m

What it means: Both independent assessments found the benefit confined to a small radius around the tower.

What to do: Require an independent efficiency study before any further street purifier is installed, as Jaipur has none yet.

Only AlgaeTree among three algae ventures has a named public deployment

  1. 1 May 2026Solar-assisted microalgae unit inaugurated at Swami Vivekananda Park, Bhopal
  2. July 2026Expanded to IOCL's Gujarat Refinery in Vadodara and Amanora Park Town, Maharashtra

What it means: All three ventures' CO2-capture figures are company claims, with no independent measurement found.

What to do: Ask for independent capture measurements under Delhi winter conditions before any deployment.

What would fix this

  • The Rajasthan State Pollution Control Board should commission and publish an independent efficiency study of Jaipur's four street-level purifiers, measuring PM2.5 at set distances as IIT Bombay and CSIR-NEERI did in Connaught Place, before any further units are bought.
  • The Central Pollution Control Board should issue a standard field-testing protocol for outdoor air-purification devices, including towers and algae bioreactors, and require publicly funded installations to publish results under it within a year, so company claims are replaced by comparable measurements.
  • The Ministry of Defence should publish a consolidated Ecological Task Force dataset by battalion and site, including Bhatti mines and Dera Mandi, giving saplings planted, area treated and measured survival, so the 70–80 per cent survival figure rests on an official record rather than secondary press.
  • The Delhi Forest Department should publish site-wise survival counts for saplings planted under the city's “Ek Ped Maa Ke Naam” drive, checked one and three years after planting, so progress towards the 70-lakh target is measured in living trees, not saplings distributed.
  • The Delhi Development Authority should publish a site-wise compliance report on the Supreme Court's 185-acre afforestation order and its five-saplings-per-tree condition, with survival counts, so compensatory planting on the Ridge can be checked against what was felled.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

What this piece does not establish. This piece's research could not directly fetch five sources central to Sections 5 and 6 — Scroll.in's and Down To Earth's Central Ridge reporting, IIT Delhi's own CERCA website, Zaara Biotech's own product page for Obelia, and Carbelim's own site — all returned blocked-egress errors on direct fetch; each account above is reconstructed from search-engine summaries of that reporting or company material, not a direct read of the source itself. All three algae-bioreactor performance figures in Section 6 (Zaara Biotech, Carbelim and AlgaeTree) are company-stated claims, not measurements from a peer-reviewed study, an independent lab, or a government monitoring body; AlgaeTree's Bhopal Smart City deployment and its subsequent expansion to an IOCL refinery and a Maharashtra township are real, dated, named public events confirmed by multiple outlets, but the deployment itself confirms the units exist and were installed, not that the claimed CO2-capture rate holds in practice, and no third-party measurement of that rate at any of the three sites was located for this piece. The vertical-garden particulate- and greenhouse-gas-removal figures circulating in advocacy and lifestyle coverage could not be traced to a peer-reviewed or government study and are presented above explicitly as unverified, claimed figures. The Territorial Army's Ecological Task Force survival-rate and scale figures (6 crore saplings, ~70,000 hectares, 70–80% survival) are as reported by secondary press citing the Territorial Army itself; this piece did not locate a single official, itemized, publicly available Territorial Army dataset that would allow independent verification of those national totals, and no separate, site-specific survival figure was found for the Bhatti mines or Dera Mandi rehabilitation specifically. This piece takes no position on whether the Central Ridge felling described in Section 5 was lawful; it describes what has been reported, not a legal conclusion. Nothing here is a recommendation to invest in, or against, any company or technology named above, including Zaara Biotech, Carbelim, AlgaeTree/Mushroom World Group, NanoClean Global or ShudhVayu Innovations. Update, 23 September 2026: this piece originally described the Delhi High Court's contempt notice over ~1,000 Ridge trees felled (for a road near the South Asian University; 400 trees on DDA land, 700 on forest land) as an open matter alongside a Supreme Court "detailed inquiry." Follow-up research found the matter had already progressed well beyond that stage by the time this piece was first published: the Supreme Court held DDA officials in contempt in May 2025, fined them ₹25,000 each, and ordered afforestation across 185 acres of identified land, with the compliance timeline later extended to 31 March 2026; a further 2026 order allowed DDA's own application to fell additional trees on the Ridge, conditioned on planting five saplings for every tree cut. Section 5 and the table above have been corrected accordingly. None of this changes this piece's core argument — that visible plantation activity on the Ridge does not by itself establish improving ecology — but the legal status of the specific felling episode was materially more resolved than originally described.

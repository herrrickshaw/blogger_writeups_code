If dollar-priced oil and gold are the mechanism by which global inflation lands on India's import bill, the obvious next question is whether settling those trades in rupees instead of dollars would soften the blow. The honest answer, checked against what's actually running today — real Vostro-account infrastructure, a real oil deal already settled in rupees, and a real accumulated-rupee problem with Russia that still isn't solved — is: it helps with a real but different cost, and it does almost nothing about the one this series has been describing.

What to do: Publish the exposure of the rupee surplus held by Russian entities so the treasury-bill parking is visible to the public.

India runs a deficit with four of its five largest partners, not the US

What it means: Four of the five partners are deficit partners, so the rupee has little natural demand from exporters in those countries.

What to do: Set out how the rupee settlement mechanism would generate demand for rupees from partners India runs deficits with.

The rouble pair is far more volatile than the dollar-linked Gulf pairs

What it means: The riyal and dirham are pegged to the dollar, so settling in them removes almost none of the exchange-rate exposure.

What to do: Publish an official direct INR reference rate for the yuan, rouble and riyal, since FBIL publishes none for them.

What would fix this

  • The RBI should publish a monthly table of trade settled through Special Rupee Vostro Accounts by partner country, with its share of India’s total exports and imports, so figures like ₹14,000 crore in February 2026 can be read against the $775.7 billion import bill rather than in isolation.
  • The RBI should report each quarter the aggregate holdings of government securities funded from SRVA balances, by partner country where disclosure permits, so the size of Russia’s accumulated rupee surplus is an official number rather than a “tens of billions” characterisation from secondary reporting.
  • FBIL should add daily INR reference rates for the Chinese yuan, the Russian rouble and the Saudi riyal to the six currencies it now covers, so three of India’s five largest import sources have an official benchmark rather than aggregator-sourced cross-rates through the dollar.
  • DGCI&S should add invoicing currency as a field in TradeStat’s country-wise and commodity-wise reports, so the share of crude, gold and other imports invoiced in rupees, dirhams or dollars can be tracked partner by partner from the official trade series.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

What this piece could not establish
What this piece does not establish. No source found here quantifies what share of India's total oil or gold import cost is currently settled in rupees versus dollars in aggregate — the ₹14,000 crore February 2026 figure is a real, reported number but this piece has not converted it into a percentage of India's total trade to gauge exactly how marginal or significant it currently is. Nor does this piece have a reliable, sourced figure for the total accumulated Russian rupee surplus currently held in Indian treasury instruments, only the reported "tens of billions annually" characterisation from secondary reporting.

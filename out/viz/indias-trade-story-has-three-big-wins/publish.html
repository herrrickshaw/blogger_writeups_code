A record $825 billion in exports, a landmark EU trade deal, a PLI scheme that overshot its own investment target by 26%. Every headline number is real. The gap sits underneath them: in the first half of this fiscal year, merchandise exports grew 2.4% while services grew 6.5% — and almost every big new scheme this piece covers is aimed at the slower-growing half.

India's exports, first half of the fiscal year: goods up 2.4%, services up 6.5%

Skip to article content
Text Size

Top line

  • 26% more investment than the PLI scheme's approved outlay: ₹2.40 lakh crore against ₹1.91 lakh crore.
  • 6.5% services export growth in H1 FY26, against 2.4% for merchandise exports.
  • 90.7% of India's export value gets immediate duty elimination under the India-EU FTA.
Updated 22 October 2026. Full-year figures for 2025-26, already published when this piece went up, have replaced the half-year figures it uses; its "first half of this fiscal year" refers to 2025-26. Per Commerce Ministry data as reported, total goods and services exports reached a record $863.1bn in 2025-26, up 4.6% from $825.3bn. Merchandise exports grew just 0.9%, to $441.8bn, while services grew 8.7%, to $421.3bn (News on AIR, ETV Bharat). The gap this piece describes widened over the full year. The original text below is unchanged.
Updated 7 November 2026. The first half of 2026-27 runs the other way from the pattern this piece describes. Commerce Ministry estimates for April–September 2026 put total exports at $476.20bn, up 13.8%: merchandise $259.89bn, up 18.5%, and services $216.31bn, up about 8.7%. For now goods, not services, are leading export growth, helped by petroleum, electronics and engineering goods, while imports also rose about 16%. Whether that lasts beyond the oil-price spike is open. The India–EU agreement is reported to be due for signing in mid-December 2026. Sources: Investment Guru India, Business Today.

What it means: The PLI scheme has attracted ₹2.40 lakh crore of investment against an approved outlay of ₹1.91 lakh crore, and its three biggest sectors by investment are all goods-manufacturing categories.

What to do: Compare the PLI and Export Promotion Mission results against a services-side instrument before treating goods support as the default.

Services exports outgrew goods in H1 FY26, 6.5% against 2.4%

What it means: Services exports grew 6.5% in H1 FY26 while merchandise exports grew only 2.4% despite higher US tariffs, so the growth is in services even though the policy support is aimed at goods.

What to do: Look for a services-specific export instrument of comparable scale to the Export Promotion Mission, which this piece did not find.

The India-EU FTA gives immediate duty elimination on 90.7% of India's export value

What it means: The FTA covers 99.5% of India's exports by value and eliminates duty immediately on 90.7%, concentrated in textiles, leather, footwear, marine products and gems and jewellery.

What to do: Track how much of the immediate duty elimination reaches the smaller exporters the Export Promotion Mission targets.

What would fix this

  • The Department of Commerce should publish a consolidated annual statement of all services-export support measures and their outlay, set beside the Export Promotion Mission and PLI, so that whether services lack an instrument of comparable scale is answered by government rather than left as an open question.
  • The Department of Commerce should publish a sector-by-sector summary of India’s services commitments and gains under the India–EU FTA, in the same detail as the goods tariff-line schedule, so that the agreement’s services provisions can be assessed alongside the 97% of tariff lines covered for goods.
  • The Directorate General of Foreign Trade should publish an Export Promotion Mission dashboard showing first-time exporters, district-wise support and NIRYAT PROTSAHAN disbursements each quarter, so that an outcome-linked ₹25,060 crore programme reports outcomes that can be checked year by year.
  • The Department for Promotion of Industry and Internal Trade should publish PLI cumulative investment, jobs and exports by sector as an official dataset with a stated definition of “exports enabled”, so that the ₹15.2 lakh crore figure does not reach readers only through press compilations.
  • The Department of Commerce should publish an estimate of the effect of higher US tariffs on merchandise exports by HS chapter, so that the 2.4% H1 FY26 goods growth can be split between tariff drag and broader demand softness, which this piece could not do.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

What this piece could not establish
What this article does not establish. Whether a dedicated services-export promotion scheme exists at comparable scale to the three goods-focused instruments covered here — not found in this research, but absence of evidence is not evidence of absence. Whether the 2.4% merchandise growth figure reflects US-tariff drag specifically or broader global demand softness — the Economic Survey's own framing credits tariffs, but this article did not decompose the effect. And whether the EU FTA's services provisions (which exist alongside the goods tariff schedule detailed above) are comparably ambitious to its goods provisions — not examined in this piece, which focused on the tariff-line data available.

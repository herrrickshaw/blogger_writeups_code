Updated 15 November 2026: rewritten as charts; figures unchanged.

Maize is cheapest in the central states and dearest in the south

What it means: Madhya Pradesh and Rajasthan sold maize Rs 119 to Rs 131 below the all-India price in October 2026, while Tamil Nadu paid Rs 379 more.

What to do: Buy maize from the central states at the time of their harvest, rather than from the dearer southern markets.

Maize's cheap season moves with each harvest; rice stays in a narrow band

What it means: Maize's seasonal price swing is 12% in Uttar Pradesh, 30% in Maharashtra and 45% in Punjab, against 5 to 9% for rice.

What to do: Time purchases to each state's cheap month, which runs from Tamil Nadu in January to Punjab and Uttar Pradesh in June and the kharif belt in October.

Holding maize or rice for six months lost money in all 20 state-and-grain pairs

What it means: Across the maize states shown, the average six-month carry result was a loss of Rs 135 to Rs 212 a quintal.

What to do: Stockpile only in a genuine glut, when a state's price is 15% or more below its 12-month average.

All-India maize price index ranges from 0.95 in October to 1.04 in August

What it means: The all-India index is lowest at 0.95 in October, when the monsoon crop arrives, and highest at 1.04 in August, over the 2019 to 2025 full years.

What to do: Buy in October and November, when the monsoon crop arrives, rather than storing grain through the year.

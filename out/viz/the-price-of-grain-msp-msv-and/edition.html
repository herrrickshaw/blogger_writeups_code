Top line

  • 36.3% of the rice harvest is procured at MSP; wheat reaches 22.6%, and no other crop passes 14.8%.
  • 109% year-to-year swing in tur procurement, against 24% for rice.
  • 29% below its MSP is the landed price of imported tur (CIF ₹5,659 vs ₹8,000).
Updated 7 November 2026. Kharif marketing season 2026-27 began on 1 October, so the paddy MSP in force is now ₹2,441 a quintal for common paddy (₹2,461 for Grade A), up ₹72 from the ₹2,369 used in the tables below; the Cabinet approved it on 13 May 2026 (Devdiscourse, S&P Global). On this piece’s own conversion (÷ 0.67 milling outturn) that is about ₹3,643 a quintal in rice terms, against ₹3,536. The paddy procurement target for the season is 708.64 lakh tonnes, reported as the lowest in six years, and the permitted share of broken grains in procured raw rice has been cut from 25% to 10% (5% for parboiled, from 16%) (Rural Voice, Agro & Food Processing; search summaries). The tables have not been redrawn.
Updated 27 October 2026. The 9 October note below repeated a press description of the wheat MSP rise for 2027-28 as “the smallest percentage rise in 17 years”. That could not be verified against the official MSP series, and the note now says so. All MSP levels, the ₹1,264 cost of production and the procurement figures were confirmed against PIB.

What it means: Rice and wheat are the only crops where the MSP promise has a standing buyer, so the other MSP crops are largely promises without a purchaser.

What to do: Publish a per-crop volume ratio before sowing for the crops with no standing procurement, starting with the grain book's buy rows.

Rice and wheat procurement swings least; tur, bajra and maize swing most

What it means: A purchase guarantee whose volume swings by more than 100% a year gives a farmer no reliable basis for choosing what to sow.

What to do: Announce each crop's purchase volume before sowing, so that the guarantee holds in a bad year as well as a good one.

Tur imports land 29% below its MSP, so tur buying needs duty coordination

What it means: Duty-free imports at ₹5,659 undercut the ₹8,000 MSP, so any domestic tur buffer would compete directly with cheaper imports.

What to do: Set tariff policy alongside any tur volume guarantee before the first quintal is bought.

What would fix this

  • FCI should publish the economic cost of rice and wheat each year broken down by cost head — acquisition, incidentals, milling, freight, storage and interest, so that the top rung of the price ladder is a published figure rather than an order-of-magnitude estimate near ₹4,000 a quintal.
  • The Department of Agriculture & Farmers Welfare should publish procurement as a share of production for every MSP crop, by state and season, in one annual table, so that the gap between 23 announced prices and the two crops where procurement is a standing operation is visible officially.
  • CACP should report in each season’s price policy report the share of Agmarknet mandi records below MSP for each crop, so that the distress this piece measured on a single day — such as 92% of bajra and 93% of ragi reports below MSP — is tracked across seasons.
  • MoSPI should publish, from its latest farm-household survey, the crop-wise share of sellers who sold to a procurement agency, so that MSP coverage is no longer cited from the 2012–13 NSS 70th round.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

India's record grain targets are the "nature" story — sown area, monsoon, yield. The fertiliser supply chain that actually delivers them is the "nurture" — engineered, imported, gas-priced, and increasingly exposed to events thousands of kilometres from any Indian field.

India's fertiliser-fed food security: demand forecast, import bill and gas grid

Skip to article content
Text Size

Top line

  • 120% rise in urea imports in Apr–Nov FY25–26, while domestic urea output fell 3.7% even as record foodgrain production kept demand high.
  • $959/t was the April 2026 urea tender price, against $419/t in Nov 2025 and $447/t in Jun 2026, so import costs swung by more than the subsidy planning could absorb.
  • ~$10.3/MMBtu is the gas price below which modelled domestic urea undercuts every import tender of this cycle, which makes the gas price the decisive input for make-versus-buy.
Updated 22 October 2026. This post cites a 2025-26 foodgrain target of 362.5 MT and a 2024-25 record of 353.96 MT. The Ministry of Agriculture's Third Advance Estimates for 2025-26, released in late May 2026, put output at a record 376.56 MT, above the target, with wheat at 120.66 MT. The same release revised 2024-25 to 357.73 MT. The demand-floor argument below is unchanged. The post also quotes a warning that China could tighten urea exports after August 2026. That had not happened by September. Trade press (Fertilizer Daily, citing Profercy and Bloomberg) reported lowest offers of about $390–394/t cfr in RCF's August 2026 urea tender, with China expected to supply at least 1.2 MT. That is below the $419/t used here as the cycle's cheapest. On this piece's own formula, the gas-price crossover would fall from about $10.3 to about $9.3/MMBtu. Final award prices were not confirmed. Sources: Agriculture Ministry estimates, Fertilizer Daily (August), Fertilizer Daily (September). The original text below is unchanged.

What it means: Imports, not new domestic capacity, filled the extra demand from record cropping, with urea imports up 120% to 7.17 MT and DAP import dependence rising from 56% to 67%.

What to do: Lock in firm LNG on long-term fixed-price contracts ring-fenced for fertiliser feedstock, so plant-level gas allocation is insulated from spot-market and Hormuz-transit shocks.

Urea tender prices went from $419/t to $959/t and back to $447/t in seven months

What it means: The swing came from China's export policy and a Hormuz closure thousands of kilometres away, not from India's own demand, and it reversed in the same tender mechanism within seven weeks.

What to do: Weigh domestic production against imports using the full range of tender prices, not a single snapshot, because the volatility is the real story.

Domestic urea beats every import tender only below about $10.3/MMBtu gas

Show the numbers
Gas price scenarioFeedstock gas price, mmbtu US$/MMBtuModelled domestic cost, tonne US$/tonne
APM gas (regulated, legacy fields)7~ 320
Pooled gas (historical NIP entry point)9~ 380
NIP-2012 pricing ceiling14~ 530
Crisis-level spot LNG (Mar–Apr 2026 peak)~ 20~ 710

What it means: At APM gas of $7/MMBtu modelled domestic urea at about $320/t undercuts every tender this cycle, but at crisis spot LNG of about $20/MMBtu it costs about $710/t and only beats the April peak.

What to do: Accelerate the coal-route urea model, starting with Talcher by June 2027, to sidestep the $10.3/MMBtu crossover risk for that capacity.

What it means: National spare capacity of 105.5 mmscmd looks ample, but most of it sits at terminals poorly connected to the fertiliser plant clusters, and Dahej is already at 89.9% utilisation.

What to do: Complete pipeline connectivity from the east-coast terminals, especially Dhamra, and resolve third-party access at Mundra before relying on LNG-fed substitution.

What would fix this

  • The Ministry of Agriculture and Farmers Welfare should publish state-wise Soil Nutrient Index values for every Soil Health Card cycle since 2019, in the same format as EnviStats 2019, so that whether nitrogen fertility is still Low can be checked against the 25.89 crore cards issued, not only the 2015–2019 cycles.
  • The Department of Fertilizers should publish a costed option paper for supporting organic manures and soil-fertility practices on terms comparable to urea and NBS subsidies, so that the “level playing field” question raised by field research is answered with numbers inside the ₹1.71 lakh crore subsidy architecture.
  • PNGRB should publish third-party access terms and booked versus available capacity at every LNG terminal, starting with Mundra, since Mundra holds 15.8 mmscmd of spare capacity on the same coast as a near-saturated Dahej that feeds the main fertiliser clusters.
  • PNGRB should publish quarterly progress against schedule for the pipeline links between the Dhamra terminal and the Jharkhand, Bihar and West Bengal fertiliser plants, so that capacity it already calls “commercially stranded” can be tracked towards actually reaching those plants.
  • The Department of Fertilizers should publish a register of every urea and DAP import tender, with date, quantity awarded, origin and landed price, so that swings such as $959 a tonne to $447 inside seven weeks are visible in an official record rather than trade-press tender reporting.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

Seat belt webbing and airbag fabric are two of the fastest-growing categories in India's automotive textiles, and both remain significantly import-dependent even as global safety-systems majors localise assembly here. Tyre cord fabric tells almost the opposite story: India is the world's second-largest nylon-6 tyre-cord-fabric producer, long protected by anti-dumping duties against Chinese imports (in place from 2005; their status after a 2020 sunset review is unclear). This piece separates the two stories, and traces the one raw-material dependency — imported nylon-6 polymer — that sits underneath both.

Updated 27 October 2026. The National Technical Textiles Mission’s ₹1,480 crore is its total outlay for 2020-21 to 2025-26, not money spent “since launch”: ₹517 crore had been allocated and ₹393.39 crore used by March 2025 (PIB, 2025), and its 168 research projects (about ₹509 crore) were approved, not funded, with 2 completed. The policy table now says so. The mobiltech market size (₹13,595 crore in 2021, ₹20,165 crore projected for FY2025-26) and imports (₹6,004 crore, about 26% of technical-textile imports) come from a single trade-press source that could not be checked against official data; they are now marked as such.
Updated 7 November 2026. The nylon filament yarn case on China and Vietnam reached final findings on 19 March 2026, with the Directorate General of Trade Remedies recommending anti-dumping duties; whether the Finance Ministry notified them was not confirmed, and high-tenacity yarn of 840 denier and above, the grade used in tyre cord, is reported to be excluded (TPM trade-remedy tracker, read through search summaries only). No final findings were found for the nylon-6 chip case on China and Russia. For the sector as a whole, the Ministry of Textiles put FY2025-26 technical-textile exports at ₹29,217.60 crore and imports at ₹25,797.40 crore, a surplus of ₹3,420.20 crore, without a segment split (KNN). Whether the National Technical Textiles Mission, which ran to March 2026, has been extended could not be established.
Updated 13 October 2026. Two trade-remedy statements below look wrong. First, the anti-dumping duty on Chinese nylon tyre cord fabric was reportedly revoked in early December 2020 (Global Trade Alert records 3 December 2020), so “protected … since 2019” and “actively protected” do not appear to hold for that product; the $520–1,100 a tonne range quoted here appears to belong to the tyre cord fabric recommendation rather than to a separate nylon tyre yarn case, which could not be confirmed. Second, the nylon-6 chip and granule probe on China and Russia was initiated on 31 December 2025, with an investigation period to June 2025; the 26 December 2024 date belongs to a separate nylon filament yarn case on China and Vietnam. No final findings for either case were seen. What would fix this

  • The Ministry of Textiles should publish mobiltech market size, imports and exports for the same financial year, with the HSN codes in its 207-code basket listed as a downloadable file, so readers do not have to set a 2021 baseline against an FY2025-26 export figure.
  • DGCI&S should add a memo line to its technical-textile trade statistics showing imports of seat belt assemblies (8708.21) and airbags (8708.95), so the woven fabric inside finished safety systems imported as Chapter 87 auto parts is no longer invisible in the mobiltech import figure.
  • The Ministry of Textiles should publish domestic installed capacity for airbag-grade fabric and for seat belt webbing as separate line items, so it is possible to tell how much of the restraint-systems chain is localised in material and not just in assembly.
  • The Ministry of Textiles should report National Technical Textiles Mission R&D grants and PLI Textiles approvals by segment, with mobiltech and its sub-products shown separately, so it is checkable whether any of the ₹1,480 crore NTTM outlay or the ₹10,683 crore PLI reaches seat belts, airbags or tyre cord.
  • The Ministry of Textiles should mirror its technical-textiles opportunities and challenges report on data.gov.in, so the primary source for NTTM and mobiltech figures remains reachable when texmin.gov.in refuses access, instead of analysts relying on secondary summaries.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

What this piece does not establish. The Ministry of Textiles' own technical-textiles opportunities/challenges report (texmin.gov.in) returned an access error (HTTP 403) to this piece's research tools, so the NTTM and mobiltech figures here rely on secondary reporting rather than that primary document. The exact percentage of FY2025-26 mobiltech demand met by imports is not available — Section 2's figures span different base years and should be read directionally, not as a precise current import-penetration rate. Plant-level Indian manufacturing capacity specifically for airbag-grade fabric, separate from seat belt webbing capacity, was not found. And per Section 5, the true scale of automotive-textile imports embedded inside finished, HSN-87-classified safety systems is likely larger than the textile-chapter import figures cited here capture — this piece flags that gap rather than estimating a number for it.

What this piece could not establish

And it has long been protected. India has run anti-dumping duties against Chinese nylon tyre cord fabric since 2005 (the investigation opened in October 2003). A sunset review opened in November 2019 to test whether removing the duty would let Chinese dumping resume, and in its final findings of 29 October 2020 the DGTR recommended continuing the duty for five more years, at USD 520-1,100 per tonne depending on the exporter. Global Trade Alert, however, records the measure as revoked on 3 December 2020, and this piece could not establish whether the Finance Ministry accepted the recommendation, so whether the duty is in force today is open. Either way, for most of two decades tyre cord fabric is a segment India's trade-remedy apparatus worked to keep protected, not one it was trying to substitute into from an import-dependent starting position.

Only $93.4 billion of India's $321.8 billion in BRICS imports can be settled in rupees without foreign creditors piling up unwanted rupee claims — the rest is not de-dollarisation, it is vendor financing. The proposition is seductive. India buys enormous quantities of goods from other BRICS members and pays for nearly all of it in US dollars. Settle that trade in rupees, roubles, yuan and dirhams instead, and India stops bidding for dollars it does not otherwise need — less pressure on the rupee, a smaller intervention burden for the RBI, and insulation from sanctions. The Reserve Bank proposed, in January 2026, linking BRICS central bank digital currencies to make exactly this cheaper.

India's BRICS trade in rupees: $228.5bn exposure, $93.4bn reroutable to rupee settlement

Top line

  • 41.5% of India's $774.98bn import bill is BRICS trade, but only $93.4bn of it can be netted and settled in rupees.
  • $228.5bn a year would have to be held as rupees by partners, $112.1bn of it by China.
  • 29.8% export coverage of BRICS imports in FY2025-26, down from 44% in FY2016-17.
Updated 7 November 2026. Since publication: the BRICS leaders’ New Delhi Declaration of September 2026 only acknowledged the BRICS Payment Task Force’s study of payment interoperability and said there was “no one-size-fits-all approach” to local-currency settlement. India’s proposal to link BRICS payment systems, including central bank digital currencies, was not adopted (Business Standard), and the foreign ministry said there is no proposal for a BRICS currency (Times of India). The RBI consolidated its rules for Special Rupee Vostro Accounts in a circular of 17 July 2026 (RBI). None of this changes the arithmetic below, which uses 2025-26, still the latest full year of trade data.
Updated 22 October 2026. This post says the RBI's proposal to link BRICS digital currencies is “on the 2026 summit agenda”. That summit has now been held. The 18th BRICS summit in New Delhi (12–13 September 2026) adopted the New Delhi Declaration. As reported, it backs trade settlement in local currencies, faster and cheaper cross-border payments, and linking national payment systems through the BRICS Payment Task Force. It creates no common currency. This blog has not confirmed whether the digital-currency proposal appears in the final text. Either way, the declaration deals with payment friction, not the trade-imbalance limit modelled here. Sources: SCMP, Vision IAS. The original text below is unchanged.

What to do: Pursue S2 netting and payment-friction fixes, and do not plan on S3 unless partners are willing to hold rupees.

What would fix this

  • The RBI should publish quarterly Special Rupee Vostro Account balances by partner country, so that the build-up of foreign rupee claims this post identifies as the binding constraint is tracked from official data rather than from reported estimates attributed to unnamed sources, as the Russian balances are today.
  • The RBI should publish, each year, the share of India's exports and imports invoiced in rupees and the share settled in rupees, separately and by partner country, updating the Inter-Departmental Group's 2023 figure of about 86 per cent dollar invoicing, so that settlement gains are not mistaken for pricing gains.
  • The Department of Commerce should publish annually the self-financing share of India's trade with each BRICS member and partner — the smaller of imports and exports — alongside the rupee-settled volume actually achieved, so that progress on the balanced-trade portion can be measured against the $93.4 billion ceiling this model sets.
  • The RBI should report, in its annual report, transaction-cost savings from rupee settlement against the 5–6 per cent conversion costs it has itself cited, so that the plumbing benefit this post argues is the real gain is measured rather than asserted.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

Method: "self-financing" is min(imports, exports) per country — the volume both legs can cover without either side accumulating claims; "residual" is max(0, imports − exports).

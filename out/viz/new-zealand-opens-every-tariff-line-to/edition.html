From 20 October 2026 all Indian goods enter New Zealand duty-free, but its average tariff was 2.3% and more than half its lines were already free. India cuts duties on about 70% of lines, opens quotas for apples and kiwifruit, keeps dairy out, and gets 5,000 work visas and a $20 billion investment aim in return.

Top line

  • 17.8% is India's average tariff against New Zealand's 2.3%, so most of the tariff cutting is India's.
  • 32,500 tonnes of New Zealand apples enter at half duty in year one, rising to 45,000 tonnes.
Updated 7 November 2026. The agreement entered into force as scheduled on 20 October 2026, after both sides completed ratification, according to New Zealand’s Ministry of Foreign Affairs and Trade and press reports. The second round of Indian tariff cuts described below falls on 1 January 2027. No trade data covering the period since entry into force has been published yet.

What it means: New Zealand's average tariff is 2.3% and 58.3% of its lines were already duty-free before the deal.

What to do: Check the published tariff schedules line by line before treating New Zealand's 100% opening as a like-for-like concession.

India's apple and kiwifruit quotas start small and rise over six years

What it means: Apples enter at 25% duty instead of 50% within a quota, and kiwifruit enters duty-free within its quota.

What to do: Check the apple minimum import price and seasonal window, since the volume quota alone does not set the terms.

What would fix this

  • The Directorate General of Foreign Trade should publish how the apple, kiwifruit and albumin quotas are allocated, and the monthly fill rate of each, so importers, growers and Parliament can see how much of each quota is used and by whom.
  • The Department of Commerce should publish preference-utilisation figures for the agreement every quarter: how many Indian shipments to New Zealand claim the zero duty, and in which products, so the benefit of “100% duty-free” is measured rather than assumed.
  • The Department of Agriculture, with Himachal Pradesh and Jammu and Kashmir, should publish wholesale apple prices in the main markets through each New Zealand import window, so any effect of the quota on growers is visible early enough to use the agreement’s review and safeguard provisions.
  • The Department for Promotion of Industry and Internal Trade should report New Zealand investment into India each year against the $20 billion aim, from its own FDI statistics.
  • The Ministry of External Affairs should report each year how many of the 5,000 employment visas and 1,000 working-holiday visas were used, by occupation, since the mobility chapter is the main gain claimed for India.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

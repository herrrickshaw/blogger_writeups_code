India ran two ethanol incentive experiments side by side, on similar timelines and comparable total outlays. One cut the cost of capital on loans distilleries would have raised anyway, and drew in hundreds of applicants who pushed national capacity past its 2030 target years early. The other handed out one-time capped grants toward a dozen showcase plants, and the flagship result — a ₹984 crore refinery inaugurated by the Prime Minister — now sits "barely even functioning," according to the government's own petroleum ministry. The difference is not the money. It's what the incentive was actually designed to fix.

Interest subvention built 499 distilleries; 2G has three commissioned plants

What it means: The 1G scheme let hundreds of private and cooperative applicants fund their own distilleries, and 499 are now operating.

What to do: Match each incentive to its real bottleneck, starting with feedstock logistics for 2G, before any further rollout.

A ₹150 crore grant left ₹834 crore of the ₹984 crore Panipat plant to the applicant

What it means: Only applicants with large balance sheets could try, and in practice four state-owned oil companies were the only ones who did.

What to do: Before offering another capped grant, check that the likely applicants can fund the remaining cost.

SATAT's 2025 target was 5,000 CBG plants; about 200 had been commissioned

What it means: The post links the shortfall to fragmented, capped per-stage grants that make a small pool of applicants clear several hurdles at once.

What to do: Track whether GOBARdhan's ten-year fixed price lifts uptake before adding more per-stage grants.

What would fix this

  • The Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas should publish a plant-by-plant PM JI-VAN Yojana status table giving sanction date, grant released, commissioning date, monthly ethanol output and capacity utilisation, so Panipat’s performance is measured on the record rather than through an unnamed official’s ‘zero contribution’ remark.
  • Indian Oil Corporation should publish monthly rice-straw procurement at Panipat — tonnes contracted, tonnes delivered and price paid at the gate — so the feedstock-logistics failure is quantified before any decision to resume the paused 2G rollout.
  • NABARD, as administering agency, should publish a project-wise list of ethanol interest-subvention beneficiaries with loan sanctioned, subvention paid, capacity commissioned and ethanol supplied, so the ₹2,075 crore released can be tied to the 499 distilleries and 38 pipeline projects it supported.
  • The Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas should run a single GOBARdhan plant tracker listing each CBG plant’s status, the grants it drew (CFA, BAM, DPI) and gas sold under offtake contracts, so the gap between SATAT’s 5,000-plant target and the roughly 200 commissioned can be traced to specific stages.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

BHAVYA, approved in March, will fund 100 plug-and-play industrial parks by 2031-32. The land it adds is about a tenth of what mapped parks already have available, so its test is whether its parks fill faster. The first proposals come mostly from states other than those where factory investment is waiting.

Outlay and periodRs 33,660 crore, central sector scheme, 2026-27 to 2031-32
Parks100 plug-and-play parks; 50 in phase one, chosen in two rounds by challenge
SizeAt least 100 contiguous acres; at least 25 acres in hill states, the North-East, union territories and states with under one crore people; up to 20 of the 100 parks may be 500–1,000 acres, the rest under 500; brownfield case by case; at least 90% of the land must be encumbrance-free before approval
Central supportUp to Rs 1 crore an acre, as equity through NICDIT in three milestone-linked tranches over three years, for core, value-added, social and external infrastructure; up to 25% of a park’s funding may go to external connectivity, where the Centre pays no more than 25% of the cost and the state the rest; private-developer parks get the lower of Rs 50 lakh an acre or 50% of infrastructure cost
Who buildsA special purpose vehicle of the National Industrial Corridor Development and Implementation Trust (NICDIT) and the state’s nodal agency, which owns the land and assets; with a private developer, a three-way joint venture in which the developer may keep up to 25% of developed land. NICDC is the project management agency
Targets34,000 acres of investment-ready land and 15 lakh direct jobs by 2031-32
Round one1 June – 31 July 2026: 87 proposals, up to 20 to be selected
Round two1 August – 31 October 2026 (extended from 30 September)

What it means: The government's own land bank lists 1.35 lakh hectares available, so the scheme's case rests on quality rather than quantity.

What to do: Publish occupancy of mapped parks over time so BHAVYA parks can be judged on how fast they fill.

What it means: The post's SEZ count shows that earlier zones were approved faster than they were filled.

What to do: Judge BHAVYA parks on land allotted, investment and production rather than approvals, as the Finance Minister urged in August.

What would fix this

  • The India Industrial Land Bank should publish, for each park, vacant plots and hectares at the end of every quarter, so that occupancy in BHAVYA parks can be compared with existing parks over time.
  • DPIIT should publish every BHAVYA proposal and its evaluation score, not only those selected, so states’ proposals can be compared with demand.
  • For each selected park, NICDC should report land allotted, investment committed, units in production and jobs every six months, against the scheme’s own targets.
  • Where a state proposes a BHAVYA park, it should also report the occupancy of its existing state industrial estates and any idle SEZs within the same district.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

What this piece could not establish

The scheme notification of 10 April 2026 was read in the Gazette of India’s own text (CG-DL-E-10042026-271698, via the SansadSaar index of the archive.org Gazette of India collection); the scheme-design rows above follow it. The notification does not state the 34,000-acre or 15-lakh-jobs targets, which come from the Cabinet release as reported, or the round dates, which come from the operational guidelines as reported; the Cabinet release, the guidelines and the land bank’s pages could not be opened directly. Proposal counts for states outside the top six, the second round’s results and any selections had not been published by 27 October 2026. The corridor programme’s programme-wide allotment figure comes from a single, undated report.

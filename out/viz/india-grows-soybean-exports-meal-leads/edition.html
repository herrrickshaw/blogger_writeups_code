Madhya Pradesh and Maharashtra between them sow roughly 93 lakh hectares of soybean a year — a scale that ought to make India a soy-food powerhouse. Almost none of that crop becomes tofu, soy milk or protein isolate on an Indian plate. Trace the harvested bean through a crusher and it splits into meal, most of which used to be exported, and oil, which still leaves India roughly two-thirds reliant on imports to meet its own edible-oil demand. The one soy-derived product where India genuinely leads the world — lecithin — is a byproduct of that same oil-crushing step, not a separate industry. This piece follows the bean from MP and Maharashtra's fields through crushing, lecithin, and the still-small frontier of tofu, soy protein and edamame.

Top line

  • 52.23 lakh tonnes of soybean from Maharashtra in the Kharif 2025 estimate, ahead of Madhya Pradesh's 43.25.
  • 8,582 soy lecithin shipments from India, against 1,499 from Argentina and 586 from China.
  • 51% fall in India's oilmeal exports year-on-year in November 2025, to 1.62 lakh tonnes.
Updated 7 November 2026. SOPA later revised the 2025-26 crop up to about 110.3 lakh tonnes (from the 105.36 lakh tonnes given below). Its first estimate for kharif 2026 is 107.93 lakh tonnes, down about 2%, and the state reversal described in section 2 has swung back: Madhya Pradesh is put at about 57.2 lakh tonnes (up roughly 32%) and Maharashtra at about 34.9 lakh tonnes (down roughly 33%, after dry weather), as reported. The soybean minimum support price has risen from the ₹4,892 quoted below (2024-25) to ₹5,328 for 2025-26 and ₹5,708 per quintal for 2026-27. What it means: The two states swapped places within one year, as Maharashtra's yield of 1,169 kg/ha beat Madhya Pradesh's fall to 889 kg/ha.

What to do: Watch the 2025-26 national estimate of 105.36 lakh tonnes before reading the reversal as a trend.

What it means: Lecithin is a byproduct of refining, so India's crushing base yields an export lead even with a large oil deficit.

What would fix this

  • The Department of Agriculture & Farmers Welfare should publish UPAG’s state-wise crop area, yield and production tables as dated, downloadable files with stable links, so that official soybean estimates can be cited directly rather than through an industry association’s compilation.
  • The Department of Agriculture & Farmers Welfare should publish soybean quantities actually procured at MSP, by state and week, under the ₹15,000 crore Kharif package, so that it can be checked whether procurement closed a gap of mandi prices at ₹3,800–4,000 against an MSP of ₹5,328.
  • The Madhya Pradesh Farmer Welfare and Agriculture Development Department should publish Bhavantar soybean registrations, quantities and payments by district, so that the relaunched price-deficiency scheme can be judged on who was paid and how much.
  • The Ministry of Food Processing Industries should publish a register of soybean processing capacity split between crush-for-oil-and-meal and food-grade protein lines, so that the size of India’s tofu, soy-milk and isolate base rests on plant data rather than vendor estimates that disagree.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

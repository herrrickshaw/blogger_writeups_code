Four blending mandates, one country, and only one chemistry problem. Ethanol in petrol and isobutanol in diesel both carry less energy than the fuel they're cut into, so every litre goes a little less far. CBG in CNG and SAF in ATF are close to true drop-ins — the same energy, delivered the same way — so their cost shows up as a price premium on the input, not fewer kilometres per rupee. The blend chemistry decides who pays and how; the mandate percentage is almost beside the point.

Biofuel blends and mileage: ethanol carries 66% of petrol's energy per litre

Skip to article content
Text Size

Top line

  • 66% of petrol’s energy per litre is what ethanol carries, so E20 costs drivers 2–6% in mileage: about ₹3,900–4,900 a year at a 4% loss, on Delhi fuel prices.
  • 3.9% vs 6.9% is the most mileage a 15% isobutanol-diesel blend could cost, against E20’s ceiling. The diesel mandate is proposed, not notified.
  • $1.4 a litre is a 2026 projection for India-made SAF, against $0.6–0.8 for conventional jet fuel. SAF and CBG cost users money, not range.
Updated 7 November 2026. The CBG section now gives the first year of the blending obligation: about 1.05% reported against the 1% target for 2025-26, or about 0.93% on PNGRB’s own sales figures. SAF prices are updated to BloombergNEF’s second-quarter 2026 European average of about $2,830 a tonne. The isobutanol–diesel mandate was rechecked and is still not notified.

Updated 15 November 2026: rewritten as four charts; figures unchanged from the 7 November update (CBG first-year blending, Q2 2026 SAF prices, isobutanol still not notified).

What it means: Ethanol and isobutanol cost drivers mileage because each litre carries less energy, while CBG and SAF match their host fuels and show up as a price premium instead.

What to do: Judge a blending mandate by the energy gap of its blend component, not by its headline percentage.

E20 mileage: 2 to 6% lost in tests, under the 6.9% energy ceiling

What it means: E20 sells at the price of the petrol it replaced, so a 4% loss works out to ₹0.27–0.33 a kilometre, lightest for E20-compliant vehicles made since about 2023.

What to do: The petroleum ministry should publish its own estimate of E20’s mileage loss and yearly cost, split between compliant and older vehicles.

Isobutanol in diesel: a 3.9% ceiling at 15%, just over half of E20’s

What it means: Isobutanol holds more energy per litre than ethanol, so even a deeper blend caps the loss near 3.9%, though that is a projection for a mandate that has not been notified.

What to do: BIS should publish an isobutanol-diesel specification, and the ministry ARAI results at 15%, before any mandate is notified.

Which standards govern each fuel
  • Ethanol in petrol: IS 17021 (E20 fuel), IS 2796 (petrol), IS 15464 (ethanol for blending). US comparators: ASTM D4806, ASTM D5798.
  • CBG in CNG: IS 15958:2023 (CNG and LNG fuel), IS 16087:2016 (CBG, at least 90% methane). Europe: EN 16723-2.
  • Isobutanol in diesel: IS 1460:2017 covers diesel only. No specification for the blend was found, in India or elsewhere.
  • SAF in jet fuel: ASTM D1655 (Jet A-1) and ASTM D7566 (synthetic blends; Annex A5 for alcohol-to-jet). API 1543 and 1595 cover handling, not composition.
Who is building isobutanol supply

MC² Foundation, backed by seven public-sector energy companies, launched the Grand Butanol Challenge on 17 July 2026 with up to ₹12.5 crore for bio-based butanol routes; applications closed on 15 August 2026. BPCL already makes isobutanol, but from propylene, a fossil route.

Five fixes

  1. BIS: publish a draft isobutanol-diesel specification before any mandate is notified.
  2. Petroleum ministry: publish ARAI results for isobutanol-diesel at 15%, by vehicle type.
  3. Petroleum ministry: publish an official estimate of E20’s mileage loss and yearly cost to drivers.
  4. PNGRB: require each city-gas company to report its monthly CBG share against the 1% to 5% obligation.
  5. Civil aviation ministry: publish the SAF price paid and the jet-fuel benchmark each year from 2027.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

What this piece does not establish. The isobutanol-diesel mandate is proposed, not notified; this piece's cost figures for it are a projection built on the same energy-density method used for E20, not a measured running cost, and the actual blend percentage, timeline and real-world mileage impact could all still change or be reported differently once a formal notification exists. No Bureau of Indian Standards fuel specification for isobutanol-diesel blends was found in the sourcing available to this piece, unlike the BIS specs already in force for E20 and CBG — if one exists, it was not located. SAF pricing is volatile and quoted across very different bases (per-tonne spot, per-litre pathway-specific projections, and blended-fuel benchmarks); the figures here should be read as an order-of-magnitude premium, not a precise, current India price. CBG's energy-density range is a published band, not a single certified figure, and the exact ratio to fossil CNG will vary by feedstock and plant.

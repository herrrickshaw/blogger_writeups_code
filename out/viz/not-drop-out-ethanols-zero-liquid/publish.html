Zero Liquid Discharge for Indian ethanol distilleries isn't one law with one start date — it's a regulatory ratchet that tightened over two decades, and since 2021 it's also a hard gate on government money: no CPCB-verified ZLD certificate, no interest-subvention reimbursement, for grain, molasses and dual-feed distilleries alike. That's a genuinely mandatory criterion. What it isn't connected to, despite sharing the same MLD units, is India's municipal sewage-treatment capacity — a separate system, tracked separately, and running at roughly 28% of what the country actually generates.

Top line

  • 28% of India's urban sewage generation is matched by treatment capacity in use, against 44% installed.
  • 4,263 MLD of Namami Gange sewage capacity is complete, roughly 61% of the 7,000 MLD target for December 2026.
Updated 7 November 2026. Namami Gange’s commissioned sewage treatment capacity reached 3,976 MLD across 173 plants by 9 January 2026 (3,806 MLD in October 2025), and 76 sewerage projects worth 3,200 MLD were completed in the five years to February 2026 (PIB). No newer national inventory of sewage treatment plants than CPCB’s March 2021 report was found, so Section 3 stands. The distillery count and 9.5 billion litres in the opening box are 2019-20 figures; ethanol distillation capacity alone is now about 2,000 crore litres (20 billion litres) a year, on CareEdge Ratings’ May 2026 estimate used elsewhere on this blog.

Updated 15 November 2026: rewritten as charts; figures unchanged.

Installed sewage capacity covers 44% of generation; only about 28% is in use

What it means: Of 72,368 MLD of urban sewage generated, 31,841 MLD is installed but only about 20,235 MLD is utilised.

What to do: CPCB should refresh its sewage treatment inventory, last comprehensively published in March 2021.

Namami Gange has 4,263 MLD complete, about 61% of its 7,000 MLD target

What it means: Completed capacity grew from 3,806 MLD in October 2025 to 4,263 MLD in August 2026, against a 7,000 MLD target.

What to do: The National Mission for Clean Ganga should publish quarterly the status of the 1,625.50 MLD where construction has not started.

India's ~28% treatment share sits near lower-middle-income peers at 26%

What it means: India's roughly 28% is close to the lower-middle-income figure of 26%, while high-income countries reach 74%.

What to do: Judge India against income peers rather than the EU, as the post argues.

What would fix this

  • CPCB should compile and publish a national inventory of industrial effluent-treatment and zero-liquid-discharge capacity by sector, beginning with distilleries, so that the capacity now available only project by project in environmental clearance filings is visible in one official dataset.
  • CPCB should publish a list of every distillery issued a ZLD certificate under its July 2024 verification SOP, with feedstock, treatment route and date of verification, so that compliance with the Red Category obligation can be checked distillery by distillery.
  • The Department of Food and Public Distribution should report, for the ethanol interest-subvention scheme, how many projects have submitted the CPCB-verified Clause 7 certificate and how much subvention has been withheld for non-submission, so that the scheme's ZLD gate is shown to operate in practice.
  • CPCB should refresh its National Inventory of Sewage Treatment Plants, last comprehensively published in March 2021, and commit to a fixed publication cycle, so that the 44 per cent installed and roughly 28 per cent utilised figures are not still the latest data five years on.
  • The National Mission for Clean Ganga should publish quarterly the status of the 1,625.50 MLD of treatment capacity in the five main-stem states on which construction had not started, so that the part of the gap that is unaddressed, rather than merely in progress, can be tracked to the December 2026 target.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

What this piece does not establish. No comprehensive national inventory of industrial effluent-treatment (ETP/ZLD) capacity by sector, comparable in scope to CPCB's sewage-treatment inventory, was located for this piece; state- or distillery-specific ZLD-plant capacity figures would need to be compiled project-by-project from environmental clearance filings, which this piece did not attempt. The national sewage figures in Section 3 are municipal, not industrial, and should not be read as describing distillery treatment capacity specifically. CPCB's March 2021 sewage inventory remains, per the Ministry of Jal Shakti's own August 2025 parliamentary reply, the latest comprehensive such report available — no 2023-2026 refresh of the full state-wise inventory was found. The Namami Gange progress figures in Section 4 (December 2025 release, "as of October 2025") were the most recent found at the time that section was written; Section 6 records a newer, ~6 August 2026 update (146 completed, 4,263 MLD) found in a later research pass. Even that may not be the most current by the time this is read; check pib.gov.in directly. The international comparison figures in Section 5 (regional and income-bracket wastewater-treatment shares) come from UN-Water/WHO/UN-Habitat secondary reporting rather than this piece's own retrieval of the underlying UN-Water 2024 report, whose primary page could not be accessed directly during research; the 56% global headline figure was independently confirmed against the UN Statistics Division's own SDG Goal 6 2025 report page. India's ~28% figure is this piece's own calculation from Section 3 and is not itself part of any official cross-country dataset. The US per-capita and Florida figures in the expanded Section 5 comparison combine EPA CWNS population-served data with a separately-sourced treated-volume figure (US wastewater factsheet) and Florida DEP's permitted-capacity figure; these were not drawn from one single unified US government dataset, and the volume-vs-design-capacity mismatch noted above means the US/India comparison is directionally reliable but not an exact apples-to-apples figure.

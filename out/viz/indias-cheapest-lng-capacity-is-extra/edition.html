Expanding Dahej cost about ₹370 crore per million tonnes a year of capacity; India’s new onshore terminals cost ₹950–1,290 crore, ran 22–35 months late and, in April–May 2026, worked at 24–37% of capacity while Dahej ran at 92%. PNGRB’s own data point to the pipeline out, not the tank, as the constraint.

LNG terminals in India: Dahej ran at 92% in April–May 2026, newer ones at 24–37%

Skip to article content
Text Size

Top line

  • 91.9% of Dahej's capacity was used in April–May 2026, against 10.5–40.1% at the other seven terminals.
  • ₹370 crore per MMTPA to expand Dahej, against ₹950–1,290 crore per MMTPA for new onshore terminals.
  • 22–35 months late for the four new onshore terminals, against 12 months for Dahej's expansion.

What it means: Expansions reuse jetties, land and pipeline connections, which is why they cost about a third as much per tonne of capacity.

What to do: Each terminal approval should show capital cost per MMTPA with marine works listed separately, as this piece proposes.

Four new onshore terminals ran 22–35 months late; Dahej's expansion ran 12

What it means: The recurring causes are breakwaters, evacuation pipelines not ready at commissioning, and lease or clearance disputes, not tank construction.

What to do: Terminals should be measured from board approval to commercial operation and against the first date announced, as this piece proposes.

Gujarat's gas pipelines carry about 40 MMSCMD; its terminals can regasify 95–100

What it means: GSPL's high-pressure network ran at 100.5% in 2024-25, so expansions in Gujarat can be limited by the pipes out.

What to do: GSPL and PNGRB should publish a funded plan to raise Gujarat's evacuation capacity before Hazira's expansion and Jafrabad are commissioned.

What would fix this

  • PNGRB should make registration of any new LNG terminal or expansion conditional on evacuation capacity — booked pipeline capacity from the terminal gate, scheduled to be in service within 12 months of the terminal — and publish that test with each registration under the 2025 regulations, so that Kochi’s decade at 13% is not repeated.
  • PPAC should publish terminal-wise capacity, utilisation and LNG receipts each month as a machine-readable table, stating the capacity used as the denominator, rather than only as a table in the Ready Reckoner PDF. Its May 2026 edition still listed Dahej at 17.5 MMTPA two months after the expansion to 22.5 MMTPA, and its terminal percentages for April–May imply more gas than the import figure it gives for the same two months.
  • PNGRB should publish, in one table, the regas and truck-loading charges and booked capacity that terminals must now file twice a year, as its 2025 committee recommended.
  • The Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas should require state-owned buyers to seek destination flexibility across Indian terminals in new long-term LNG contracts, and report cargo swaps between terminals, so capacity in one port can absorb cargoes contracted for another.
  • Before Hazira’s expansion and Jafrabad are commissioned, GSPL and PNGRB should publish a funded plan to raise Gujarat’s evacuation capacity from about 40 MMSCMD toward the 130 MMSCMD of regas capacity the cluster will have.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted, except where the PNGRB committee is cited.

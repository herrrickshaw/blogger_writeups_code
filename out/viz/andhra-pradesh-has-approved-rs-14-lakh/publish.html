Andhra Pradesh investment: Rs 14 lakh crore approved, 10 times what it has built

Skip to article content
Text Size

Top line

  • Rs 14.05 lakh crore approved across 387 projects, about 10 times the large factory value Andhra built in 2021–25.
  • 37% of Andhra's 2015–22 factory filings later reported production, second only to Gujarat's 48%.
  • Rs 140,445 crore of large factories built in Andhra in 2021–25, against Rs 44,627 crore in Tamil Nadu.
Updated 7 November 2026. BPCL’s Ramayapatnam refinery, one of the two IEM-type projects discussed below, received its environmental and CRZ clearances by an order dated 27 October 2026 (cost about Rs 1,03,408 crore; Deccan Chronicle, a single report). That moves it from “awaiting clearance” to “cleared to build”; it is still years from production, so the comparison with commissioned investment is unchanged. No newer State Investment Promotion Board meeting total than the one used here was found.

Updated 15 November 2026: rewritten as charts; figures unchanged.

Updated 16 November 2026: On 10 November the Andhra Pradesh government said 526 of the 714 investment proposals received since June 2024 had been converted into projects worth Rs 17.2 lakh crore, out of a Rs 22.6 lakh crore pipeline, and that 303 projects worth Rs 7.1 lakh crore had been grounded or were scheduled for grounding. These are state-reported figures; whether they are on the same basis as the Rs 14 lakh crore above has not been checked. Source: Business Standard, 10 November 2026.

What would fix this

  • Andhra Pradesh’s investment board should publish, for every approved project, the date of approval, land allotted, statutory clearances, construction start, commercial production and jobs on the payroll, updated quarterly, so “approved” can be told apart from “built”.
  • States should report investment approvals split by sector (manufacturing, power, data centres, IT, tourism), so totals can be compared with DPIIT’s IEM record and with each other.
  • DPIIT should resume publishing state-wise IEM Part A and Part B lists with investment values in machine-readable form, which would let any state’s claims be checked within a quarter.
  • Incentive packages above a threshold (such as the reported Rs 19,100 crore for the Reliance data centre and up to Rs 28,000 crore for the steel plant) should be tabled in the state assembly with their milestones.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

What this piece could not establish

The board’s project list is not public, so this piece could not tell how much of the Rs 14.05 lakh crore is manufacturing, how much is power or data centres, or whether the five large projects are all inside it. The Rs 14.05 lakh crore and 387-project totals come from one state-government release reported by the press. The IEM record covers only manufacturing projects above Rs 50 crore of plant and machinery (Rs 125 crore from April 2025), and this blog’s matching of filings to production lists misses projects whose production entry cites neither the memorandum number nor the same company name, so the follow-through percentages are lower bounds. Project costs are as reported and differ between sources (the steel plant’s first phase is put at Rs 56,000 crore or Rs 80,000 crore). The environment ministry granted the BPCL refinery its environmental and coastal-zone (CRZ) clearances by an order dated 27 October 2026, at a cost of about Rs 1.03 lakh crore, according to one press report; construction, not commissioning, is the next step.

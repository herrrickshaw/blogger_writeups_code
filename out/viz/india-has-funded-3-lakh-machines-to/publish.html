The 2023-24 crop residue guidelines added a 65:25:10 window to fund balers, rakes and loaders for straw aggregators tied to a buyer, but only in four northern states. Coal plants, pellet makers and biogas plants across India need far more straw than that chain can collect.

Crop residue machines in India: 3 lakh subsidised, about 4,500 collect straw

Skip to article content
Text Size

Top line

  • 4,500 of some 3 lakh crop-residue machines funded since 2018 are balers and rakes, the kit that collects straw.
  • 43 million tonnes of biomass would meet a 5% co-firing mandate, against a 1.5 Mt collection target for 2023-24.
  • Rs 1,330 a tonne to bale straw 15 km away, rising to Rs 2,500–3,000 if the bales travel 50 km.
Updated 2 November 2026. Co-firing figures updated to the Ministry of Power’s annual report for 2025-26 (SAMARTH): 72 plants and about 40.2 lakh tonnes co-fired cumulatively by 31 December 2025, 24.76 lakh tonnes in 2025. The mandate is described under the November 2025 policy (5%, with 2% municipal-waste charcoal added in the NCR), which replaced the 2023 path to 7%; the stat card, chart and scheme table have changed.

Updated 15 November 2026: rewritten as charts; figures unchanged.

Co-firing 5% would need about 43 million tonnes, far above the 1.5 Mt collection target

What it means: Procurement targets cover a small share of what coal plants and kilns could burn, while most funded machines stay in the field.

What to do: Size collection targets to the 5% co-firing demand, not to the number of machines bought.

4 of the 7 top surplus states lie outside the machinery scheme

What it means: Outside the scheme the atlas shows 9,783 MW of potential, against 7,176 MW inside it.

What to do: Extend the aggregation window to Gujarat, Maharashtra, Andhra Pradesh and Madhya Pradesh, starting near coal plants and CBG plants.

Hauling bales 50 km costs about Rs 2,500–3,000 a tonne, against Rs 1,330 at 15 km

What it means: Cutting and baling make up about half of procurement cost, and storage adds 20–30% of the delivered cost.

What to do: Site aggregation machinery near buyers, not spread evenly across districts.

What would fix this

  • The Ministry of Agriculture should publish, for every ex-situ project, the aggregator, the buying industry, the machines funded and the tonnes delivered each season.
  • The ex-situ window should be opened to all states, with eligibility tied to a signed offtake contract with a co-firing plant, pellet plant, CBG plant or 2G ethanol plant rather than to geography.
  • Part of the subsidy should be paid per tonne delivered, so that public money follows straw collected, not machines bought.
  • The Ministry of Power should publish biomass co-fired by plant and by source state each month, so that collection projects can be sited where demand is.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

What this piece could not establish

The 2023-24 guidelines could not be opened from this blog’s research environment; their terms come from the PIB release of July 2023 and consistent secondary summaries. The number of ex-situ projects sanctioned and tonnes collected under them was not found. Machine totals differ between sources (3.0 lakh in a PIB reply, 3.24 lakh in an explainer) and so do fund totals (Rs 3,623 crore and Rs 3,926 crore), probably because of different cut-off years. Straw prices are older reported figures. Machine prices are listing-site figures, and Shaktiman’s product range is from its own site and trade-fair listing. The co-firing requirement in tonnes is an illustration on a weight basis, not an official estimate. SAMARTH’s own status table dated 31 August 2026 could not be read; a secondary report of 75 plants and about 6.1 million tonnes co-fired cumulatively by 15 June 2026 could not be verified and is not used. The National Biomass Atlas page could not be opened from this blog’s research environment; its state figures were read from the page’s indexed text and the SAMARTH copy, and two state totals could not be read cleanly. No standalone SSS-NIBE report on biomass collection and use was found; this piece draws on the institute’s published review, its director general’s statements and its 2024 seminar, and on CEEW’s cost estimates for Punjab.

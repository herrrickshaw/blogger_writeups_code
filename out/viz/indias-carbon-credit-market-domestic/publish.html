Top line

  • 490 obligated entities under CCTS, from two notified batches in about three months (8 Oct 2025 to 13 Jan 2026).
  • 5.8% aluminium reduction target under the GEI rules; cement is 3.4%, chlor-alkali 7.5%, pulp and paper 7.1%.
  • 13.8878 tCO2e per tonne: the 2023-24 baseline GEI of Vedanta's Jharsuguda smelter, against 1.1875 at Nalco's Damanjodi refinery.
Updated 7 November 2026. Trading of Carbon Credit Certificates has not started. At a hearing on 23 October 2026 the electricity regulator CERC admitted the petitions of PXIL, IEX and HPX for certificate contracts, recorded that the Bureau of Energy Efficiency had yet to frame the Detailed Procedure the regulations require, and listed the petitions for 12 January 2027. The iron and steel targets described below are still a revised draft of 26 June 2026 (about 253–255 plants), not a final notification, and fertiliser has no draft targets. Sources: CERC record of proceedings, 23 October 2026, Madhyamam (revised steel draft).
Updated 26 October 2026. CCTS has nine designated sectors, of which seven had notified targets by January 2026: aluminium (including secondary aluminium), cement, chlor-alkali, pulp and paper, petroleum refining, petrochemicals and textiles. Iron and steel and fertiliser are the other two. The table below counted secondary aluminium separately as an eighth sector; it is corrected. The 490 entities are unchanged.

Compressed bio-gas is on India's Article 6.2 eligible list, notified in 2023

  1. 30 May 2022NDAIAPA notified by Gazette as the National Designated Authority for the Paris Agreement
  2. 17 Feb 2023MoEFCC finalises the eligible Article 6.2 activities; compressed bio-gas is item 5

What it means: The Article 6.2 host list, notified on 17 February 2023, includes compressed bio-gas, so the CBG carbon leg rests on a primary notice.

What to do: Check whether MoEFCC has revised the list, since its stated initial three-year window has now elapsed.

What would fix this

  • The Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change should publish, with each final GEI-target notification, a plant-wise table of every baseline and target changed since the draft, with a stated reason, so that moves like Jharsuguda Smelter II’s re-baselining are explained by the regulator, not discovered by comparing gazettes.
  • The Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change should publish a dated timetable for draft and final GEI targets for the fertiliser sector, which remains designated under CCTS without even a draft notification, so that obligated plants and readers know when the compliance cost is expected to land.
  • The National Designated Authority for the Implementation of the Paris Agreement should publish whether the February 2023 Article 6.2 activity list has been revised or extended, and keep a dated version history online, since its stated three-year initial window has now elapsed.
  • The National Designated Authority for the Implementation of the Paris Agreement should maintain a public register of Article 6.2 project authorisations and mitigation outcomes transferred, by project and buyer country, so that eligibility for activities such as compressed biogas can be distinguished from actual transactions.
  • The Bureau of Energy Efficiency should publish a single consolidated document on the CCTS Offset Mechanism covering eligible project types, crediting rules and how offset credits interact with Compliance Mechanism certificates, so that the scheme’s second pillar is as legible as the first.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

Update: the "worth tracking" thread above — Fertiliser and Iron & Steel remaining CCTS-designated sectors without notified GEI targets — turns out to matter beyond India's borders too. A follow-up piece, "Greenhouse Emissions Reduction and CBAM Compliance — Real or Ideal Gases?", finds that those exact two sectors are also where India's quantified EU CBAM export exposure concentrates (~$20.7bn), and covers the India-EU FTA's CBAM provisions and the India-EFTA TEPA alongside it.
Update, 27 September 2026: Iron & Steel has moved, though it's not resolved. MoEFCC issued a revised draft GEI-target notification for 255 iron and steel units on 26 June 2026 (public 2 July), naming JSW Steel, Tata Steel, SAIL and ArcelorMittal Nippon Steel among the covered plants, with a 60-day objection window and 2026-27 as the compliance-year target. That is a status change from "not yet notified at all" to "draft published, pending finalisation" — this piece's own re-check found no equivalent movement on the Fertiliser side, which remains without a final or draft GEI-target notification as of this update.

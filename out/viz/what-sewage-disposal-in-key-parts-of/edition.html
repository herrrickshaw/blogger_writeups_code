Tamil Nadu's Madurai sewerage figure making the rounds is off by a thousand crore rupees. A separate claim about drainage funding for Chennai road repairs doesn't match any source found. And the mosquito that upgraded drainage actually helps against isn't the one carrying dengue — it's the one carrying malaria, and that disease is already down to a few hundred cases statewide for reasons that have little to do with sewer pipes.

Top line

  • ₹2,400 crore is Madurai's underground sewerage figure per the 2 September 2026 announcement, not ₹1,400 crore.
  • 5,587 malaria cases in 2015 fell to roughly 200-300 in 2025, credited to elimination and migrant screening rather than drains.
  • 27,378 dengue cases in 2024 and 23,407 in 2025, and the main Aedes mosquito breeds in household containers, not open drains.
Updated 27 October 2026. The summary at the top said Tamil Nadu’s dengue counts should not be cited as a single settled number, while the dengue section said the official series settles them. The section was right: the NCVBDC full-year figures are 27,378 cases in 2024 and 23,407 in 2025. The two press figures for 2025 are interim counts, now labelled with their periods and sources: about 15,796 to early October 2025 (The Federal, citing the state’s Directorate of Public Health) and about 20,866 for January to November 2025 (IANS).

Updated 15 November 2026: rewritten as charts; figures unchanged.

Madurai's sewerage modernisation is ₹2,400 crore, not ₹1,400 crore

What it means: The corrected Madurai figure is a correction, not a rounding difference.

What to do: Check the Madurai figure against the announcement before citing either number.

Malaria fell from 5,587 cases in 2015, but drainage is not shown as the cause

What it means: No Tamil Nadu study found links the malaria decline to drainage investment.

What to do: Treat drainage as a plausible contributor to malaria control, not a proven cause.

Dengue's main mosquito breeds in stored water, so drains are a weak lever

What it means: Aedes breeds mainly in household water containers, so underground sewerage reaches it less directly.

What to do: Count dengue against household-container controls, not drainage upgrades alone.

What would fix this

  • Tamil Nadu's Municipal Administration and Water Supply Department should publish one table of every FY2026-27 sewerage and drinking-water commitment, tagged by source — interim budget, Revised Budget, Rule 110 announcement, AMRUT 2.0 or KfW loan, so figures from three fiscal events in seven months are not conflated.
  • The Tamil Nadu government should publish the component-wise cost of the 2 September 2026 ₹4,800 crore announcement, stating the Minjur desalination plant's own cost separately, so the conflict between outlets reporting it as a package total and as the plant's cost alone is settled from a primary document.
  • CMWSSB and the TWAD Board should commission and publish a feasibility assessment for producing compressed biogas from sludge at their largest sewage treatment plants, so the opportunity this post identifies under SATAT is either costed or ruled out, rather than left as an untested gap.
  • CPCB should publish, in a single notified table on its website, the sewage treatment plant discharge limits in force after the NGT's 30 April 2019 order, including COD, TSS and any total-nitrogen limit, so the conflicting figures this post found can be checked against official text.
  • Tamil Nadu's Directorate of Public Health should publish annual district-wise dengue and malaria case counts, with indigenous and imported cases separated, so dengue figures stop conflicting across sources and the malaria decline can be traced to its causes.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

Corrected 25 October 2026. Bengaluru's ₹120 crore is a revenue target over about two decades (around ₹6 crore a year), not annual revenue (ETV Bharat, 20 April 2026); the Ghaziabad plant is a planned WABAG/PEAK BOT project and its 1.8 tonnes/day CBG figure and derived 0.026 t/MLD benchmark have been removed as unsourced (WABAG filing, 13 May 2026); the faecal coliform standard is “desirable” 100 / “permissible” 230 MPN/100 mL, with COD 50, TSS 20 and total nitrogen 10 mg/L confirmed (CPCB report in NGT OA 200/2014); and Tamil Nadu dengue cases were 27,378 (2024) and 23,407 (2025) per NCVBDC.

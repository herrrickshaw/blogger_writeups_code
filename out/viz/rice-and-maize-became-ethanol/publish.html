India built its ethanol programme by giving grain a guaranteed buyer, a price fixed in advance, a market and warehouse finance. Biomass for power has a mandate and a 2023 benchmark price, but no grades, no index and no warehouse receipts. Europe and Japan show how residues become tradeable.

Straw for power in India: 2023 benchmark price, no grades, index or warehouse receipts

Top line

  • Rs 68.86 a litre: the 2026-27 price oil companies pay for maize ethanol, fixed before the year begins.
  • Rs 2,390 a quintal: the notified price of FCI rice for distilleries from November 2026, below FCI's cost.
  • Rs 6,356–9,080 a tonne: the 2023 benchmark pellet price, converted at the specification's calorific range.
Updated 9 November 2026. WDRA added nine more commodities to its warehouse-receipt lists by a notification of 16 June 2026 (Gazette CG-DL-E-27062026-273885): karchura, chia seeds, mango pulp, roasted chicory powder, kalonji and mahua flower, plus DAP, rock phosphate and magnesium sulphate. As in 2024 and 2025, no biomass pellets, briquettes or baled straw were added; the text now covers the 2026 addition too. The 2026-27 ethanol tender closed for bids on 5 November and allocations have not yet been published.
Updated 28 October 2026. Added a section on bamboo for the Numaligarh 2G ethanol plant of Assam Bio Ethanol Pvt Ltd: the 2017 change that took bamboo grown outside forests out of the definition of a tree, bamboo’s addition to WDRA’s warehouse-receipt list in 2025, and the plant’s chipping-unit and direct-payment collection model.

Updated 15 November 2026: rewritten as charts; figures unchanged.

Each feedstock has its own ethanol price, fixed before the supply year starts

What it means: A distiller can see in advance that maize earns Rs 68.86 a litre and C-heavy molasses Rs 57.97.

What to do: Publish a price per tonne for straw before each kharif season, as the ethanol programme does for each feedstock.

FCI sells rice to distilleries well below its own cost of about Rs 3,889

What it means: FCI sold 6.35 million tonnes of rice worth about Rs 14,597 crore to distilleries between June 2025 and June 2026.

What to do: Report FCI's cost beside each notified rice price, so the gap the government absorbs is visible every year.

Pellet prices clear estimated cost; grades, an index and storage are missing

What it means: The benchmark works out to about Rs 6,356 to 9,080 a tonne across the specification's calorific range.

What to do: Direct effort at pellet grades, a price index and warehouse receipts, since the price already clears estimated cost.

Bamboo supply lagged: the plant was in start-up because raw material was short

What it means: About 4,200 farmers had registered by February 2026, against a target of 30,000 within 300 km over three years.

What to do: Publish a price per tonne for bamboo, the one input the post says the plant still lacks, so suppliers can plan.

What would fix this

  • The Ministry of Power should notify a per-tonne benchmark price for agro-residue pellets and briquettes each year before the kharif harvest, by region and grade, and publish what plants actually paid.
  • BIS should publish grades for agro-residue pellets and briquettes aligned with ISO 17225, and the model co-firing contract should reference them.
  • WDRA should notify biomass pellets, briquettes and baled straw as commodities for negotiable warehouse receipts, with storage norms for moisture and fire safety.
  • DGFT and CBIC should create separate tariff lines for agro-residue pellets and briquettes, so that production and trade can be tracked.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

What this piece could not establish

The benchmark pellet price is the November 2023 figure, valid for a year; no later revision was found, and the per-tonne range is this blog’s conversion at the specification’s calorific range. Only WDRA’s 2024 and 2025 additions were read in the gazette, and the June 2026 one from a summary of it; its full list of notified commodities was not, so this piece does not claim no biomass item has ever been notified. Exchange trading volumes for wood-pellet futures were not found. Maize’s exchange trading in 2026 was not checked. Ethanol prices are tender prices, not what was actually allocated by feedstock this year, and every award in the 2026-27 tender is provisional pending Supreme Court cases. ABEPL’s sourcing figures are from February 2026 and its price per tonne of bamboo was not found; capacity is reported as 49,000 tonnes or 50,000 tonnes of ethanol a year, and feedstock as 5 lakh tonnes of green bamboo or about 3 lakh tonnes of dry bamboo. The public support found for its sourcing came from the Assam forest department’s nurseries; no announcement of a separate state bamboo board role was found.

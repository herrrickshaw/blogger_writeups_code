Three real countries have already built three structurally different answers to "how much should a litre of clean fuel earn beyond the pump price": America strips a paper credit off every gallon and lets refiners trade it; Europe doesn't trade anything, it just weights which fuels count more toward a supplier's target; Brazil hands the producer a directly sellable, exchange-traded tonne of avoided carbon. India has none of the three, an ethanol-blending programme that already runs into how fast pump prices can politically move, a Paris Agreement credit list that excluded ethanol the first time it was written and has already been revised once, and a new EU carbon border tax creating real demand for exactly the kind of verified carbon performance these three systems were built to certify. This piece works out what a hybrid built from pieces of all three could actually look like here — and where the real gaps are.

On RED: the EU’s revised directive (RED III, Directive 2023/2413, in force since November 2023) replaced RED II’s 14% transport target with a choice of a 29% renewable share or a 14.5% cut in transport greenhouse-gas intensity by 2030, plus a combined 5.5% sub-target for advanced biofuels and renewable fuels of non-biological origin; the RED II description below should be read with that in mind. India’s Article 6.2 list briefly grew to 14 activities when clean cooking was added, then went back to 13 when cookstoves were dropped (reported September 2025); ethanol blending was still not on it in reports found for this update.

CBAM certificate price rose to €82.32 in Q3 2026, but ethanol is outside its scope

What it means: The CBAM prices are €75.36 for Q1, €75.28 for Q2 and €82.32 for Q3 2026, and its scope covers steel, aluminium, cement, fertilisers, hydrogen and electricity, not ethanol.

What to do: The Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change and the Department of Commerce should publish whether CCTS instruments qualify as a carbon price under CBAM Article 9.

What would fix this

  • The National Designated Authority for the Implementation of the Paris Agreement should publish the criteria it uses to add or drop Article 6.2 activities, and state whether ethanol blending is under consideration, since the initial three-year window has elapsed and ethanol remains off a list that already includes CBG and SAF.
  • The Bureau of Energy Efficiency should publish a draft lifecycle carbon-intensity methodology for Indian ethanol by feedstock route, in the manner of RenovaCalc, so that any tiered or producer-specific ethanol credit has an agreed basis for distinguishing molasses, cane-juice and grain ethanol.
  • The Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change and the Department of Commerce should publish India’s assessment of whether CCTS instruments qualify as a carbon price under CBAM Article 9, so that exporters of steel, cement and fertiliser know whether domestic payments can be deducted from EU certificate bills.
  • The Directorate of Marketing and Inspection should add daily arrival quantities to the Agmarknet price feed published on data.gov.in, so that moves like maize up 7.7% and rice up 12.1% in three weeks can be read as demand-led or supply-led, which price-only data cannot show.
  • The Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas should publish a quarterly feedstock-cost index for maize, FCI rice and molasses set against ethanol offtake prices, so that the squeeze on distillers locked into fixed-price contracts is measured before a credit mechanism is designed to absorb it.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

What this piece could not establish
Caveat. The three-year window from the original February 2023 notification has now technically elapsed. Whether NDAIAPA has a further revision scheduled, and whether ethanol blending (as distinct from CBG, which is already eligible) is under consideration for addition, is not established in the sources gathered for this piece — it's a live, open question, not a settled gap, and worth checking MoEFCC's own releases directly before assuming either outcome.

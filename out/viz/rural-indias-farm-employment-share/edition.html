India's own quarterly labour-force bulletins show rural agriculture's share of employment falling 5.6 percentage points in just two quarters — a move this size is usually described as a decades-long structural trend, but two quarters is also too short a series to rule out an ordinary seasonal swing in farm labour demand.

India's rural farm-employment share: down 5.6 points in 2 quarters, cause unclear

Top line

  • 52.9% of rural workers were in agriculture in Apr–Jun 2026, down 5.6 points from 58.5% two quarters earlier.
  • 24.4% of rural workers were in the secondary sector in Apr–Jun 2026, up from 20.9%.
  • 4.8% rural unemployment in Apr–Jun 2026, up from 4.0% over the same quarters.
Updated 7 November 2026. The same-quarter comparison this piece said was missing exists. MoSPI’s press note for the July–September 2025 bulletin gives agriculture’s share of rural employment as 53.5% in April–June 2025, rising to 57.7% in July–September 2025, which MoSPI attributed to kharif farm operations, before the 58.5% (October–December 2025), 55.8% and 52.9% shown below. Year on year, April–June 2026 is only 0.6 points below April–June 2025, and the rural female share is almost unchanged (70.9% then, 70.7% now). On this five-quarter series, most of the 5.6-point fall below is the farm calendar, not a structural shift; the headline’s open question is largely answered in favour of seasonality. The July–September 2026 bulletin, due later this year, will give the next same-quarter check.

What it means: Agriculture's share of rural employment fell from 58.5% to 52.9% across three quarters, a 5.6-point drop.

What to do: Compare the same two quarters over several years before reading this drop as a structural shift rather than the farming calendar.

The secondary sector absorbed most of the shift, up 3.5 points

What it means: The secondary sector gained 3.5 points over the two quarters, more than the 2.1-point rise in services.

What to do: Check the state-level tables in the same bulletins to see whether this gain is broad-based or concentrated in a few states.

What it means: At least some people leaving farm work are not shown as employed anywhere else, since unemployment rose as farm share fell.

What to do: Treat the farm-to-non-farm transition story as incomplete until later bulletins show where the unemployed rural workers went.

What would fix this

  • The National Statistical Office should add a same-quarter, year-on-year comparison table to every rural PLFS quarterly bulletin once a second year of data exists, so that whether a 5.6-point fall in agriculture's share is seasonal or structural is answered in the release itself.
  • The National Statistical Office should publish back-cast quarterly rural estimates for earlier PLFS years, as far as the earlier survey design allows, so that the seasonal pattern between October–December and April–June can be measured now rather than after waiting a further year.
  • The National Statistical Office should report the rural unemployed by the sector of their last job, so that whether the rise from 4.0 to 4.8 per cent in rural unemployment comes from people who left agricultural work can be read directly rather than inferred.
  • The National Statistical Office should publish state-wise rural sector shares from the quarterly bulletins as a machine-readable series through its data API, so that whether the secondary-sector gain is broad-based or concentrated in a few states can be checked without extracting PDF tables.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

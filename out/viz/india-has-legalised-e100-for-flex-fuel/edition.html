Flex-fuel cars in India: E100 legalised, E85 costs more per kilometre than petrol

Top line

  • Rs 72.1 a litre is where E100 matches petrol per km, just above the Rs 71.2 that ethanol already costs delivered.
  • 6% more per km for E85 at Rs 82.12 than E20 petrol at Rs 102.12.
  • 8% of petrol demand is all that today's unsold ethanol could move to E100.
Updated 9 November 2026. The final rules, G.S.R. 911(E), are dated 23 October 2026 and were published in the Gazette on 26 October (SCC Online’s summary of the notification). Besides putting E100 next to E85 in rule 115, they send E100 vehicles to a new Annexure SA and update the E10 and B10 references to E10/E20 and B100. Brazil’s regular petrol has carried a temporary 32% ethanol (E32) since 1 August 2026, not 30%. Delhi E20 petrol is still Rs 102.12 (6 November), so the per-kilometre arithmetic stands.
Updated 28 October 2026. Checked against the Gazette of India: the April 2026 draft (CG-DL-E-28042026-272125) proposed changing “(E85)” to “(E85) or (E100)” in the emission and certification tables of the Central Motor Vehicles Rules, with a separate annexure for E100 vehicles, as described here. A second draft, G.S.R. 634(E) of 13 July 2026 (CG-DL-E-27072026-274834), added a reference fuel for E100 and would require makers to state, on a sticker on each vehicle, whether it is compatible with E85 and E100. The final G.S.R. 911(E) is not in the index this blog used, so it is still described from press reports. No figure changed.

What it means: Ethanol at Rs 71.2 delivered leaves under Rs 1 a litre for excise, VAT, the dealer and the oil company's own costs.

What to do: Give E100 a tax rate below petrol's, as Brazil's constitution requires for biofuels, before expecting E100 sales.

E85 costs about 6% more per km than E20 petrol, even at Rs 82.12 a litre

What it means: E85 carries about 76% of petrol's energy per litre, so it costs more per km even though its pump price is 20% lower.

What to do: Price E85 at or below about Rs 77.7 a litre, or raise flex-fuel engine efficiency on ethanol, before drivers gain from it.

Unsold ethanol of 576 crore litres would cover flex-fuel cars taking about 8% of petrol

What it means: India's distilleries offered 1,776 crore litres for 2025-26, and about 576 crore litres still had no buyer after the top-up.

What to do: Size any E100 programme to the unsold surplus first, because every litre in a flex-fuel tank is one not used to raise the petrol blend.

What would fix this

  • The Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas and the states should publish how E85 and E100 are priced and taxed, and set a rule that keeps their price per kilometre below petrol’s, as Brazil’s constitution does, before asking people to buy flex-fuel vehicles.
  • Oil companies should post E85 and E100 prices with a per-kilometre comparison at the pump, so buyers can see whether the cheaper litre is the cheaper journey.
  • The ethanol roadmap should state how much ethanol is reserved for flex-fuel vehicles and how much for raising the blend in ordinary petrol, since the same supply cannot do both.
  • Vehicle makers should publish certified fuel economy for each flex-fuel model on both E20 and E100, so the efficiency gain on ethanol is measured rather than assumed.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

What this piece could not establish

The final notification, G.S.R. 911(E) of 23 October 2026, is not in the Gazette of India index this blog used, so its contents come from press reports and SCC Online’s summary of its text; they match the two drafts, of 27 April and 13 July 2026, which were read in the Gazette of India’s own text through the SansadSaar index of the archive.org Gazette of India collection (CG-DL-E-28042026-272125; G.S.R. 634(E), CG-DL-E-27072026-274834). No E100 retail price has been published, so the E100 figures are break-even prices, not quotes. The per-kilometre comparison assumes the same efficiency per unit of energy on every fuel, with a 5% gain as a sensitivity; real results will depend on each engine. The tax arithmetic leaves out the dealer’s commission and oil company costs, and treats Delhi’s VAT as 19.4% of the pre-VAT price. Brazil’s energy shares use this blog’s assumed energy content for hydrous ethanol (20.2 MJ/L) and an average 2025 blend of 28.25%. Reports differ on whether Indian Oil’s 2024 “Ethanol 100” was pure ethanol or a blend with additives.

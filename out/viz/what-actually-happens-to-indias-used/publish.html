India generates used lubricating oil the same way every industrial economy does — but the paper trail around it is thin. A 2023 rule finally puts a recycling target and a government portal behind it, one association is trying to build the collection network that target requires, and the trade data shows almost none of the used oil crosses a border either way. This is what actually holds up.

Used motor oil in India: rules changed in 2023, the cost is not yet estimated

Top line

  • $196 million is the peak year for India's used-oil exports, after a nine-year swing from under $1 million.
  • 61% of EU collected waste oil was regenerated to base oil in 2018, per the JRC.
  • 5% to 50% is India's EPR recycling target by FY2030-31, measured against sales.
Updated 7 November 2026. CPCB’s own used-oil EPR dashboard, read for a later piece on this blog, showed 119 producers granted registration out of 252 applications as of 10 August 2026, against a 2024-25 recycling target of 68,946 tonnes (5% of base-year sales). That target implies roughly 13.8 lakh tonnes of base and lubricating oil sold in 2022-23 — oil sold, not used oil generated, so it neither confirms nor replaces the “1.3 million tonnes” industry claim discussed below. No amendment to the G.S.R. 677(E) target schedule was found as of November 2026. See India’s Used-Oil EPR Wants Half of All Lubricant Recycled by 2030. What to do: Watch for a generation-versus-collection figure from CPCB to replace the vendor-blog estimate.

India's EPR recycling target climbs from 5% to 50% of sales by FY2030-31

What it means: The rule sets a stepped target that producers meet by buying certificates from registered recyclers.

What to do: Check whether the target is met, and whether collection centres can report volumes against it.

The EU regenerates 61% of collected waste oil, with the rest burned for energy

What it means: The EU benchmark is the only institutional figure here, and it favours regeneration on emissions and cost.

What to do: Ask for a cost-benefit study like the JRC's before India sets targets beyond its current 50% goal.

What would fix this

  • CPCB should publish annual used-oil generation, collection and re-refining volumes by state, compiled from the returns filed on its EPR portal, so that the vendor-sourced “1.3 million tonnes, under 15% recycled” figure is replaced by a government number.
  • The Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change should commission and publish a cost-benefit study of the used-oil EPR trajectory to 50% by FY2030-31, of the kind the EU Joint Research Centre ran before advising against pushing regeneration far beyond 61%.
  • CPCB should publish, in one primary document, the Environmental Compensation rate for used oil and the rule linking EPR certificate prices to it, so that the ₹30–60 per kg rate and 30–100% band no longer rest only on compliance-consultancy summaries.
  • The Bureau of Energy Efficiency should assess and publish whether used-oil re-refining can qualify as an offset activity under India’s Carbon Credit Trading Scheme, given that the only published methodology this post found, from the American Carbon Registry, is restricted to North America.
  • DGCI&S should publish quantity alongside value, and port of shipment, for exports under HS 27109900, so that swings from under $1 million to $196 million a year can be traced to volumes and consignments rather than left unexplained.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

Sources and caveats

Update of 7 November 2026: CPCB used-oil EPR portal registration and target figures as reported in this blog’s used-oil EPR registration piece (CPCB national dashboard, Producer tab, 10 August 2026).

The re-refining process description (dehydration, vacuum distillation, product cuts, hydrofinishing/solvent extraction/clay treatment mechanisms, and API Group I/II thresholds) draws on a combination of sources of mixed strength: a peer-reviewed review article (MDPI Energies, 14(10):2937) and a ResearchGate paper on solvent-extraction recycling are the stronger sources; several Scribd-hosted student presentations and course documents, and vendor process-description pages (Crystal Clean, GFL), were also used for descriptive detail and are weaker, non-peer-reviewed sources included because the underlying process description is well-established industry practice independently corroborated across sources, not because any single Scribd document should be treated as authoritative. The piston-ring/blow-by mechanism explanation was corrected during drafting from an initial "O-ring" framing to the accurate "piston ring" mechanism. India's EPR rule (G.S.R. 677(E), 18 September 2023) is sourced to a FAO regulatory mirror of the MoEFCC gazette text, cross-checked against independent trade-compliance summaries, because CPCB's own EPR portal returned a certificate error during this research; verify directly against CPCB or the official e-Gazette before relying on this for compliance purposes. EOSA's description is drawn entirely from its own website (eosa.in), which states no collection volumes or centre counts; nothing about its actual operating scale is independently verified here. The DGCI&S trade table (HS 27109100/27109900) is this article's own analysis of the same 8-digit trade dataset used elsewhere on this blog. The Indian testing-laboratory and EPC company list (IOCL R&D, Intertek India, SGS India, Bureau Veritas India, TUV Rheinland/TUV SUD India, Engineers India Limited, L&T Energy Hydrocarbon, Tata Consulting Engineers, Reliance's EPC arm, Technip Energies India, Petrofac India) establishes general industry capacity and was not independently verified against any specific used-oil re-refining project for any of these companies; the Italian comparison set (Eni/Enilive, Saipem, Snam, Maire Tecnimont/KT, RINA, SGS/Bureau Veritas/Intertek Italy, Innovhub SSI) carries the same caveat. The widely circulated "1.3 million tonnes generated annually, under 15% formally recycled" figure traces to vendor and consultancy blogs (not CPCB or MoEFCC) and is reported here explicitly as an unverified industry claim, consistent with this blog's standing treatment of vendor market-size estimates as weak by default. The American Carbon Registry methodology details are drawn directly from the published PDF (Version 1.0, February 2019, americancarbonregistry.org). This article does not attempt to size a market opportunity, recommend an investment, or draw a policy conclusion; nothing here is investment, environmental compliance, or trade advice.

India's double-taxation network is nearly universal — 97 countries, built up over six decades, still being amended monthly. Its free-trade network is a much newer and much smaller thing: a handful of agreements inherited from the SAARC and ASEAN era, plus a fast-moving cluster of "new generation" deals signed only in the last four years. The two networks answer different questions — one about where income gets taxed, the other about what tariff a shipment pays — and reading them side by side shows how much further India's trade-agreement reach still has to run to catch its tax-treaty reach.

Top line

  • 97 countries have a comprehensive tax treaty (DTAA) with India in force, per the Income Tax Department's live database.
  • 38 countries sit in the government's nine new-generation trade agreements, far fewer than the tax network reaches.
Updated 12 October 2026. The India–New Zealand FTA, listed below as “announced December 2025”, was signed on 27 April 2026; New Zealand’s Parliament passed the implementing law in mid-September, and Commerce Minister Piyush Goyal said it enters into force on 20 October 2026, so it is not yet in force at the time of this update. The India–EU FTA was concluded in January 2026 and is in legal vetting; both sides were reported in July as aiming to sign by end-2026, with entry into force likely in early 2027. Updated 7 November 2026. The India–New Zealand FTA entered into force on 20 October 2026, as scheduled (New Zealand’s foreign ministry; Deccan Herald); the table now shows that date. So of the four agreements the government announced in 2025-26, three are in force (UK, Oman, New Zealand) and the India–EU FTA is still awaiting signature, now reported for mid-December 2026. On the tax side, the comprehensive-agreement count of 97 is unchanged, but two more amending protocols have taken effect: India–Sri Lanka (Notification 88/2026, 16 July 2026) and India–Mauritius (in force 10 August 2026, notified as S.O. 5611(E) on 13 October 2026), both adding the anti-treaty-shopping Principal Purpose Test.

What would fix this

  • The Department of Commerce should name every agreement counted in its “Nine FTAs spanning 38 countries” figure and restore the release’s linked detail PDF, so readers can check the headline count agreement by agreement instead of finding only eight named.
  • DGFT should extend Appendix 2A into one maintained table of all FTAs and PTAs, with signing date, in-force date and partner countries, updated whenever an agreement enters into force, so status changes like India–UK and India–Oman do not have to be pieced together from separate PIB releases.
  • CBDT should add a one-row-per-country view to its DTAA database showing the comprehensive agreement, latest protocol, MLI synthesised text and any TIEA, so the gap between 173 instruments and 97 countries is shown by the database itself rather than explained by readers.
  • The Department of Commerce should publish a partner-country table that shows, for each of the 97 DTAA countries, whether an FTA, PTA or neither is in force with India, so the difference between the tax-treaty and trade-treaty networks can be measured directly.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

What this piece could not establish
What this article does not establish. The exact distinct-country total the DTAA database's 173-instrument count would produce if every comprehensive agreement, protocol and synthesised text were correctly de-duplicated to one country each — this article reports the database's own 97-count for “Comprehensive Agreements” type directly rather than attempting that de-duplication itself, since the department's own filter already performs it. Whether the India–UK CETA, India–Oman CEPA, India–New Zealand FTA and India–EU FTA have entered into force since the 6 March 2026 PIB release that first announced them — not confirmed; this article states their status as of that release rather than assuming a later force-entry date.

Semaglutide's Indian patent expired on 20 March 2026; within weeks, 13 companies had launched 26 branded generics, Novo Nordisk cut its own list prices by up to 48% in response, and the government opened a parallel crackdown on unauthorised sales of the same drugs it had just let a dozen companies copy. All three things are true at once, and none of them cancel each other out.

Semaglutide in India after patent expiry: 26 brands from 13 firms in weeks

Skip to article content
Text Size

Top line

  • 26 semaglutide brands from 13 companies launched within weeks of the March 2026 patent expiry.
  • 33% of India's GLP-1 segment in March 2026, up from 25% a month earlier.
  • 49 entities inspected by the drug regulator in the same month, with irregularities flagged.
Updated 22 October 2026. Correction: this post says the semaglutide compound patent “is scheduled to lapse” in China, Brazil, Turkey and South Africa later in 2026 and into 2027. According to IP-firm and industry summaries, it had already lapsed in China and Brazil around 20 March 2026, the same time as in India. Compound protection has also reportedly ended in South Africa, and Canada's exclusivity ended in January 2026. Turkey is described only as “expired or expiring soon”. Entry into these markets now depends on regulatory approvals and any secondary patents, not on a later expiry date. Dr. Reddy's has said it will export to Canada, Turkey and Brazil. Sources: Abel + Imray, Reddie & Grose, Pearce IP. The original text below is unchanged.
Updated 7 November 2026. Later Pharmarack data put India’s whole GLP-1 market at ₹1,906 crore in the year to May 2026, up 237%, with tirzepatide at ₹1,207 crore and semaglutide at ₹589 crore. Torrent led the semaglutide generics with about 38% of generic sales in April (about ₹17 crore). By June the rush had levelled off: the semaglutide market was about ₹27 crore that month (₹29 crore in May), of which generics were about ₹8 crore, though generics made up most of the units sold. Dr. Reddy’s has since cut its semaglutide guidance after a supply problem; see this blog’s separate piece on that. The March share figures below are kept as the first month after expiry. Sources: Business Today, Medical Dialogues, BioSpectrum (Pharmarack, June).

Updated 15 November 2026: rewritten as charts; figures unchanged.

Generics launched at ₹1,300–4,200 a month, against Novo's ₹10,000–22,000

What it means: Named generic makers, including Sun Pharma and Dr Reddy's, were in market within days of the 21 March launch, with prices from ₹1,300.

What to do: Track whether generic prices hold as the launch rush levels off.

Within a month, semaglutide's share of India's GLP-1 category rose from 25% to 33%

What it means: Eli Lilly's overall share fell from 61% to 56% in the first month after expiry, while Novo's own branded share held at 25%.

What to do: Watch the next monthly share data to see whether semaglutide's gain holds.

Regulators tightened GLP-1 enforcement in the same fortnight as the launch

  1. 10 March 2026CDSCO advisory bars surrogate advertising and indirect promotion of GLP-1 drugs
  2. 20 March 2026Semaglutide composition patent expires in India
  3. 24 March 2026Government has inspected 49 entities and flagged irregularities

What it means: By 24 March the government had inspected 49 entities, flagging irregularities in unauthorised sales and non-compliant promotion.

What to do: Watch whether the notices issued to several entities lead to licence cancellations or prosecution.

What would fix this

  • CDSCO should publish the outcome of its March 2026 GLP-1 inspections — how many of the 49 entities had irregularities, what notices were issued and what action followed, so the enforcement half of the launch can be checked rather than known only through broadcast summaries.
  • CDSCO should publish a list of every approved semaglutide formulation, with manufacturer, indication and approval date, so the count of 26 brands from 13 companies, and the 50-plus expected, can be verified against the regulator's own record rather than trade press.
  • The National Pharmaceutical Pricing Authority should publish the launch and current retail prices of each semaglutide brand, so the fall from ₹10,000–22,000 a month to ₹1,300–4,200 is tracked from an official source as the market expands.
  • The Pharmacovigilance Programme of India should publish a periodic summary of adverse-event reports for semaglutide and tirzepatide, so the 'serious adverse effects' the government warned about are measured as use widens, rather than asserted.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

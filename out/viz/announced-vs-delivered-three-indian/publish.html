Every state industry department publishes a policy PDF full of targets. Far fewer publish what actually happened afterward. We went looking for the after-numbers on five state policies — three had a verifiable trail from the start; two took a second, deeper pass to find one.

Top line

  • 241% rise in UP EV subsidy approvals in FY2025-26, after a dip the year before.
  • 30 of 100 pack-houses targeted this fiscal year by Haryana's cluster programme were built.
  • ₹36,500 crore revised Karnataka excise estimate for 2024-25, down from the ₹38,525 crore budget.
Updated 18 October 2026. The Karnataka excise table below appears to have two years’ targets reversed. Search-result summaries of budget coverage (The Week, March 2025) put the 2025-26 excise target at ₹40,000 crore, up from a 2024-25 revised estimate of ₹36,500 crore, while the ₹45,000 crore figure is reported as the 2026-27 target set alongside the 2026 liquor-tax changes. For the outcome, Deccan Herald reported ₹16,358 crore collected in April–August 2025 against a target of ₹16,290 crore, and a June 2026 Deccan Chronicle report, citing a Tamil Nadu government white paper, put Karnataka’s 2025-26 collection at about ₹41,000 crore. If that is confirmed, 2025-26 met its target and is an exception to the “misses or downgrades it mid-year” pattern described below. Neither figure has been checked against Karnataka’s own budget documents or finance accounts. The original text below is unchanged.
Updated 12 October 2026. The statement below that Haryana’s MSME-specific successor policy “has not been notified” is out of date. Press reports say the Haryana Cabinet approved the Haryana Progressive MSME and Export Promotion Policy 2026 on 28 July 2026 and that it was notified on 29 July 2026 (a ₹55,000 crore investment target, zone-based incentives replacing district-based ones). Whether it closes the gap for food-processing MSMEs is not yet visible in outcome data, and the article’s verdict wording on Haryana should be read in that light. The notified text was not opened; check the exact title. Sources: Tribune, KNN India. The original text below is unchanged.
Updated 7 November 2026. Two gaps below now have partial numbers. UP bio-energy: reports put the state’s operational compressed-biogas capacity at about 350 tonnes a day (a state official at IBET 2026, AgroSpectrum) or 213 tonnes a day across 25 plants (Bioenergy Times, date unclear), against the policy’s 1,000 tonnes a day for 2026-27; plant counts range from about 20 (Asianet, August 2026) to 36 (GOBARdhan portal, via KNN India), so the ~32 below is within a wide range. Karnataka excise: the 2025-26 target was reported raised from ₹40,000 crore to ₹43,000 crore, with ₹33,371 crore (77.6%) collected by end-January (The Hans India); 2024-25 collections are reported at about ₹35,500–35,800 crore against the ₹36,500 crore revised estimate. The final 2025-26 figure was not found. These are search-result summaries.

What it means: Approvals ran at 15,091, then 13,950, before the FY2025-26 surge to 47,514.

What to do: Publish a running scoreboard for the manufacturing and jobs side, as the portal already does for buyer subsidies.

Haryana's cluster programme has built 30 of 100 targeted pack-houses this year

What it means: Thirty pack-houses are built against a target of 100 for this fiscal year, with 35 under way.

What to do: Report in-year targets against built counts for every linkage scheme, rather than only at year end.

Karnataka cut its 2024-25 excise estimate from the budget figure mid-year

What it means: The original budget of ₹38,525 crore was cut to a ₹36,500 crore revised estimate.

What to do: Judge excise performance by the year-on-year collection trend, not by the announced target.

What would fix this

  • Invest UP should publish, project by project, the capital subsidy sanctioned and disbursed under the UP EV Manufacturing & Mobility Policy 2022, including whether the two ultra-mega battery project slots have been filled, so manufacturing delivery can be scored against the ₹30,000-crore target.
  • UPNEDA should publish the operational capacity in tonnes per day of every CBG plant supported under the UP Bio-Energy Policy 2022, plus bio-coal and bioethanol/biodiesel output, so the 1,000 TPD target for 2026–27 is checked against an aggregate rather than a plant count.
  • Haryana's Industries and Commerce Department should report how many of the maximum 20 collection centres and distribution hubs under Section 5.2.4 of the 2018 food processing policy were sanctioned and built, and notify a date for the pending MSME & Export Policy, closing the gap food processors now sit in.
  • Andhra Pradesh's food processing department should publish commissioned-versus-approved status for the 29 projects worth ₹11,800 crore it cites, so progress towards the ₹30,000-crore Food Processing Policy 4.0 target is tracked in delivered investment rather than approvals.
  • The CAG should take up a scheme-specific performance audit of at least one of these policies, starting with the UP Bio-Energy Policy 2022, since none of the five has ever had one and that scheme has ₹1,040.75 crore sanctioned with no state-published scorecard.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

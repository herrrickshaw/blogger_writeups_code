One Nation One Ration Card sounds like it should strain the machinery that moves grain around India — a family that migrates from Bihar to Kerala still has to eat, after all. It doesn't, and the reason why is itself the more interesting story: interstate portability is roughly 2% or less of all transactions on the system. The genuine food-versus-fuel tension in Indian grain policy is real, well-documented, and running through a completely different pipe — FCI's surplus rice being sold to ethanol distillers at roughly 40% below what it cost to buy, under a tender clause that specifically requires distillers to use it.

Top line

  • 2.4% is the most that interstate use can be of ONORC’s monthly transactions (under 0.5 million against over 20 million within states), so portability moves entitlement, not grain.
  • Under 12% of the 554.99 lakh tonne NFSA allocation is what ONORC portability delivered, about 64 lakh tonnes in the first 10 months of 2025.
  • ₹9,000–10,000 crore is this post’s own back-of-envelope gap on the 6.35 million tonnes of FCI rice sold to ethanol distillers roughly 40% below acquisition cost; it is not an official total.
Updated 7 November 2026. The ESY 2026-27 ethanol tender this piece said had not yet appeared was issued on 16 October 2026 for about 1,100 crore litres. It drops the 40% FCI-rice floor described below: FCI rice (72 LMT to 30 June 2027, at Rs 2,390 a quintal from 1 November) is instead capped at 108 crore litres of ethanol a quarter, and cane juice, syrup and B-heavy molasses are not bought at all; see India’s 2026-27 Ethanol Tender Drops Cane Juice. Central-pool stocks stood at 870.45 LMT on 1 September 2026 (wheat 479.92, rice 390.53), against a 1 October buffer norm of 307.70 LMT (Business Today).

What it means: A beneficiary authenticates by Aadhaar at any Fair Price Shop and draws grain the shop already lifted from its own state’s allocation, so interstate use is at most about 2.4% (0.5 of roughly 20.5 million) and nothing is trucked between states for the transaction.

What to do: Read Maharashtra’s 8-to-1 net inflow of other-state card-holders as a signal for that state’s own allocation planning, not as a national figure, until dated interstate counts are published.

Two different 40% figures, and the other pressures on the same grain
  • Two 40%s: the ESY 2025-26 tender (Tender 22376, 23 September 2025) required FCI surplus rice to be at least 40% of a bidder’s grain-based feedstock offer, a sourcing floor. The 40% in the price gap above is a discount, not a rule. The 7 November 2026 note says the ESY 2026-27 tender drops the floor.
  • Sugar side: sugar-based feedstocks got 289 crore litres, 28% of the ESY 2025-26 allocation, against the roughly 54% the NITI Aayog roadmap envisaged. ISMA asks for at least 50%.
  • Economic Survey 2025-26: reportedly flags early warning signals that maize expansion (production growth roughly 8.8% a year, area roughly 6.7%) is displacing pulses and oilseeds in Maharashtra and Karnataka. The Petroleum Ministry told Parliament that E20 has not affected food crop availability.
  • An inflated number: a reported ₹1,160 crore of rice diverted in Madhya Pradesh turned out, per FCI, to concern 490 bags worth roughly ₹5.63 lakh; the larger figure was the value of rice legitimately issued.
  • Storage plan: the cooperative grain storage plan targets 700 lakh tonnes, and 313 godowns completed so far deliver 1.80 lakh tonnes, roughly a quarter of one percent. It is a farmer-level scheme, not an ethanol-feedstock storage plan.
What does not follow, and what this piece could not establish
  • None of this shows ONORC is poorly designed or under strain; it was built as an entitlement and settlement system so it would not need to move grain, and the data suggest that is working.
  • It is not a claim that FCI’s ethanol-rice sales are illegal or mismanaged. The government’s surplus-only, notified-price framing is a coherent administrative position, even where the arithmetic suggests an implicit transfer.
  • The Economic Survey describes a risk of future cropping-pattern distortion, not a claim that current food security is compromised.
  • This piece could not establish whether the cane-cost complaint belongs to ISMA or to the All India Distillers’ Association, or whether ISMA’s position changed over time.
  • Single or search-indexed-only sourcing: the exact tender-clause wording, the Food Secretary’s statement, the ESY 2025-26 feedstock percentages and the Economic Survey growth rates. Advance estimates and the Maharashtra snapshot are directional, not exact.

Five fixes

  1. Department of Food and Public Distribution: publish monthly ONORC transactions on IM-PDS, split into intra-state and inter-state, so the inter-state share (at most about 2.4% on the cited abstract) is an official, dated figure.
  2. Department of Food and Public Distribution: publish each year the gap between FCI’s economic cost of rice and the OMSS(D) price, multiplied by tonnage, so the estimate of ₹9,000–10,000 crore becomes an official figure.
  3. Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas: publish each tender’s allocation by feedstock beside the roadmap’s planned split, so 28% sugar-based against roughly 54% is tracked.
  4. Department of Food and Public Distribution: publish the state-wise phase-in of the broken-grain specification and the tonnes actually freed, so the projected 8–9 million tonnes can be checked.
  5. Ministry of Cooperation: publish quarterly capacity completed under the grain storage plan against the 700 lakh tonne target.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

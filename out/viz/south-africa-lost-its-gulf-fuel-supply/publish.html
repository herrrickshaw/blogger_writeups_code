India already sells South Africa nearly two billion dollars of refined fuel a year, and lost a third of that market in FY2025-26 — before anything went wrong. Then the Strait of Hormuz closed, South Africa’s Gulf suppliers were cut off, and the replacement cargoes docking in Durban came from the United States. The real opening is not a cargo. It is that South Africa is currently rewriting the formula that prices every litre it imports.

Top line

  • −32.6% fall in India's refined-fuel exports to South Africa in FY2025-26, before the Gulf supply stopped.
  • 165,000 t of US refined fuel landed at Durban in April 2026 alone, replacing Gulf supply.
Updated 7 November 2026. Two things have moved since this piece was written. Indian refiners did pick up African demand after March: Kpler data showed India’s petroleum-product exports to Africa up 110% to about 370,000 barrels a day in May 2026, although later reporting said strong Gulf demand pulled barrels away from East and South Africa in June and July, and no South Africa-specific Indian export figure after March 2026 has been found. On pricing, South Africa’s Department of Mineral and Petroleum Resources says it has reviewed the Basic Fuel Price component and is still reviewing the Regulatory Accounting System, with completion targeted before 31 March 2027 (reported; search-snippet source). The biofuels question left open below now has a partial answer: regulations for an initial 2% biofuels penetration target were gazetted in August 2025, according to a November 2026 commentary (search-snippet source), with implementation still lagging.

The pump-price formula is indexed to three markets, none of them Indian

What it means: South Africa prices imported fuel off Mediterranean, Singapore and Arabian Gulf quotes, so India has no leg in the formula.

What to do: Seek an Indian assessment in the Basic Fuel Price basket while the formula is under review.

Replacement tankers came from the United States, the farther supplier

  1. FY2025-26Indian refined-fuel exports to South Africa fall 32.6% on the year
  2. April 2026About 165,000 t of US refined fuel lands at Durban, which takes about 80% of imports

What it means: Reported replacement cargoes for Durban came from the United States, even though India is roughly 6,000 nautical miles closer by sea.

What to do: Check South African trade data from April 2026 onward before claiming India captured none of the gap.

What would fix this

  • DGCI&S should populate the quantity columns for country-wise HS 2710 refined-petroleum exports in TradeStat, so a 32.6% fall in the value of sales to South Africa can be split into lost barrels and lower prices, which this post could not do.
  • PPAC should publish monthly refined-product export volumes by destination country, so whether India captured any of South Africa's lost Gulf supply after March 2026 can be checked against official data rather than one month of ship-tracking reports.
  • The Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas should state publicly whether India has asked South Africa's Department of Mineral and Petroleum Resources to add an Indian product assessment to the Basic Fuel Price basket, so progress on the one opening this post identifies can be tracked while the formula is under review.
  • South Africa's Department of Mineral and Petroleum Resources should publish the current status of its biofuels blending policy on its website, so exporters and analysts can establish whether a mandate exists, is gazetted but unimplemented, or is absent — the question this post could not answer.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

What this piece could not establish
What this article does not establish. Volumes: TRADESTAT returned no quantity columns, so every Indian figure is in dollars. South Africa’s total refined-product imports, its remaining refining capacity and the specific closures are not quantified here; the South African import-side statistics were not obtained, and gov.za was unreachable from this network. The April 2026 tanker figures are ship-tracking data reported by Reuters, not customs statistics, and cover one month at one port. No Indian export data exists in this article for the period after March 2026, so the two halves of the story are adjacent in time, not overlapping.

Solar cannot generate a single watt after sunset, which is precisely when India's residential demand — driven overwhelmingly by air conditioners and coolers — can rival or exceed the daytime peak. Biomass power can run at night. It is one of the few renewable sources that actually can. And yet, while India's total renewable capacity (excluding large hydro) grew 178% between December 2019 and July 2026, biomass power grew just 19% — the slowest of any renewable category, in the one slot on the grid where a dispatchable renewable should be winning.

Top line

  • 19% growth in bio-power capacity since December 2019, against 384% for solar.
  • 4.9% is bio-power's share of non-hydro renewable capacity in 2026, down from 11.4% in 2019.
  • ₹9.6–11.1 per kWh is the biomass levellised tariff in the proposed CERC order, against ₹2.5–3.5 for solar and wind.
Updated 7 November 2026. Two newer figures. Smart meters: a Rajya Sabha reply of 27 July 2026 put installations at 5.73 crore under the Revamped Distribution Sector Scheme, against 20.33 crore sanctioned, and 7.24 crore across all schemes, by 30 June 2026 (the 22 October note’s 5.8 crore was a February press figure). Capacity: the Central Electricity Authority’s biomass power line, a narrower definition than the MNRE bio-power total used below, was flat at 10,869 MW in its July and August 2026 reports, while total renewable capacity reached about 299 GW by September 2026 on MNRE data, so the gap this piece describes has widened. Sources: Ministry of Power, Rajya Sabha reply, CEA installed capacity, August 2026, Energetica India (MNRE data).
Updated 22 October 2026. Two points below have moved. First, the goal of 250 million smart meters by 2026 was not met. Press reports put installations at about 58 million (5.8 crore) by 15 February 2026, and say the Revamped Distribution Sector Scheme behind the rollout has been extended to 31 March 2028 (Business Standard, The Secretariat). That makes the point below stronger: most consumers are still not on Time-of-Day rates. Trade summaries say the final order kept the normative capital costs used below (TeamLease RegTech). We have not checked the final biomass tariffs against the ₹9.6–11.1/kWh range quoted here. What would fix this

  • MNRE should split its state-wise bio-power capacity table into grid-connected capacity selling under power purchase agreements and captive bagasse cogeneration at sugar mills, so the share of the 11,747.57 MW actually available to supply the evening peak is visible.
  • The Central Electricity Authority should publish monthly biomass generation by state split between the State Commission’s solar hours and peak hours, so it can be checked whether bio-power is in fact being dispatched into the window the Time-of-Day tariff rewards.
  • The Forum of Regulators should publish a state-by-state table of Time-of-Day tariff implementation under G.S.R. 437(E), by consumer category, with effective dates and smart meters installed, so the uneven roll-out the piece describes can be measured against the 250 million target.
  • State Electricity Regulatory Commissions should publish every biomass power purchase agreement they approve with its contracted tariff, date and any Time-of-Day component, so buyers at CERC’s ₹9.6–11.1/kWh normative tariff can be compared with legacy contracts that predate the 2023 rule.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

What this piece does not establish. The 2018 CSE Delhi demand analysis is cited only to illustrate a structural mechanism (AC-driven night demand rivalling day demand); its specific MW figures are eight years old and should not be read as current 2026 conditions, for Delhi or nationally. Whether biomass generators are actually receiving ToD-linked pricing in practice (as opposed to legacy power purchase agreements predating the 2023 rule) was not established in this piece's sourcing. The CSEP paper's grid-balancing analysis is based on 2019 data; more recent equivalent studies may exist but were not part of this piece's source set.

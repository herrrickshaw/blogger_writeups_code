On 14 October the Cabinet renewed India’s national pension scheme for the poor to 2031 with ₹53,428 crore, and raised only the disability pension. The old-age and widow amounts stay where they were set in 2007 and 2012, so what a pensioner gets depends almost entirely on the state.

Centre's old-age pension: ₹200 a month since 2007, worth what ₹63 bought then

Skip to article content
Text Size

Top line

  • ₹200 a month: the old-age pension for those aged 60 to 79, unchanged since 2007 and kept for 2026-31.
  • ₹63 is what ₹200 bought in 2007-08 rupees, deflated by the farm-labourer price index.
  • 3.20 crore people on the Centre's pensions and family benefit, across 36 States and UTs.

Updated 15 November 2026: rewritten as charts; figures unchanged.

₹200 now buys what ₹63 bought in 2007-08

What it means: The ₹200 old-age pension buys about a third of the 2007-08 value, and the Cabinet left it unchanged.

What to do: Index the central amounts to prices, as the Standing Committee asked in April 2025.

Where you live decides the pension: state top-ups run from ₹50 to ₹3,800

What it means: State top-ups on the Centre's ₹200 range from ₹50 in Assam to ₹3,800 in Andhra Pradesh.

What to do: Report each state's total beside the central amount so pensioners can see the gap.

The old-age ₹200 was set in 2007 and the Cabinet left it unchanged in 2026

  1. 1995Old-age pension starts at ₹75 a month
  2. 2007Raised to ₹200 a month for those aged 60 to 79
  3. 2011₹500 a month for those aged 80 and over
  4. 2012Widow and disability pensions rise from ₹200 to ₹300
  5. 14 October 2026Cabinet extends the programme to March 2031; old-age ₹200 unchanged

What it means: The Cabinet changed only disability pensions, raising them by a quarter, and kept the old-age amount.

What to do: Review the central amounts periodically against inflation, as the Standing Committee recommended in April 2025.

What would fix this

  • Index the central amounts to CPI-AL and revise them every year, as rural job-guarantee wages already are, so the minimum stops being set only when a five-year renewal comes up.
  • Restore the old-age pension in steps. Moving the ₹200 to its 2007 value would roughly double the programme’s cost, so a phased path over the 2026-31 cycle, published with the guidelines, would be easier to fund than a single jump.
  • Set a floor for the combined pension in states that add little or nothing, with central matching for poorer states, so a pensioner in Assam or Punjab is not left on ₹200 to ₹250.
  • Publish each state’s payment frequency, days of delay and deaths struck off on the NSAP portal every month, so the CAG’s findings can be tracked between audits.
  • Publish one annual table of each state’s total pension by category, rather than leaving it to a Parliament question annex.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

What this piece could not establish

The CPI-AL calculation ends in 2024-25 because the index changed base in June 2025 and no official linking factor was found, so the real loss to today is understated. The World Bank conversion uses 2021 prices. The state top-up annex was read in part; some states were cut off, and its figures do not always match the states’ own announcements: Haryana’s ₹2,800 top-up implies ₹3,000, below the ₹3,200 it has paid since November 2025, and Rajasthan’s ₹800 top-up implies ₹1,000, while secondary sources give ₹1,450. The annex’s date and reference period were not established. The disability guidelines, including how the 40% threshold will be certified and when payments at the new rate start, were not yet public. The restoration cost assumes every old-age beneficiary gets the 60–79 amount and uses the 2024 beneficiary count. The CAG audit covers 2017-21; no later audit was found.

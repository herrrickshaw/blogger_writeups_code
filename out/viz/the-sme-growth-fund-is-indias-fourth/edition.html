The Cabinet approved a ₹10,000 crore SME Growth Fund on 6 October, with no published manager, term, private-capital share or eligibility test. The first startup fund of funds had disbursed ₹3,400 crore of its ₹10,000 crore by end-2022 while reporting ₹14,077 crore invested, and the SRI Fund reports its progress in more than one way.

What it means: The government's own disbursement, the smallest of the three figures, is the one that answers how much reached firms.

What to do: Publish the government's disbursed amount beside each fund's announced corpus, so the new fund can be judged on the same figure.

What it means: The fund is the fourth government equity programme with a ₹10,000 crore government share in a decade, and each reports progress its own way.

What to do: Ask the ministries to report all four funds on one set of measures, starting with government money disbursed.

The new fund does not say how much private money must follow the government

What it means: The SRI Fund's target set private money at 4 times the government's ₹10,000 crore, while no private share has been published for the new fund.

What to do: Publish the private-money share and the term for the SME Growth Fund before its first investment, so it can be checked against this precedent.

What would fix this

  • The Department of Financial Services and the Ministry of MSME should publish the fund’s terms before its first close: the investment manager and how it is chosen, the fund’s life, the private-capital share required, the fees, and a written eligibility test tied to the Udyam size classes.
  • SIDBI and NSIC Venture Capital Fund should report each quarter, for every government equity programme, four numbers on one page: amount committed to funds, amount drawn down by funds, amount invested in firms, and how much of each is government money, so “invested” can no longer mean three different things.
  • The Ministry of MSME should commission and publish an independent evaluation of the Self-Reliant India Fund before the new fund’s first investment, as the Department for Promotion of Industry and Internal Trade did for the first startup fund of funds with CRISIL.
  • The Department for Promotion of Industry and Internal Trade and the Ministry of MSME should keep one public register of every fund and manager supported across the four programmes, so overlap between funds and managers can be seen.
  • The Ministry of Finance should state in the fund’s framework how the SME Growth Fund differs from the SRI Fund and the innovation-led manufacturing segment of Fund of Funds 2.0, with the size of firm each is meant to serve.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

The terms of Fund of Funds 2.0 are from law-firm and trade summaries of the DPIIT guidelines. Eligibility lists for the new fund are from broker and trade commentary, not an official text. SIDBI’s venture arm also ran an older fund called the SME Growth Fund; it is a different, much smaller product. This article takes no position on whether the fund should exist.

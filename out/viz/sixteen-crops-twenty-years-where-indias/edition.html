How much to trust it
  • All figures are from the Department of Agriculture’s Unified Portal for Agricultural Statistics (UPAG): all-India area, production and yield from 1966-67, state figures from 1997-98 and district figures from 2015-16, with 2025-26 at the third advance estimate.
  • “2003-06” means the average of 2003-04 to 2005-06 and “2023-26” the average of 2023-24 to 2025-26, so one drought or bumper year does not drive the comparisons.
  • Growth is split into area and yield on a log scale, so the two parts add up exactly.
  • Cotton production is in bales of 170 kg of lint; UPAG’s download labels it lakh tonnes, but the figures only reconcile with its published yield as bales.
  • State yield gaps compare only states producing at least 3% of a crop.
  • Telangana appears from 2014, when it was carved out of Andhra Pradesh, so its earlier output sits in Andhra’s figures.
  • District shares use the 2022-23 to 2024-25 average of UPAG district-wise production, 2015-16 to 2024-25.
What it adds up to
  • The fastest growers, maize and moong, are the crops where both land and yield expanded, pulled by new demand from ethanol and poultry feed in maize’s case.
  • The crops with the most room to grow output without more land are those with the widest gaps between their best and worst states: jowar, maize, moong, urad and bajra.
  • The crops that matter most for price stability, the irrigated cereals, are also the steadiest harvests, while the rain-fed oilseeds and pulses swing by a fifth or more in a typical year.
  • Sugarcane, moong and tur have become more concentrated, while jowar and gram have spread out; Uttar Pradesh, Maharashtra and Karnataka now grow 83% of India’s cane, against 69% in 2003-06.
  • This post is AI-assisted analysis of public data, not investment advice.
Crop by crop: output, yield and leading states
  • Rice. Output 147 million tonnes in 2023-26, up from 88 million in 2003-06, with a record 154 million in 2025-26; yield rose from 2,055 to 2,909 kg a hectare. Uttar Pradesh grows 13%, and West Bengal’s share fell from 17% to 11% as Telangana rose to 12%.
  • Wheat. Output 117 million tonnes, with a record 121 million in 2025-26; yield rose from 2,645 to 3,582 kg a hectare. Madhya Pradesh’s share doubled from 10% to 21% while Punjab’s fell from 21% to 15% and Uttar Pradesh’s from 34% to 30%. Its worst fall since 2000 was 10%, in 2014-15.
  • Maize. Output rose from 15 million tonnes in 2003-06 to a record 55 million in 2025-26, with area up from 75 to 126 lakh hectares and yield from 1,962 to 3,588 kg. Madhya Pradesh grows 15%, Karnataka 13% and Bihar 12%; Bihar’s yield is 6,113 kg a hectare.
  • Jowar. Output averaged 4.7 million tonnes in 2023-26, about a third of its 1989-90 record of 12.9 million, as area fell from 90 to 39 lakh hectares. Yield rose from 798 to 1,210 kg a hectare. Maharashtra grows 37%, down from 48%.
  • Bajra. Output rose from 9.2 to 10.9 million tonnes and has not beaten its 2003-04 record of 12.1 million. Area fell from 98 to 72 lakh hectares while yield rose from 934 to 1,511 kg. Rajasthan grows 42% at 1,105 kg a hectare.
  • Ragi. 1.9 million tonnes, against a record 3.2 million in 1978-79. Yield has hardly moved, at about 1,430–1,480 kg a hectare. Karnataka grows two-thirds; Tamil Nadu’s yield is 3,240 kg a hectare.
  • Gram. Output doubled from 5.6 to 11.6 million tonnes, with a record 13.5 million in 2021-22; yield rose from 811 to 1,221 kg a hectare and area from 69 to 95 lakh hectares. Madhya Pradesh’s share fell from 45% to 20%, Maharashtra’s rose from 9% to 26% and Gujarat’s from 2% to 13%.
  • Tur. Output rose from 2.5 to 3.5 million tonnes, below the 2016-17 record of 4.9 million; yield rose only from 701 to 823 kg a hectare. Karnataka’s share more than doubled to 26% at 597 kg a hectare. Kalaburagi district grows 11% of the crop.
  • Moong. Output more than tripled, from 1.2 to a record 4.5 million tonnes in 2025-26, with area and yield each roughly doubling. Madhya Pradesh grows 42% at 1,212 kg a hectare and Rajasthan 29% at 464 kg. Madhya Pradesh’s figures are missing for 2003-06, so its earlier share is not shown.
  • Urad. Output rose from 1.4 to 2.2 million tonnes, almost all from yield, which rose from 423 to 682 kg a hectare on an unchanged area of about 33 lakh hectares. It remains below the 2017-18 record of 3.5 million tonnes. Madhya Pradesh grows 21%, Uttar Pradesh and Andhra Pradesh 17% each.
  • Lentil. Output rose from 1.0 to 1.7 million tonnes, mainly through yield, which reached 1,000 kg a hectare. Madhya Pradesh grows 39%, up from 26%, and Uttar Pradesh 37%, down from 48%.
  • Groundnut. Output reached a record 13.1 million tonnes in 2025-26 even though area fell from 65 to 54 lakh hectares; yield rose from 1,188 to 2,155 kg a hectare. Gujarat grows 46%; Rajasthan’s share rose from 6% to 21% while Andhra Pradesh’s fell from 17% to 3%.
  • Rapeseed and mustard. Output nearly doubled, from 7.3 to 13.2 million tonnes, with a record 13.8 million in 2025-26. Rajasthan grows 44%, down from 51%; Uttar Pradesh’s share rose from 11% to 17%. Haryana’s yield is 2,034 kg a hectare.
  • Soybean. Output rose from 7.7 to 13.6 million tonnes, peaking at 15.3 million in 2024-25 and falling to 12.6 million in 2025-26; area rose from 73 to 129 lakh hectares at about 1,060 kg a hectare in both periods. Maharashtra grows 43% and Madhya Pradesh 41%.
  • Cotton. Output averaged 30.4 million bales of 170 kg in 2023-26, against 16.2 million in 2003-06, below the 2019-20 record of 36.1 million and 29.0 million in 2025-26. Lint yield rose from 329 to 435 kg a hectare. Gujarat’s yield, 547 kg, is 69% higher than Maharashtra’s 323 kg.
  • Sugarcane. Output averaged 469 million tonnes in 2023-26 and reached a record 500 million in 2025-26, with yield up from 64 to 82 tonnes a hectare. Uttar Pradesh grows 47%; Maharashtra’s share rose from 11% to 25% while Tamil Nadu’s fell from 10% to 3%.

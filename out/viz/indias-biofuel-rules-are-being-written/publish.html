Indian courts have backed E20, handed States power over denatured alcohol, kept biodiesel outside petroleum-control law, and opened the oil companies' ethanol contracts to judicial review. This is how five rulings are reshaping the ethanol blending programme.

Updated 7 November 2026. Two developments since this piece was revised. The 4 August interim arrangement let the oil companies award about 149 crore litres more for August–October 2026, taking 2025–26 procurement to about 1,197 crore litres. The 2026–27 tender, issued in October, keeps a preference for plants with long-term offtake agreements but says it is “not a guarantee of offtake” and that allocations can be cut, moved or cancelled without compensation; it also drops cane-juice ethanol and is about 55% of installed capacity (see this blog’s 2026–27 tender piece). This blog found no reported outcome of the Supreme Court hearing listed for 23 September 2026 on the VINP appeals and transferred cases.

States won the alcohol case 8–1, and the majority never mentions fuel

What it means: The majority's test very likely places fuel ethanol under State regulation, which is the post's own inference rather than the court's words.

What to do: Watch State permit and fee rules on fuel ethanol and the first challenges to them.

VINP got 3.92 crore litres of the 9.26 it bid, less than half

What it means: The cut left the plant with less than half its bid, and that allocation dispute started the VINP case.

What to do: Check the outcome of the Supreme Court hearing listed for 23 September 2026, which the post does not report.

The Supreme Court set aside the Karnataka orders, leaving the offtake merits open

Combining official crop statistics with seven years of mandi prices: maize, mustard and moong have taken the most new land in a decade while jowar lost 42%; yields of maize, pulses and groundnut are now growing faster than rice and wheat; k-means clustering sorts India’s states into five farming regions and its 217 traded commodities into four kinds of price behaviour; and an independent check confirms that urea use follows the crop map.

India's farm map in 7 charts: maize spreads, jowar area down 42%, 4 price clusters

Skip to article content
Text Size

Top line

  • 64% growth in India's maize area over ten years, to 144 lakh hectares in 2025-26; mustard grew as much.
  • −42% fall in jowar area over the same decade, the steepest of any major crop.
  • 11.5% typical monthly price move in the onion-potato-tomato price cluster, against 3.4% for harvest-bunched field crops such as wheat.

Updated 15 November 2026: rewritten as charts; figures unchanged.

Maize and mustard area grew 64% in ten years, while jowar area fell 42%

What it means: Land is moving toward crops with new demand or price support and away from the traditional dryland cereals.

What to do: Plan inputs, storage and procurement around the crops gaining area, led by maize and mustard.

What it means: Rice and wheat yields grew about 2% a year, slower than the fastest crops, while tur and soybean have barely moved.

What to do: Direct seed and extension effort to tur, soybean and cotton, where yield has barely moved or slipped.

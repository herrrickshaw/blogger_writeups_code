The swap window held the rupee and lifted reserves to a record $785.7 billion, but the dollars are owed back from 2029, nearly half in 2031. Reserves are down $51 billion in four weeks, the forward book is at $200 billion, and the maturity ladder is not published.

RBI's FCNR(B) swap window: $133 billion drawn, reserves down $51 billion from peak

Skip to article content
Text Size

Top line

  • $133 billion of FCNR(B) deposits swapped with the RBI by 18 September, against about $27 billion in 2013.
  • $51.1 billion fall in reserves from the 4 September peak to 2 October, four weeks in a row.
  • $200.1 billion RBI net short dollar forward book at end-August, up $63.3 billion in a month.
Updated 28 October 2026. The swap circular has been identified as RBI/2026-27/99 (FMOD.MAOG.No.S-56/01.06.016/2026-27, 8 June 2026), and the terms in the table were checked against the Foreign Exchange Dealers’ Association of India’s summaries of it (June and August 2026 newsletters): the swap is at the FBIL reference rate on a par basis, deposits are three to five years with a one-year lock-in, swaps cannot be cancelled, and the window closed early on 31 August. The CRR and SLR exemption comes from separate RBI amendment directions of 8 June 2026, not the circular; the table now says so. The circular itself is published by the RBI, not in the Gazette, and its own text was still not opened.

Updated 15 November 2026: rewritten as charts; figures unchanged.

The 2026 window drew $133 billion, nearly five times the 2013 scheme

Who could use itBanks taking fresh FCNR(B) deposits (renewals on maturity also eligible) from non-resident Indians in any freely convertible currency; deposits mobilised 8 June to 31 August 2026 (the end date was brought forward from 30 September)
Tenor and lock-inThree to five years. A one-year lock-in; after that, banks may allow early withdrawal under their own policies. The swap itself cannot be cancelled or reversed before maturity
What the swap doesThe bank sells the dollars to the RBI for rupees at the FBIL reference rate, on a par basis, and buys them back at the same rate at maturity; no ISDA agreement with the RBI is needed. Swaps could be executed with the RBI until 11 September
Who bears the exchange-rate riskThe RBI. Commentary puts the hedging cost the RBI absorbs at 280–300 basis points a year, on principal only; banks keep the interest risk
Reserve requirementsFresh and renewed three-to-five-year deposits mobilised to 30 September 2026 exempt from CRR and SLR, under separate RBI amendment directions of 8 June 2026 (as summarised in legal newsletters), not under the swap circular itself
What depositors were paidReported rates of 5.5% to 7.1%, and up to 7.4% in some offers; banks cut rates after the window closed

What it means: Banks swapped about $133 billion of three-to-five-year dollar deposits with the RBI by 18 September ($127.2 billion at the 31 August close), against about $27 billion of FCNR(B) money in 2013.

What to do: Publish the tenor mix of the deposits and the renewal assumptions, which the post says are not public.

Reserves fell $51.1 billion in the four weeks after their 4 September peak

What it means: Reserves reached a record $785.7 billion on 4 September and then fell in four straight weeks to $734.6 billion.

What to do: Report the split of weekly reserve changes between dollar sales and revaluation, which the RBI does not publish.

What it means: Each swap adds to the RBI's short position in dollar forwards, so the book grew from $136.8 billion to $200.1 billion in one month.

What to do: Publish the maturity profile of the forward book each month so the repayments created by the swaps can be tracked.

What would fix this

  • The Reserve Bank of India should publish the swap book each month by year of maturity, separately from its other forward positions, with the tenor mix, so that the 2029 to 2031 repayments can be seen in its own forward-book release.
  • The Reserve Bank of India should publish net spot intervention and valuation change next to the weekly reserves figure, so a fall such as the $51 billion since 4 September can be split between dollar sales and revaluation.
  • The Reserve Bank of India should state its policy on rollover and early withdrawal: whether it will invite renewals, and what happens to a swap that cannot be cancelled when a depositor leaves after the one-year lock-in.
  • The Reserve Bank of India should report in its annual accounts how it values the swap position and any hedging cost it absorbs, so the cost is recognised when it arises and not at maturity.
  • The Department of Economic Affairs should add reserves net of the forward book, and the scheduled FCNR(B) repayments, to its external-sector tables and the annual Economic Survey, so reserve adequacy is shown after the liability as well as before it.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

Sources and caveats

RBI releases on cumulative inflows ($127.2 billion FCNR(B) and $136.4 billion in all at 31 August; $133.0 billion and $143.6 billion at 18 September) and the weekly reserves figures (21 August to 2 October) are as reported by Business Standard, BusinessToday, PTI, Free Press Journal, The Daily Jagran and others; the RBI weekly statistical supplement was not read. Some trade posts give much lower totals for dates in August; the RBI figures are used. The forward-book figures ($106.7 billion at end-May, $136.8 billion at end-July, $200.1 billion at end-August with $176.6 billion over one year, and the short-dated net of $23.5 billion) are from Business Standard and Bloomberg of 30 September and 31 August, as seen in search summaries, not from the RBI bulletin; the June figure was not found. The scheme’s terms are from law-firm and trade summaries, the RBI’s press release for the dates, and this blog’s earlier piece for the CRR and SLR exemption. The 280–300 basis point hedging cost is from one law-firm note; the $3.5 billion a year and $15 billion estimates are one columnist’s. The Governor’s remark that nearly half the deposits are five-year, and that the rupee is undervalued, are as reported. The analyst split of the month’s reserve fall and the RBI’s $22.4 billion of dollar sales are estimates from press reports. The 2013 and 2016 figures are from press coverage and an RBI release on the 2013 scheme’s maturity. Arithmetic (ladder, 27% ratio, cost per 1% and per year) is this blog’s and rests on the stated assumptions. This article does not say the scheme was a mistake.

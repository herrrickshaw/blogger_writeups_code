Top line

  • 101 mn Indians have diabetes by the ICMR-INDIAB estimate, with 136 million more with prediabetes.
  • $28.4 mn of sucralose imported in FY26, up 49.3% in value and 71.0% in volume.
  • 0 Indian sucralose factories verified; most directory sellers list Chinese-made product.
Updated 12 October 2026. On 23 September 2026 FSSAI proposed a single-phase front-of-pack regime that would move the “contains non-caloric sweetener” declaration from the back of the pack to the front, alongside red warning symbols; it expects about four months to finalise draft rules, then 60 days of comment and a 365-day voluntary period, and the Supreme Court has reserved its verdict on the matter. On diabetes, the International Diabetes Federation’s 2024 estimate is 89.8 million adults aged 20–79, below the 101 million ICMR-INDIAB projection used here; the two differ in method and age range. ISMA’s domestic sugar requirement for 2025-26 is 28.5 million tonnes.

About 101 million Indians have diabetes, and state rates differ widely

What it means: The spread between states is wide, and the national figure is a projection from a survey run from 2008 to 2020.

What to do: Quote the ICMR-INDIAB figure with its survey dates and projection caveat, not the older claim about who uses intense sweeteners.

What would fix this

  • The Department of Commerce should publish sucralose, aspartame, saccharin and acesulfame imports by origin and tonnage at the eight-digit tariff line, including 29251100, 29242990, 29349990 and 29389090, which sit below this blog’s registry threshold, so it is known who supplies India and at what price rather than inferred from a value figure.
  • FSSAI should publish its non-sugar sweetener intake estimates against the acceptable daily intake and state whether the 2020 labelling draft was notified, since the draft’s status could not be confirmed.
  • The Indian Council of Medical Research should report non-sugar sweetener use among people with diabetes and prediabetes, by sweetener, in its next INDIAB round, because the WHO advice excludes this group and India has no figures of its own.
  • The Department of Chemicals and Petrochemicals should publish a costed route for sucralose within its import-substitution screening, with the technology licence, capital and effluent cost, so a mill or chemical firm can decide on numbers.
  • The Central Pollution Control Board should state the solvent-recovery and effluent norms that would apply to a sucralose plant, covering DMF, thionyl chloride off-gases and chlorinated waste, so compliance cost is known before a plant is planned.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

Sources and caveats

Diabetes: press reports of the ICMR-INDIAB study in The Lancet Diabetes & Endocrinology (2023): 11.4% prevalence, 101 million, 15.3% prediabetes, 136 million, Goa 26.4%, Uttar Pradesh 4.8%; the 101 million is an extrapolation from a 2008–2020 survey. Sugar use: ISMA estimates for 2024-25 (about 28 million tonnes) and 2023-24 (29 million), and the ISMA institutional-consumption study (60–65% institutional, 35–40% retail; the 20 kg a head is this blog’s division, not a sourced figure). Rules and safety: an FSSAI-focused summary of the five approved sweeteners and their ppm limits; the FSSAI draft labelling notification of 16 September 2020 (final status not confirmed); the WHO guideline on non-sugar sweeteners (May 2023, 283 studies); press reports of the FSSAI single-phase front-of-pack proposal (23–28 September 2026; a proposal, not a final rule); the IDF Diabetes Atlas country page (2024) and The Lancet Global Health survey of adults aged 45 and over (February 2026); ISMA’s first advance estimate for 2025-26; the WHO and IARC release of 14 July 2023 on aspartame; press reports of the 2023 North Carolina study of sucralose-6-acetate (in vitro). Market: Zydus Wellness investor releases (Nielsen share in the low to mid 90s, a company claim). Trade: this blog’s chemical import-substitution registry (HS 29321400, FY26: imports $28.4 million, deficit $27.7 million, value +49.3%, volume +71.0%, price −12.4%); tariff-site listings for 29251100 (saccharin; 7.5% basic duty; a countervailing duty on Chinese saccharin has been notified and its status was not confirmed) and 29389090 (7.5% basic duty); the codes 29242990 (aspartame) and 29349990 (acesulfame potassium) are expected lines that the sources did not confirm; registry rows for 29054900, 38246010 and 38246090; a third-party customs aggregation (FY25 imports ₹161.79 crore); tariff sites (7.5% basic duty, unverified); two market reports on Chinese capacity share and 2024 output and price. The 1,300 tonne, 775,000 tonne and 1,100 tonne figures are this blog’s arithmetic from the import value, a $22 a kilogram price and a sweetness ratio of about 600 times sugar, which is the commonly quoted figure and was not taken from the sources searched. Chemistry: US patents 8283464, 7884203, 5498709, 4617269 and a Chinese patent summary on sucrose-6-acetate; yields are as reported in patent examples. Options: a 2023 CICEQ study and a 2022 Frontiers in Nutrition study on xylitol (see this blog’s bagasse piece); a Himachal Pradesh stevia plant report; a report of FSSAI approval for allulose trading; a GLG and Katra stevia memorandum. Most sources were abstracts, patents or search summaries, not full texts. No Indian sucralose factory or mill project could be verified; trade-directory listings of Indian sellers, some self-described as manufacturers and some selling Chinese-made branded powder, were seen but not checked, and one listing quoted ₹4,800 a kilogram at a 100 kg minimum. That is a limit of the search, not proof there is none. No process or investment advice is given.

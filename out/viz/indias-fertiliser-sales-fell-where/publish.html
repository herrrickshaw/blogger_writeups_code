Farmers bought less of every fertiliser this kharif, and the weak monsoon is part of it. But the steepest falls were in complex fertilisers whose price rose about 42%, while DAP, held at its old price despite a large import loss, was almost flat.

India's fertiliser sales: NPK down 22% at Rs 2,450 a bag, DAP at Rs 1,350 almost flat

Skip to article content
Text Size

Top line

  • -22.2% fall in sales of NPK complex fertilisers, April–September 2026, the steepest of any major fertiliser.
  • +42% rise in the price of a bag of NPK 12-32-16 or 10-26-26, to Rs 2,450, while DAP's price was held at Rs 1,350.
Updated 7 November 2026. Government figures for the rabi season as of 16 October 2026 put fertiliser availability at 199.48 lakh tonnes against a full-season requirement of 380.96 lakh tonnes, with closing stocks of 175.32 lakh tonnes. NPK complex sales in October to that date were only 3.20 lakh tonnes against a month’s requirement of 18.51 lakh tonnes, in line with the NPK slump described below. The same release reportedly showed DAP with the thinnest closing-stock cover relative to its requirement, which qualifies this piece’s point that rabi starts with DAP stocks built up (these figures are from a trade report of the release, not the release itself). Rabi 2026-27 subsidy (NBS) rates had still not been announced in reports found by 7 November.

Updated 15 November 2026: rewritten as charts; figures unchanged.

Sales fell most where the price moved

What it means: Sales fell most for the fertilisers whose retail price rose, and least for DAP, whose price was capped.

What to do: Watch the rabi subsidy rates when they are announced, since they decide whether DAP stays at Rs 1,350.

NPK bags became dearer per kilogram of nutrient, while DAP and urea did not

What it means: A farmer who switches from a complex fertiliser to DAP and urea pays less but gets no potash.

What to do: Check whether the government's push for balanced nutrition is backed by prices that make complexes affordable.

NPK sales ran ahead of new supply, while urea and MOP had supply to spare

What it means: Urea was available and farmers bought less of it, which points to the weak monsoon; NPK was limited by price and supply.

What to do: Track NPK stocks into rabi, since the post says they are being run down while DAP stocks have built up.

What would fix this

  • The Department of Fertilizers should publish monthly sales, production, imports and closing stocks by product and state, as the Fertiliser Association of India’s members already report them, so the effect of each season’s subsidy rates can be measured within weeks.
  • Subsidy rates for complex fertilisers should be reviewed with DAP’s, so that a policy that holds DAP’s price does not make balanced grades relatively dearer.
  • The potash subsidy, unchanged at Rs 2.38 a kilogram, should be reviewed against the potash ratio the government wants farmers to use.
  • The government should publish the cost of the informal DAP price cap to companies each season, so that it is visible as policy rather than absorbed in balance sheets.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

What this piece could not establish

The April–September figures come from news reports citing industry sources and the Fertiliser Association of India; no official Department of Fertilizers release for the half-year could be opened. Several 2025 values are back-calculated from reported percentage changes. The NPK bag prices are mainly Madhya Pradesh cooperative prices and company quotes from May 2026, and prices of individual grades vary by company and state. Closing stocks were not available, so the supply-minus-sales figures ignore opening stocks. The relative weight of rain, price and supply cannot be separated precisely without state-level sales, which were not found.

India's data centre tax holiday to 2047: approvals dropped, cost not published

Top line

  • 2047 tax years of exemption for foreign cloud companies serving customers abroad from Indian data centres; the August Act dropped both notifications.
  • 3.3% of what a foreign cloud company pays its Indian related operator goes to Indian tax under the 15% safe harbour.
Updated 28 October 2026. Checked against the Gazette of India: the Taxation and Other Laws (Amendment) Act, 2026, assented to on 17 August 2026 (CG-DL-E-17082026-275521), matches the provisions described here. It defines a qualifying data centre as one “operated by an Indian company, whether by way of owning or leasing” that “satisfies such other conditions as may be prescribed”, so the old scheme approval is replaced by rules still to come; the table now says so. The Act is deemed to apply from 1 April 2026 and repeals the June ordinance (CG-DL-E-05062026-273164). No figure changed.

What it means: The exemption became an entitlement, so any foreign company meeting the conditions and filing the prescribed information now qualifies.

What to do: Notify the data centre rules, which the Act leaves to rules that had not been notified.

India still taxes the Indian operator: about Rs 3.78 on a Rs 115 payment

What it means: The Indian operator's tax is small, about 3.3% of what the foreign company pays it, while the foreign company's resale income abroad is exempt.

What to do: Publish an official estimate of the revenue forgone, since none was found and none could be made without knowing the booked volume.

What to do: Check the enacted text against the data centre rules once they are notified, since those rules are not yet out.

What would fix this

  • The Finance Ministry’s annual statement of revenue forgone should list the data-centre exemption separately, with the number of foreign companies claiming it and the income exempted.
  • The prescribed information form should require each claimant to report the data centres it uses, their IT load, and their power and water use, so the exemption can be compared with what the campuses draw.
  • States allocating power and water to data-centre campuses should publish the allocation and the tariff, since the central tax regime no longer gives the government a project-by-project approval point.
  • The safe-harbour margin should be reviewed after three years against the margins actually reported by Indian operators.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

What this piece could not establish

The Act (CG-DL-E-17082026-275521) and the June ordinance (CG-DL-E-05062026-273164) were read in the Gazette of India’s own text through the SansadSaar index of the archive.org Gazette of India collection; the provisions and the assent date of 17 August 2026 above match them. The eligibility rules and the information form had not been seen. the 14 September UPI notification rests on a single report. The tax illustration depends entirely on its stated assumptions, and no official revenue-forgone estimate was found.

On 24 September 2026 the government cut the basic customs duty on crude palm and crude soybean oil from 10% to 5% and on crude sunflower oil to nil. With cesses, the effective duty on crude palm and soybean oil fell from 16.5% to 11%. It was the second cut in sixteen months. The first, by notification on 30 May 2025 with immediate effect, had halved the basic duty from 20% to 10%.

Two years ago the policy pointed the other way. In September 2024 the government raised the basic duty on crude oils from nil to 20% to support oilseed farmers before the kharif harvest. Since then, India has stepped back down most of that hike, while the National Mission on Edible Oils, approved in October 2024, asks farmers to grow about 60% more oilseeds by 2030-31 than they did in 2025-26. The tariff and the mission are pulling in different directions, and retail cooking-oil prices have kept rising through both cuts.

Updated 9 November 2026. The vegetable oils price index for September 2026 is out: 135.8, up from 133.9 in August and 16% above May 2025 (was 14% to August); refined oil was up 15.0% on a year earlier. September’s prices were collected almost entirely before the 24 September duty cut, so October’s index, due on 12 November, is the first real test. The Solvent Extractors’ Association’s September data put edible oil imports for November 2025 to September 2026 at 151.6 lakh tonnes, up 3% (was 136.2 lakh tonnes, up 5%, to August). The chart still ends in August. No further duty change since 24 September was found.

Updated 28 October 2026. The 2024 and 2025 duty changes have been checked against the Gazette of India. The 2025 cut was made by Notification No. 31/2025-Customs, G.S.R. 355(E), 30 May 2025 (CG-DL-E-30052025-263480), which took effect on 30 May, not 31 May as first given here; the date has been corrected in the text, table and chart. The 2024 rise to 20% was made by Notification No. 43/2024-Customs, G.S.R. 573(E), 13 September 2024, in force from 14 September 2024 (CG-DL-E-13092024-257153). The September 2026 notification (No. 31/2026-Customs) was not in the gazette index searched and is still as reported.

One thing the cuts did protect is the gap between crude and refined oil. When crude duty fell in May 2025 and refined duty did not, the difference widened from 8.25 to 19.25 percentage points, and the September 2026 cut kept it there. Refined oil imports fell from 16.4 lakh tonnes to 6.6 lakh tonnes over the first ten months of the oil year: refining has moved back to Indian plants.

MoSPI consumer price index (base 2024), All India, rural and urban combined, vegetable oils sub-class, January 2025 to August 2026, retrieved through MoSPI’s eSankhyiki API. September 2026 (135.8, retrieved 9 November) is in the text but not yet in the chart; its prices were collected almost entirely before the 24 September cut.

The September 2024 hike reached shops quickly. On MoSPI’s older 2012-based series, the oils and fats index rose from 161.5 in August 2024 to 183.7 by December, almost 14% in four months. The May 2025 cut did not reverse it. The vegetable oils index stood at 117.0 in May 2025 and kept rising, to 124.2 by September 2025, 133.9 by August 2026 and 135.8 in September: 16% higher sixteen months after the cut. In September 2026 refined oil prices were up 15.0% on a year earlier, groundnut oil 14.3% and mustard oil 7.3%. Coconut oil, which had spiked in 2025, was down 29.5%.

The duty is not what sets those prices. The Solvent Extractors’ Association, welcoming the latest cut, put the rise in international edible oil prices over the past year at about 25%. A cut in a 16.5% duty cannot offset that, and the Indian Vegetable Oil Producers’ Association has had to ask its members to pass the cut on to consumers. Whether the September cut shows up in the October and November price index is the test.

India imported 136.2 lakh tonnes of edible oil in the first ten months of the 2025-26 oil year, 5% more than a year earlier, and 151.6 lakh tonnes in the first eleven, to September, 3% more; palm oil was 73.5 lakh tonnes of that. The Indian Vegetable Oil Producers’ Association expects domestic edible oil output of about 9.6 million tonnes in 2025-26 and imports of about 16.7 million tonnes, so imports would be close to two-thirds of supply. India’s import bill for animal and vegetable oils and fats (HS chapter 15) was $19.8 billion in 2025-26, up from $10.0 billion in 2018-19, according to the commerce ministry’s trade data.

## The mission on the other side

The National Mission on Edible Oils – Oilseeds was approved on 3 October 2024 with ₹10,103 crore for 2024-25 to 2030-31. It aims to raise oilseed production to 69.7 million tonnes by 2030-31 and, with the separate oil palm mission, domestic edible oil to 25.45 million tonnes, enough for about 72% of projected demand.

The government told the Lok Sabha in August 2026 that oilseed output rose from 39.67 million tonnes in 2023-24 to 43.06 million tonnes in 2025-26 (third advance estimate). That is about 4% a year. Reaching 69.7 million tonnes by 2030-31 needs about 10% a year for five years. Edible oil output would have to go from about 9.6 million tonnes to 25.45 million, more than two and a half times.

Farm prices are what decide whether farmers plant more. The soybean processors’ association called the May 2025 cut “anti-farmer” and asked for a 10-point duty increase. The September 2026 cut lands in the middle of the kharif marketing season for soybean and groundnut. A duty that moves twice in sixteen months, in both directions, is hard for a farmer to plan around.

India's cooking oil duty: cut from 27.5% to 11% in 2 steps as prices rose

Top line

  • 11% effective duty on crude palm and soybean oil after the 24 September 2026 cut, down from 16.5%.
  • 16% rise in the vegetable oils price index between the May 2025 cut and September 2026.
  • 10% a year oilseed growth needed for the 2030-31 target, against about 4% a year since 2023-24.
Updated 9 November 2026. The vegetable oils price index for September 2026 is out: 135.8, up from 133.9 in August and 16% above May 2025 (was 14% to August); refined oil was up 15.0% on a year earlier. September’s prices were collected almost entirely before the 24 September duty cut, so October’s index, due on 12 November, is the first real test. The Solvent Extractors’ Association’s September data put edible oil imports for November 2025 to September 2026 at 151.6 lakh tonnes, up 3% (was 136.2 lakh tonnes, up 5%, to August). The chart still ends in August. No further duty change since 24 September was found.
Updated 28 October 2026. The 2024 and 2025 duty changes have been checked against the Gazette of India. The 2025 cut was made by Notification No. 31/2025-Customs, G.S.R. 355(E), 30 May 2025 (CG-DL-E-30052025-263480), which took effect on 30 May, not 31 May as first given here; the date has been corrected in the text, table and chart. The 2024 rise to 20% was made by Notification No. 43/2024-Customs, G.S.R. 573(E), 13 September 2024, in force from 14 September 2024 (CG-DL-E-13092024-257153). The September 2026 notification (No. 31/2026-Customs) was not in the gazette index searched and is still as reported.

What it means: The basic duty on crude palm and soybean oil went up to 20% in September 2024 and has since been cut to 5%, which with cesses gives an effective 11%.

What to do: Watch the October price index as the first real test of whether the September cut reaches shops.

Vegetable oil prices kept rising through both duty cuts

What it means: Prices kept climbing after the May 2025 cut, and the September 2026 reading was collected before the latest duty cut.

What to do: Read the October index as the first test of whether the September duty cut reaches consumers.

What it means: Total edible oil imports reached 136.2 lakh tonnes in the first ten months, 5% more than a year earlier.

What to do: Treat rising imports as the baseline, since domestic oilseed output is not yet growing fast enough to replace them.

Oilseed output must grow about 10% a year to meet the 2030-31 target

What it means: The mission asks farmers to grow about 60% more oilseeds by 2030-31 than in 2025-26, while the duty has moved in both directions.

What to do: Set a steadier duty path so farmers can plan their kharif sowing.

What would fix this

  • The Department of Revenue and the Department of Agriculture should publish a rule for edible oil duties: a band of rates tied to published triggers, such as domestic oilseed prices against the minimum support price and international oil prices, with changes announced before sowing rather than at harvest.
  • The Department of Consumer Affairs should publish monthly retail and wholesale prices for each edible oil for three months after every duty change, against landed import costs, so whether a cut reaches consumers can be checked rather than requested.
  • The Department of Agriculture and Farmers Welfare should publish a year-by-year path for the oilseed mission (production, area, yield and edible oil output) and report each year’s result against it.
  • The Department of Agriculture and NAFED should publish oilseed procurement under PM-AASHA by crop and state each season, against the quantity sold below the minimum support price, so the price support that is meant to sit behind the mission is visible.
  • The Central Board of Indirect Taxes and Customs should publish edible oil import volumes and customs revenue by duty rate after each change, so the cost of each cut and its effect on the mix of crude and refined imports are on record.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

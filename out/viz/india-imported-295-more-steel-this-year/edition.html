India's steel imports: up 29.5% this year despite an 11.5% safeguard duty

Skip to article content
Text Size

Top line

  • +29.5% finished steel imports in April–August 2026, to 3.49 million tonnes, with a safeguard duty in force.
  • 11.5% safeguard duty on flat steel since 21 April 2026; domestic producers have asked for more than 20%.
Updated 9 November 2026. Newer official data: the Ministry of Steel’s monthly release of 5 November (PIB 2333256, provisional JPC data) puts finished steel imports for April–October 2026 at 4.90 million tonnes, up 28.9% from 3.80 million tonnes a year earlier, and exports at 3.98 million tonnes (+15.5%), so India is still a net importer. In October alone imports rose 65.8% to 0.76 million tonnes while exports fell 32%. Consumption rose 7.5% to 99.3 million tonnes against 3.9% growth in finished steel output (95.5 million tonnes). Reuters-based reports in early November name South Korea, not China, as the largest source so far this fiscal year, at about 1.4 million tonnes, followed by China, Japan and Russia; the 31.8% China share below is for April–August. A seventh open house was held on 28 October, and a Reuters-based report of 2 November says the government is considering extending the safeguard duty on select steel products; no notification had been issued. The April–August analysis below is unchanged.
Updated 28 October 2026. The safeguard duty schedule has been checked against the customs notifications in the Gazette of India (No. 01/2025-Customs (SG) of 21 April 2025 and No. 02/2025-Customs (SG) of 30 December 2025). The rates are confirmed: 12%, then 11.5% from 21 April 2026, then 11% from 21 April 2027. One correction: the first-year duty was not continuous. The 12% provisional duty ran for 200 days from 21 April 2025, lapsed after 6 November 2025, and the final notification expressly levied no duty between then and its publication on 30 December 2025. The CIF price thresholds that exempt higher-priced imports are now given in the table note.

What would fix this

  • The Central Board of Indirect Taxes and Customs should publish each month the volume of flat steel imports that paid the safeguard duty and the volume exempted, by exemption route (CIF price threshold, developing-country list), so the effect of the duty can be separated from the headline import numbers.
  • The Ministry of Steel should publish SIMS registrations each month by product, country of origin and importer category, including how many came through SARAL SIMS, from the portal it already runs.
  • The Ministry of Steel should publish a short note after each open house on import issues: the categories of issue raised and what, if anything, was changed, so the sessions become a record rather than a closed meeting.
  • The Ministry of Steel and the Department for Promotion of Industry and Internal Trade should keep one public list of steel quality control orders and their exemptions with the start and end date of each, to remove conflicts such as the dates reported for the stainless steel exemption.
  • The Directorate General of Trade Remedies should publish a timetable and the injury data for the hot-rolled steel anti-dumping investigation, and report domestic and import-parity prices by product alongside the safeguard review, so users can see what a duty change would do to their costs.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

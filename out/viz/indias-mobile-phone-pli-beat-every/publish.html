India's mobile-phone PLI scheme beat its own five-year investment target by 294% and its production target by 142% — a genuine, government-reported overperformance. Its chip-fabrication cousin, the Semicon India Programme, has 12 approved plants and, as of SEMICON India 2026 in September, 5 producing anything (up from 3 when this piece was first published). The gap between the two tells you which parts of India's electronics-manufacturing push are working and which are still mostly paper.

Top line

  • 294% of PLI-LSEM's five-year investment target is reached cumulatively to March 2026, per the government's own answer.
Updated 7 November 2026. Smartphone exports kept climbing after the scheme ended: about $15.96 billion in April–August 2026, up about 36% on a year earlier, with iPhones about $13.2 billion (82%) of it, per industry estimates reported by Business Today (citing the Economic Times). No ECMS approval round after the 17 August tranche (106 projects) was found, and the chip programme still has 5 of 12 plants producing; this blog’s October piece on India’s 12 approved chip plants has the project-by-project status.

What it means: The government reports PLI-LSEM beating each of its three five-year targets.

What to do: Check the domestic value addition figure of 23% against the target before counting this scheme a full success.

ECMS approvals already pass its investment target, with jobs at 81%

What it means: Approvals so far already exceed the scheme's original investment envelope, while job approvals lag behind it.

What to do: Track the 38 plants already producing and the 16 under advanced construction as the next measure of ECMS progress.

Only five of twelve Semicon projects are producing so far

What it means: Five of the twelve approved projects are running, and all five are assembly, test or packaging lines rather than wafer fabs.

What to do: Watch whether the remaining seven projects reach commercial production before India's first fab is due in 2028.

What would fix this

  • MeitY should commission and publish an external domestic value addition evaluation for PLI 2.0 for IT Hardware, on the same method as PLI-LSEM’s, so the scheme’s 23-to-1 production-to-investment ratio can be read as efficient manufacturing or low-value assembly.
  • MeitY should publish domestic value addition for mobile-phone manufacturing every year, and set year-by-year DVA milestones in the Mobile Phone Manufacturing Scheme guidelines, so the 23% FY2023-24 figure is followed by a series against which MPMS’s stated priority can be judged.
  • MeitY should publish a project-wise status table for all 12 Semicon India projects, showing committed investment, investment actually made and date of commercial production, so progress from 5 of 12 is tracked from an official list rather than ministerial remarks at events.
  • MeitY should adopt and publish one definition of “direct jobs” across PLI-LSEM, IT Hardware PLI, ECMS and MPMS, so figures such as MPMS’s 60,000 target and ECMS’s 74,628 approved jobs can be compared directly.
  • MeitY should publish the demand estimates and method behind its claims that ECMS output covers 110%, 350% and 200% of domestic demand in anode material, optical transceivers and relays, so those coverage percentages can be checked independently.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

What this piece does not establish. This piece does not know why IT Hardware PLI's production-to-investment ratio is so much higher than LSEM's, and did not find a DVA evaluation for that scheme to compare against LSEM's 23% figure — the "capital-light vs. low-value-add" question is posed, not answered. It does not have MPMS-specific execution data yet, since the scheme was only notified 21 August 2026 and no applications have been reported approved under it in the sources checked. It does not independently verify MeitY's own claimed demand-coverage percentages for ECMS output categories (anode material ~110%, Optical Transceiver-SFP 350%, relays 200%) against any non-government market-sizing source.

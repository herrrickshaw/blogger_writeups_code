India’s new ethanol tender puts maize last in line for oil-company orders. A maize distillery that also buys grain in price dips and sells its spare maize to poultry and feed mills earned about four times the EBITDA of a pure ethanol plant on 2025-26 prices. On 2024-25 prices, and in Uttar Pradesh’s weak 2025 window, it earned less. The model works only with strict buying rules.

Maize distilleries that trade grain: about 4 times the EBITDA at 2025-26 prices

Skip to article content
Text Size

Top line

  • About 4x the EBITDA of a pure ethanol plant on 2025-26 prices, for a maize distillery that also trades grain.
  • Rs 45.6 crore a year more for an integrated plant in the 2025-26 glut, but Rs 13.6 crore less on 2024-25 prices.

Updated 15 November 2026: rewritten as charts; figures unchanged.

Buying grain in a dip lifts contribution per kilolitre to Rs 14,178

What it means: Buying half the year's maize in a price dip and reselling the spare puts the integrated plant well ahead per kilolitre.

What to do: Stock up only when the price is at least 15% below its trailing 12-month mean and arrivals are surging.

The trading desk gains in a real dip and loses when buying at harvest prices

What it means: The integrated plant gained Rs 45.6 crore a year in the 2025-26 glut but fell behind when it bought at harvest prices in 2024-25.

What to do: Buy ahead only in a real dip, and otherwise buy just the 30 to 45 days of maize the plant will use.

Owned silos cost Rs 728 to Rs 1,456 a quintal, so rented storage is the cheaper route

What it means: Owned silos cost far more than a season of rented warehouse space, which the post prices at about Rs 10 a quintal a month.

What to do: Rent warehouse space for one season rather than building silos to hold grain for years.

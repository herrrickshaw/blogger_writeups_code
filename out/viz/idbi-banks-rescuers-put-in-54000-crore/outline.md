On 14 October 2026 the Cabinet Committee on Economic Affairs approved the sale of 60.72% of IDBI Bank to 10955230 Canada Inc., a company owned by Fairfax Financial Holdings of Toronto, for ₹52,878 crore. The government sells 30.48% and LIC sells 30.24%, both at ₹81 a share. The Finance Minister called it “not a disinvestment, but a restructuring”. The government described it as the largest foreign investment in India’s financial sector.

The question this piece answers is narrower than whether ₹81 was a good price. It adds up what the two owners put into the bank while it was failing, and what they get back from this sale, the stakes they keep and the dividends already paid.

## How the price was set

The sale began with an expression-of-interest notice on 7 October 2022, when the share traded at ₹42.40. Five parties expressed interest, four qualified and two bid. By January 2026, when first bids were invited, the share had run up to ₹108.12, and the reserve price was set at ₹94.42. The best bid, about ₹75, fell short and was rejected. The government then asked for best offers in May 2026, when the share was at ₹70.38.

For that round two independent valuers estimated the bank’s worth from its March 2026 accounts, one by comparing it with listed public-sector banks and one by valuing its assets net of liabilities. Their average, ₹75.99 a share, was the benchmark. Fairfax’s ₹81 is 6.6% above that and above each valuer’s figure. It is 15% above the May price, and about 10% below the ₹89.85 close on the day of the decision. The share fell about 7% the next day.

At ₹81 the whole bank is valued at about ₹87,094 crore, or 1.37 times its September 2026 book value of ₹59.22 a share (1.46 times on the March 2026 book of ₹55.41). Profit for 2025-26 was ₹9,513 crore, so the price is about 9.2 times that year’s earnings per share of ₹8.85.

## The money that went in

IDBI Bank’s gross bad loans peaked at 31.78% of loans in September 2018, and the Reserve Bank of India kept it under Prompt Corrective Action, which limits lending and spending, from May 2017 to March 2021. The government recapitalised it almost every year from 2010-11, more heavily than any other state bank in 2017-18. In 2018-19 LIC bought control, and both owners added more in October 2019.

The government. Its whole 45.48% stake at ₹81, plus dividends, comes to about ₹41,860 crore against ₹27,441 crore put in since 2010-11, about 1.5 times. That flatters the government in one way and understates it in another. The government owned the bank long before 2010, and the value of that original stake is not counted as money in. Against that, grown at 7% a year to March 2027 (roughly what the government paid to borrow over the period), the money put in comes to about ₹57,103 crore. The value coming back, with dividends grown the same way, is about ₹42,274 crore, about 26% less.

LIC. The shares it bought in 2018-19 cost ₹26,717 crore, an average of ₹53.86 a share. At ₹81 they are worth about ₹40,179 crore, and they have earned about ₹2,282 crore in dividends, about 1.6 times the money put in. Grown at 7% a year, that money would have reached about ₹46,273 crore by March 2027, about ₹3,392 crore more than what comes back. The return works out at about 6% a year, and only if LIC can also sell the 19% it keeps at about ₹81. LIC invests policyholders’ money. On these figures its rescue made a profit, but a smaller one than a government bond would have paid.

Both results depend on the price of the stakes still held. A ₹10 move in the share price changes the value of the government’s 15% by about ₹1,613 crore and LIC’s 19% by about ₹2,043 crore.

## What still has to happen

- The RBI must confirm Fairfax as fit and proper and approve its plan for meeting ownership rules. The government expects completion by March 2027.

- Fairfax must make an open offer to the remaining public shareholders, who hold about 5.3%. The offer price will be set under SEBI’s takeover rules, which can put it above ₹81 if the share has traded higher.

- Fairfax already controls CSB Bank. It has two years to merge it with IDBI Bank or sell out, and the IDBI name is to be kept for three years after completion, Indian Express reports.

- CRISIL put IDBI Bank’s rating on watch with positive implications on 23 October 2026.

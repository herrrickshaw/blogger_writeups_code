Fairfax is paying ₹52,878 crore for 60.72% of the bank. Counting the stakes they keep at ₹81 and the dividends paid, the government gets back about 1.5 times the ₹27,441 crore it put in since 2010-11, and LIC earns about 6% a year on its 2018-19 rescue. Both fall short of what the same money would have earned at 7%, roughly the government’s own borrowing rate.

What would fix this

  • When the government sells a company, the Department of Investment and Public Asset Management (DIPAM) should publish one table of every rupee put in since the company first needed support, split into cash and bonds, next to the sale proceeds.
  • The cost of recapitalisation bonds should be reported bank by bank, including the interest paid to date, so that a “cash-neutral” rescue shows its true cost.
  • LIC should report in its annual report the cost, dividends and sale value of its IDBI holding, as it would for any large single investment of policyholders’ money.
  • The two independent valuations should be published in summary once the deal closes, so that the ₹75.99 benchmark can be checked.
  • The government’s remaining 15% should be sold on a published timetable, so that the overhang on the share price is known in advance.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

What this piece could not establish

Government capital before 2010-11, and the value of the government’s stake when IDBI became a bank in 2004, are not counted, so the government’s “money in” is a floor, not the full cost. The interest actually paid on IDBI’s share of recapitalisation bonds could not be found; the 7% rate stands in for it. Dates for the pre-2017 tranches are placed at mid-year. The FY 2025-26 dividend could not be confirmed either way. LIC’s cost for the shares it held before 2018 was not found, so those shares are left out. The open-offer price, the two valuers’ separate figures and how the ₹52,878 crore will count against this year’s disinvestment target were not public at the time of writing. Price-to-book figures for other banks were not compiled for comparison. Several 2026 figures (Q2 results, the CRISIL action, the post-deal share moves) were read through news reports and search-engine text rather than the filings themselves.

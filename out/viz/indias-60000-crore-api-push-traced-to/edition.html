India's "₹60,000 crore API push," announced in December 2025, is not one new scheme — it is at least six existing ones added together, plus the private co-investment they're expected to pull in. Traced to its parts, alongside the real (if still modest) progress the flagship Bulk Drugs PLI has made since, the number tells a more specific story than the headline does.

Top line

  • ₹43,000–44,000 crore of government outlay sits behind the ₹60,000cr "API push" headline, which is a roll-up, not one appropriation.
  • ₹5,211cr actually invested under the Bulk Drugs PLI by June 2026, against a ₹6,940cr outlay.
  • 43.45% of India's pharma imports still come from China, a share these schemes have not moved much yet.
Updated 7 November 2026. The Bulk Drugs PLI has moved since the March 2026 figures below. A government statement reported in June 2026 put investment at ₹5,210.74 crore (up from ₹4,814 crore in December 2025), 39 projects commissioned, and cumulative sales of ₹3,792.49 crore including ₹560.16 crore of exports; the trend table now carries that row, with import substitution taken as sales less exports, the same relation the earlier rows show. No Bulk Drug Park was found to be in commercial production: Gujarat approved an incentive scheme for units in its Jambusar park in October 2026.

Updated 15 November 2026: rewritten as charts; figures unchanged.

Government outlays behind the ₹60,000 crore headline add up to about ₹43,000 crore

What it means: Government outlays alone total about ₹43,000–44,000 crore, and the gap to ₹60,000 crore is private and state co-investment the schemes are built to attract.

What to do: Judge the push by each scheme's outlay and results, not by the ₹60,000 crore roll-up.

Investment attracted ran ahead of the ₹4,322 crore commitment by December 2025

What it means: The Bulk Drugs PLI attracted ₹4,814 crore by December 2025 against a committed ₹4,322 crore, so investment is ahead of its own commitment.

What to do: Check whether investment keeps pace with the ₹6,940 crore outlay while the 43.45% China import share stays put.

What would fix this

  • The Department of Pharmaceuticals should publish one table reconciling the ₹60,000 crore “API push” to its component schemes, separating government outlay from expected private and state co-investment, so the roughly ₹43,000–44,000 crore of public money is not reported as a single December 2025 decision.
  • The Department of Pharmaceuticals should publish Bulk Drugs PLI results molecule by molecule each quarter — capacity created, sales, exports and import substitution — with the reporting date of each figure, so restated data, like the near-identical December 2025 and March 2026 numbers, can be told apart from fresh growth.
  • The Department of Pharmaceuticals should publish, for each of the 41 critical molecules, India's annual import value from China before and after the scheme, using DGCI&S data, so cumulative substitution of about ₹2,190 crore can be measured against the 43.45% dependency it was built to reduce.
  • The Department of Pharmaceuticals and the Andhra Pradesh, Gujarat and Himachal Pradesh governments should publish a dated completion schedule for each Bulk Drug Park, with central and state funds released and spent, so a scheme with none of its three parks in commercial production after five years has milestones that can be checked.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

BIS is the name everyone reaches for, but it's one of roughly three dozen statutory regulators, accreditation boards and voluntary marks that certify, license and grade quality in India, stacked across four layers most people never see below the logo on the box. A public reference repository maps all 37, grounded in the actual Acts and gazette notifications rather than descriptions of them — and cross-checking its two "this just changed" examples against current sources turned up a third: one of the two schemes it cites as a recent, live example of the system in motion was actually repealed outright in January 2026, months before the date the source material still lists it as taking effect.

Top line

  • 721 products still covered by Quality Control Orders in March 2026, down from 773 in August 2025.
  • 142 Quality Control Orders in force; the "voluntary" ISI mark is compulsory wherever one names a product.
Updated 12 November 2026. This piece said “over 150” product categories were under compulsory certification. DPIIT told the Lok Sabha on 10 March 2026 that 142 Quality Control Orders covering 721 products were in force, down from 190 orders and 773 products in August 2025, after ministries withdrew compulsory certification for dozens of chemicals, polymers, metals and fibres from July 2025. A new section sets out those withdrawals and the orders added since. Scheme-X’s history now includes its open-ended deferral of November 2025, and packaged drinking water is no longer given as an example of a compulsory ISI mark, since FSSAI’s 2024 amendment, described below, removed that requirement.

What would fix this

  • Each ministry that rescinds, suspends or defers a Quality Control Order should publish its reasons, and the report of the committee whose review reportedly preceded the 2025 withdrawals should be released, so that manufacturers who invested to comply can see why the requirement went.
  • DPIIT should publish one consolidated, dated register of every Quality Control Order in force, with product category, issuing ministry, effective date and current status, and reconcile it with the Department of Consumer Affairs’ count (142 orders in DPIIT’s March 2026 answer, 191 in Consumer Affairs’ year-end review), so that a business can check whether a mark is compulsory without piecing together 37 bodies’ separate notifications.
  • BIS should keep a public change log recording each postponement, suspension and repeal of a certification scheme with its gazette reference, so that a reversal like Scheme-X’s repeal on 14 January 2026 is visible on the regulator’s own site rather than only in trade-compliance coverage.
  • The Ministry of Heavy Industries should publish a short reasoned note whenever it postpones or repeals a technical regulation order, so that the move from an August 2025 target to 1 September 2026 and then to outright repeal is explained to the manufacturers who prepared for it.
  • The Legislative Department should make India Code available as a stable, bulk-downloadable archive of Acts and amendments, so that researchers mapping quality law are not forced onto IndianKanoon copies and Wayback snapshots when official hosts are unreachable.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

What this piece does not establish

This is an orientation map, not a compliance checklist — the same caveat the source repository puts on itself, and worth repeating rather than softening. Whether a specific product needs BIS registration, which QCO actually applies to it, or which CDSCO device class it falls into depends on the current gazette notification, not a summary of one. The source repository itself documents that several official .gov.in/.nic.in hosts — including indiacode.nic.in, legislative.gov.in and beeindia.gov.in — were consistently unreachable to its own automated research, with IndianKanoon copies and Wayback Machine snapshots covering most of the resulting gaps; this piece did not independently re-fetch every primary document behind every one of the 37 entries, and relied on the repository's own sourcing for anything not explicitly re-checked above. The Scheme-X correction above was independently verified against multiple trade-compliance sources describing the same repeal date; the BEE and FSSAI items were corroborated the same way. Readers relying on any specific product's certification status for an actual filing should go to the regulator's current notification directly, not to this piece or its source.

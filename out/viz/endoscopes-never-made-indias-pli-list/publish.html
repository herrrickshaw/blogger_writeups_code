PLI-MD's own gap list names endoscopes as one of ten device categories the scheme never touched — $112mn in imports, Japan-dominated, no applicant. But cross-checked against the same FY2025-26 TradeStat fetch, endoscopes already run 57% export coverage — better than every device family PLI actually subsidised except renal care. Here's what that contradiction looks like, and why the scheme meant to fix it never touched this line.

Endoscopes and India's PLI scheme: never listed, at 57% export coverage

Skip to article content
Text Size

Top line

  • 57% export coverage for endoscopes in FY2025-26, against 48% for the surgical and diagnostic instruments family.
  • $112mn of endoscope imports in FY2025-26 (provisional), with Japan-dominated supply.
  • 38 devices on PLI-MD's official list, and none of them is an endoscope.
Updated 12 October 2026. The Department of Pharmaceuticals’ call for proposals under the SMDI scheme opened on 23 June 2026 and closed on 23 July 2026 (6 pm, portal smdi.lsssdc.in). No extension or new round has been announced in the coverage seen, so the components and accessories window described below as open is now closed; a future applicant would have to wait for the next call. Sources: DoP call for proposals, All India Radio. The original text below is unchanged.
Updated 7 November 2026. Two developments since this piece was written. Fujifilm India began endoscopy manufacturing in Jodhpur on 27 February 2026, through a contract manufacturing partner with localisation promised over time, and in September 2026 dedicated its first serial-numbered “Made in India” EP-6000 endoscopy system at AIIMS New Delhi; this is outside PLI-MD and does not change the FY2025-26 trade figures below, but it means finished endoscopy systems are now assembled in India. And under SMDI, 24 projects worth about ₹101.5 crore had been approved under the import-reduction (MISRI) sub-scheme by late July 2026, covering items such as X-ray tubes and RT-PCR machines; no endoscope project has been reported among them.

Updated 15 November 2026: rewritten as charts; figures unchanged.

Endoscopes export 57% of their import value, ahead of most PLI-covered lines

What it means: Endoscopes export 57% of their imports, beating every PLI-covered line in this data except dialysis machines.

What to do: Break the 57% figure into finished scopes, sub-assemblies and re-exports before reading it as a strength.

Endoscope imports of $112mn were partly offset by about $64mn of exports

What it means: Endoscope imports of $112mn in FY2025-26 (provisional) were offset in part by roughly $64mn of exports.

What to do: Count capsule, disposable and AI-detection devices separately, since the $112mn line likely leaves them out.

PLI-MD's 38-device list omits endoscopes, and new applications close in March 2027

  1. 16 December 2025Lok Sabha statement: PLI-MD closes to new applicants in March 2027
  2. 13 February 2026Official 38-device PLI-MD list published; endoscopes are not a line item
  3. June 2026SMDI components and accessories window opens
  4. March 2027PLI-MD closes to new applicants

What it means: PLI-MD's official 38-device list does not name endoscopes, and the scheme closes to new applicants in March 2027.

What to do: Confirm whether the SMDI components window is still open before planning an endoscope application.

What would fix this

  • CBIC and DGCI&S should create separate eight-digit tariff lines for capsule endoscopes and single-use endoscopes, so India's import exposure to these growing segments is measured directly instead of being hidden inside, or missing from, the $112 million rigid and flexible endoscope line.
  • DGCI&S should publish the endoscope export line split between finished scopes, parts and accessories, and re-exports, so it can be established whether the 57 per cent export coverage reflects Indian manufacturing or goods passing through.
  • The Department of Pharmaceuticals should publish SMDI applications and approvals by device category and component, naming optics, imaging chips and scope assemblies where they appear, so it is checkable whether the open components window actually reaches the upstream endoscope gap PLI-MD never covered.
  • The Department of Pharmaceuticals should publish an annual table for the 148-code device basket listing imports, exports, export coverage and PLI-MD or SMDI applicants for each line, so scheme gaps are ranked by trade data rather than by whether a category appears on a scheme list.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

Nine years into GST, more than a third of every rupee India's states raise from their own taxes still comes from two things GST was never allowed to touch: fuel and liquor. This piece pulls together the national composition of state own-tax revenue and a state-by-state breakdown to show exactly how load-bearing that "fuel plus bottle" combination actually is — and why, constitutionally, one of the two is excluded from GST forever while the other is merely deferred.

Fuel VAT and liquor excise supply 34% of state own-tax revenue, outside GST

What it means: Fuel VAT and liquor excise together make up a third of what states raise themselves, from two commodities outside GST.

What to do: Treat petrol and diesel as roughly a fifth of state own-tax revenue in any GST Council decision about them.

What it means: Kerala's 40.6% runs mostly through fuel VAT at 2.4% of GSDP, while its liquor excise is only 0.2%.

What to do: Compare states on their fuel and liquor shares together with each state's own-tax total, not on either line alone.

Bihar, Mizoram and Nagaland collect no liquor excise under prohibition

What it means: Bihar's fuel-plus-liquor share is just 18.2%, with fuel VAT at 1.0% of GSDP and no excise.

What to do: Model a prohibition state's revenue on fuel VAT alone, not on a combined fuel-and-liquor base.

What would fix this

  • The RBI should report each state’s sales tax/VAT receipts split into petroleum products, alcohol and other goods in its annual study of state finances, so that the ‘Fuel VAT’ line no longer has to be read as predominantly, not exclusively, fuel.
  • The GST Council should publish a state-wise analysis of the revenue at stake if petrol, diesel, natural gas and ATF were brought into GST, so that the nine-year deferral of a notification date is argued on published numbers rather than left as an unexplained non-decision.
  • The CAG should publish, alongside budget estimates, each state’s audited actual collections of fuel VAT and state excise for the same year, so that dependence figures built on 2025–26 Budget Estimates can be checked against what states actually collected.
  • State finance departments should show in their budget documents the revenue effect of each fuel-VAT and liquor-excise rate change they make, so that a state drawing a third or more of its own-tax revenue from these two lines can be held to its own estimates.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

Sources

  • PRS Legislative Research, "State of State Finances 2025" (October 2025) — national and state-wise composition of own-tax revenue as % of GSDP, 2025-26 Budget Estimates, compiled from state budget documents
  • Constitution of India, Article 366(12A), as inserted by the Constitution (101st Amendment) Act, 2016 — definition of "Goods and Services Tax" and its exclusion of alcoholic liquor for human consumption
  • This blog's own GST devolution piece — on the GST Council's still-unexercised power to notify a date for petroleum crude, petrol, natural gas and ATF to enter GST
  • This blog's own CNG VAT and ethanol SGST reform series — state-level petrol VAT rates and CNG VAT rates referenced in Section 5

All state and national figures in this piece are Budget Estimates for 2025-26 as compiled by PRS Legislative Research from state budget documents, not audited actuals — actual year-end collection can and does diverge from budget targets, sometimes materially (this blog's own reporting has separately flagged Uttar Pradesh's own-tax revenue as consistently realising below its budget target in past years). The "Fuel VAT" column is this piece's own label for the source report's "Sales Tax/VAT" category; the source report describes this category as covering fuel and alcohol together in principle, but reports it net of the separately-itemised Excise Duty line, so this piece treats the two as distinct and labels them accordingly. Figures are rounded to one decimal place as published in the source; percentage-of-own-tax shares are this piece's own arithmetic on those rounded figures and carry the resulting small rounding uncertainty.

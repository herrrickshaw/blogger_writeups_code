The Cabinet raised the pay limit for compulsory provident fund and pension cover on 16 September 2026, bringing about 51 lakh more workers in. Adjusted for prices it restores the 2014 ceiling rather than extending it, and nothing stops it eroding again: there is no rule for raising it, and minimum wages in several states had already passed the old limit.

EPFO pay ceiling: ₹25,000 after 12 years, slightly below the old ₹15,000 in 2014 rupees

Skip to article content
Text Size

Top line

  • 12 years since the pay ceiling last moved; ₹25,000 is worth about ₹14,520 in 2014 rupees.
  • 67% rise in the employer's pension share for members already earning above ₹15,000.
  • 51 lakh more workers the government expects to bring under compulsory cover.
Updated 28 October 2026. Checked against the Gazette of India: the new ceiling is notified under section 2(89) of the Code on Social Security, 2020, for Chapter III of the Code (the provident fund, pension and insurance schemes), at Rs 25,000 a month from the date of publication, 17 September 2026 (CG-DL-E-17092026-276299). It supersedes S.O. 2702(E) of 29 May 2026 (CG-DL-E-30052026-273002), which had re-notified the Rs 15,000 ceiling under the Code. The figures and dates in the piece match. No figure changed.

Updated 15 November 2026: rewritten as charts; figures unchanged.

The ₹25,000 ceiling is worth about ₹14,520 in 2014 rupees, less than the old ₹15,000

What it means: The ₹15,000 ceiling set in 2014 was worth about ₹8,710 by August 2026, and the new ₹25,000 only restores it to about ₹14,520 in 2014 money.

What to do: Index the ceiling to prices or wages so it moves without waiting for a new decision.

What it means: Each long gap let the ceiling fall behind wages, and the ceiling has been held fixed for 12 to 18 years at a time.

What to do: Set a fixed review date for the ceiling so a gap of more than a decade cannot build up again.

Minimum wages in Delhi and Haryana already exceed the old ₹15,000 ceiling

What it means: The minimum wage in Delhi and Haryana already sat above the ₹15,000 ceiling, so workers there were outside compulsory cover.

What to do: Align the provident fund ceiling with the ESI threshold so the two schemes cover the same workers.

What it means: Contributions for members already earning above ₹15,000 are worked out on pay up to the new ceiling, so the statutory base rises from ₹15,000 to ₹25,000.

What to do: Publish the scheme's actuarial valuation so the larger pension promise can be checked.

What would fix this

  • The Ministry of Labour and Employment should index the wage ceiling to a published measure, such as the Consumer Price Index for Industrial Workers or the median wage of EPFO members, revised every two or three years by notification, so the ceiling does not lose two-fifths of its value between decisions, as it did from 2014 to 2026.
  • The Ministry of Labour and Employment should align the provident fund and ESI thresholds and revise them together, so the same worker is not compulsorily covered for retirement but not for health.
  • EPFO should publish the actuarial valuation of the Employees’ Pension Scheme with the new ceiling, showing the cost of the higher pension promise and how the government’s 1.16% contribution and the employers’ 8.33% are expected to meet it.
  • EPFO should publish monthly enrolment by wage band, so it is possible to see how many of the expected 51 lakh workers are actually enrolled, and in which sectors and states.
  • The Ministry of Labour and Employment should publish how the 51 lakh estimate and the ₹11,339 crore outgo were calculated, so the figures can be checked against enrolment as it happens.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

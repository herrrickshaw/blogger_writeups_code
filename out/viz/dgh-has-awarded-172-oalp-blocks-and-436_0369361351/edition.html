India has awarded roughly 172 exploration blocks under OALP, backed by $4.36 billion in committed investment. Fourteen of them have produced a discovery. One has actually reached production. The bidding process itself — DGH's data rooms, year-round block nomination, work-programme scoring — is a genuinely well-designed system for handing out acreage. What happens after the award is the part worth scrutinising.

Top line

  • 172 OALP blocks awarded through Round IX, but only 14 discoveries reported when the piece was written.
  • 4 bidders in total for Round IX's 28 blocks; most individual blocks drew only two bids.
  • 16.66% to 10% onshore crude royalty, cut on 8 May 2026.
Updated 7 November 2026. The petroleum ministry’s own progress figures (reported by BusinessLine, 10 September 2026) put OALP Rounds I–IX at 172 blocks, 3.79 lakh sq km, about $4.36 billion committed, 114 exploratory wells drilled and 21 hydrocarbon discoveries as of March 2026, against the 14 this piece first reported; the chart below still shows the older 14 and 13 counts. No second producing field has been reported. Round X’s area is corrected to DGH’s 1,82,589 sq km. Bids for the deepwater and ultra-deepwater blocks of Rounds X and XI are now due on 15 November 2026 after a further extension, and on 26 October 2026 DGH posted advance information on a separate “Special OALP” for deepwater blocks, following the Cabinet’s approval of the ₹84,084 crore Samudra Manthan offshore exploration scheme on 31 July 2026.

Updated 15 November 2026: rewritten as charts; figures unchanged.

Awarded blocks far outnumber reported discoveries; one field is in production

What it means: Only 14 discoveries had been reported against roughly 172 awarded blocks, and the ministry's March 2026 count was 21.

What to do: Report discoveries and production alongside block counts when judging the licensing system.

Round IX drew four bidders in total; Round X's bid deadline has been extended repeatedly

What it means: Round IX's 28 blocks drew only four bidders in total, and most individual blocks received two bids.

What to do: Read Round X and XI as offers until the deadline closes and an award is announced.

Royalty rates were cut on 8 May 2026 for onshore, offshore, gas and nominated blocks

What it means: The cut covers onshore, offshore and gas royalties, and ICRA says it should improve upstream project returns.

What to do: Check the revised royalty rates against the government notification, since the article did not verify them against it.

What would fix this

  • The Directorate General of Hydrocarbons should publish a block-by-block OALP tracker showing award date, minimum work programme completed, wells drilled, discoveries, declarations of commerciality, field development plan status and production, so the drop from about 172 awarded blocks to one producing field can be traced stage by stage.
  • The Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas should publish a reconciliation of committed against actually spent exploration investment, round by round, so the $4.36 billion, 172-block figure and the $3.137 billion, 144-block figure from its own parliamentary answer can be read on one stated scope.
  • The Directorate General of Hydrocarbons should state the reason in every notice extending a bid deadline, so the six extensions of Round X, for which no official explanation was found, are explained on the record rather than by trade press.
  • The Directorate General of Hydrocarbons should keep its OALP bid procedure, scoring weights including the nominator’s bonus, and the Model Revenue Sharing Contract permanently posted as accessible primary documents, so relinquishment percentages and evaluation timelines need not be taken from secondary sources.
  • The Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas should report annually which OALP blocks are awaiting defence, environmental or other clearances, naming the agency and days pending, so whether NELP-era clearance conflicts recur under OALP can be checked rather than left open.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

Sources and caveats

This piece was researched through web search rather than direct page fetches: DGH's own procedure and model-contract PDFs (online.dghindia.org and several dghindia.gov.in documents) were inconsistently reachable in this research environment, meaning several of the more granular figures in this piece — the relinquishment percentage, the OALP originator's scoring bonus, DGH's evaluation-timeline target — rest on secondary/aggregator sources rather than a primary document read in full, and are flagged individually above rather than presented with uniform confidence. DGH's founding, mandate and advisory (non-statutory) status (Section 1) are corroborated across a Tesseract Academy explainer, DGH's own indexed mandate page, Oil & Gas Journal's independent-regulator commentary, and a PRS Legislative Research report summary. HELP's 2016 design (Section 2) is corroborated across a Wikipedia summary and a Consulate General of India, Houston hosted PDF. OALP's mechanics (Section 3) draw on drishtiias and a DGH-hosted NSP dashboard PDF; its round-by-round figures draw on PIB press releases directly for the Rounds I-VII cumulative total and Round IX's detailed breakdown (a stronger, primary-source basis than most of this piece), and on trade coverage from psuwatch, The Statesman, Business Standard and discoveryalert.com.au for Round X and XI's still-unresolved bidding timeline, with the 144-block/242,056 sq km Rounds I-VIII total and Round X/XI's interim closing dates additionally corroborated on DGH's own indexed site. CBM's separate bidding history, CIL's coal-mining-lease CBM right, the 2018 unconventional-hydrocarbons extension policy, and the resource/production figures (Section 4) are drawn directly from PIB press releases throughout — the strongest primary-source basis of any section in this piece. The award-to-relinquishment lifecycle (Section 5) draws on DGH's own Model Revenue Sharing Contract and Site Restoration and Abandonment Guidelines 2018, both reached via search index. The 2025 Act and 2026 royalty reform (Section 6) are corroborated across TeamLease RegTech, Legality Simplified, and psuwatch. The conversion-rate finding and friction points (Section 7) draw on Whalesbook for the core 172-blocks/$4.36bn/14-discoveries/one-field figures, now independently corroborated in its central qualitative finding by a Lok Sabha unstarred question (No. 4397, MoPNG, answered 27 March 2025, via sansad.in) reporting 13 discoveries and one producing Gujarat gas block at 0.44 MMSCMD against a $3.137bn/144-block scope — a meaningfully stronger evidentiary basis than a single trade outlet alone, even though the two sources' exact block-count scopes were not reconciled. BusinessToday's April 2026 foreign-bidder finding and Anil Agarwal's own public statement remain single-source and are flagged as such. Nothing in this piece is investment, legal, or bidding advice; a reader making an actual exploration-licensing, bidding, or compliance decision should consult DGH's own current primary procedure documents, the Model Revenue Sharing Contract, and MoPNG's current rules directly, not this summary.

Under CORSIA's own default carbon-intensity tables, corn/maize ethanol converted to jet fuel scores worse than the fossil fuel it is meant to replace. India's ethanol policy has been actively promoting maize as a feedstock since November 2023. The two facts sit on a collision course that neither programme's own paperwork resolves.

Top line

  • 90.8 gCO₂e/MJ is corn ethanol's CORSIA default carbon intensity, above the 89 fossil-jet baseline it must beat.
  • 94% of India's used cooking oil, the feedstock SAF can use today, goes uncollected.
  • 34.3 gCO₂e/MJ for molasses, but only on an isobutanol route India does not use.

Updated 15 November 2026: rewritten as charts; figures unchanged.

Corn ethanol's CORSIA default (90.8–100.6 gCO₂e/MJ) sits above the 89 baseline

What it means: A simplified calculator's maize value of 43 gCO₂e/MJ passes, while CORSIA's own default table puts maize ethanol above the baseline.

What to do: Check any maize-ethanol SAF claim against the 8th-edition ICAO table or an approved body's actual-value calculation, not a generic calculator value.

Molasses passes CORSIA only through the isobutanol route India does not use

What it means: Molasses has a default of 34.3–36.1 gCO₂e/MJ only on the isobutanol route, and no default at all on the ethanol route India uses.

What to do: An Indian molasses-to-ethanol-to-jet producer needs a full actual-value lifecycle calculation through an approved certification body.

Only 6% of India's used cooking oil is collected, the feedstock SAF uses today

What it means: India generates an estimated 1.8–2.6 million tonnes of used cooking oil a year, and only 110,000–156,000 tonnes is collected.

What to do: Build collection before capacity, since the 5%-by-2030 target needs roughly 0.4–0.5 million tonnes of SAF a year.

India's SAF targets reach only 5% by 2030, and cover international flights only

What it means: India's indicative targets are 1%, 2% and 5% for 2027, 2028 and 2030, and cover international flights only.

What to do: Notify a domestic-flight SAF mandate, which India has not yet issued.

What would fix this

  • Rule maize ethanol out of SAF policy explicitly, in writing, now. Nothing currently stops maize-ethanol capacity built for the road-fuel mandate from being pitched at SAF investors or procurement programmes; an explicit statement that it does not qualify under CORSIA's own default values would prevent capital being misallocated toward a route that cannot be certified as built.
  • Get an actual-value CORSIA calculation done for molasses-ethanol-to-jet, once, and publish it. A single approved-body actual-value study, done centrally and made public, would let every Indian molasses-ethanol producer cite a validated number instead of each commissioning its own audit from scratch.
  • Publish a UCO collection target with a funded mechanism, not just a feedstock estimate. A 94%-uncollected figure restated every year without a collection-infrastructure plan attached is a fact, not a policy.
  • Name a domestic-flight SAF trajectory, even a modest one. International-only targets leave the much larger domestic aviation market with no signal at all to plan capacity against.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

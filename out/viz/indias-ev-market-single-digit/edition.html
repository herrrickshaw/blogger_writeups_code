India's EV sales: 12% of vehicles in April–September, cars in single digits

Skip to article content
Text Size

Top line

  • 8.5% of India's vehicle sales in FY26 were electric, up from 6.8% in FY24.
  • 4% of India's car sales are electric, against 25% of car sales worldwide in 2025.
Updated 9 November 2026. Since this piece was written, EV penetration has moved into double digits. On Vahan registrations, India sold 17.89 lakh EVs in April–September 2026 (H1 FY2027), up 59.3% on a year earlier, and EVs were 12% of all vehicles sold, against 8% in FY2026 on the same basis (JMK Research, 7 October 2026). Electric cars reached 1.83 lakh units in those six months, and their penetration rose from 4% to 7%. September 2026 set a record of about 3.34 lakh EVs, about 13% of retail sales, with e-2Ws at 11.6% (FADA). October was higher still, at about 3.54 lakh (Times of India, from Vahan), while the electric share of car sales eased to 7.3% (ETAuto, from FADA). The FY2026 figures below are unchanged. The “single-digit” framing now holds for cars, not for the market as a whole. The headline, which said penetration was in single digits, has been changed: that is now true for cars but not for the market as a whole.
Updated 28 October 2026. The 12 October note below has been checked against the Gazette of India. S.O. 1617(E) of 27 March 2026 had set 31 July 2026 as the last day for the e-2W incentive; S.O. 4424(E) of 10 August 2026 extended it to 31 March 2028 at ₹2,500 a kWh, capped at ₹5,000, raised the e-2W allocation to ₹2,767 crore, set 31 December 2027 as the last date for claims and raised PM E-DRIVE’s total outlay from ₹10,900 crore to ₹11,900 crore. The full chain of notifications: S.O. 4259(E) of 29 September 2024 (CG-DL-E-30092024-257594), which notified the scheme; S.O. 3626(E) of 7 August 2025 (CG-DL-E-07082025-265287), which extended it to 31 March 2028 within ₹10,900 crore but kept 31 March 2026 for e-2Ws and e-3Ws; S.O. 1617(E) of 27 March 2026 (CG-DL-E-27032026-271345), which moved the e-2W end date to 31 July 2026 with ₹1,772 crore, kept e-rickshaws and e-carts to 31 March 2028 and recorded the L5 e-3W segment closed on 26 December 2025; S.O. 3191(E) of 17 June 2026 (CG-DL-E-17062026-273533), which added e-ambulances; and S.O. 4424(E) of 10 August 2026 (CG-DL-E-10082026-275344), which raised the outlay to ₹11,900 crore for 1 April 2024 to 31 March 2028, extended the e-2W incentive (₹2,500 a kWh, capped at ₹5,000) to 31 March 2028 with ₹2,767 crore, and set 31 December 2027 as the last date for claims; read in the Gazette of India’s own text via the SansadSaar index of the archive.org Gazette of India collection.
Updated 12 October 2026. On 10 August 2026 the Ministry of Heavy Industries extended the electric two-wheeler incentive to 31 March 2028, after about ten days with no subsidy. The rate is unchanged at ₹2,500 a kWh, capped at ₹5,000 a vehicle; the e-2W allocation rose from ₹1,772 crore to ₹2,767 crore, and claims must be filed by 31 December 2027 (confirmed in S.O. 4424(E) of 10 August 2026, Gazette of India, Extraordinary, CG-DL-E-10082026-275344). This article describes the subsidy as ending on 31 July 2026 and “tapering on a fixed clock”; that taper did not take effect, though the scheme remains limited by its funds. Updated 15 November 2026: rewritten as charts; figures unchanged.

Electric cars are 4% of India's car sales, against 25% worldwide

What it means: Passenger cars are the segment where India is furthest behind the world average.

What to do: Localise the component chain before car volumes arrive, as the post recommends.

Small vehicles carry India's electrification, at about 10% of two- and three-wheelers

What it means: Two- and three-wheelers, not cars, are where India's electrification is actually happening.

What to do: Finish the two- and three-wheeler transition, which the post names as the near-term prize.

An EV hatchback runs at Rs 1.41 a km, under half the Rs 2.97 of CNG

What it means: Running cost already favours EVs over CNG, so the personal-car gap is about upfront price, financing and charging.

What to do: Target upfront price, financing costs and charging access for personal buyers, the constraints the post identifies.

What would fix this

  • The Bureau of Energy Efficiency should publish a quarterly official count of public charging points by state, type and power rating under one stated methodology, so that India’s charging network is not described as 29,000+ stations by one source and about 88,000 points by another.
  • The Ministry of Heavy Industries should publish PM E-DRIVE disbursements and vehicles supported by segment, state and month, so that how much of e-2W demand is pulled forward by subsidy deadlines, such as the pre-July 2026 surge, can be measured directly rather than inferred from registration spikes.
  • The Ministry of Heavy Industries should maintain a public matrix of state EV incentives with each scheme’s start and end dates, benefit formula and annual fiscal cost, so that the “quality and continuity vary widely” patchwork across 14+ states can be compared and its cost to states tracked over time.
  • The Reserve Bank of India should publish average interest rates on new vehicle loans split by EV and internal-combustion vehicles, by segment, so that the financing-cost gap named as one of four constraints on personal EV adoption is measured rather than asserted.
  • The Ministry of Heavy Industries should publish the imported share of cells, power electronics and drivetrains in electric vehicles sold each year, so that the localisation gap flagged in 2021 and again in 2026 is tracked as a number rather than restated as a constraint.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

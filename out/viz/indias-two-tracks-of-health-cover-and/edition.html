India now runs two large, separately-growing health-cover systems at once. On one track, the government's Ayushman Bharat PM-JAY has issued over 48.5 crore cards (up from 43.52 crore when this piece was first written) and reached every state and union territory as of June 2026, after West Bengal became the last holdout to join. On the other, private individual health insurance premiums grew nearly 30% in the six months after a September 2025 GST exemption, and international reinsurers are opening branches in GIFT City at a pace that's tripled the sector's local capacity this year. Both are real growth stories. What this piece checks is whether they're actually the same story — whether public scheme coverage and private insurance uptake move together, substitute for each other, or barely touch — using the state-level and research data available, not assumption.

Top line

  • 29.7% growth in individual health premiums in Oct–Mar 2025-26, against 7.0% a year earlier, after the GST exemption.
  • 48.54 crore Ayushman cards created by the scheme's 8th anniversary, per government figures.
  • 3.7% of GDP is India's insurance premium share, against a 7.3% global average.

PM-JAY has issued 43.52 crore cards, rising to 48.54 crore by its 8th anniversary

What it means: The card count is the scheme's headline reach figure, and it grew quickly in the months after the original reporting.

What to do: Check the date of each card count before comparing it with other scheme figures, since the two come from different points in time.

India's premiums are 3.7% of GDP, against a 7.3% global average

What it means: Even after a decade of PM-JAY, private premiums remain a small share of GDP, which leaves a large gap to close.

What to do: Read the private growth as a low-base story, not as evidence that the market is already close to saturation.

What would fix this

  • IRDAI should publish monthly premium growth for individual health (including family floater) and individual life policies in its own statistical release, so that the post-GST-exemption jump from 7.0% to 29.7% can be checked against primary data rather than a secondary tax-news source.
  • The National Health Authority should publish, state by state, PM-JAY claim settlement time and rejection rate alongside each state’s operating model — trust, hybrid or insurance, so that the speed-versus-cost trade-off research has found can be tracked from official data every year.
  • The National Health Authority should publish PM-JAY admissions split by public and private hospitals for each state annually, so that the shift towards private facilities that studies associate with enrolment can be monitored directly as the scheme reaches all 36 states and UTs.
  • The health departments of Tamil Nadu, Odisha and Rajasthan should publish annual beneficiary and claim figures for CMCHIS, BSKY and Chiranjeevi separately from the PM-JAY component, so that how much state top-ups add above the ₹5 lakh floor is measurable.
  • IFSCA should publish annual health reinsurance premium written by GIFT City insurance offices, so that whether the roughly 24 active offices actually build the health line several entrants have flagged can be checked, rather than inferred from licence counts.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

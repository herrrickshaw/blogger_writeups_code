The government’s online marketplace has handled over Rs 20 lakh crore in ten years, but its 2025-26 purchases appear to have fallen for the first time. Small firms clear their 25% target easily; women- and SC/ST-owned firms still fall short across central purchases as a whole.

GeM procurement in 2025-26: small firms won two-thirds of orders and under half the money

Skip to article content
Text Size

Top line

  • Rs 5.0 lakh crore bought through GeM in 2025-26, about 8% less than the Rs 5.43 lakh crore of 2024-25.
  • 68% of GeM orders went to micro and small firms, but they took only 47.1% of the value.
  • 2.2% of central procurement went to women-owned small firms in 2024-25, against a 3% target.
Updated 7 November 2026. For 2026-27 so far, the Commerce Ministry reported Rs 1,47,888 crore bought through GeM in the first four months (April–July), as part of its ten-year milestone release of August 2026. No figure for the same months of 2025-26 was published alongside it, and government buying is heavily weighted to the last quarter, so four months cannot yet show whether growth has resumed. No first-half (April–September) total was found.

Updated 15 November 2026: rewritten as charts; figures unchanged.

GeM purchases stalled in 2025-26, about 8% below 2024-25

What it means: The ministry gave only 'about' Rs 5 lakh crore for 2025-26, so the total was derived from its small-firm figure.

What to do: Publish buyer-wise GeM figures so the 2025-26 dip can be traced to specific ministries.

What it means: Their orders were on average less than half the size of other orders, which is why the value share trails the order share.

What to do: Report the small-firm share of orders and of value against the 25% target on one stated basis.

The 3% women-owned target is met on GeM but not across central buying

What it means: 'Women-led' on GeM and 'women-owned' under the policy may not be the same test, and the periods differ.

What to do: Measure the women-owned target on GeM and on Sambandh with the same definition and period.

What would fix this

  • GeM should publish annual purchases by buying ministry, public company and state, with the share going to small, women-owned and SC/ST-owned firms for each, so a fall like 2025-26’s can be explained.
  • The MSME ministry should reconcile Sambandh with GeM and require every central buyer to report all procurement there, so the 25%, 4% and 3% targets are measured on the full base.
  • GeM should report women’s participation using the policy’s definition of a women-owned firm, alongside its own “women-led” count.
  • Both portals should report the time taken to pay small sellers, since late payment matters as much to them as winning orders.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

What this piece could not establish

The exact 2025-26 total was not published; the figure here is derived from the ministry’s small-firm numbers and could be off if those were rounded. The 2022-23 and 2023-24 figures are rounded as reported. No buyer-wise breakdown for 2025-26 was found, and statements on the public-sector share of GeM’s value differ by year and source. The Sambandh figures are for part of 2024-25; no full-year figure was found. GeM and government releases were read through news reports and search-engine text, since the official sites could not be opened.

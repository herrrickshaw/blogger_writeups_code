India sold a record Rs 38,424 crore of defence goods in 2025-26, 25 times the 2016-17 level. Public-sector exports grew 151% in a year while private exports grew 14%. Exports now need only 9% a year to hit Rs 50,000 crore; production needs 19% to hit Rs 3 lakh crore. India remains the world’s second-largest arms importer.

India's defence exports: up 63% to Rs 38,424 crore in 2025-26

Skip to article content
Text Size

Top line

  • Rs 38,424 crore of defence exports in 2025-26, up 62.7% from Rs 23,622 crore.
  • 86% of last year's Rs 14,802 crore rise came from public-sector firms.
  • 9% a year is all exports now need to reach the Rs 50,000 crore target by 2028-29.

Updated 15 November 2026: rewritten as charts; figures unchanged.

Defence exports have risen every year since FY21, reaching Rs 38,424 crore

What it means: Exports rose from Rs 1,522 crore in 2016-17 to Rs 38,424 crore, while production grew far more slowly.

What to do: Treat the exports-to-production ratio as indicative only, since the two series are measured differently.

Public-sector firms overtook private firms as the main exporters in 2025-26

What it means: Private exports still grew 14% to Rs 17,353 crore, so the record rests on a few state firms and large orders.

What to do: Watch whether private firms regain growth, since their two-year pace is about 7% a year.

Exports need about 9% a year to reach the 2029 target, production about 19%

What it means: Exports need about 9% a year against 34% over the last three years, so the export goal looks likely to be met.

What to do: Track deliveries on the large domestic orders, since the 2029 production goal depends on them.

What would fix this

  • The ministry should publish exports by broad product group (complete platforms, ammunition, components, body armour and so on) and by region each year. This would show how much of the record is one-off missile orders or wartime ammunition demand.
  • Exports should be reported in the same units as production, with the authorisations executed (not just issued) and the delivered value shown separately, so that the export share of production can be calculated properly.
  • When production figures are revised, as 2023-24 and 2024-25 both were, the release should say so and give the old figure.
  • Private-sector exports, flat at around Rs 15,000–17,000 crore for three years, need their own review. Export finance, faster authorisations for repeat orders and government-to-government backing should not be available mainly to state firms.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

What this piece could not establish

The ministry’s press releases on pib.gov.in and mod.gov.in could not be opened from the research machine, so every figure here comes from news coverage and study-guide summaries quoting them. Where those disagreed, the figure most often repeated with an exact value was used. The 2017-18 to 2020-21 export figures and the 2017-18 and 2018-19 production figures come from secondary tables, and the 2025-26 production figure is calculated from the stated growth rate. No country-wise or product-wise export data were found, the number of export authorisations for 2025-26 was not found, and reports differ on whether Indian defence goods reach more than 80 or more than 100 countries. The period covered by the Bloomberg ammunition figure is not clear from the reports. Whether the Rs 50,000 crore target is for 2028-29 or 2029-30 also varies between reports.

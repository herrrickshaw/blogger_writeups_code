The Corporate Laws (Amendment) Bill would raise the net profit that triggers mandatory CSR from Rs 5 crore to Rs 10 crore. Nearly half of today’s CSR spenders would be released. Their combined obligation is about Rs 1,282 crore, against Rs 40,794 crore spent in 2024-25.

Reported CSR spending rose from Rs 10,066 crore to Rs 40,794 crore since 2014-15

What it means: Reported CSR spend grew 13% to 17% a year in each of the last three years, so the loss from the Bill is small by comparison.

What to do: Check actual 2025-26 CSR spending against the ministry's Rs 1,282 crore estimate once the Factbook reports it.

What would fix this

  • Publish the ministry’s estimate in full. The 11,204-company figure and its Rs 1,282 crore were given to the committee but reached the public only through press reports. A table by state, development sector and implementing agency would show which NGOs and districts lose money.
  • Let leaving companies finish what they started. Companies with multi-year “ongoing projects” at the time of the change should be required, or at least allowed, to complete them under the old rules, so that a scholarship or clinic funded for three years is not dropped after one.
  • Simplify the form rather than only shrinking the list. For obligations under Rs 50 lakh, a one-page CSR filing would cut most of the cost of compliance while keeping the spending.
  • Keep thresholds in the Act, and review them on a schedule. The committee is right that a figure which decides whether 11,000 companies pay should be set by Parliament, not by rules. A requirement to review all three triggers every five years, against inflation and company size, would avoid another long gap like 2013 to 2026.
  • Count voluntary CSR. The CSR portal already records spending by companies with no obligation. Reporting it separately each year would show whether companies released from the mandate keep giving.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

What this piece could not establish

The 11,204-company and Rs 1,282 crore figures are the ministry’s, as reported by ETCFO and The News Minute from the committee’s report; the relevant pages of the report were not read directly. They are obligations for 2025-26 and are compared here with actual spending in 2024-25, the latest complete year; the true share of spending lost could be a little higher or lower. How many of the leaving companies would keep spending voluntarily, and which states, districts and causes receive their money now, could not be found. The IICA Factbook gives spend shares only for the smallest and largest spending bands. The yearly series mixes portal snapshots: 2018-19 to 2020-21 come from earlier extracts quoted in Parliament and by Factly, and the portal’s figures for past years are revised as late filings arrive. Whether the Bill will be taken up in the Winter Session, and in what form, is not known. Nothing here is legal advice.

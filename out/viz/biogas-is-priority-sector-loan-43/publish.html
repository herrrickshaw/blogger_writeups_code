A biogas developer on a floating-rate loan pays more interest after the RBI’s hike to 5.50% while earning more per kilogram of gas. On the published numbers the price rise dwarfs the rate rise, except for plants that never receive the new price, and RBI data on CBG lending do not exist.

Compressed biogas plants: 43% higher price worth about 50 times the cost of the repo hike

Skip to article content
Text Size

Top line

  • 43% rise in the administered CBG price, Rs 1,478 to Rs 2,110 a MMBtu, from September 2026.
  • 52 times the extra interest from a 25 bp repo hike, for a one tonne-a-day plant that receives the new price.
  • 13 pts rise in the interest rate on the whole loan that would cancel the price gain.
Updated 7 November 2026. RBI data for September 2026, released on 30 October, put the weighted average lending rate on fresh rupee loans at 8.51%, down from the 8.61% for August shown below, and on outstanding loans unchanged at 8.96%. September predates the 7 October repo hike, so the hike’s pass-through to outstanding loans is still not visible in published data; Delhi’s CNG price is still ₹86.98 a kilogram, with no later revision found. Sources: All India Radio News, IANS via NewKerala (search summaries).

Updated 15 November 2026: rewritten as charts; figures unchanged.

A 42.8% higher CBG price outweighs a 25 bp repo hike by about 52 times

What it means: On the published inputs, a plant that receives the new price gains about Rs 77.6 lakh a year, against Rs 1.5 lakh of extra interest from a 25 bp hike.

What to do: Confirm whether plants already commissioned under SATAT receive the administered price, since no source says so.

At 80% utilisation the new price lifts debt-service cover from 1.25 to 2.04

What it means: At 80% utilisation the hike moves debt-service cover only from 1.25 to 1.23, while the new price lifts it to 2.04.

What to do: Check whether a loan resets quarterly to the repo rate or annually on a lender's own rate card before sizing debt.

The Rs 100 crore priority-sector cap falls below the 20-24 tonne plants analysts favour

What it means: Above the Rs 100 crore limit the whole exposure stops counting as priority sector, so a large CBG loan can lose that status.

What to do: Ask the lender which priority-sector category a planned CBG loan is booked under before assuming the higher cap.

What would fix this

  • The RBI should publish bank credit to compressed biogas, or to bio-energy generally, as a line in its sectoral deployment of bank credit and its priority-sector disclosures, so the sector’s debt does not have to be inferred from single deals.
  • The Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas should state in the GOBARdhan guidelines whether the administered price applies to plants already commissioned, and print the per-kilogram figure with its calorific basis, so the Rs 98 and Rs 105 figures are reconciled.
  • The RBI should clarify, for a multi-plant developer, whether the Rs 100 crore agri-infrastructure limit applies per plant company or per promoter group, since it sits at the size the economics favour.
  • The Ministry should name the issuer, corpus, fee and per-loan cap of the 85% credit guarantee, so a lender can price it.
  • IREDA should publish when it reprices its rate card after a repo change, so borrowers know whether a hike reaches them in one quarter or one year.
  • The Project Approval Board should publish the formula it will use in its annual price review, so a 12-year loan can be sized against a known rule.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

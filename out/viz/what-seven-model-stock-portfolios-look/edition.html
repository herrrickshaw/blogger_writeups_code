Stack eight stock-picking factors — breakout, quality, value, momentum, and a machine-learning bullish flag among them — and group what survives into co-movement model portfolios, and two separate questions come apart: does the result actually differ from an index fund, and does it actually beat one? Checked against public sector benchmarks, this factor stack's portfolios are almost uniformly differentiated from the index — but only one of seven is currently outperforming it once the broad market's own rise is stripped out.

94 of 125 live picks show a gain, a rate a rising market can produce alone

What it means: A high hit rate on individual picks does not show that combining the factors beats a plain index fund.

What to do: Test the hit rate against the market's move over the same window before reading it as skill.

Technology leads the five-session trend while Energy's has turned negative

What it means: Energy's one-day bounce sits inside a weaker five-day trend, which a single-day snapshot would miss.

What to do: Read each sector's one-day move against its five-session median before treating a bounce as a turn.

What would fix this

  • SEBI should require registered research analysts and investment advisers who publish model portfolios to report returns alongside a named benchmark's return over the same window, so a high hit rate on individual picks, like the 75% here, is never presented without the alpha figure that can tell a different story.
  • NSE Indices should publish full daily constituent lists with weights for every index it maintains, free and in machine-readable form, so active share against Indian benchmarks can be calculated properly rather than from membership lists alone.
  • AMFI should publish a consolidated, machine-readable file of the monthly portfolio holdings of every Indian index fund and ETF, so overlap with Indian sector and broad-market funds can be checked as easily as this piece checked US bundles against SPDR daily holdings.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

What this piece does not establish. This piece does not establish that an eight-factor stack of this kind is sound or unsound in general — one bundle producing real alpha and six not doing so over a roughly five-week window since formation (23 July to 28 August 2026) is a very short track record to draw a durable conclusion from, in either direction. It does not establish benchmark differentiation for the same approach's Japan, Korea, or EU model portfolios: those markets currently offer, at best, a factsheet-level top-10 rather than a full public constituent list, so no free feed exists yet to check overlap against — six model portfolios in three markets are running with an unverified differentiation claim as a result, an asymmetry this piece is surfacing rather than quietly dropping. It does not establish why a fixed-length eviction rule occasionally precedes a rebound in the evicted name, only that it has happened at least four times in the sample reviewed here. It does not establish that any factor, filter, or portfolio-construction choice described here is a good or bad approach going forward; every figure above describes what one specific rules-based methodology has already produced historically, not a forecast, and nothing in this piece should be read as investment advice.

Sources and caveats

All figures in this piece describe the output of a personal, rules-based multi-factor stock-screening system covering the India and US markets: recommendation counts, hit rate, average and median returns, model-portfolio composition and since-formation returns, sector and liquidity-tier breakdowns, the exit-signal list, and the eviction log, all first-party and self-generated rather than third-party reported. Benchmark-overlap figures (active share against SPDR sector ETF daily holdings for XLV/XLF/XLK/XLE, and against NSE constituent-membership lists for Nifty Financial Services/Nifty 500) come from a separate check run against those same model portfolios. Alpha figures (return in excess of the broad market) are the system's own calculation, not independently recomputed for this piece. The eviction-rebound examples reflect returns visible in the system's own eviction log as of 28 August 2026 and have not been independently verified against a separate price source. This piece does not recommend buying, selling, or holding any security; it describes the historical and current output of one specific rules-based methodology, not investment advice, and past performance of any factor, filter, or portfolio-construction method is not a guarantee of future results.

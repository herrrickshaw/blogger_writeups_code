State chip subsidies: Andhra Pradesh leads by 15 points on silicon fabs, 0 to 10 elsewhere

Skip to article content
Text Size

Top line

  • 6 of 6 silicon fab percentages in Andhra Pradesh’s 18 November comparison (80, 65, 65, 60, 60 and 56) reproduce from each state’s own rule and the Centre’s 40%; the sources for Andhra Pradesh and Madhya Pradesh are press reports.
  • 15 points is Andhra Pradesh’s lead for silicon fabs; it is 10 points for compound fabs and advanced packaging and 0 for legacy packaging, where Odisha ties at 50% on the 25% reading of its rule.
  • 24 points is the spread in combined silicon fab support now (56% to 80%), up from 10 points (70% to 80%) under the first programme, when the Centre paid 50%.
KindWhat this piece includes
RuleThe Centre’s Semicon 2.0 support is 40% of eligible capital cost for silicon fabs, 35% for compound fabs and advanced packaging, and 25% for legacy packaging. Each state adds either a share of that support or a flat share of capital cost.
CheckedAndhra Pradesh’s comparison for silicon fabs, from six state rules. The same rules are then applied to the other three categories, which the comparison did not cover, and to Bihar, which was not in it.
Method noteThe percentages are shares of the capital cost the Centre approves, before ceilings, land, power and tax benefits; they are not rupees paid.
OutcomesNone found: no project has been reported as sanctioned under any of the new state rules.

What would fix this

  1. Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology: publish on the Semicon 2.0 portal one table of each state’s top-up as a percentage of eligible capital cost, by category, with the notification number.
  2. Government of Andhra Pradesh: publish G.O.Ms.No.87 and state in every comparison which categories it covers and on which central rate it rests.
  3. Government of Odisha: publish the third amendment to its semiconductor policy and state whether the State’s share is 25% or 35% of eligible capital cost.
  4. Governments of Bihar, Uttar Pradesh, Gujarat and Madhya Pradesh: amend or confirm in a notified order how their shares of central support apply to the Semicon 2.0 categories, including machines and materials.
  5. All seven state governments: report, for each sanctioned project, the State subsidy committed and the amount released, in rupees.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

Method and definitions
  • Combined support is the Centre’s share plus the State’s share, each as a percentage of the eligible capital cost the Centre approves. It leaves out land, power, interest, stamp duty and payroll benefits, ceilings and the time over which money is paid.
  • The Centre’s Semicon 2.0 rates were read in the EY alert, the KPMG note, Business Standard and the Industrial Economist, and on the India Semiconductor Mission page for fabs; the four agree (40, 35, 35 and 25).
  • State shares written as a share of central support were converted to a share of capital cost by multiplying by the central rate; this is arithmetic on the state’s own text and is checked by an assertion for each of the seven states. The Bihar, Uttar Pradesh and Gujarat policies pre-date Semicon 2.0, so applying them to its categories is an assumption, flagged as such.
  • The first-programme comparison uses a central rate of 50% for fabs and for compound and packaging units, as reported by Deloitte and by an Odisha official. The first-programme state shares for all seven states also match the India Semiconductor Mission investor-support page, an official table; Andhra Pradesh’s own policy text was not read.
  • Document text was read for the Tamil Nadu scheme, the Uttar Pradesh policy, the Gujarat policy, the Odisha 2023 policy, the Bihar resolution and the India Semiconductor Mission pages. Everything else is press, read as excerpts, and is secondary.
  • Weekdays were checked against the calendar.
What this piece could not establish
  • Whether Odisha’s State share is 25% or 35% of eligible capital cost. The New Indian Express, from the cabinet briefing of 12 August 2026, says 25%; ETGovernment on 18 August 2026, quoting an official through Moneycontrol, says 35%. The 25% reading reproduces Andhra Pradesh’s figure of 65% and is used here; the 35% case is shown separately.
  • The text of Andhra Pradesh’s G.O.Ms.No.87, Odisha’s third amendment and Madhya Pradesh’s policy. The Madhya Pradesh rule is as Business Standard and PTI report it.
  • Whether the Bihar, Uttar Pradesh, Gujarat and Madhya Pradesh rules apply to the Semicon 2.0 categories, including machines and materials; none of the four has issued a Semicon 2.0 amendment that was found.
  • Bihar’s memo date (30 January 2026 in one secondary source; the date is illegible in the OCR), an ‘additional 20%’ reading in ETV Bharat and two other reports that the resolution does not support, and the power-tariff, SGST and target terms, which come from a consultant’s page. The resolution covers fabs, display fabs, compound semiconductor and packaging units, not machines and materials; an ICAI note reads the 60% as a share of capex, which would pay 110% of cost.
  • Any state subsidy sanctioned or paid under a rule above, and the eligible capital cost of any project; the percentages are not rupees paid.
  • Whether other states, such as Assam, Karnataka and Rajasthan, offer more than the seven compared (the six in Andhra Pradesh’s comparison and Bihar); they were not rebuilt.

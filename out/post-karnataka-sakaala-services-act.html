Karnataka Sakaala Services Act: Rs 20 a day for delay, capped at Rs 500, amended 2026

Skip to article content
Text Size

Top line

  • Rs 500 is the most an official owes per application under section 9: Rs 20 a day of delay, reached after 25 days, recovered from the official’s salary after the citizen is paid.
  • 99.82% of the 432,426,014 applications on the Sakala portal’s home counter show a recorded disposal, leaving 793,571 open; the page carries no date, and a disposal is not the same as an on-time service.
  • 30 and 60 days are the windows for a citizen’s first appeal to the competent officer and second appeal to the appellate authority.

What it means: Each missed deadline gives the citizen two rungs of appeal inside the administration, and the civil courts have no jurisdiction over matters those authorities decide (section 18).

What to do: A citizen who is refused or kept waiting should keep the acknowledgement number, because the first appeal clock runs from the date of rejection or the end of the stipulated time.

What it means: The Act’s own figure is Rs 20 a day to a Rs 500 maximum, but since the 2026 amendment the Schedule can set other rates, so the figure that applies to a given service depends on the Schedule entry.

What to do: Karnataka’s Department of Personnel and Administrative Reforms should publish the notified compensation rate beside each service in the Sakala service compendium.

What it means: Karnataka pairs a statutory guarantee for notified services with portal upgrades and grievance drives, but only the statute carries an appeal ladder and a compensation figure, so it is the part a citizen can enforce.

What to do: A citizen should check the Sakala service compendium for the specific service and time limit before choosing between a Sakala appeal and a grievance petition.

What would fix this

  1. Department of Personnel and Administrative Reforms, Karnataka: date-stamp every counter on the Sakala portal home page.
  2. Sakala Mission: publish monthly delivered-in-time, delivered-late, rejected and pending counts for each department, not only cumulative receipts and disposals.
  3. Sakala Mission: publish the number of compensation payments and total rupees paid and recovered from salaries each year.
  4. Karnataka Government: list the notified compensation rate and the competent officer beside each service in the service compendium now that the Schedule can vary rates.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

How the law changed
  • Karnataka Guarantee of Services to Citizens Bill, 2011: enacted as Karnataka Act 1 of 2012. The compiled text carries the header ‘Amended vide Notification No. SAMVYASHAVI 32 Shasana 2012, Dated: 3.9.2012’, and the 2014 amending Act already calls the principal Act the Karnataka Sakala Services Act, 2011, so the name had changed by September 2014. The instrument that renamed it was not read.
  • Karnataka Act 31 of 2014 (assent 2 September 2014, first published 6 September 2014): section 7 now covers delivery electronically or by post on payment of prescribed fees; section 9 extends liability to appellate authorities and competent officers; section 11(4) requires the debit note to be entered in the HRMS software within thirty days; section 14(4) sends an official who defaults more than seven times to enquiry, with Government action within one month of the report.
  • Karnataka Act 39 of 2026 (assent 3 September 2026, Gazette 4 September 2026; the Bill passed the Legislature on 24 August 2026 according to a report dated 25 August): section 4 lets the Government amend the Schedule, including compensation and punitive cost rates; section 9 allows rates set in the Schedule; section 13-A adds revision by the Government; section 13-B adds costs for malicious or vexatious complaints, recoverable as land revenue arrears; section 18 extends the bar on civil courts to matters the Government decides.
  • Exclusions and limits in the text: only services listed in the Schedule are covered; compensation does not apply where the Government has banned or restricted the service (section 9); a default is not counted as misconduct unless habitual and wilful (section 14(1) and (2)); no suit lies for action in good faith (section 17).
What this piece could not establish
  • The Schedule of notified services and time limits: it was not in the compiled text, and the service compendium PDF was not opened.
  • Why the portal shows 1,446 services while the Principal Secretary of the Praja Seva Department said on 11 September 2026 that 890 of 2,456 government services were under Sakala. Both are reported as stated; one may count a service once per department and the other once per service, but neither source says so. The 17 October 2026 order, reported to bring all services under Sakala, was not read.
  • The date of the portal counters (they were read from a search-tool excerpt of the home page, not from the live page), and any split between on-time, late and rejected applications; the portal’s MIS report page did not load from the build environment.
  • District and department figures reported elsewhere (for example Mysuru placing second statewide for April 2026 with 1,19,801 applications received, 9,937 rejected and 98.34% cleared, and Rural Development and Panchayat Raj having 33,000 pending): none was found in a source that could be read, so none is used.
  • The Karnataka High Court order of 1 August 2025: The Hindu reports that a Division Bench (Chief Justice Vibhu Bakhru and Justice C.M. Joshi), on a petition by a Bengaluru resident, directed the State to file a status report on applications received and pending. The pending counts the State filed were not found.
  • The October 2026 slab: the Rs 20 to Rs 100 a day bands and the Rs 1,500 maximum are from an excerpt of one newspaper report; the notification and the middle bands were not read.
  • The renaming instrument, the commencement date of Act 1 of 2012 and the statutory penalty provisions for malicious complaints beyond sections 13-A and 13-B.
  • Whether Sakala reduced delays or corruption: the sources read count applications, not outcomes. Karnataka’s other measures here are announcements and counts, not evaluations.
  • Spelling: the Gazette and the 2026 Act use ‘Sakaala’; the portal and most reports use ‘Sakala’.

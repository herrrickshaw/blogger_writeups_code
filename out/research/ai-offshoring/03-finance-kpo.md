## Sector notes

### (a) Finance & accounting outsourcing (AP/AR, reconciliations, bookkeeping, US tax prep, audit support)

**Size in India:** No credible figure found for the total F&A outsourcing workforce in India. Neither NASSCOM nor Everest surfaced a figure in search. Proxies:
- Genpact, which has the largest F&A franchise, has about 130-146k staff, of whom about 80% are in India (Revelio estimate).
- WNS had 66,085 staff globally in June 2025.
- RSM US had about 2,000 in India (2025).
- One careers site estimates 4,000-6,000 US CPAs work in Big Four India. Treat this as L.

**AI impact evidence:**
- **Genpact (primary, via summary, May 2026):** the company is shifting revenue away from per-FTE billing. Non-FTE models are 48% of revenue, and the AI-led ATS segment earns more than 2x revenue per head. The CFO's "2x, 2x, 70%, 70%" framing means 2x revenue per head, 2x growth, 70% annuitized revenue and 70% non-FTE commercials. That is an explicit plan to decouple revenue from headcount. By my derivation, the rest of the business grew only about 1.5% YoY in Q1 2026. Revelio shows 2026 postings down 22.5% (L).
- **Capgemini/WNS (Jul-Oct 2025):** Capgemini paid US$3.3bn and framed WNS as a "springboard" to "agentic AI-powered intelligent operations". In other words, an acquirer priced BPS on the premise that agents will reshape it. Investors were sceptical: shares fell about 5%, and Morgan Stanley flagged the risk that GenAI disrupts labour-heavy BPO. Capgemini's group headcount fell 5,800 in H1 2026 even after consolidating WNS. Whether that fall came from the WNS side is not disclosed in what I saw.
- **Client-side automation of AP:** The evidence is mixed and mostly vendor-sourced. Hackett reports a 60% touchless rate among vendor customers (Nov 2025). A broad mid-market survey found only 4% fully touchless and 48% reporting little or no savings (2025). Ardent "best-in-class" figures range from 35% to 75% depending on definition, so they cannot be compared. I found no study that isolates GenAI or agentic AI as distinct from older OCR/RPA AP automation.
- **Big Four:** UK graduate intakes were cut 6-29% in 2025, attributed to AI taking junior work and to cost pressure. The same reports say all four firms are expanding offshoring to India, Malaysia and the Philippines. That is a substitution away from UK juniors, and part of it goes *towards* India.

**Offsetting job creation / counter-evidence:**
- **US accountant shortage:** BLS shows about 1.78mn US accountants in 2024, around 10% fewer than in 2019, and the AICPA cites a "talent pipeline issue". This pushes work to India. RSM US, Moss Adams, Sikich and CohnReznick are expanding in India (Reuters, Apr 2025). RSM plans to go from 2,000 to 5,000 India staff. One Indian university reportedly had 3,000 applicants for 120 specialised commerce seats (L, single source).
- **EXL keeps growing:** revenue was +15.6% in Q2 2026, and headcount was reportedly +11% in Q1 2026, although management says revenue will outpace headcount.
- Offshore CPA-firm centres report attrition and 30-40% salary-hike demands, which is a sign of tight supply, not of surplus (L).

**What is not known:**
- The India F&A headcount trend for 2025-26.
- Genpact's official 10-K headcount, and whether it fell.
- Firstsource's F&A headcount or AI commentary (no results; the firstsource.com host is blocked).
- Big Four India hiring numbers for FY26.
- Whether US tax-prep offshoring volumes are falling as AI tax tools spread.

### (b) KPO: equity/credit/market research, analytics

**Size in India:**
- No credible aggregate figure found.
- **Acuity Knowledge Partners:** "over 6,000" (company, 2024) vs about 11,700 (Revelio, Sep 2025, +7.8%). The two conflict.
- **Evalueserve:** about 7,400 (Revelio, 2025, -4.5% YoY; L, single source).
- **CRISIL/Coalition Greenwich, Moody's and S&P India centres:** No credible figure found.
- **EXL:** the firm calls itself a "data and AI company". It is acquiring iMerit (expected close 31 Jul 2026), a data-annotation firm, which is itself AI-adjacent job creation.

**AI impact evidence:**
- **Bank side:**
  - JPMorgan (May 2025) guided to about 10% fewer operations staff from AI. In Oct 2025 the CFO said managers were told to limit new hires as AI expands.
  - Goldman CEO Solomon's Oct 2025 memo said the firm needs "greater speed and agility" to benefit from AI. Search results did not confirm the "OneGS 3.0" name or any hiring constraint.
  - These are onshore statements, but bank operations and research support are a large share of GCC and KPO work, so cuts flow through to offshore volumes.
- **Research specifically:**
  - OpenAI reportedly launched an offering aimed first at investment banking and equity research (IBTimes, 2026).
  - A Dallas Fed analysis found postings for automatable roles down about 8-9% by early 2026 at AI-exposed firms. The fall in young-worker employment came from fewer new entrants, not layoffs. This is the most likely mechanism for KPO too: slower junior hiring rather than visible cuts.
  - Acuity launched an agentic platform, "Agent Fleet" (2025).
- **Evalueserve:** the Revelio -4.5% headcount figure is suggestive only.

**Offsetting job creation / counter-evidence:**
- Acuity's growth (+7.8% per Revelio).
- EXL's analytics growth.
- Capgemini/Genpact building "data & AI" practices that hire engineers.
- No hard evidence was found of KPO research headcount cuts attributed to AI.

**What is not known:**
- Any bank-disclosed change in offshore (India) research-associate headcount.
- CRISIL/Coalition Greenwich, Moody's and S&P India workforce trends.
- Measured productivity from AI in research note production (only the 2018 Commerzbank "75%" vendor estimate, which is old).

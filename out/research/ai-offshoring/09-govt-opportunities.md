## Government workload notes

**1. Grievances and helplines.** *Volume:* CPGRAMS handles about 1.4-1.6 lakh central grievances a month, or roughly 17-19 lakh a year on 2025 run rates (my derivation). That excludes state portals. The 1930 cyber-fraud line may be the biggest single government contact stream: about 89,000 complaints a day in 2025, but from one weak source; an official figure was 50,000 calls a day in Feb 2024. UP's 1076 is a true call-centre operation of 500 seats, 24x7, with a stated design load of 135,000 calls a day. *Gap:* CPGRAMS disposal time is about 15 days and about 66,000-78,000 cases are pending at any time. The usual criticism is quality of disposal, not speed. That is a judgment from wider coverage, not a figure. *AI/outsourcing:* the UP helpline is a vendor-run call centre with outbound feedback calls, which is classic BPO work. I found no 2025-26 figure on AI triage inside CPGRAMS. *Not known:* no credible 2025-26 call volumes found for the 1915 National Consumer Helpline, 14567 elder line, Kisan Call Centre (latest found: 61 lakh calls in 2016-17), 112, or MP's 181 CM Helpline. No current seat counts either.

**2. Claims.** *Volume:* EPFO settled 8.31 crore claims in FY26, up 38%. That is about 3.3 lakh claims per working day (my derivation, using 250 days). PMFBY has handled about 14.2 crore farmer applications paid over 2020-21 to Kharif 2024. *Gap:* PMFBY has Rs 5,405 cr pending (5.9%), with reported delays of up to 14 months caused by yield-data disputes and late state subsidy. This is a document- and data-verification bottleneck, not a call-volume problem. *AI/automation:* EPFO is the clearest example of rules-based automation at scale. 71% of advance claims were auto-settled in FY26, up from 59%. For the article this cuts both ways: automation absorbed a 38% volume jump without a reported staff increase. *Not known:* EPFO headcount or outsourced staff; ESIC claims volumes (No credible figure found); PMFBY tech-enabled yield estimation (YES-Tech) coverage was not searched.

**3. Courts.** *Volume:* about 5.6 crore pending cases in total (4.98 cr district + 63.76 lakh HC + 95,936 SC, as of Jul 2026). *Language gap:* SUVAS had translated about 53,000 SC judgments by 2024 (36,271 Hindi + 17,142 regional). High Court and district judgments, which make up the bulk of the volume, are largely untranslated. That is a large potential post-editing workload for human translators. *Budget:* e-Courts Phase III is Rs 7,210 cr, but only Rs 53.57 cr is earmarked for AI/blockchain. Scanning and digitising legacy records is part of Phase III, but I found no figure on pages scanned in this round. *Not known:* SC live-transcription volumes or vendor (No credible figure found); SUPACE deployment status in 2025-26.

**4. Tax.** *Volume:* about 8.8 crore ITRs for AY 2025-26. 24.6 lakh were still unprocessed after 90 days in Feb 2026, down from about 50 lakh in January. *AI/data:* the department relies on automated matching (AIS, 26AS, TDS, bank and MF data) and the NUDGE campaign, which produced 1.11 crore revised returns. That is algorithmic compliance work replacing manual scrutiny. Refund delays come mainly from returns flagged for manual review, which is where human-in-the-loop capacity is short. CPC-Bengaluru has historically been run by Infosys; that is background knowledge not re-verified here, so do not cite it without a source. *Not known:* GST returns and notice volumes, faceless-assessment backlog (No credible figure found in this round).

**5. Land records.** *Progress:* digitisation is nearly complete (RoR 99.81%, maps 97.42%), and over 37 crore ULPINs have been issued. *Gap:* the remaining work is integration and correction, not data entry: only 84.32% of villages have integrated records, mutation is automatic in only 17 states, and the proposed DILRMP 3.0 (2026-31) targets pending surveys and links to courts. Record verification and resurvey reconciliation are labour-intensive back-office tasks. *Not known:* size of the verification or mutation backlog (No credible figure found).

**6. Language.** Bhashini passed 100 million inferences a month in Jan 2025 (no 2026 figure found) and runs on 2,353 public platforms. Sansad Bhashini (Lok Sabha with MeitY) covers real-time translation of proceedings and speech-to-text of legacy debates. No usage volume found. AI4Bharat was not searched in this round.

**7. Vendors and delivery networks.** There are about 5.0 lakh CSCs (about 4.07 lakh at Gram Panchayat level), each run by a Village Level Entrepreneur. This is an existing last-mile, human-assisted service layer that AI tools could be fitted onto. Passport Seva V2.0 went to TCS (Rs 6,000-8,000 cr, media estimate, Jan 2022). MCA21 and GSTN vendor and contract values: No credible figure found in this round. IndiaAI governance challenges offer work orders of up to Rs 50 lakh per problem statement; two shortlisted IDP-challenge teams are being deployed at UPSC. *Job-creation estimates for domestic AI-plus-human government services: No credible figure found.*

## Claims seen but not usable

- **1930 "3.24 crore complaints / 88,976 a day" (2025):** single outlet, and another report calls the same number "calls". Use only as "one report", or use the Feb 2024 figure of 50,000 a day.
- **PMFBY "delays up to 14 months" (IIM study):** I could not locate the study; it appears only in exam-prep summaries.
- **e-Courts Phase III "Rs 1,500 cr"** (Frontiers paper): conflicts with the Rs 7,210 cr Cabinet figure; probably a single-year allocation.
- **SUVAS "31,184 judgments in 16 languages":** undated, and inconsistent with the Frontiers figures.
- **ULPIN "40 crore parcels":** a real-estate site; the committee figure is "over 37 crore".
- **UP 1076 "80,000 inbound + 55,000 outbound per day":** design capacity at launch (2019), not actual load. The 2021 actual was about 35,000 a day.
- **Passport Seva "US$1 bn" / Rs 6,000-8,000 cr:** media estimates; TCS did not disclose the value.
- **CPGRAMS "157 days to 15 days":** a government talking point; definitions of disposal have changed, so it is not like-for-like.

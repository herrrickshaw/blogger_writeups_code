## Sector notes

### (a) Insurance operations (claims, policy admin, underwriting support)
**Size in India:** No credible figure found for total India insurance BPM or insurer-captive employment. The best proxies are vendors. EXL has ~67,000 staff (secondary source) and insurance is its largest vertical (~$194m a quarter). Capgemini's WNS deal brought in a large insurance book (Aviva is a named client). Insurer GCCs are individually small to mid-sized: AIG India ~4,000 (businessofgcc, internally inconsistent); Guardian Life ~2,500 (YourStory, Mar 2025). Searches returned no headcount figures for Swiss Re, Prudential, Aviva or Xceedance.

**AI impact evidence:**
- The clearest signal is in how vendors' revenue is shifting. EXL's digital operations revenue fell for two straight quarters (Q1 2026 -2%, Q2 2026 -1.5%). Management linked the fall directly to AI being embedded into operations, while total and insurance revenue grew 12-16%. Labour-based ops revenue is shrinking inside a growing firm.
- Genpact said headcount is "decoupling from revenue" (7 May 2026). Its growth segment earns more than twice the revenue per head, and 70% of that segment is priced on non-FTE models. This is the mechanism that cuts offshore jobs per dollar of revenue.
- Capgemini cut ~5,800 people in H1 2026 after the WNS deal and set aside ~EUR700m for restructuring to adapt its workforce to AI demand. No India split was found.
- Client side: AIG's India centre lists AI for claims and underwriting as its largest investment (L).

**Offsetting creation / counter-evidence:** EXL's insurance revenue is growing at double digits "as insurers accelerate AI adoption in underwriting, claims". EXL is moving into AI data and model-training work (its iMerit acquisition). Insurer GCCs (AIG, Guardian) and insurance-software firms such as Guidewire (India staff to double to ~1,000, 2025) are still hiring for tech roles.

**What is not known:** India-specific headcount at EXL, WNS or Genpact for insurance; any disclosed FTE reductions on insurance claims contracts; insurer-captive figures for Swiss Re, Prudential, Aviva or Xceedance.

### (b) Banking & mortgage operations (KYC/AML, lending, trade finance, payments)
**Size in India:** BFSI GCCs employ ~540,000 people across 185-190 centres (consultancy estimate, M-L). Named banks: JPMorgan ~55,000 (about one-fifth of global staff), HSBC ~42,000, Citi ~33,000 (single source). Goldman, Wells Fargo and Barclays: no 2026 India figure found in this round.

**AI impact evidence (parent-bank statements):**
- JPMorgan: guided in May 2025 to ~10% fewer ops and account-services staff, called "conservative". By Feb 2026, ops staff were down ~4% with total headcount flat, and ops staff handled 6% more accounts each (M).
- Goldman OneGS 3.0 (Oct 2025): AI applied to client onboarding, lending processes, regulatory reporting and vendor management, all standard GCC and offshore workstreams.
- Wells Fargo: down 65,000 staff since 2019. More cuts are expected, with gradual AI rollout in 2026.
- Citi: up to 20,000 roles cut in a multiyear plan; headcount to "trend down".
- Standard Chartered (May 2026): ~7,000 corporate-function roles to go by 2030. It is **the only case found where the reporting names Indian centres (Chennai, Bengaluru) as most affected.**
- HSBC (Mar 2026): considering ~20,000 cuts in middle and back office. No India split.

**Offsetting creation / counter-evidence:** India headcount keeps rising even as global ops headcount falls:
- JPMorgan plans India growth of 5-7% a year and is adding ~1,000 GCC tech roles in cloud, cyber and AI data.
- Citi moved ~1,000 tech jobs to India (L-M). Analysts link this partly to the US$100,000 H-1B fee.
- Goldman expected net headcount growth in 2025.
- BFSI ops hiring is forecast at +8-9% a year through 2026-27 (consultancy, L).

The likely pattern is that ops roles are shrinking within the global total while India takes a larger share and shifts toward tech, risk and analytics. This is inference, not a measured India ops figure.

**What is not known:** India-specific ops headcount at any bank. Whether JPMorgan's -4% ops change applies to India staff. Whether Standard Chartered's India cuts are quantified. No Barclays data. Mortgage and trade-finance offshore volumes.

### (c) Travel & airline back office (revenue accounting, ticketing, reservations)
**Size in India:** No credible figure found for total India travel/airline BPM employment. Background: WNS began as British Airways' in-house back office (late 1990s) before being spun out. BA's move of accounting work to India is historical (computing.co.uk). Indian BPOs typically run passenger and cargo revenue accounting, proration and refunds (Voice&Data, older piece). Air India outsourced revenue accounting to Accelya (undated).

**AI impact evidence:** Weak. WNS's own blog (Sep 2026) argues airline revenue operations need an "intelligent decision layer", which is vendor marketing. Search found no hard numbers on automation of revenue accounting, and nothing on Amadeus or Sabre India job changes.

**Offsetting creation / counter-evidence:** New airline GCCs are mainly tech, AI and cyber, not ops:
- American Airlines Hyderabad: ~400 staff, ~800 by early 2027.
- Southwest Hyderabad: opened May 2026, ~1,000 planned.
- Qatar Airways runs a delivery centre in Ahmedabad.
- Qantas is considering moving up to 1,000 roles to India through Accenture (Aug 2026).

The evidence in this sector shows more work moving to India, not AI removing it.

**What is not known:** Revenue-accounting FTE trends; Amadeus or Sabre India headcount; WNS's travel-vertical India headcount after the Capgemini deal.

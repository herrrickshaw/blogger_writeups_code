## Sector notes

### Poland (Kraków, Warsaw, Wrocław)
- **Size and trend:** ABSL counted about 500.5k people in 2,179 centres at end Q1 2026. Growth slowed to +1.8% from +6.2% a year earlier, so the sector is at record output with near-flat hiring. The arithmetic does not reconcile: 500.5k minus 8,860 is about 491.6k, not the 488.7k ABSL published for Q1 2025. The base was probably revised; check the report before quoting both numbers. Randstad separately cites +3.8% growth for "the last year" and +47% since 2019, but the year it refers to is unclear. Kraków is the largest city hub, with ~108k staff in 312 centres in Q1 2025, roughly a fifth of the sector. I found no Warsaw or Wrocław headcount in the results.
- **Mix:** The work has moved away from transactional processing. Mid-office work passed 50% of processes in 2024 (52.9%), nearly 60% of services are described as knowledge-intensive, entry-level staff fell from 31% to ~25% between 2020 and 2024, and the share of staff aged 35+ rose from 29% to 43% between 2019 and 2024.
- **Official view of AI:** ABSL presents AI as an opportunity. Its 2026 release says the sector is "on the front line of AI revolution, testing and implementing solutions that will soon become the standard across the entire economy". Chair Janusz Dziurzyński says the sector is becoming "architects of global business value". ABSL forecasts that by 2030 up to 70% of centres will rely on "autonomous systems working alongside specialists". Mariusz Mulas (ABSL) is summarised as saying AI lets the sector move up the value chain "while stabilizing employment levels". A speaker at the ABSL 2026 summit put the question as whether AI is "the final nail in the coffin" or "the next competitive advantage". The 2025 report had a chapter titled "Gen AI - threat or opportunity?", which I could not read. In the 2025 report, AI is used for cognitive tasks in 90% of firms (single-source summary).
- **Measured effects:** In Kraków, 4,195 cuts were notified by end Oct 2025, about 70% more than in 2024. They were concentrated in data processing, accounting and tax. Bloomberg (20 Nov 2025) frames the pressure as **AI plus soaring wages**, with Shell, Heineken, HSBC and UBS cutting lower-level roles and shifting some work to Asia or India. Shell's Kraków centre went from a peak of ~5,000 to ~4,700 at end 2025, which the company called a global reorganisation. Two items are reports, not confirmed announcements: Heineken was rumoured in Oct 2025 to cut up to 700 Kraków roles and move them to Hyderabad, and Electrolux was reported in Feb 2026 to be moving up to 200 AP/AR/GL roles to India. Earlier named cutters (2024-25) include Intel, Credit Suisse, HSBC, Fujitsu, Pepsi and Aptiv. The Kraków labour-office director says the city is entering "a new development stage" rather than losing its position.
- **Positioning:** Poland is selling specialist, AI-supervised process ownership, and new centres skew to IT (42.6%) and R&D (26.2%). An ABSL figure (Paweł Luty, date unclear) is quoted as expecting Poland to stop being a cost-competitive location. I found no evidence that the EU AI Act or GDPR is being marketed as a selling point.
- **Not known:** The 2026 share of centres using GenAI, AI-attributed job losses as distinct from cost-driven offshoring to India, and 2026 headcount for Kraków and Wrocław.

### Bulgaria (Sofia)
- **Size:** The latest AIBEST headcount I found is ~105k (2022); I found no 2025 or 2026 headcount. Revenue growth halved, from 13.7% in 2023 to 7.6% in 2024. BPO is 43.3% of revenue, ITO 38.7% and R&D 18%. Labour costs rose 12.3%, which AIBEST puts down to the shift to higher-value work.
- **AI evidence:** A BTA media review (30 Jan, probably 2026) reports that Bulgarian tech firms sharply cut hiring over two years, mostly at junior programmer level, and cite AI code generation, though other factors are also mentioned. An EY survey (Capital, 1 Oct 2026) finds Bulgarian firms stepping up AI adoption, still mostly for routine tasks, while planning to hire more.
- **Not known:** AIBEST's official AI stance and BASSCOM's views; neither showed up in the results.

### Romania, Czechia, Hungary
- **Romania:** ABSL/PwC (Nov 2025) counts >280k professionals and says AI integration is pushing firms to build skills for process engineering, high-risk operations and fraud prevention. ABSL/Deloitte (Apr 2025) warned of slower growth after tax incentives were removed and because there is no education strategy, and expects Romania to stay among the cheapest EU locations to 2030. Bucharest is the largest hub. I found no Cluj figures.
- **Czechia:** ABSL reports that 68% of centres grew headcount in 2025, 80% seek "greater productivity with current people", and >40% of staff are foreigners, which shows how much the language offer matters. PwC (an ABSL partner) says AI agents can do 25-40% of typical business-services tasks; this is a consultancy estimate.
- **Hungary:** HIPA/ABSL/Deloitte count 118.5k staff (+7%) in 245 BSCs in 2025, and BSCs are described as becoming "strategic digital innovation hubs". I found no 2025 data on HOA and AI.

### Philippines (briefly; covered by another agent)
- In 2025 the sector had 1.89mn staff and US$40.3bn in revenue, adding ~80k jobs. IBPAP cut its 2028 roadmap from 2.5mn workers to a downside of 1.85mn "AI-enabled" workers (best case 2.14mn), citing AI and competition. Its new aim is "2 million AI-enabled Filipino workers".

### Egypt and South Africa
- **Egypt:** Outsourcing earned US$5.2bn in FY2025/26 (~70% of digital exports) with ~195k workers. ITIDA's Ahmed El Zaher (22 Sep 2026) says AI will "reshape" jobs rather than erase them. Concentrix plans 28k staff by end 2026, rising to 35k by 2028. The government targets US$6bn of exports in 2026.
- **South Africa:** In Oct-Dec 2025, 84% of new GBS hires (5,579) were frontline voice agents, so the work is still mostly voice-based CX. BPESA CEO Reshni Singh calls AI a "positive disruption". Its future-skills lead says "we must not dismiss the concerns of our employees about the impact of AI on their jobs" (BPESA guide, Aug 2026). Sector targets for 2030 range from 350k to 500k jobs depending on the source.

## Contrast with India
- **Scale:** India's ~6.0mn IT-BPM workforce plus 2.36mn in GCCs (archive) dwarfs every rival. It is about 12x Poland's centre workforce, about 3x the Philippines and over 30x Egypt or Hungary. The whole of CEE (Poland, Czechia, Romania, Hungary and Bulgaria, about 1.2mn) is smaller than India's GCC headcount alone.
- **Cost and wages:** I found no credible per-FTE figure. The qualitative evidence all points the same way. Polish wages are "soaring" (Bloomberg) and ABSL itself expects Poland to lose cost competitiveness. Bulgarian labour costs rose 12.3% in a year. Several Kraków cuts (Heineken and Electrolux, as reported; Shell, HSBC and UBS per Bloomberg) move transactional finance work **to India**. In CEE, India is a destination for transactional work, not a peer under the same pressure.
- **Mix:** CEE is already majority mid-office and knowledge work (Poland 52.9% mid-office; entry-level about 25%) and leans on multilingual EU delivery. The Philippines, South Africa and Egypt are voice- and CX-heavy (84% of South African new hires are voice agents). India sits in between: its scale is in transactional BPM and application maintenance, with GCC growth at the top end.
- **Earlier or later than India?** The evidence is mixed by hub.
  - **CEE is seeing pressure earlier on its remaining transactional layer, but for a double reason:** AI plus wage inflation. Lower-value work is being either automated or moved to India. This is why Polish employment growth is about 2% while India's GCC headcount keeps rising. ABSL's own line is that the sector is "first to face" changes the wider economy will see later.
  - **The Philippines (and probably South Africa) is the hub where AI hits the core product directly,** voice and CX. IBPAP has already cut its 2028 headcount targets, which NASSCOM has not done publicly (per the archive).
  - **India is hit later on headcount** because its cost advantage still draws work in from CEE even as AI deflates pricing. The weakness is that the work flowing in, transactional F&A and AP/AR, is the work AI automates fastest.
- **Positioning:** Every association uses the same "AI-enabled workforce" and value-ladder story: ABSL calls it "architects of value", IBPAP "2mn AI-enabled workers", ITIDA "reshape, not erase", BPESA "positive disruption". None of the results showed a hub selling the EU AI Act or GDPR as an advantage over India.

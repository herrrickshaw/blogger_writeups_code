Why it is useful

  • Policy researchers, journalists and investment analysts who ask 'how many announced factories were actually built?' get a reproducible pipeline instead of reading hundreds of monthly PDFs by hand.
  • It replaces manual look-ups: it splits filings with no Part B into companies that no longer exist and companies still active, using a two-tier name and state match against the company register.
  • Evidence from the repo: its own GLEIF cross-check found that about 6% of tier 1 and 16% of tier 2 matches were a different company with the same name, so it tells you to treat a single identification as a lead, not a fact.
  • In this demo the command-line tool installed and ran from a clean clone, but no DPIIT data ships with the repo, so the counts shown are the repo's documented status, not a fresh run.

What it is for

An Industrial Entrepreneur Memorandum (IEM) is the filing an Indian manufacturer makes to DPIIT. Part A says the company intends to build; Part B says production has started. iem-tracker parses both sets of PDF lists, links them by memorandum number and date and then by name, and looks up each company in registries (data.gov.in company master, the GLEIF LEI register and public company sites).

The pipeline is resumable and rate-limited, and one command, iem, runs each stage: parse, build, web, report. The background article with methods and caveats is linked from the README.

This post covers company-level, public-registry information only. It does not use or show directors' names or contact details.

Who it is for

  • Researchers studying industrial investment follow-through in India
  • Journalists checking whether announced projects were delivered
  • Data engineers who want a worked example of messy-PDF parsing and fuzzy registry matching
Not for.
  • Treating a company's status as an official certificate. Status from free company sites is not official
  • Anyone expecting a ready-made dataset: PDFs and company tables are git-ignored and must be rebuilt
  • Investment decisions

Install

  1. Clone the repo.
    git clone https://github.com/herrrickshaw/iem-tracker.git
cd iem-tracker
  2. Create a virtual environment and install the requirements. The optional curl_cffi line in requirements.txt is only for a TLS-fingerprint test.
    python3 -m venv .venv
.venv/bin/pip install -r requirements.txt
  3. Put the DPIIT PDFs in PartA/ and PartB/, then parse and build.
    ./iem parse && ./iem build

Quick start: commands and what you should see

Sample prompts for your AI assistant

Copy, paste and edit the parts in [brackets].

  • Read README.md and IEM_VERIFY_METHOD.md and explain in plain English what Part A and Part B are and how the two-tier matching rule works.
  • Set up a virtual environment, install requirements.txt, run ./iem help and ./iem status, and tell me what I need to download before anything can run.
  • Explain why the IEM state is the plant state while the CIN state is the registered-office state, and how that affects matching.
  • Show me how compile_iem_ab.py links Part B to Part A, first by memorandum number and date, then by name.
  • List the known data gaps in the README and suggest how I could fill them without scraping anything that blocks bots.

Limits and honest caveats

  • No data ships with the repo. PDFs, parquet, DuckDB and CSV files are git-ignored on purpose, so the quick start only shows setup unless you download the DPIIT lists.
  • A single identification is a lead, not a fact: about 6% of tier 1 and 16% of tier 2 matches pointed to a different company with the same name.
  • Status from free company sites is not an official certificate, and the data.gov.in master is labelled 'data up to 3 November 2023'.
  • Known gaps from the README: Part A 2024 only to August, several 2020 and 2021 months missing locally, 2023 cumulative files not parsed.
  • Third-party company sites and APIs may rate-limit or block; the data.gov.in host was unreachable from the author's network.
  • The parsing and lookup stages were not run in the demo, so every count other than the status output is the repo's own documentation.

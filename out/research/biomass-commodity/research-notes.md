# How countries treat biomass as a commodity — research notes (28 Oct 2026)

Read through search-engine text; primary documents not opened unless stated.

## 1. Exchange-traded and price-indexed: wood pellets (Europe)
- EEX launched monthly wood-pellet futures in 2017 (100 t lots, up to 36 months); first trade 15 Jan 2018 (Vattenfall/Total, 1,000 t via Tradition). Cash-settled on the Argus cif ARA index, changed to Argus cif NWE (deliveries within 750 km of Rotterdam) from 4 Jul 2018. (EEX/ECC circulars; Argus; Biodiesel Magazine)
- Euronext lists wood-pellet futures with ENplus A1 as the underlying quality, ARA delivery.
- No liquidity figures found; pellets are price-indexed (Argus, other PRAs) more than actively traded on screen.

## 2. Standards make residues fungible
- ISO 17225 series (ISO TC 238; from CEN work) gives one quality classification and test regime for solid biofuels; ISO 17225-2 fixes pellet moisture, energy density, durability, size (COFORD review 2021; Uni Utrecht paper).
- ENplus (A1/A2/B) adds stricter requirements on top of ISO for small-scale heating; European Pellet Council reports 700+ fraud cases in a decade.
- Lamers, Junginger et al. (2012), "Developments in international solid biofuel trade": trade rose ~56 -> 300 PJ (2000-2010), pellets 8.5 -> 120 PJ; commodity value set by homogeneity, heating value, bulk density.
- Proskurina, Junginger et al. (2019), "Global biomass trade for energy" Parts 1-2: ~780 PJ (2004) -> ~1,250 PJ (2015); trade statistics inconsistent.
- IEA Bioenergy Task 40 book "International Bioenergy Trade" (2013): pellets, ethanol and biodiesel reached "true commodity volumes" by 2013.

## 3. Customs classification
- HS 4401.31 wood pellets (binder <= 3%, diameter <= 25 mm, length <= 100 mm); 4401.32 briquettes; 4401.39 other agglomerated; 4401.41 sawdust; 4401.49 other wood waste (HS 2022). Straw (1213) and bagasse (2303) sit in agricultural chapters, so agro-residue pellets are harder to track in trade data. (CBSA tariff; StatCan)

## 4. Legal status: waste, by-product or product (EU)
- Waste Framework Directive 2008/98/EC excludes straw and other natural non-hazardous agricultural/forestry material used in farming, forestry or energy from biomass; Article 5 sets a four-condition by-product test; Article 6 end-of-waste criteria. Agricultural residues remain a grey area (Frontiers 2022). Classification matters: "waste" triggers permits and transport rules that block trading as a commodity.

## 5. Sustainability certification as a market gate (Japan, EU)
- Japan's FIT/FIP requires certified sustainable palm kernel shell (PKS) for supported plants from 1 Apr 2024; recognised schemes GGL, RSB, ISCC, MSPO; domestic certifier JIA later approved. Japan is the largest PKS importer (478,000 t in June 2024; Indonesia then Malaysia). (Argus; RSB)
- Certification turns a residue into a graded, documented product that can be priced and financed.

## 6. China
- Modelled supply curves: ~250 Mt dry crop residue available at ~$100/t (EfD/RFF); 153-244 Mt at >$90/t. Treated as a price-responsive resource in research; no national exchange found.

## 7. India
- No exchange or national price index for agro-residue. Price signals come from: Ministry of Power/CEA benchmark pellet prices (Nov 2023: Rs 2.27 per 1,000 kcal northern region excl. NCR; Rs 2.24 western; non-torrefied; valid one year, subject to revision); the model long-term pellet contract; NTPC/utility tenders.
- 2017 GMEX-CGanga agreement for a waste/biomass exchange with warehouse receipts: no evidence it launched.
- Private platforms (BioTradX app; PelletRates price intelligence) — marketplaces/data, not exchanges.
- Demand created by the co-firing mandate (5%; 5% + 2% MSW charcoal in NCR under the Nov 2025 policy); 24.76 lakh t co-fired in 2025 (MoP Annual Report 2025-26).

## Possible blog angle
India has the demand (co-firing mandate) and a benchmark price, but not the three things that made pellets a commodity in Europe and PKS in Japan: a mandatory quality standard for agro-pellets tied to contracts, a published price index, and a clear legal status for straw as a product rather than waste. A warehouse-receipt system (as for agri commodities under WDRA) for pellets/briquettes is the missing piece for financing collection.

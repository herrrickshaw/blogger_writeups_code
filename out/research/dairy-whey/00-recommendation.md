# Integrating cheese and whey processing in India: recommendation

Prepared 22 October 2026 from five uploaded documents and an India research brief (`01-india-whey.md`):
- NIFTEM/MoFPI, *Detailed Project Report: Cheese Making Unit under PMFME* (the "DPR")
- Ng'ang'a et al., *Economic, Environmental, and Social Returns to Whey Valorisation in Kenya's Dairy Industry* (Research Square preprint, Dec 2025)
- USDA AMS, *Whey Protein Concentrate*, technical evaluation report for the National Organic Program (Apr 2015)
- Pan et al., *Whey Protein Nutrition in Sports*, Current Nutrition Reports (2025)
- USDEC, *U.S. Dairy Proteins* overview (2015)

India figures in the brief come from search-result extracts, not source pages. They are marked as such and need checking before publication.

## Bottom line

Do not bolt a whey-protein plant onto every cheese unit. Instead:

1. **Small units (PMFME scale)** should plan and fund whey use inside the cheese project: whey drinks, whey (ricotta-type) cheese, food use, feed and biogas.
2. **Shared whey hubs** should take pre-concentrated whey from clusters of cheese and paneer units and make WPC and permeate. They would be run by cooperatives or financed under AHIDF/DIDF.
3. **Large cheese plants** should integrate membrane filtration and drying on site. Amul, Parag and Milky Mist already are.

The binding constraint is scale. A cheese unit's whey holds too little protein to pay for its own drying line, but India pays import prices for the same protein.

## What the numbers say

### 1. The model cheese project ignores 90% of its milk

The DPR sizes a 400 kg/day cheese unit:
- Rs 33.61 lakh total cost; 108 t/yr output at full capacity; 40-60% utilisation in years 1-5.
- Net margin of only 1.2-4.1% of sales.

Whey is mentioned only as a process step ("draining of whey"). It has no line in costs, revenue, equipment or effluent treatment.

Mass balance, using the USDA report's composition (about 9 kg whey per kg cheese; whey is 0.8% protein and 4.9% lactose):

| DPR utilisation | Cheese, t/yr | Whey, t/yr (kg/day) | Protein in whey, t/yr | Lactose, t/yr | WPC80 at 85% protein recovery, t/yr |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 40% (year 1) | 43.2 | 389 (1,296) | 3.1 | 19.1 | 3.3 |
| 60% (year 5) | 64.8 | 583 (1,944) | 4.7 | 28.6 | 5.0 |
| 100% | 108.0 | 972 (3,240) | 7.8 | 47.6 | 8.3 |

This is our own arithmetic. Two further inconsistencies in the DPR:
- **Milk price.** Its raw-material cost of Rs 250 per kg of cheese implies milk at about Rs 25 a litre, at a 10:1 yield.
- **Reception capacity.** Its 1,000-litre milk reception and chiller is short of the 1,600-4,000 L/day the stated cheese output needs.

### 2. On-site WPC does not pay at this scale; shared processing might

- **Plant cost.** The only plant-cost figure found is a European one: EUR 7-11 m for a WPC80 ultrafiltration, diafiltration and drying line (press, M).
- **What one unit could earn.** At 3-8 t/yr of WPC80, one DPR-sized unit would gross only about Rs 0.8-2.5 crore a year. That uses landed import prices of Rs 2,300-3,000/kg in mid-2026, which are near a peak; in 2024 they were Rs 700-800/kg.
- **What a hub could earn.** A hub taking whey from a few hundred such units would approach the scale of Milky Mist's single stream, about 1 million L/day (press, M). Milky Mist is now planning its own WPC plant. At 8.5 kg WPC80 per 1,000 L, a stream that size could yield on the order of 2,500 t a year (our arithmetic).
- **Transport.** Whey is about 93% water, so hauling it raw is costly. Units would need to concentrate it first:
  - reverse osmosis to remove water, or
  - small ultrafiltration skids that keep the protein-rich retentate for the hub, and leave the lactose-rich permeate on site for drinks, feed or biogas.

### 3. India is importing the protein it pours away

- Supplement-grade whey protein is reported as 80-90% imported, mainly from the US, New Zealand, the EU and Australia (press, L-M).
- Import volume estimates conflict: over 45,000 t (FY23) against about 23,000 t (2025).
- The world market is tight:
  - USDA Dairy Market News had WPC80 at USD 12.50-13.00/lb in mid-July 2026.
  - EU WPC80 roughly doubled between October 2025 and July 2026 (press citing Rabobank).
- **India's whey stream:** total generation is estimated at 3.3-5 Mt a year, mostly acid or heat-acid whey from paneer and chhena (2025 review, L-M). No national figure exists for the share that is used.

### 4. Small-scale uses work, but only at small volumes

- **Kenya returns.** All four processors studied had positive NPV at an 8% discount rate. Their products were whey drinking yoghurt (50:50 whey and milk), whey smoothies, and ricotta made from whey alone or whey plus milk:

  | Product | IRR | Benefit-cost ratio |
  |---|---|---|
  | Whey smoothie | 49% | 2.43 |
  | Ricotta, whey plus milk | 85% | 3.54 |
  | Whey drinking yoghurt | 97% | 4.11 |
  | Ricotta, whey only | 180% | 7.55 |

- **Kenya scale.** Together the four used only about 145 t of whey a year. One DPR-sized Indian unit produces 389-972 t. So drinks and ricotta can absorb part of a unit's whey but probably not all of it.
- **Wider benefits.** Counting avoided effluent treatment, energy recovery, emissions and jobs added 12-18% to social net benefit in the Kenya study.

### 5. Regulation sets the floor

- **Effluent limit.** Dairy effluent must meet BOD 100 mg/L, or 30 mg/L where the receiving water is a drinking-water source (EP Rules; secondary source).
- **Why that is hard for whey.** The USDA report notes that ordinary dairy wastewater runs at 2,000-3,000 mg/L BOD. It describes whey as high in solids, lactose and salt, which makes disposal a problem; untreated disposal on land or into sewers is now barred in most jurisdictions.
- **The gap.** A cheese unit with no whey plan therefore has an unpriced compliance liability.

## Recommendation

### A. Small cheese units (PMFME, up to about 5,000 L milk/day)

1. **Make whey a costed line in every cheese DPR.** NIFTEM's template should carry:
   - the whey mass balance;
   - a chosen outlet;
   - its equipment cost;
   - its revenue or disposal cost.

   The PMFME subsidy (35%, up to Rs 10 lakh) should be available for this equipment as part of the same project.
2. **Default outlets, in order:**
   - whey drinks, using ICAR-NDRI's licensable jaljeera, tamarind and mango formulations or a 50:50 whey-milk drink;
   - whey (ricotta-type) cheese;
   - whey as an ingredient in bakery, sweets and paneer-based foods;
   - whey sold as cattle feed under a written contract;
   - permeate or surplus whey to a biogas digester.
3. **Never** let whey go to drains or land untreated.

### B. Clusters and cooperatives: shared whey hubs

1. **Where.** Locate hubs where cheese and paneer units cluster. Candidates include the paneer belts around Erode/Perundurai and western UP, and the cooperative milksheds of Gujarat, Maharashtra and Karnataka. All are illustrative; site selection needs local volume data.
2. **Split of work.** Units chill and pre-concentrate (RO, or UF to a WPC35-type retentate). The hub runs ultrafiltration, diafiltration, evaporation and spray drying for WPC34/WPC80. It also makes a permeate or lactose stream, as Parag already does with a 40 t/day lactose plant.
3. **Who runs it.** Cooperative milk unions or FPO federations, financed under:
   - AHIDF (3% interest subvention);
   - DIDF (loans up to 80%);
   - NDDB technical support.

   This is the "cooperative processing model" the Kenya study recommends for small processors.
4. **How suppliers are paid.** By protein and solids delivered, as milk is paid by fat and SNF, so units have a reason to keep whey clean and cold.

### C. Large plants (Amul, Parag, Milky Mist, Hatsun and similar)

1. **Integrate on site.** Fit membrane filtration and drying, with permeate going to lactose or ethanol. Amul has made WPC80 at its cheese plant since August 2025 and is trialling ethanol from whey.
2. **Take in outside whey.** Buy pre-concentrated whey from nearby small units on the hub terms above. That uses spare capacity and solves the small units' disposal problem.

### D. Policy

1. **Add whey processing to the PLI eligible list** alongside mozzarella. Name "whey and whey-protein processing equipment" in AHIDF and PMFME guidelines; none of these schemes currently targets whey.
2. **Publish the numbers.** The Department of Animal Husbandry and Dairying should publish annual cheese, paneer and chhena output and an estimate of whey generated and used. None of these is published today.
3. **Enforce effluent rules for dairies, with a grace period** tied to adopting a whey plan.
4. **Keep imports open while capacity builds.** India's protein-supplement market is estimated at about USD 0.9-1.2 bn (paid estimates). Raising duty on whey protein would raise consumer prices before domestic supply exists. The case for domestic WPC rests on import substitution at today's high prices, not on protection.

## What is not known

- India's cheese, paneer and chhena tonnage, and how much whey is used versus discharged.
- Customs duty on whey (HS 0404.10). For WPC under HS 3502.20, sources conflict.
- Indian capex and minimum economic scale for a WPC line. Only a European benchmark was found.
- How much paneer and chhena acid whey can be turned into WPC economically, compared with drinks, feed or biogas. Acid whey is higher in minerals and lactic acid than sweet cheese whey; this is general dairy-science knowledge, not taken from the uploaded documents.
- Whether mid-2026 WPC prices will hold. They roughly tripled from 2024 levels, so an investment case should be tested at 2024 prices too.

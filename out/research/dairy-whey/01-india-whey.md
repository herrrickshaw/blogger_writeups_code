## Notes

### 1. Whey streams
- **Cheese tonnage:** I found no published national tonnage. USDA GAIN gives only a growth rate of about 15%/yr. The three market-value estimates for 2025 are not comparable with one another. The likeliest primary sources are the full USDA GAIN Dairy and Products Annual 2025 (IN2025-0057, 29 Sep 2025, https://www.fas.usda.gov/data/india-dairy-and-products-annual-8) and the DAHD Basic Animal Husbandry Statistics. Neither was readable from here.
- **Paneer and chhena:** No national volume found. Company capacities give a sense of scale:
  - Milky Mist produces ~150 t/day of paneer at 50-60% utilisation.
  - Parag produces ~20 t/day of paneer and plans ~80 t/day by June 2027, with Rs 100 cr capex (https://dairynews.today/news/parag_milk_foods_will_invest_100_crore_to_expand_paneer_capacity_3207908.html).
  - Unorganised producers make about 90-94% of paneer. Their acid whey is dispersed across small units and mostly not collected.
- **Total whey:** The only estimates are 3.3-5 Mt/yr, derived from paneer production in a 2025 review, and ~3 Mt in 2009. These support the brief's hypothesis that acid/heat-acid whey from paneer and chhena is the larger stream than sweet cheese whey. That is an inference, not a measured split. The single largest identified sweet-whey stream is Milky Mist at ~1 million L/day.
- **Utilised vs discharged:** No credible Indian figure found.

### 2. Trade
- **Import volumes conflict.** One press report gives >45,000 t of WPC/WPI worth Rs 1,200 cr in FY2022-23. Ken Research estimates ~23,000 t of whey protein in 2025. The ~USD 3.2/kg unit value implied by the first figure suggests it may include lower-grade whey or whey powder. Both figures are low confidence.
- **Sources of imports:** The US, NZ, EU and Australia are reported as the main suppliers. HS 3502 data for 2024 shows the US at USD 73.7 m and NZ at USD 57.0 m, but 3502 also covers egg albumin.
- **Export figures:** No credible figure found for India's whey or WPC exports. Ken Research gives total dairy exports of 113,350 t in 2024-25, which is not specific to whey.
- **Duties:** For HS 3502, aggregators show BCD 20% + IGST 18%, but another site shows a total of 49.15% on 35022000. Use the CBIC tariff to confirm. No rate was found for 0404.10.
- **FTAs:**
  - The India-US interim framework (Feb 2026 reporting) excluded dairy. Ministers named milk powder, cheese, paneer, butter and ghee, but whey was not named (https://visionias.in/current-affairs/upsc-daily-news-summary/article/2026-02-04/business-standard/international-relations/india-us-trade-deal-agri-dairy-interests-protected ; https://www.5paisa.com/index.php/news/india-to-keep-agriculture-dairy-outside-us-trade-deal-framework).
  - The EU-India FTA (Jan 2026) reportedly made no dairy tariff cuts (https://dairynews.today/global/news/eu-india-trade-deal-leaves-dairy-sector-unchanged-amid-high-tariffs.html).
  - No whey-specific information was retrieved for the UK CETA, EFTA TEPA, Oman or NZ deals. Their dairy treatment is unverified in this pass.

### 3. Prices
- **US Dairy Market News, mid-July 2026:**
  - WPC80 was USD 12.50-13.00/lb. DMN describes WPC markets as "at record highs".
  - WPC34 was mostly USD 1.85-2.00/lb.
  - Edible dry whey was mostly USD 0.62-0.71/lb. Makers are reportedly shifting whey away from dry whey into WPC.
- **EU WPC80:** EUR 20,250/t in April 2026 per DCA. Rabobank data via press puts it at ~EUR 27,500/t by July 2026.
- **Lactose, sweet whey powder (EU) and GDT:** No credible figure found.
- **India:** Landed WPC roughly tripled, from Rs 700-800/kg (2024) to Rs 2,300-3,000/kg (mid-2026). These are industry quotes reported by The Core and BW Retail.
- **Implication:** A domestic WPC80 maker currently competes against an import price of about USD 28/kg or more before duty. That is a much stronger margin signal than when the NOP and USDEC sources were written. The supply crunch could reverse, so the brief should model a lower long-run WPC80 price as well.

### 4. Indian producers
- **Amul/GCMMF:** NDDB praised the start-up of WPC80 and MPC80 at the Dr Kurien Cheese Plant in Aug 2025 (https://www.amuldairy.com/detailevent.php?id=2753). No tonnage was given. Amul has two whey plants, reported as expanded from 1,200 to 2,000 t (undated). It sells Amul Whey Protein and Amul Protein Water (10 g WPI per 500 mL), and is running a whey-to-bioethanol trial.
- **Parag:** Manchar cheese and whey plant with Rs 110 cr combined capex (2017). Lactose plant of 40 MT/day using whey permeate (2021). Avvatar brand.
- **Schreiber Dynamix:** Baramati, 500,000 L/day of milk. Products include WPC, whey powder and lactose. The Schreiber stake data is old.
- **Mahaan Proteins:** Makes casein, lactose and WPC. Only total milk capacities were found (10 lakh L, or 500,000 kg/day, in different profiles).
- **Bhole Baba:** 150 MT/month WPC (undated).
- **Milky Mist (Hatsun was not found):** ~1 million L/day of cheese whey. Currently sells whey powder and plans a WPC plant.
- **Prabhat:** No whey-specific information found.
- **NDDB:** No NDDB whey or permeate project found.

### 5. Scale, capex and small-scale options
- **Large WPC plant:** The only capex benchmark found is EUR 7-11 m for a WPC80 UF/DF and drying line (Kizelmann, European). The litres/day basis was not stated. No credible Indian figure was found for capex or minimum economic scale. Generic IMARC and manufacturingplantindia project reports give no rupee figure.
- **Indian scale reference points:** Milky Mist is building WPC on ~1 m L/day of whey. Parag's 2017 whey line started at 1.5-2 t output.
- **Small-scale options:**
  - ICAR-NDRI's 2026 list of technologies available for commercialisation includes whey jaljeera, whey tamarind and whey mango drinks (https://ndri.res.in//sites/default/files/2026-07/Technologies%20available%20at%20NDRI%20for%20Commercialization.pdf). I could not confirm any licensees.
  - There is also an "acido-whey beverage" process. Its page is on the blocked domain https://www.indiascienceandtechnology.gov.in/hi/innovations/industrial-innovations/acido-whey-process-preparation-beverage, which I saw only in search results.
  - A low-cost acido-whey beverage is described in the Indian Journal of Nutrition and Dietetics (https://informaticsjournals.com/index.php/ijnd/article/view/4851).
  - Biogas: I found no Indian example of whey-fed biogas. Dairy biogas projects use dung, e.g. NDDB/Suzuki/Banas at Agthala, 100 t dung/day.
  - RO/UF skid costs and Indian ricotta or whey-cheese examples: No credible figure found.

### 6. Regulation and schemes
- **Effluent:** Under Environment Protection Rules Schedule VI (dairy), the limits are BOD 100 mg/L (30 mg/L where the receiving water is a drinking source), SS 150 mg/L, O&G 10 mg/L, and pH 6.5-8.5. These come from a municipal annexure and a consultancy summary, not from CPCB directly.
- **Whey strength:** No credible whey BOD/COD figure was retrieved. A vendor quotes raw dairy effluent at 32,000 mg/L COD and 6,500 mg/L BOD₅, but that is not whey (https://www.netsolwater.com/composition-of-dairy-industry-effluent.php?blog=4279). The user's NOP report may carry whey BOD.
- **Implication:** Discharging whey would need roughly 2-3 orders of magnitude of BOD reduction. That is the regulatory driver for any whey use.
- **FSSAI:** WPC, MPC and colostrum standards were notified on 19 Nov 2018. WPC is defined as made from whey or acid whey by physical separation followed by drying. The WPC protein minimum was not retrieved. No whey powder standard was confirmed.
- **Schemes:**
  - AHIDF gives a 3% interest subvention to private firms and cooperatives.
  - DIDF loans are routed via NDDB/NCDC and cover up to 80% of project cost.
  - The food-processing PLI (Rs 10,900 cr) covers mozzarella. Its beneficiaries are Parag, GCMMF, Sunfresh Agro and Indapur Dairy.
  - PMFME and PMKSY details were not retrieved; the user already has the PMFME DPR.
  - I found no whey-specific incentive.

### 7. Protein supplement market
- **Market size:** Estimates for 2025 range from USD 0.86 bn to 1.16 bn, depending on definition. IMARC gives USD 912.9 m (CAGR 6.27% to 2034). Ken Research gives USD 1,159 m for sports nutrition. IMARC's broader "protein-based products" category is Rs 44,048 cr. All of these are paid-report estimates.
- **Import dependence:** 80-85% per press, or >90% per one report. These are estimates, not trade statistics.

## Findings
### 1. india-ended-63-years-of-state-nuclear (HIGH)
- Claim in post: "SHANTI repealed the 2010 Act outright, but the supplier-liability fix that could actually unlock Jaitapur and Kovvada is a separate reform still working its way through government, aimed specifically at bringing India's liability regime closer to the international norm without reopening the whole nuclear liability question in public debate." The callout then says the headline compresses "a domestic ownership reform that is now law, and a foreign-liability reform that is not". The fixes section asks DAE to "release its draft supplier-liability reform for public consultation".
- Current (as of 18 Oct 2026):
  - **The supplier-liability change appears to be in the SHANTI Act itself, not in a separate pending reform.** Law-firm and legal-press summaries (law.asia, JSA) say section 16 of the SHANTI Act does not keep the operator's statutory right of recourse against suppliers. Under section 17(b) of the 2010 CLND Act, an operator could recover from a supplier for defective equipment or substandard services. Under SHANTI, recourse survives only where a written contract provides it, or for intentional acts. Commentators read this as a step to bring in foreign vendors. One exam-prep summary (Vision IAS) says the Act "completely removes supplier liability". The other summaries describe something narrower.
  - **The removal has been challenged in court.** A petition filed in the Supreme Court in February 2026 challenges the liability framework, including the removal of the recourse right (SC Observer summary).
  - **Draft rules were out before this post.** DAE released draft SHANTI Rules, 2026 on 14 August 2026, with comments due by 4 September 2026. They set operator liability caps by reactor size: ₹3,000 crore for reactors above 3,600 MWt, tapering to ₹100 crore for those up to 150 MWt. The government covers damage above the cap, and the caps are to be reviewed every five years.
  - All of this predates the 15 September 2026 publication, so the "separate reform still working its way through government" framing was already out of date when written. The post's own source list cites Vajiram & Ravi for that framing. Vajiram's own SHANTI explainer is titled as covering "nuclear liability reform".
  - Seen: summaries only.
  - https://law.asia/shanti-act-nuclear/
  - https://www.jsalaw.com/?p=57582
  - https://vajiramandravi.com/current-affairs/shanti-bill-2025-explained-private-entry-nuclear-liability-reform-and-transparency-concerns/
  - https://www.scobserver.in/cases/challenge-to-provisions-of-the-shanti-act-2025/amp/
  - https://visionias.in/current-affairs/news-today/2026-08-18/science-and-technology/draft-shanti-rules-2026-released-by-the-government
  - https://swarajyamag.com/amp/story/news-brief%2Fnuclear-accident-liability-caps-to-be-reviewed-every-5-years-under-shanti-rules
- Suggested wording: "Updated [date]. This piece describes the supplier-liability fix as a separate reform still pending. Legal commentary on the SHANTI Act says the Act itself dropped the operator's statutory right of recourse against suppliers (section 17(b) of the 2010 law), leaving recourse to written contracts and intentional acts. A Supreme Court petition filed in February 2026 challenges that change. DAE's draft SHANTI Rules (14 August 2026) set tiered operator liability caps of up to ₹3,000 crore. Whether this is enough to bring EDF and Westinghouse back to Jaitapur and Kovvada is still open, but the legal change is no longer pending."
- Confidence: Three legal or exam-prep summaries agree that the statutory recourse right was dropped. Exactly how far it goes (contractual recourse kept or not) differs between summaries. The Act's gazetted text was not read; check sections 16–17 before posting. The draft-rules details come from several secondary summaries that agree with each other.

### 2. india-ended-63-years-of-state-nuclear (MEDIUM)
- Claim in post: "select fuel-cycle activities, including conversion, refining and enrichment of uranium-235, are now open to private entities up to a threshold value the government has yet to set, though the Act keeps unspecified "activities of a sensitive nature" under exclusive government control." The fixes section asks DAE to notify that threshold and publish the list of sensitive activities.
- Current (as of 18 Oct 2026):
  - **The draft rules partly answer this.** The draft SHANTI Rules, 2026 (14 August 2026, comments to 4 September 2026) provide one composite licence to build, own, operate and decommission a plant. Private firms and joint ventures could also do nuclear R&D and manufacture nuclear fuel.
  - **Enrichment may not be open to private firms after all.** One summary says the draft keeps uranium and thorium enrichment or isotopic separation, and spent-fuel management, with the government. That would cut against the post's line that enrichment is open up to a threshold.
  - No threshold figure was found in the summaries. Whether the rules have been finalised since the comment period closed: unverified.
  - Seen: summaries only.
  - https://visionias.in/current-affairs/news-today/2026-08-18/science-and-technology/draft-shanti-rules-2026-released-by-the-government
  - https://www.civilsdaily.com/news/draft-rules-under-the-shanti-act-open-nuclear-power-to-captive-industrial-use-and-a-composite-licence/
  - https://www.shankariasparliament.com/current-affairs/draft-shanti-rules-2026
- Suggested wording: "Updated [date]. DAE published draft SHANTI Rules on 14 August 2026, with comments invited until 4 September. Summaries describe a single composite licence for private and joint-venture operators. They also say enrichment, isotopic separation and spent-fuel management stay with the government, so the fuel-cycle opening described below may be narrower in practice. We have not seen the final rules."
- Confidence: Several exam-prep summaries agree on the release date and the composite licence. The reserved-activities list comes from a single summary and is unverified. The question is whether enrichment is reserved in the rules even though the Act allows it.

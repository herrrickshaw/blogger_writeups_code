# 03 — Domestic side: how far India's own digital health tracking has got, and who builds it

Research date: 18 October 2026. Method: 14 web searches (13 standard, 1 extended). Official *.gov.in sites (abdm.gov.in, nha.gov.in, pib.gov.in) were blocked, so most figures come from press or secondary summaries of government releases. These are labelled. Where a search result pointed to a pib/abdm PDF, the figure was read from the search engine's summary, not from the PDF itself. Out of scope (already on the blog): ABHA 93+ crore accounts, 104+ crore linked records, HPR ~67% of doctors, HPR not mandatory, NHCX not mandatory for insurers.

Confidence: **H** = two or more independent sources agree, or an official text is quoted by the press. **M** = one reasonable secondary source. **L** = single source, vendor blog, or unclear date.

## Notes by question

### 1. National Health Claims Exchange (NHCX)

- **Launch:** Insurer onboarding started in late 2023. Outlook Business reported 12 insurers onboarded by about November 2023, with HDFC Ergo first (https://www.outlookbusiness.com/news/nhcx-gains-momentum-more-medical-insurers-joining-platform-for-efficient-claim-processing). The first live claim went through in **June 2024** (Wikipedia, citing press). Moneylife and Businessworld covered it as an imminent launch around mid-2024.
- **Insurers:** Digital Health News ran the headline "33 major health insurance companies" (https://www.digitalhealthnews.com/33-major-health-insurance-companies-boarded-nha-s-nhcx-platform), but its own text says the full list was not disclosed. Business Standard gives **34 insurers and TPAs live, ~300 hospitals preparing** as of July 2024. No later count was found.
- **Hospitals:** The reports conflict. One May 2024 piece claims "~5,000 hospitals linked". Another says "~250 major private hospitals engaged in integration". The Business Standard "~300 hospitals preparing" figure fits better with the smaller number. Treat "5,000" as unreliable.
- **Claims volume:** **No public figure found** for 2024, 2025 or 2026. The search engine could not reach NHA dashboards. A Business Standard editorial (Nov 2025) says NHCX adoption "remains uneven across states and hospital networks" (https://www.business-standard.com/opinion/editorial/high-claims-low-payouts-why-india-s-health-insurance-cover-isn-t-enough-125111901436_1.html). For scale, PM-JAY itself settled 2 crore+ claims worth Rs 28,000 crore+ in FY2024-25 (Rajya Sabha reply, Dec 2025, via https://www.newsonair.gov.in/government-informs-rajya-sabha-of-28-thousand-crore-rupees-claims-under-ayushman-bharat-scheme). Most PM-JAY claims still run through NHA's own TMS, not NHCX (vendor blog: https://nirmitee.io/blog/nhcx-pmjay-tms-ayushman-bharat-claims/, L).
- **IRDAI direction:** No mandatory deadline was found. IRDAI's Health Insurance Master Circular (May 2024) asks insurers and the Insurance Councils to "endeavor to" onboard hospitals onto NHCX (quoted in https://nirmitee.io/blog/is-nhcx-mandatory-hospitals-hmis-2026/, Sep 2026, L). On **28 Aug 2026**, the IRDAI Insurance Advisory Committee sub-committee on health discussed incentives to encourage insurer and hospital onboarding (search summary of taxguru, https://taxguru.in/?p=1074392, L–M). That it is still discussing *encouragement* in Aug 2026 supports "not mandatory".
- **Governance twist:** In July 2025, Business Standard reported that the **Finance Ministry sought control of NHCX from the Health Ministry** (link above). Its outcome was not found.
- **Incentive:** DHIS originally paid Rs 500 per NHCX claim or 10% of the claim amount, whichever was lower (2023 release). A Sep 2026 vendor blog cites a DHIS corrigendum giving Rs 200 per PM-JAY claim filed in FHIR format (L).

### 2. Facility side

- **HFR registrations:** **5.47 lakh+ facilities (12 Aug 2026)**, up from 5.36 lakh on 20 Jul 2026 (Digital Health News state-wise 2026 piece). Older or other figures: 4.51 lakh, ~4.8 lakh (early 2026), 3.3 lakh (MoHFW release at NHPR launch), 1.14 lakh (old explainer). The HFR covers hospitals, clinics, labs, imaging centres and pharmacies, public and private.
- **Registered is not the same as active:** One MoHFW release (via search summary) says **1.3 lakh+ facilities are "ABDM-enabled", including only 17,000+ private ones**. The rest are public. If that ratio holds, most HFR entries are not actively creating or sharing records, and private-sector participation is thin. *Caveat:* the release date is unclear and the page was not opened.
- **HIP/HIU counts and "records shared":** No current count of Health Information Providers or Users was found, and no count of consent-based record shares (as opposed to records *linked*). This is a key gap: "linked records" (the blog's 104 crore figure) are not the same as records exchanged between providers.
- **Scan-and-Share:** **23.21 crore+ tokens as on 18 Jun 2026** (PIB backgrounder via secondary). This is the most-used ABDM feature, but it is an OPD queue or registration token, not a clinical record exchange.
- **EMR/HMIS adoption:** **No national survey exists.** A 2019 pilot study of 15 hospitals (BJSTR, https://thebastion.co.in/pdfs/BJSTR.MS.ID.005378.pdf) explicitly says EHR adoption "is not aggressively tracked" in India. Data points:
  - Nalgonda, Telangana PHCs: EMR available in **19.14%** of 75 (IJCM 2021).
  - A hospital executive's opinion piece estimates 70–80% "advanced systems" in corporate chains and 50–60% in large private hospitals (https://www.digitalhealthnews.com/bridging-the-digital-divide-in-indian-hospitals-from-fragmented-systems-to-integrated-healthcare-it). This is an estimate, not a measurement, so do not cite it as a rate.
  - The absence of a national number is itself a finding for the thesis.
- **DHIS payouts:** The scheme launched 1 Jan 2023 and has been extended several times. Payouts were Rs 4.84 crore by Jun 2023 and **~Rs 136 crore cumulative by 18 Jun 2026**: hospitals Rs 107 cr+, labs/pharmacies Rs 2.95 cr, digital solution companies Rs 26 cr+ (PIB backgrounder via search summary). About a fifth went to software vendors, not to care providers.

### 3. Money

- **Outlay confirmed:** In Feb 2022 the Cabinet approved **Rs 1,600 crore for five years**, ending March 2026 (biovoicenews and others). Approval date 26 Feb 2022 is from memory and matches the press.
- **Actual spend:** One Digital Health News report (date unclear, probably 2025) says **only about a third of the allocated funds had been used**. It also says NHA sought **~Rs 2,000 crore to extend ABDM to 2030**, with cabinet approval "underway". This is single-sourced. No report confirming cabinet approval of the extension was found.
- **2026-27:** **BE Rs 350 crore** (RE 2025-26 Rs 324.26 crore). Because the mission has a 2026-27 budget line, it is effectively continuing past March 2026, whether or not a formal extension was approved. For scale, Rs 350 crore is about 0.33% of MoHFW's Rs 1,06,530 crore budget.

### 4. Who builds it

- **ABDM core (ABHA, HPR, HFR, gateway, consent manager):** The public sources found say NHA designs and builds the core in-house, with open APIs, sandbox certification and private "integrators". **No evidence was found that TCS, Infosys, Wipro or HCLTech built the ABDM core, NHCX or TMS, and no named contract was found.** This is an absence of evidence from limited searches, not proof that they are not involved.
- **Hosting:** NHA tendered a **Managed Cloud Service Provider** for PM-JAY IT and digital health (NIT on abdm.gov.in, not opened). A **Tata Communications** case study on NHA exists (https://tatacommunications.com/hubfs/47271964/TaCO-2024/case-studies/doc/tata-comm-nha-case-study-1.pdf). Per the summary, it describes PM-JAY growing from 5 system integrators at launch to 200+ today. Tata Communications' exact scope (network or cloud) was not verified. This is the only large Indian firm found with a documented NHA role, and it is a network/cloud company, not an IT-services major.
- **PM-JAY TMS:** It is one of three core PM-JAY IT systems, with BIS (beneficiary identification) and GMS (grievance management). TMS 2.0 is current (Jan 2025 manual, per vendor blog). **Developer not identified.**
- **NHCX:** Built by NHA on FHIR/HCX open specifications. Developer or contractor not identified in searches.
- **eSanjeevani:** Built by **C-DAC Mohali**, a government R&D body, which has provided "end-to-end technological development and engineering support since inception" (multiple Oct 2026 reports) (H).
- **U-WIN / eVIN:** Developed by **MoHFW with technical support from UNDP** and funding from Gavi (UNDP's own pages). "Built by UNDP" overstates its role (H).
- **HMIS for facilities:** **C-DAC e-Sushrut@Clinic** (NHA–C-DAC MoU), **NIC e-Hospital**, plus a long tail of private ABDM-certified HMIS vendors and startups (e.g., Pristyn Care named as an ABDM health-tech partner, https://www.indianpharmapost.com/healthcare/lite/nha-enlists-pristyn-care-as-health-tech-partner-for-ayushman-bharat-digital-mission-12113).
- **For the thesis:** What was found points to India's public health-IT stack being built by **government bodies (NHA in-house, C-DAC, NIC), UNDP and a crowd of small private integrators**, not by the TCS/Infosys/Wipro/HCLTech tier that serves foreign payers and providers. This is a contrast worth testing further (see Gaps).

### 5. Data protection

- **DPDP Act 2023:** Its Rules were **notified in Nov 2025** (13/14 Nov) after the Jan 2025 draft. The phase-in has three stages: the Data Protection Board is set up immediately, consent-manager provisions follow at ~12 months (Nov 2026), and main data-fiduciary obligations apply at ~18 months (**~13 May 2027**) (AZB, AMSS, Hogan Lovells). So as of Oct 2026, **most obligations on hospitals and health apps are not yet in force.**
- **Health data under DPDP:** The Act has **no separate "sensitive" category for health data**. It treats all digital personal data alike, unlike the old SPDI Rules 2011 under the IT Act. This is from my knowledge of the Act's text; the searches did not address it, so verify before publishing. From memory and unverified: the notified Rules exempt clinical establishments and healthcare professionals from verifiable parental consent for children's data, to the extent needed for health services.
- **DISHA (Digital Information Security in Healthcare Act):** A 2018 draft that was never introduced in Parliament. It is widely reported to have been folded into the general data-protection track. *The searches returned nothing on DISHA, so this is from memory; mark it unverified.* No health-specific data law is in force. ABDM relies on its own Health Data Management Policy, which is not a statute.

### 6. International comparison (hospital EHR adoption)

- **US:** ONC data briefs give certified-EHR possession in non-federal acute-care hospitals of **85% (2012)** and **93% (2013)** (primary ONC brief). The figure is **>99% by 2024** per an ONC brief summarised by Healthcare Innovation, which also reports little variation by hospital size or location since 2018. The brief's "~96%" figure is commonly quoted for 2017–2021. It was not located in this search, so use ">99% (2024)" or "93% already in 2013" instead.
- **Contrast for the blog:** By 2013 the US had near-universal hospital EHRs, backed by HITECH incentive money (~US$30–35 bn, from memory, unverified). India has no national EHR-adoption statistic at all. Its incentive scheme has paid out ~Rs 136 crore (~US$16 m) in 3.5 years.
- No second country was searched (budget used up).

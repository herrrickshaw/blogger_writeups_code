# Health IT exports: how much Indian IT/BPM revenue comes from healthcare and life sciences (research notes)

Compiled 18 Oct 2026. Part of the "health-it" article research (masaladeutsch.blogspot.com). This file covers the export side only.
Method: 14 web searches. Figures come from search-result summaries of company filings, fact sheets and press reports, and are labelled that way. I did not open the primary PDFs myself, because many IR/NSE PDFs are blocked. "Derived" means my own arithmetic on the sourced figures.
Confidence: H = filing-based and consistent across sources; M = one good source or a garbled table extract; L = secondary or single source, or ambiguous scope.

## Notes by question

### 1. Vertical revenue by company

- **Cognizant** is the biggest single exposure. Health Sciences (payers, providers, pharma, biotech, medical devices) brought in **US$6.35bn, about 30% of CY2025 revenue**, according to the SEC annual report/10-K (search-result summary). Q4 2025 Health Sciences revenue was about US$1.62bn, up 5.2% (secondary summary).
  - India headcount: press reports from Q1 2025 say more than 85% of 336,300 staff were in India. The year-end 2025 India share was not found.
  - Cognizant is US-listed. Whether to count it as "Indian IT" is a framing choice the article needs to make explicitly. Its delivery base is overwhelmingly Indian.
- **TCS:** Life Sciences & Healthcare was **10.41% of FY26 revenue**, roughly flat as a share versus FY25 (10.4%) but down 1.8% in constant currency. On FY26 revenue of US$30,017m, that implies **~US$3.1bn** (derived).
  - Q2 FY27 (fact sheet dated 8 Oct 2026, garbled extract): about 10.3% share, about flat in CC YoY.
  - Trend: stagnant.
- **Infosys:** Life Sciences was **about 8% of Q1 FY27 revenue** and the fastest-growing vertical, at about +24% YoY. The search summary said "well above company average" in Q4 FY26 too.
  - FY26 full-year share was not retrieved: the SEC 6-K segment table was cut off in the extract.
  - The "6.8%" figure I tested in the query was **not** supported by any source.
- **Wipro:** the Health vertical was about 14.4% of revenue in the latest datasheet column (likely Q4 FY26), stable at 14–15% across recent quarters. Brokers describe healthcare as a "source of stability" in FY26.
  - The USD figure was not retrieved.
- **HCLTech:** Life Sciences & Healthcare was 14.5% in Q1 FY26 (secondary source). Its FY26 and Q1 FY27 shares were not found.
  - Q1 FY27 growth was only +0.4% CC YoY.
  - Earnings-call summaries say revenue dropped from a prior ~US$500m run rate. The period basis of that figure is unclear: per quarter or otherwise is not stated. Management attributed this to the end of med-device regulatory work and "ongoing US healthcare market stress". Trend: weakening.
- **Tech Mahindra:** Healthcare & Life Sciences was 7.4% of Q4 FY26 revenue, down 0.8% QoQ. Its exposure is small.
- **LTIMindtree (now "LTM"):** it reports a combined "Healthcare, Life Sciences & Public Services" vertical, so it is not comparable. No percentage was found.
  - A FY26 review says the vertical's share rose. ICICI Direct reported Healthcare +9.6% QoQ in Q2 FY26.
- **Rough floor (derived, indicative only):** TCS (~US$3.1bn, FY26) + Cognizant (~US$6.3bn, CY2025) ≈ **US$9.5bn** from just two firms.
  - Wipro, HCLTech and Infosys plausibly add several billion more, but I did not find USD figures for them, so no total is given.
- **Recent-quarter trend (Apr–Sep 2026):** mixed.
  - Up: Infosys LS is growing strongly (+24%).
  - Flat: TCS and HCLTech are roughly flat in CC.
  - Down: Tech Mahindra fell slightly QoQ in Q4 FY26.
  - Cognizant won a large payer deal (Centene).
  - The HCLTech "US healthcare market stress" remark is worth quoting as a risk signal.

### 2. Industry totals (NASSCOM)

- **No current NASSCOM figure was found** for the healthcare/life-sciences share of IT-BPM exports.
  - The only data point is an old IBEF/NASSCOM chart (FY2016) that, read in order, suggests healthcare at about 4% of exports. That reading is my inference from a garbled extract (https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/it-and-ites-sector-reports-november2016/69980481). Do not use it as a current figure.
  - The Economic Survey 2021-22 lumps healthcare into "Others" (https://www.indiabudget.gov.in/economicsurvey/ebook_es2022/files/basic-html/page353.html).
- **No US-healthcare-specific share** of exports was found.
- The company data above suggests healthcare + life sciences is typically **~8–15% of revenue at the big Indian vendors** and **~30% at Cognizant**. It is well below BFSI (about 28–35%).
  - This is a company-level pattern, not an industry statistic.
- **Implication for the thesis:** the claim that Indian IT "runs a large part" of global healthcare needs to be scaled to these numbers. Healthcare is a significant but minority vertical, about 10% for TCS.

### 3. The kind of work

- **Payer platforms and claims:**
  - Cognizant owns the **TriZetto** payer platforms, and the Centene deal is TriZetto work.
  - Wipro's FY2026 SEC 6-K filings mention two wins (https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1123799/000119312526147595/d127245dex991.htm and related 6-Ks; I did not confirm which filing holds which win):
    - a renewal and expansion of a "decade-long strategic partnership" with a US national health insurer, built on its **PayerAI** platform (client unnamed);
    - an extension with another US health insurer for large-scale IT modernisation.
  - Wipro bought HealthPlan Services (a US health-insurance BPaaS firm) in 2016 for US$460m. This is background only, not recent.
- **Revenue-cycle management and medical coding (mostly mid-size specialists, often PE-owned):**
  - Omega Healthcare: more than 26,000 employees across the US, India and the Philippines. A headline about hiring 18,000 in India is unverified, since I saw only the headline: https://cxotoday.com/media-coverage/omega-healthcare-to-hire-18000-personnel-in-the-next-one-year-to-expand-operations-in-india/
  - R1 RCM: "about 14,000 strong in India" of more than 30,000 globally, according to its own job-posting text (https://r1rcm.dejobs.org/noida-ind/specialist-quality/17C38BF37C6B4571AE837B328C929696/job). This is single-source.
  - India Healthcare Solutions / GHBP: about 2,476 staff in Sept 2025 (Revelio Labs).
  - Ken Research names Access Healthcare, Omega, AGS Health, IKS Health and Infinx as the major players.
  - No AGS Health, Access Healthcare or GeBBS India headcount was found.
  - The market-size estimates (RCM US$4.0–6.1bn in 2025; coding US$0.87bn in 2024) come from commercial research vendors. They conflict, and it is **unclear whether they measure offshore export work or the domestic Indian market**. Use with heavy caveats or not at all.
- **EHR implementation (Epic/Oracle Health):**
  - Infosys agreed in **March 2026** to buy **Optimum Healthcare IT** (a US firm serving hospitals and health systems) for up to US$465m. Its exact Epic-consulting focus was not verified in this search.
  - No 2025–26 Epic-specific deal by TCS, HCLTech, Wipro or Cognizant was found.
- **Pharma R&D, clinical data and med-device regulatory work:**
  - HCLTech's commentary confirms med-device regulatory work as a revenue line, which is now winding down.
  - Infosys LS growth (+24%) is the strongest signal of pharma/life-sciences demand.
  - No specific clinical-data-management deal was retrieved.
- **Named deals 2025–26 (use these):**
  1. Cognizant–Centene, July 2026: multi-year, more than US$500m and possibly up to US$1bn (press citing people familiar; unconfirmed), TriZetto platform.
  2. Infosys acquiring Optimum Healthcare IT, March 2026: up to US$465m, provider-side health IT.
  3. Wipro renewal/expansion with an unnamed US national health insurer (PayerAI), from its SEC 6-K filings for FY2026.
  - Minor: Newgen Software got a US$5.48m, three-year contract with a US health insurer in Sept 2026.

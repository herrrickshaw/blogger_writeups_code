## Findings

### 1. electrification-is-lngs-biggest (HIGH)
- **Claim as stated:** "LNG Canada's own proposed Phase 2 is the clearest live example of electrification meeting a grid constraint... The plan under discussion would run refrigeration compressors on electric motors fed by BC Hydro's hydroelectric grid instead of gas turbines."
- **Current:** LNG Canada's partners took a final investment decision on Phase 2 on 29 September 2026. The project adds two trains, takes Kitimat from 14 to 28 mtpa, and has an announced cost of about C$33 billion. Commercial operations are expected in the early 2030s. Partners are Shell 40%, PETRONAS 25%, PetroChina 15%, Mitsubishi 15% and KOGAS 5%.
  - Sources:
    - [Shell takes FID to double LNG Canada capacity, Offshore Energy Digital, Sept 2026](https://www.oedigital.com/news/543318-shell-takes-fid-to-double-lng-canada-capacity)
    - [Enerdata, LNG Canada takes FID on Phase 2, Sept 2026](https://www.enerdata.net/publications/daily-energy-news/lng-canada-takes-fid-phase-2-expansion-reach-28-mtyear.html)
    - [Prime Minister's office release, 29 Sept 2026](https://www.pm.gc.ca/en/news/news-releases/2026/09/29/prime-minister-carney-welcomes-lng-canada-phase-2-second-largest)
- **Caveat on electrification:** the search results did not say whether Phase 2 will use electric-drive compressors. One report quotes LNG Canada leadership saying they "identified possible further electrification" and urging BC Hydro and the province to unlock it ([Offshore Energy](https://offshore-energy.biz/?p=668322)). That suggests the electric-drive question is still open. Do not assert either way.
- **Suggested wording:** "LNG Canada's Phase 2, which the partners sanctioned on 29 September 2026 (two more trains, taking Kitimat from 14 to 28 mtpa), is the clearest live example of electrification meeting a grid constraint rather than an engineering one. Whether its compressors will run on BC Hydro power or on gas turbines depends on grid capacity the company says it is still working with the utility to secure." Also change "proposed" and "the plan under discussion" to past or present as appropriate. Check the Shell FID release for the drive design before publishing.
- **Severity:** HIGH, because a pending project has resolved.
- **Confidence:** confirmed by 2+ sources for the FID. Unverified for the electrification detail.

### 4. pngrb-hydrogen-blending-pilots-real (MEDIUM)
- **Claim as stated:** PNGRB "has not finalised a regulation that sets a hydrogen-blending percentage" and is still drafting rules; the most recent status cited is the Business Standard report of 30 September 2025. Pilots: Kawas "started at 5%... plan to scale to 20%", and "Nothing found... shows PNGRB finalising a national hydrogen-blending percentage."
- **Current:** The search summary reports newer items. None is confirmed from the primary page.
  - PNGRB's Draft Typical Guidelines for Hydrogen Blending with Natural Gas were reportedly unveiled at a national conference at IIT Ropar on 9-10 October 2026, two days ago.
  - PNGRB's own knowledge paper says the PNGRB Act does not currently extend to hydrogen. It lists proposed amendments, and none has been enacted.
  - The Kawas network has reportedly reached 8% by volume, and Jorhat (Assam) has reportedly been approved for up to 2%.
  - PNGRB and Germany's DVGW signed a non-binding MoU on 12 January 2026.
  - Sources: [PNGRB Hydrogen Knowledge Paper](https://pngrb.gov.in/pdf/hydrogen-corner/PNGRB_Hydrogen_Knowledge_Paper.pdf), [Policy Edge on the PNGRB-DVGW MoU](https://www.policyedge.in/p/pngrb-and-germanys-dvgw-partner-to), [PNGRB press note](https://pngrb.gov.in/pdf/press-note/20250306_Hydrogen.pdf).
- **Suggested wording:** Keep "no binding blending percentage has been finalised" if the draft guidelines are still a draft. Add: "In October 2026 PNGRB circulated draft typical guidelines for hydrogen blending with natural gas; the PNGRB Act has not yet been amended to cover hydrogen." Update the Kawas row if the 8% figure is confirmed.
- **Severity:** MEDIUM. The status is newer than the post's, but the core "still not a rule" conclusion is probably still right.
- **Confidence:** single source and unverified. The October draft-guidelines item and the Kawas 8% figure need confirmation from PNGRB's hydrogen corner.

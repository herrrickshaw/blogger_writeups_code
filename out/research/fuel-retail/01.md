Key findings:
- The ONLY revision found since 2016 is 30 Oct 2024. Found NO revision in 2023, 2025 or 2026 (to 16 Oct 2026). Not found is not proof of none: PPAC Dealers Commission page was not reachable.
- Timing: the hike came 4 months after the 1 Jun 2024 launch era of E20 supply (E20 was widely rolled out through 2024-25) but was explicitly tied to settlement of litigation, NOT to E20, ethanol handling or E20 equipment cost. No source links any commission revision to E20. [1-2, negative finding]
- Because the commission is Rs per kl (plus a small % of price), it does not move with margins of OMCs or ethanol economics. The dealer's "post-E20 improvement" is the one-off Oct 2024 step, not an E20 effect.
- CONFLICT: a search summary of AIPDA's Sept 2026 letter says commissions "have not been revised upward since October 2017"; another says the last revision date is not stated. This contradicts the Oct 2024 revision (announced by all three OMCs). Treat the "2017" phrase as unverified (possibly dealers refer to a different component, or a summary error). Do not repeat it.

## 2. Dealer association demands and statements

- AIPDA (president Ajay Bansal) wrote to the Finance Minister on 16 Sep 2026 seeking a full exemption from the new UPI MDR (flat Rs 5 on person-to-merchant UPI above Rs 2,000, effective 15 Oct 2026; fuel sector). Argues commission is fixed per litre so dealers cannot earn more on a larger ticket; says costs (electricity, wages) keep rising; dealers estimate 30-40% of purchases exceed Rs 2,000. Some dealers in MP and Punjab said they would stop UPI above Rs 2,000 from 15 Oct. Govt sources (24 Sep): DFS likely to examine. Outcome after 15 Oct not found. Sources (search summaries): newkerala.com/news/a/all-india-petroleum-dealers-association-seeks-complete-mdr-764.htm; telanganatoday.com/petrol-pump-dealers-seek-exemption-from-upi-mdr; theweek.in/news/biz-tech/2026/09/16/mdr-fee-rules-will-you-need-cash-for-petrol-now-pumps-warn-of-stopping-upi-for-purchases-over-indian-rupee2000.html; moneylife.in/article/upi-mdr-charges-trigger-backlash-from-retailers-and-fuel-dealers-as-festive-season-approaches/81678.html [2+ on the demand].
- Earlier AIPDA ask (2018 Moneylife): raise commission to 5% from ~3%. Older margin demands (2016 Deccan Herald): Rs 3.34/l wanted vs Rs 2.56/l then. Both dated, context only.
- 2026 supply stress (March onward, crude up about 50% from 28 Feb 2026, retail prices frozen): Petrol Pump Dealers Association Punjab alleges PSU OMCs cut supplies to limit their losses, HPCL outlets worst hit (Tribune: tribuneindia.com/news/ludhiana/petrol-dealers-in-punjab-allege-oil-companies-supplying-less-fuel-to-save-losses/); Rajasthan dealers' association challenged OMC verbal sale-restriction orders in Rajasthan High Court (english.punjabkesari.com); a central order (Motor Spirit and HSD Temporary Regulation of Supply through Retail Outlets Order, 2026, 90 days, 200 l HSD cap per vehicle per day) reported by thehitavada.com (13 Jun 2026) [1 each]. Dealer income effect: lower volume means lower Rs/kl commission; no quantified dealer loss found.
- Excise: 26 Mar 2026 special additional excise cut (petrol Rs 13 to Rs 3/l, diesel Rs 10 to nil) with retail prices unchanged, to cut OMC losses (moneylife.in 80059; theweek.in 2026/03/27) [2+]. This is a government/OMC measure; dealer commission is not affected on paper. 2022 precedent: dealers reported inventory/excise refund losses of Rs 12-15 lakh after cuts (Hitavada, 2022) [1]; no 2026 dealer-loss report found.
- Not found: FAIPT, CIPD, FAPTCI 2025-26 statements; any negotiated 2025-26 hike; any dealer association statement tying margins to E20.
- E20 dealer side: dealers' association circular disclaiming responsibility for water in E20 fuel; pump owners (The Hindu, via search summary) say underground tanks were built for conventional petrol and phase separation occurs above 0.5% water; OMCs say ~90,000 outlets started mandatory UST inspections (cartoq.com; indiaipo.in; madhyamam) [1-2]. No compensation or commission linked to this found.

## Premise test (my slice only)

- Dealer commission: one step up of about Rs 0.62-0.65/l on petrol and 0.44 on diesel on 30 Oct 2024, justified by litigation settlement, unrelated to E20. Before that, none since Oct 2016 (about 8 years, in which cost inflation and falling throughput per outlet eroded real income). No increase since. So "dealer margins improved after the E20 launch" is only true by coincidence of timing, and only once; real dealer income is hit by lower throughput per outlet, UPI MDR (from 15 Oct 2026), and 2026 supply restrictions.

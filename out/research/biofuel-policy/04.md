### A1. ICAO CORSIA and ICAO aviation goals
- **Instrument/level:** CORSIA is a global market-based measure for international aviation. Operators offset growth above the baseline with eligible units, and may reduce the offsetting requirement with CORSIA Eligible Fuels (CEF). Phase dates (pilot 2021-23, first phase 2024-26, second phase 2027-35, mandatory for most states from 2027): **U**, background knowledge, not shown in any search result. Check ICAO.
- **CEF document set (ICAO CORSIA Eligible Fuels page, seen via search summary, "last updated June 2026"):**
  - "Default Life Cycle Emissions Values for CORSIA Eligible Fuels": **Eighth Edition, November 2025**.
  - "Methodology for Calculating Actual Life Cycle Emissions Values": **Seventh Edition, November 2025**.
  - "CORSIA Sustainability Criteria" and "Eligibility Framework": each listed as **Fourth Edition, June 2025**. This is probably what "Fourth Edition updates" refers to.
  - Approved Sustainability Certification Schemes: **Fourth Edition, June 2026** (ISCC, RSB, Bonsucro, ClassNK etc. listed). The "Fourth Edition" label comes from one search summary, so check it. S.
  - LCA Methodology supporting document: Version 7, June 2025. No 2026 version seen.
- **What changed in the 2025 periodic review (ICAO newsletters, summaries only):**
  - July 2025: all ILUC values and most core LCA values updated, using newer LCA databases.
  - Aug 2025: updated default ILUC values; updated core LCA values for stand-alone ATJ-ethanol pathways; new ILUC values for the corn-grain ATJ pathway.
  - Dec 2025: ICAO Council approved amendments, including a **new default core LCA value for HEFA-Palm Oil Mill Effluent (POME)**.
  - Actual-emissions methodology: new feedstocks classified as processing residues (spent bleaching earth, sugarcane vinasse).
  - The actual numeric default values (gCO2e/MJ) were NOT seen. Do not quote any numbers.
- **Hydrogen link:** CEF includes SAF made from non-fossil feedstock through HEFA, FT, ATJ and others. A power-to-liquid (PtL) pathway with a default value was NOT confirmed in my searches. Hydrogen in HEFA enters through the process LCA, and I saw no figure.
- **CAAF/3 (Dubai, Nov-Dec 2023):** global goal that aviation fuel in 2030 is **5% less carbon-intensive** than today's fossil fuel, through SAF and, as an interim tool, lower carbon aviation fuels (LCAF). The goal is aspirational, with an annual ICAO stocktake and a CAAF/4 no later than 2028. 100+ states. Context: ICAO's long-term aspirational goal (LTAG) is net-zero carbon emissions by 2050. C (several news and ATAG summaries).

### A5. South Africa
- **Instruments:** Petroleum Products Act 120 of 1977; Regulations on Mandatory Blending of Biofuels with Petrol and Diesel (GN R.671), effective 2015. Minimum biodiesel 5% v/v of diesel; bioethanol min 2%, max 10% v/v of petrol (per IEA policy database). Amended 2020 to add 2nd-gen (crop/forest residue) and 3rd-gen (algae) biofuels. The Cabinet-approved framework targets 4.5% of the national fuel pool, with 2% from first-generation biofuels. (IEA entries last updated 2023-24.)
- **Reported 2025-26 development (contested):** a DMPR notice (No. 7233) on a regulated biofuels price under the Petroleum Products Act, with a notice date of 13 March 2026 but a signature date of 05-08-2025 in the same record. It describes phase 1 of the framework as 2% penetration from locally produced first-gen biofuel. The date is inconsistent, so verify in the Gazette. Also, the "5% diesel minimum in R.671" and "2% phase 1 target" are not reconciled in the sources. Whether the diesel mandate is actually enforced was not found. S.
- **Coverage/mechanism:** road petrol and diesel; obligated parties are licensed manufacturers/wholesalers; volumetric mandate, with a regulated biofuel price (basic fuel price set by the Department). Aviation: not found.
- **Hydrogen:** Hydrogen Society Roadmap (Dept of Science and Innovation, **Feb 2022**): 70 priority actions in 6 outcomes. It is reported to say there are currently **no direct incentive mechanisms or business models** for hydrogen production, but it recognises existing tax incentives. Tax provisions named in results: s11D (R&D) and SEZ provisions s12S/12R (15% corporate rate, 10% building allowance). Hydrogen Valley corridor is a flagship (Mokopane to Johannesburg to Durban). Bowmans: no standard/certification for low-carbon hydrogen yet. I found nothing on s12B/12I applied to hydrogen. No 2025-26 incentive update found. No biofuel-hydrogen interaction rule found.
- Sources (summary only): https://www.iea.org/policies/13383-south-african-biofuels-regulatory-framework ; https://www.iea.org/policies/5297-regulations-regarding-the-mandatory-blending-of-bio-fuels-with-petrol-and-diesel-government-notice-no-r-671 ; https://api.acts.co.za/petroleum-products-act-1977/n7233_notice_no__7233_of_20.php ; https://www.iea.org/policies/16974-hydrogen-society-roadmap ; https://bowmanslaw.com/insights/south-africa-embracing-green-and-blue-hydrogen/ ; https://research.csiro.au/hyresource/policy/international/south-africa. ### B2. Refinery/co-processing hydrogen under EU RED III
- **RED III (Directive (EU) 2023/2413), Art. 22a:** RFNBOs must be **42% of hydrogen used in industry (energy and non-energy) by 2030 and 60% by 2035**. Can be cut by 20% if the Member State's fossil-hydrogen share is no more than 23% in 2030 and 20% in 2035 (and a second condition). The exclusions listed in a Bundestag-commissioned opinion include hydrogen made as a by-product and hydrogen "used as an intermediate in fuel production" (sentence truncated; **verify against EUR-Lex**). C for 42/60 (several), S for exclusions.
- **Transport crediting of refinery hydrogen:** Hydrogen Europe's guidance note asks that 100% of RFNBO hydrogen used as an intermediate in refineries count toward the transport targets (the "refinery route"). That is an industry position, not the rule text. TotalEnergies asks for a separate RFNBO sub-quota of about 2.5-4% by 2030. Rule text for Art. 27 not seen. U.
- **Germany (RED III transposition into GHG-quota law):** RFNBO sub-quota in transport: 0.1% (2026), 1.2% (2030), then **10% by 2040 per one report, or 8% per another (sources conflict)**; one report also has 0.5% in 2028. Penalty reports: EUR 120/GJ shortfall (one report), or a draft 70 EUR/GJ with a stakeholder asking for 115 EUR/GJ for RFNBO (a different source). May 2026 report: Bundestag approved, Bundesrat did not object, final formalities pending. How refinery hydrogen is treated in the German text: not found. Status as of Oct 2026: unverified whether it has entered into force.
- Delegated acts on RFNBO: published 20 June 2023, in force 10 July 2023 (additionality; installation up to 36 months older than the electrolyser in some cases). The low-carbon hydrogen delegated act and its counting: not found.
- Other jurisdictions (US, etc.): not searched; no figures.

### B4. Cost gap (published figures)
- **IEA** (via Hydrogen Insight): e-kerosene about **USD 2,150/t (USD 50/GJ) by 2030** under big cuts in electrolyser (-60%) and renewable power (-25%) costs, still **2-3x** fossil jet. Fossil jet USD 750-1,000/t; biomass-based SAF USD 1,500-3,000/t. A 10% e-fuel blend would raise airline fuel bills ~15% and ticket prices ~5%. (Hydrogen Insight article; IEA original not seen.)
- **CEA KERMIT study (France, 2026):** e-kerosene EUR 3,000-4,000/t vs EUR 600-1,000/t fossil kerosene.

- **Deloitte:** average EU HEFA SAF price in 2023 EUR 2,768/t, 340% above fossil kerosene (2023 base).
- **IATA:** SAF costs about 2x fossil jet, up to 5x in mandated markets (advocacy source).
- Figures use different bases (years, cost vs price, currency) and are not comparable one to one.

## 1. Rules in force for 2026 imports

### 1a. Timeline and Omnibus simplification (Reg. (EU) 2025/2083)
- The definitive period started on 1 Jan 2026, when financial obligations (buying and surrendering certificates) began. The transitional, reporting-only period ran from 1 Oct 2023 to 31 Dec 2025. — "The_CBAM_Definitive_Guide.pdf" (NotebookLM deck, Google Drive id 1zOwIkz4CoYT6sSZ2RQum9RvmxgvzBW6y) — undated — [secondary]
- De-minimis threshold: authorisation is needed only for imports **above 50 tonnes per calendar year** of CBAM goods (single mass-based threshold). Electricity and hydrogen are covered regardless of quantity. — same Drive deck; Anthesis / osapiens summaries (https://www.anthesisgroup.com/regulations-hub/carbon-border-adjustment-mechanism/ ; https://osapiens.com/en/regulations/cbam) — 2025–26 — [secondary]
- Authorised CBAM declarant status: importers must apply via their National Competent Authority, "latest by 31 March 2026" (Drive deck). Mayer Brown: importers expecting to exceed the threshold must apply **before** they exceed it. — Drive deck; Mayer Brown, "EU adopts CBAM simplification regulation: 10 key amendments" (https://www.mayerbrown.com/de/insights/publications/2025/10/eu-adopts-cbam-simplification-regulation-10-key-amendments-and-challenges-ahead) — 17 Oct 2025 — [secondary]
- Importing above the threshold without authorisation: penalty of **3–5 times** the standard penalty, which may be reduced if the threshold was exceeded by no more than 10%. — Mayer Brown (above) — Oct 2025 — [secondary]
- Certificate sales: certificates for **2026 imports** are bought on the CBAM Registry **from 1 Feb 2027**. The first annual declaration and surrender are due by **30 Sep 2027**; the Omnibus moved this deadline from 31 May. — osapiens; Andersen Italy (https://it.andersen.com/en/cbam-the-first-certificate-price-will-be-published-on-7-april-2026/); search summary of GreenSutra / cbamguide — 2026 — [secondary]
- 2026 certificate price = **quarterly** average of EU ETS auction clearing prices, weighted by volume and published in the first calendar week after each quarter. **Weekly** pricing starts in 2027. — Andersen Italy; S&P Global (https://www.spglobal.com/energy/en/news-research/latest-news/energy-transition/040726-brussels-confirms-first-cbam-certificate-price-for-q1-2026-at-eur7536mtco2e) — Apr 2026 — [primary-via-summary]
- Superseded: a 2023 WTS Global note gives a 31 May declaration deadline, which the Omnibus has replaced. — WTS Global (https://tiberghien.com/images/publications/WTSGlobal_CPGTE_CBAM_update_fin.pdf) — 2023 — [secondary]

### 1b. Free-allocation phase-out ("CBAM factor")
Rule: certificates to surrender = embedded emissions − (CBAM factor × CBAM benchmark), minus any carbon price
effectively paid in the country of origin. The CBAM factor is the share of free allocation that EU producers still
receive under Art. 10a(1a) of Directive 2003/87/EC. — cbamguide.com/learn/free-allocation (https://cbamguide.com/learn/free-allocation/), as seen in a search summary — 2026 — [secondary]

| Year | Law in force (Dir. 2003/87/EC Art. 10a(1a)) | COM(2026) 616 proposal (not adopted) |
|---|---|---|
| 2026 | 97.5% | 97.5% |
| 2027 | 95% | 95% |
| 2028 | 90% | 91.5% |
| 2029 | 77.5% | 81% |
| 2030 | 51.5% | 59% |
| 2031 | 39% | 48% |
| 2032 | 26.5% | 37.5% |
| 2033 | 14% | 27% |
| 2034 | 0% (no free allocation) | 15% (held until 2037) |
| 2038 | 0% | 0% |

- Source for the table: cbamguide.com news, "EU ministers debate the ETS review: CBAM phase-in to 2038 stays a proposal" (https://cbamguide.com/news/2026-10-12-environment-council-ets-review-cbam-phase-in-carbon-price/) and the free-allocation page, both via search summary — 12 Oct 2026 — [secondary]. The Commission's explanatory memorandum, as quoted there, proposes that "15% of the free allocation phased out due to the CBAM factor be reintroduced, starting in 2028" [primary-via-summary].
- The ETS review (COM(2026) 616) was proposed on 17 Jul 2026. Parliament and Council positions are due by end-2026 and trilogues start in Jan 2027 (Liese). — cbamguide.com (https://cbamguide.com/news/2026-08-20-liese-ets-review-trilogues-january-2027/ ; https://cbamguide.com/news/2026-07-17-eu-ets-phase-5-revision-slows-cbam-phase-in/) — Jul–Aug 2026 — [press]
- **Conflict:** another cbamguide page says the CBAM factor goes from "22.5% to 48.5%" between 2029 and 2030. This appears to describe the *phased-out share* (100 − factor) rather than the factor itself, and the arithmetic fits that reading (100 − 77.5 = 22.5; 100 − 51.5 = 48.5). Not a substantive conflict.
- **Conflict (method):** GreenSutra ("Why do importers pay only 2.5 per cent of CBAM emissions in 2026?", https://greensutra.in/?p=238470) and its worked example (a 500 t hot-rolled flat line costs ~EUR 4,400 in 2026, i.e. ~EUR 9/t) apply a flat 2.5% to *total* embedded emissions. SMM and Argus deduct factor × benchmark instead, which gives ~EUR 270–278/t at default values (see §5). The benchmark method follows the structure of Implementing Reg. 2025/2620, which governs the "calculation of the free allocation adjustment" (title via EUR-Lex search result https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg_impl/2025/2620/oj/eng). **The 2.5%-of-total shortcut understates the 2026 cost for any producer above the benchmark.** The two methods agree only when embedded emissions equal the benchmark.
- A superseded 2021 Council working draft had a factor starting at 90% in 2026 and falling 10 points a year. Do not use it. — Council WK-13245-2021 (https://data.consilium.europa.eu/doc/document/WK-13245-2021-INIT/en/pdf) — 2021 — [primary-via-summary]

### 1c. Default values and mark-ups (Implementing Reg. (EU) 2025/2621, as corrected by 2026/1740)
- Default values carry a year-based **mark-up of 10% in 2026, 20% in 2027 and 30% from 2028 onward**. — GreenSutra, "What is the CBAM mark-up on steel default values?" (https://greensutra.in/?p=237208); SMM (https://news.metal.com/newscontent/104160201-smm-analysis-cbam-costs-and-emissions-data-reshape-european-procurement-of-indian-steel) — 2026 — [secondary]
- Defaults are **country-specific**, and India's are among the highest. India pig iron = 2.53 tCO2e/t, against China ~1.66 and Türkiye 1.85. — GreenSutra (https://greensutra.in/?p=237166) — 2026 — [secondary]. SMM attributes the country cost gap "to country-specific default values rather than the certificate price".
- If an origin has no published country default and no verified data, a **fallback** default applies (e.g. 3.48 tCO2e/t for pig iron). — GreenSutra (https://greensutra.in/?p=238259) — 2026 — [secondary]
- Corrigendum **Implementing Reg. (EU) 2026/1740** (published 31 Jul 2026, applies retroactively from 1 Jan 2026) fixed missing or incorrect production-route indicators in Annexes I and IV of 2025/2621. It also changed some aluminium-product defaults and precursors of unknown origin. Where no route is defined for an 8-digit CN code, the benchmark is route-independent. — alcircle (https://www.alcircle.com/news/eu-corrects-cbam-default-values-selected-aluminium-products-and-unknown-origin-precursors-affected-120859); europalov.no (https://europalov.no/rettsakt/eus-karbongrensejusteringsmekanisme-rettelser-i-vedlegg-om-fastsatte-standardverdier/id-35391) — Jul 2026 — [primary-via-summary]
- When default values are used, the free-allocation adjustment uses the **default benchmark route matched to the defaults**. Most origins, India included, are classed **BF-BOF** for steel, whatever the actual route. — OPIS / McCloskey and GreenSutra "How is SEFA calculated with default values" (https://greensutra.in/question/sefa-default-values-vs-actual-data/) — 2025–26 — [secondary]

### 1d. Scope: direct emissions only for steel and aluminium
- Iron and steel and aluminium are Annex II goods, so **only direct emissions** count. Indirect emissions from electricity are excluded, and the defaults are direct-only. — GreenSutra (https://greensutra.in/?p=237166 ; https://greensutra.in/?p=237178) — 2026 — [secondary]. Background, not re-verified this session: cement and fertilisers include indirect emissions, and Art. 30 of Reg. 2023/956 requires a review of whether to extend indirect emissions to more goods.

### 1e. Verification
- Only an **EU-accredited CBAM verifier** can verify installation data. NABCB accreditation (ISO 14065 / ISO/IEC 17029) helps prepare the data but **does not replace** EU CBAM accreditation. — GreenSutra, "Can an Indian verification body become a CBAM verifier?" (https://greensutra.in/?p=238462) — 2026 — [secondary]
- Third-country route to accreditation: as of the Commission's **10 Jul 2026** table, 7 EU National Accreditation Bodies had agreed to accredit third-country verifiers. Four already accept applications: Accredia (IT), RvA (NL), PCA (PL) and Swedac (SE). The first CBAM verifiers were expected to be accredited **around Sep 2026**. — GreenSutra / The Sustainability Cloud (https://www.thesustainabilitycloud.com/blog/eu-cbam-verifiers-complete-guide-and-checklist-for-indian-exporters-2026-ready/) — 2026 — [secondary]
- The Commission published "Guidance on CBAM Verification and Accreditation for Verifiers and NABs" on **24 Aug 2026**. It was not opened, so site-visit rules were not confirmed. — same sources; DEKRA (https://www.dekra.com/en/cbam-verification-the-accreditation-framework-takes-shape/) — 2026 — [primary-via-summary]
- If the operator cannot provide a verified emissions report, the declarant **must use default values**. — The Sustainability Cloud (above) — 2026 — [secondary]
- **Conflict (legal reference):** GreenSutra cites delegated act 2025/2551 for verification and accreditation; The Sustainability Cloud cites C(2025)7845. These may be the same act (Commission document number versus OJ number), but this was not confirmed.

### 1f. Penalties
- Failing to surrender enough certificates by 30 Sep 2027: **EUR 100 per tCO2e**, indexed to EU inflation (the same as the ETS excess-emissions penalty). Paying the penalty **does not** release the declarant from surrendering the missing certificates. — Anthesis / osapiens / Mayer Brown search summaries — 2025–26 — [secondary]
- Unauthorised imports above 50 t: 3–5× the standard penalty (see §1a).
- Customs check the CBAM account number during clearance, so goods can be **held at the border** if authorisation is invalid. — Drive deck — [secondary]

### 1g. Who is liable (background)
- The legal obligation sits with the **EU importer / authorised declarant** (or an indirect customs representative), not with the Indian producer. Indian mills carry the cost through price negotiation. Background from Reg. 2023/956, not re-verified this session.
- Who is liable when supplier data proves wrong: GreenSutra Q&A (https://greensutra.in/question/cbam-liability-wrong-supplier-data/) — [secondary]. Not opened.

## 4. Indian producers' actual emission intensities

### Steel
- India average **2.54 tCO2/t crude steel** (NITI Aayog 2025 baseline; CSE), against a global average of 1.91–1.92. — CSE report (https://www.cseindia.org/content/downloadreports/13216); IMPRI (https://www.impriindia.com/?p=75125); GMK Center (https://gmk.center/en/news/indian-steel-industry-leads-the-world-in-co2-emissions/) — 2024–25 — [secondary]
- **Conflict:** Climate Policy Initiative puts the sector average at **2.18** tCO2/t, rising to ~2.27 if announced fossil capacity is built (https://www.climatepolicyinitiative.org/taking-stock-of-steel-indias-domestic-production-outlook-and-global-investments-in-green-steel-production/) — [secondary]. A 2022 European Commission study gives ~2.6 tCO2/t of *finished* steel, which is a different basis from per-tonne crude steel. — via GMK — [secondary]
- By route (BigMint, https://bigmint.co/insights/detail/coal-based-dri-key-to-indian-steel-industrys-higher-emissions-intensity-466985) — [secondary]: **BF-BOF 2.5–2.7**; **gas-DRI-EAF 1.5–1.7**; **scrap-EAF 1.1–1.3** (fossil power and coke); **coal-DRI-EAF/IF higher than BF-BOF**, with the figure truncated. The ~3.1 often quoted for coal-DRI was **not confirmed**.
- Tata Steel consolidated **2.22 tCO2/tcs**, probably FY2024-25 (column header not visible in the snippet). Target 1.85 by 2030. — Tata Steel ESG Factsheet, Integrated Report 2025-26 (https://www.tatasteel.com/investors/integrated-report-2025-26/pdf/ESG%20Factsheet.pdf) — [primary-via-summary]. Note that the consolidated figure blends India and Europe. Tata Steel UK stopped crude-steel production in Oct 2024.
- JSW Steel target **1.95 tCO2e/t** (Scope 1+2) by FY2030. No verified current figure found. — Hellenic Shipping News (https://www.hellenicshippingnews.com/jsw-steel-sets-target-for-reduction-in-co2-emissions/); Tracenable — [press]
- SAIL, AM/NS India, JSL: **no figures found** (gap).
- India's Green Steel Taxonomy (Ministry of Steel, Dec 2024): 5-star below 1.6, 4-star 1.6–2.0, 3-star 2.0–2.2 tCO2e per tonne of *finished* steel. Scope 1 + 2 + limited Scope 3; NISST is the MRV agency. — ClearIAS / GreenSutra / Mercom (https://www.mercomindia.com/green-steel-taxonomy-and-ratings-decarbonize-faster) — [secondary]
- The CCTS draft GHG-intensity targets cover 255 iron and steel units (JSW, Tata, SAIL, AM/NS). Baseline 2023-24; compliance from 2026-27. — Madhyamam (https://madhyamamonline.com/amp/india/centre-issues-revised-draft-emission-targets-for-iron-and-steel-sector-1534723) — [press]
- **Caveat for CBAM use:** company and national intensities usually include Scope 2 (electricity), while CBAM steel counts **direct only, including precursors**. A BF-BOF figure of 2.5–2.9 tCO2/t is therefore a conservative (high) proxy for CBAM-relevant embedded emissions in HRC.

## 6. "Not comply" options and consequences
- **Default values with mark-up.** Without a verified installation report, the declarant must use the India default plus 10% (2026), 20% (2027) or 30% (2028+). For Indian HRC this is 4.71 → 5.56 tCO2e/t, against an actual ~2.5–2.9 (§2a, §4). In 2026 the default route costs about **EUR 145–177/t more** than a verified 2.5–2.9 (§7).
- **Buyers price carbon into contracts.** EU buyers now "negotiate the carbon bill" and assess emissions data alongside the steel quote. They push clauses that pass CBAM costs back to mills, and the key metric becomes "carbon-adjusted landed cost". — SMM (above) — 2026 — [secondary]. No measurable switch of EU buyers away from Indian suppliers was found in the sources (gap).
- **Volumes so far.** EU-27 imports of Indian non-alloy HRC were **517,315 t in Jan–Jul 2026**, against 89,641 t a year earlier. 99.5% arrived in Jan, Apr and Jul (quota openings). — SMM — [secondary]. India shipped ~4 Mt of CBAM steel to the EU in 2025 (Sandbag via Argus). There was heavy front-loading in H2 2025: >0.7 Mt/month from Aug 2025 and >1 Mt in Dec 2025 (BigMint, https://www.bigmint.co/insights/detail/indias-steel-exports-surge-by-over-36-y-o-y-in-10mfy26-will-india-turn-net-exporter-in-fy26-725310) — [secondary]. cbamguide headline: "24.4% export drop in 2025" (https://cbamguide.com/countries/india/; detail not visible) — [secondary]. **Conflict:** the BigMint front-loading story and the cbamguide "24.4% drop" may refer to different products or periods.
- **Diverting exports.** Mills were reported to be seeking buyers in **Africa and the Middle East**. — GMK Center (https://gmk.center/en/news/india-to-support-steel-exporters-amid-cbam-implementation/) — Feb 2026 — [press]
- **Quota and FTA.** The India-EU FTA gives a steel quota of up to **1.64 Mt/yr** (SteelOrbis, Sep 2026, https://www.steelorbis.com/steel-news/latest-news/india-secures-larger-eu-steel-quota-under-fta-but-cbam-burden-remains-1477418.htm). Earlier reports said ~1.6 Mt, about half of supplies (GMK, Feb 2026). Concessions applied from Jul 2026. **CBAM is not waived**. The FTA only provides for engaging the EU on recognising a carbon price paid in India. — Deccan Chronicle / Outlook Business / Vision IAS (https://www.outlookbusiness.com/amp/story/economy-and-policy/indiaeu-seal-mother-of-all-ftas-but-cbam-remains-for-indian-steel-aluminium) — Jan–Sep 2026 — [press]
- **Carbon-price deduction.** A deduction is possible only for a carbon price *effectively paid* in India, with proof. CCTS has no traded price yet (see the Sep 2026 blog post), so the deduction is currently **0**.
- **Sandbag's "reallocation" route.** Indian groups can steer their lowest-emission output (gas-DRI, scrap-EAF, renewable-powered lines) to the EU and send BF-BOF/coal-DRI output elsewhere. This cuts India's 2034 bill from EUR 762 mn to EUR 407 mn (§5).
- **Indirect exporters and traders (background, not re-verified).** When goods pass through a trader or service centre, the EU declarant still needs installation-level data from the producing mill. Without it the declarant falls back to defaults, so traders lacking mill data face the default cost. Only an EU-established importer or indirect customs representative can be the declarant, and a non-EU producer cannot be. — Reg. 2023/956 Art. 5, background — [secondary].
- **Downside of non-compliance for the EU importer:** EUR 100/tCO2e penalty plus the surrender obligation; 3–5× penalties for unauthorised imports; border holds (§1f).

## 7. Illustrative computation — CBAM cost per tonne (labelled ILLUSTRATIVE)

**Formula:** cost (EUR/t) = [embedded emissions (tCO2e/t) − CBAM factor × CBAM benchmark] × certificate price.
India carbon-price deduction = 0. Direct emissions only.

**Assumptions:**
- Prices: **EUR 80** for 2026 (≈ 2026 YTD mean of 79.43 and the Reuters poll at 79.97); **EUR 100** for 2030 and 2034, a round figure near the 2029–30 poll range of 100–109.
- Factors (law in force): 2026 = 0.975; 2030 = 0.515; 2034 = 0. COM(2026) 616 alternative: 0.59 (2030) and 0.15 (2034).
- Benchmarks: HRC BF-BOF **1.370**. Unwrought Al **1.423** (draft; sensitivity at 1.464).
- Defaults with mark-up: HRC 4.28×1.1 = **4.708** (2026); 4.28×1.3 = **5.564** (2030 and 2034, assuming the 30% mark-up and base value are unchanged). Al 1.87×1.1 = **2.057** (2026); 1.87×1.3 = **2.431** (2030 and 2034).

### 7a. Indian BF-BOF hot-rolled coil
Free-allocation deduction: 2026: 0.975 × 1.370 = 1.336; 2030: 0.515 × 1.370 = 0.706; 2034: 0.

| Case | 2026 (EUR 80) | 2030 (EUR 100) | 2034 (EUR 100) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Actual 2.5 (verified) | (2.5 − 1.336) × 80 = 1.164 × 80 = **EUR 93/t** | (2.5 − 0.706) × 100 = **EUR 179/t** | 2.5 × 100 = **EUR 250/t** |
| Actual 2.9 (verified) | (2.9 − 1.336) × 80 = 1.564 × 80 = **EUR 125/t** | (2.9 − 0.706) × 100 = **EUR 219/t** | 2.9 × 100 = **EUR 290/t** |
| India default + mark-up | (4.708 − 1.336) × 80 = 3.372 × 80 = **EUR 270/t** | (5.564 − 0.706) × 100 = **EUR 486/t** | 5.564 × 100 = **EUR 556/t** |
| Penalty for non-compliance (2.5 vs default) | EUR 177/t | EUR 306/t | EUR 306/t |

- Check: the 2026 default figure (EUR 269.78) matches Argus exactly. At the Q3 price of EUR 82.32 it gives 3.372 × 82.32 = **EUR 277.6/t**, which matches SMM's ~EUR 278. This supports the benchmark-deduction method.
- Under COM(2026) 616: 2030 = EUR 169 (2.5) / 209 (2.9) / 476 (default); 2034 = EUR 229 / 269 / 536.
- As a share of price (assumption, not sourced): at an HRC price of ~EUR 550/t, 2026 = 17% (actual 2.5) versus 49% (default); 2034 = 45% versus 101%.
- Caveat: the actual 2.5–2.9 figures are crude-steel intensities that may include Scope 2. True CBAM direct embedded emissions for HRC (including precursors and the hot-rolling step) could be somewhat lower or higher. This needs plant data.

### 7b. Indian unwrought aluminium ingot (direct emissions only)
Free-allocation deduction (benchmark 1.423): 2026: 0.975 × 1.423 = 1.387; 2030: 0.515 × 1.423 = 0.733; 2034: 0.

| Case | 2026 (EUR 80) | 2030 (EUR 100) | 2034 (EUR 100) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Actual direct 1.75 | (1.75 − 1.387) × 80 = **EUR 29/t** | (1.75 − 0.733) × 100 = **EUR 102/t** | **EUR 175/t** |
| Actual direct 2.0 | (2.0 − 1.387) × 80 = 0.613 × 80 = **EUR 49/t** | (2.0 − 0.733) × 100 = **EUR 127/t** | **EUR 200/t** |
| Actual direct 2.5 | (2.5 − 1.387) × 80 = **EUR 89/t** | **EUR 177/t** | **EUR 250/t** |
| India default + mark-up | (2.057 − 1.387) × 80 = 0.670 × 80 = **EUR 54/t** | (2.431 − 0.733) × 100 = **EUR 170/t** | 2.431 × 100 = **EUR 243/t** |

- With benchmark 1.464, 2026 costs fall slightly: EUR 46/t (actual 2.0) and EUR 50/t (default). This is close to Reuters' ~EUR 51/t.
- Under COM(2026) 616: 2030 = EUR 116 (actual 2.0) / 159 (default); 2034 = EUR 179 / 222.
- **Key point:** India's aluminium default (1.87) is low because indirect power emissions are excluded. Verifying actual data only pays off if direct emissions come in **below ~1.9–2.4**. For a smelter with high anode or PFC emissions (>2.43), **the default can be cheaper than actuals**, so "not complying" (using the default) may be rational for aluminium.
- As a share of price (assumption, not sourced): at a primary ingot price of ~EUR 2,300/t, the cost is about 2% in 2026 and 9–11% in 2034.
- Hypothetical only: if indirect emissions were ever added (~18 tCO2e/t total), the 2034 cost would be ~EUR 1,800/t. Indirect emissions are **not** in the current rules.

## 5. Rules on repricing (RBI)

- External benchmark: retail and MSME floating-rate loans must be benchmarked externally (since 1 Oct 2019; Business Standard 2019 headline seen). Benchmarks: policy repo, 3m/6m T-bill (FBIL), other FBIL benchmark. RBI text relayed: "The interest rate under external benchmark shall be reset at least once in three months." Spread changes only at credit-event/ borrower-linked reasons (not verified this round). Source: RBI Master Direction page https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_ViewMasDirections.aspx?id=10295 (summary only; Interest Rate on Advances (Amendment) Directions, 29 Sep 2025, effective 1 Oct 2025, content not seen). A taxguru summary says Commercial Banks (Interest Rates on Advances) Directions, 2025 dated 28 Nov 2025 consolidated rules. SINGLE/secondary.
- MCLR: loans reset per the tenor of MCLR they are linked to (RBI text relayed); existing MCLR/Base Rate loans continue until repayment or renewal. Corporate loans: MCLR or EBLR - corporate EBLR requirement not confirmed this round (UNVERIFIED; do not claim "corporates must be EBLR").
- EMI-based personal loans (floating): reset framework (Aug 2023 circular, updated 1 Oct 2025): lenders must give borrower the choice of increasing tenor/EMI, move to fixed rate, partial/full prepayment; Sep 2025 amendment made the fixed-rate switch optional for lenders (Vinod Kothari summary). This is for personal loans, NOT project/business loans. Link: https://website.rbi.org.in/web/rbi/-/notifications/reset-of-floating-interest-rate-on-equated-monthly-instalments-emi-based-personal-loans-12529
- Pre-payment charges: RBI directions 2025 bar prepayment charges on floating-rate loans to individuals and MSEs (and, per YourStory, business-purpose floating loans; scope for non-MSE/large borrowers unclear), applying to loans sanctioned/renewed on or after 1 Jan 2026. Sources: foxmandal.in, yourstory.com/2025/07/pre-paying-loans-msmes-easier-rbi-abolishes-charges-advances-business-loans. Confidence: 2 sources; scope detail unverified.
- PROPOSED, not enacted: 5 Aug 2026 Statement on Developmental and Regulatory Policies announced a principle-based rationalisation of interest-rate rules on advances (harmonise across REs, MCLR/EBLR operational aspects, day-count and benchmark reset dates). Draft directions to follow; one report says existing loans unaffected. An RBI draft titled "Reserve Bank of India (Interest Rates on Loans and Advances) Directions, 2026 - Draft" appears at https://rbi.org.in/scripts/Bs_viewcontent.aspx?Id=5143 (unread). One outlet's claim of effect from 1 Oct 2026 is UNVERIFIED and likely wrong (draft stage).
- Loan-level mechanics (media, single): repo-linked loans reprice at next reset date, so they feel the hike first; MCLR loans with lag; fixed-rate only at refinancing.

## 6. Pricing of renewable/CBG project loans

- SBI CBG (SATAT) scheme page: MSME: Repo + 2.65%; non-MSME: 6-month MCLR-linked; term loan 10-12 yrs, total tenor (moratorium + repayment) not beyond offtake agreement, 15 years; borrower contribution at least 30% (so debt up to 70%). Seen via search summary only.
- Indian Bank CBG scheme: MCLR (8.95% at time of page) + 1.75% = 10.70% minimum; max MCLR + 4.50% = 13.45%; 10-15 yr repayment; moratorium 6 months-2 yrs; margin 25%. Page dates MCLR figures, so the rate is dated/outdated. https://indianbank.bank.in/en/compressed-bio-gas-plant-financing-under-sustainable-alternative-towards-affordable-transportation
- IREDA CBG scheme (policy PDF dated 16 Sep 2025): debt:equity 70:30, loan max 70% of project cost, up to 10 yrs excluding moratorium and construction period (moratorium up to 1 yr, construction up to 1.5 yrs), at least 50% of promoter contribution upfront; interest rate "as per IREDA policy". https://www.ireda.in/images/HTMLfiles/cbg%20policy-16_09_2025.pdf ; also listed under priority sector (start-ups up to Rs 50 crore for CBG) in the same summary.
- IREDA interest rate matrix wef 1 Jun 2026: pricing "cost of domestic borrowing + 1% to 2% spread", reset at commissioning or one year from first disbursement, then annually; hydro >25 MW +25 bp. https://ireda.in/images/interestratematrix/Interest-Rate-Matrix-wef-01.06.26.pdf (summary only; IREDA's actual percent rate not seen). IREDA cost of borrowings 7.2% (Q2 FY26, ICICI Direct). IREDA response to the Oct hike: NOT FOUND.
- Bank loans therefore run roughly at repo + 2.65% to 2.85% (about 8.15% to 8.35% now) for rated MSME/SBI-type borrowers; riskier/small CBG projects in older bank schemes up to MCLR + 4.5%. A "typical CBG spread" is not published in a single authoritative source - use "2.65% (SBI MSME, stated) and up".
- Project-finance tenors of 10-15 years (SBI/Indian Bank/SBICAP assumption: 15-year loan, 1-year moratorium, 70:30) - the loan is far longer than the quarterly reset, so every reset passes rate changes through; benefit of a fixed rate is only available if the lender offers it (not confirmed for project loans).

## 7. Capital cost and debt share (for computing sensitivity)

All SECONDARY (trade press quoting SBI Capital Markets); primary SBICAP study not opened.
- Powerline (Sept 2024) - "Feedstock to Fuel": ~Rs 50-60 million per tonne of gas capacity (early projects) = Rs 5-6 crore per TPD CBG; current large projects ~Rs 80-90 million = Rs 8-9 crore per TPD CBG. https://powerline.net.in/2024/09/05/feedstock-to-fuel-challenges-and-opportunities-in-the-cbg-segment/
- Powerline (June 2024) "Project viability and bankability key to CBG uptake" quoting SBICAP: 50 TPD agri-residue feed (about 6 tpd CBG) costs Rs 400-450 million = Rs 40-45 crore, i.e. about Rs 6.7-7.5 crore per tpd CBG; DSCR ~1.0-1.2 (6 TPD), 1.5-1.8 (12 TPD), 1.9-2.2 (24 TPD); assumption 70:30 debt:equity, 15-yr loan, 1-yr moratorium. https://powerline.net.in/2024/06/06/money-matters-project-viability-and-bankability-key-to-cbg-uptake/
- The three figures are consistent: Rs 5-9 crore per tpd of CBG output; central ~Rs 7 crore.
- Debt share: 70% (IREDA max; SBICAP assumption); SBI min 30% contribution; Indian Bank 25% margin. Some early OMC-contract projects reportedly 5% equity / 95% debt (single, Powerline).
- AP govt sanction of about Rs 60 crore for five 25 TPD plants (newsonair, Mar 2026) is a state outlay, NOT a project capex per TPD - ignore.
- Units caution: "TPD" is used for both feed processed and CBG produced. Capital cost per tonne of feed is far lower. Always specify CBG output.

### Sensitivity (my arithmetic, assumptions stated)
Assume 70% debt, loan outstanding at a full-sanction level (early years), 330 operating days, 100% utilisation (1 tpd = 330,000 kg/yr).
| Capex per tpd CBG | Debt per tpd (70%) | +25 bp interest per year | Per kg CBG |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rs 5 crore | Rs 3.5 crore | Rs 0.875 lakh | Rs 0.27 |
| Rs 7 crore | Rs 4.9 crore | Rs 1.225 lakh | Rs 0.37 |
| Rs 9 crore | Rs 6.3 crore | Rs 1.575 lakh | Rs 0.48 |
- Full-rate view: at ~8.15% on Rs 4.9 crore, annual interest is about Rs 40 lakh per tpd (first year, before amortisation) = about Rs 12 per kg; the hike adds about Rs 0.37 per kg (about 3% of that interest bill). At 55-60 Rs/kg-order realisation (unverified, other agents' slice) +25 bp is below 1% of revenue. Rs 0.27-0.48/kg falls as principal amortises; lower utilisation raises per-kg figure.
- Interest on 70:30 at the SBICAP DSCR (1.0-1.2 for 6 TPD) leaves little cushion; if DSCR 1.1 and debt service about 91% of CFADS, a 25 bp rise raises interest by about 3% of interest -> DSCR drop on the order of 0.01-0.03. This is a rough estimate, not a published figure.

Conclusion: the blog's "Rs 54/kg" is not supported; the last official per-kg figure found is Rs 77.4/kg (incl. Rs 8 compression) in 2025. Like-for-like, the new ACP excludes compression, so compare 2,110 with 1,478 per MMBtu, not 105 with 77.4.

Indexation: old regime = formula (85% of average CNG retail price at GAIL-associated CGDs, revised in fixed windows, e.g. Jun-Oct 2025), i.e. indirectly tied to CNG pump prices. New regime = administered price set by PAB annually, "market-based cost-sharing", pooled with domestic gas; NO escalation clause or index (APM/LNG) found [U]. Because CNG retail prices rose in 2026 (below), the old formula would have drifted up too - worth noting but I did not compute it.

## 4. Conversion Rs/kg <-> Rs/MMBtu (stated basis)

- 1 MMBtu = 1.055 GJ = 1,055 MJ (standard).
- Govt's own implied factor: Rs 10/kg = ~Rs 215/MMBtu at 95% methane -> ~21.5 kg per MMBtu (~49 MJ/kg). 2,110/21.5 = Rs 98.1/kg (matches Business Standard's ~98).
- 2025 SATAT figures imply 21.3 kg/MMBtu: (77.4-8)=69.4 -> 1,478/69.4 = 21.3; (72.8-8)=64.8 -> 1,380/64.8 = 21.3. 2,110/21.3 = Rs 99.1/kg.
- PIB's Rs 105/kg implies 20.1 kg/MMBtu = ~52.5 MJ/kg, which is about the GCV of 95% methane (my estimate: 0.95 x ~55.5 MJ/kg = 52.7). So PIB seems to use a gross-calorific-value basis; Business Standard/MoPNG rebuttal use ~49 MJ/kg. I could not confirm PIB's basis; the 105 vs 98 gap is a calorific-basis difference, unconfirmed.
- Rule of thumb for the blog: Rs 2,110/MMBtu = Rs 98-105/kg; Rs 1,478/MMBtu = Rs 69-73/kg (ex-compression), i.e. gross gain ~Rs 29-32/kg (my arithmetic).

## 6. Plant economics (rough; sources mixed)

Capex and size
- Large projects ~Rs 80-90 million (Rs 8-9 crore) per tonne/day of gas capacity; 20-24 TPD described as more financially sustainable (Renewable Watch, 25 Jul 2025) [1]. Same article: 10 TPD plant Rs 40-80 crore depending on technology. Powerline 2024: 10 TPD paddy-straw project ~Rs 100 crore (CBEII est.) - inconsistent, treat as wide range.
- Parliamentary committee (via Powerline): 100 TPD feedstock plant Rs 25-80 crore, producing 5-12 t CBG/day [1].
- Central grant Rs 2 crore/TPD therefore covers roughly 22-25% of Rs 8-9 crore/TPD (my arithmetic).

Yield per tonne of feedstock (sources, not mine)
- Pressmud ~25 t per t CBG (= ~40 kg CBG/t); cattle dung ~50 t per t CBG (= ~20 kg/t); paddy straw 8-9 t per t CBG per Mongabay (= ~110-125 kg/t - looks high; check before use) [1 each: DownToEarth pressmud piece https://www.downtoearth.org.in/renewable-energy/sugarcane-byproduct-pressmud-can-be-a-sweet-spot-for-india-s-compressed-biogas-sector-93022 ; Mongabay https://india.mongabay.com/2023/03/organic-agriculture-is-critical-for-bio-cng-growth/]
- Napier yield: not found.

Revenue per TPD (my arithmetic, 1 TPD = 1,000 kg/day, 330 operating days = 330,000 kg/yr, 100% utilisation)

|---|---|

Costs (single sources)
- Opex Rs 5-8 million per year for a 1 TPD plant, varying by feedstock (Powerline) = ~Rs 15-24/kg at 330 t [1].
- Feedstock: pressmud Rs 500-600/t (up from ~Rs 100/t in two years); agri residue Rs 1.5-2/kg; dung Rs 1-2/kg (Down To Earth/CSE) [1].
- Compression Rs 8/kg (SATAT notification). Transport support Rs 1.5-2.5/kg beyond 50 km.
- CSE 5 TPD MSW-based model: revenue Rs 1.07 crore, expenses Rs 81.9 lakh, profit Rs 24.7 lakh [1] - ambiguous (revenue looks too low for 5 t CBG/day at any price above; may be partial/feedstock-based year); do not quote without checking https://cdn.cseindia.org/attachments/0.86053500_1785406558_national-bluepint.pdf.

## 7. By-products and carbon

- FOM/LFOM/PROM Market Development Assistance (DoF): Rs 1,500 per tonne; introduced 2023; budget Rs 1,451.82 crore for FY2023-24 to FY2025-26 (single source); a Pelletrates explainer says the new GOBARdhan framework also carries MDA of Rs 1,500/MT [1, unverified, not in the PIB text I saw]. Only plants registered on the Unified GOBARdhan Portal qualify; product must meet FCO. MDA is not the selling price.
- FOM selling price: ~Rs 3/kg (Rs 3,000/t) in a CSE case; Rs 2.5/kg for 5% FOM at a Surat plant [1]. Yield ~1.75 t FOM per t paddy straw (Mongabay) [1].
- Carbon credits: no per-tonne price found. Indore plant (17,000 kg/day) reportedly earned Rs 9 crore in first two years from international carbon trading [1]; my derived ~Rs 7/kg if it ran at full output the whole time (assumption mine). Industry panel (Jul 2026): useful but "supplementary", constrained by price uncertainty. As of 2023, only 4 CBG projects under VCS and 22 under Gold Standard [1].

## Illustrative link to interest cost (mine, for the blog's thesis; assumptions flagged)
- Per 1 TPD: capex Rs 8.5 crore (midpoint of Rs 80-90 million), 70% debt = Rs 5.95 crore (2024 model assumed 70:30 and 15-yr tenor). Each 25 bp rise on floating-rate debt = ~Rs 1.5 lakh/yr; each 100 bp = ~Rs 6 lakh/yr. Against ~Rs 97 lakh/yr gross price gain at full utilisation, a 25 bp rise absorbs ~1.5% of the gain. Repo move size (to 5.50%) and pass-through to the borrower's rate not verified here.
- Caveat: gain only reaches a plant that is on the ACP and running near capacity; plants not on the ACP, or with offtake/pipeline limits, see none of it.

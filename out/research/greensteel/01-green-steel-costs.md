## 1. Production cost per tonne by route

### 1a. Published cost estimates
- US, H2-DRI-EAF: green steel can be made in the US for about USD 812/t. A greenfield H2-DRI-EAF plant costs about 34% more than a relined BF-BOF; the premium falls to 32% (brownfield) or 24% (greenfield) against a new BF-BOF. 45V hydrogen credits plus other federal incentives could cut the premium to about 5% vs a relined BF-BOF — ICCT working paper, https://theicct.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/ID-634-US-green-steel-working-paper-letter-70177-v4.pdf and https://theicct.org/?p=95166 (Aug 2026) [secondary]
- EU, H2-DRI-EAF opex: Argus put the green-hydrogen DRI-EAF opex cost at EUR 1,074/t at end-December (2024), against a NW EU HRC price of EUR 558.25/t ex-works ("pure green steel costs almost double NW EU HRC price") — https://argusmedia.com/ja/news-and-insights/latest-market-news/2643320-pure-green-steel-costs-almost-double-nw-eu-hrc-price (Jan 2025) [press]
- Derived: Argus's EUR 1,074/t is about 1.9x the HRC price on the same date, a gap of about EUR 516/t. It is an opex index for a notional 100% green-H2 route, not a traded price.
- India, H2-DRI-EAF (modelled, 2030): about USD 562/t, about 5% above new BF-BOF at about USD 536/t. Once coking-coal inflation and rupee depreciation are included, new BF-BOF rises to about USD 588/t, so green steel is about 4% cheaper. Green steel could fall below USD 500/t by 2035 as H2 reaches about USD 2.50/kg (from about USD 3/kg delivered in 2030) — Berkeley IECC, https://iecc.gspp.berkeley.edu/resources/the-economic-case-for-green-steel/ ; https://live-iecc-gspp.pantheon.berkeley.edu/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/IECC-Economic-Case-for-Green-Steel-Production-in-India-Report-May26.pdf ; policy brief https://iecc.gspp.berkeley.edu/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/IECC-Green-Steel-Turning-Point-Policy-Brief-May26.pdf (May 2026) [secondary]
- India, TransitionZero "Green Steel, Hour by Hour": India could decarbonise its EAF and gas-based routes with only about a 3% rise in production cost by pairing round-the-clock carbon-free power with green hydrogen — https://indianpsu.com/decarbonising-india-steel-clean-power-green-hydrogen-transitionzero-report/ ; https://www.prabhatkhabar.com/business/india-world-leader-in-green-steel-decarbonisation-clean-energy-green-hydrogen-transitionzero-report (2025, exact date not shown) [press]
- Hydrogen-price sensitivity (academic): the cheapest green route costs about 50% more than BF-BOF at USD 3.5/kg H2; parity is reached at about USD 4/kg once ore prices and a carbon price are included — Cell Reports Sustainability, https://doaj.org/article/54e225966b044c0cb8f795e965e3fd44 (Jan 2025) [secondary]
- Global Efficiency Intelligence (seven-country levelised cost study): H2-DRI-EAF nears parity with conventional routes below about USD 2/kg H2 by 2030 in many countries. Japan at USD 5/kg H2: extra cost about USD 231/t of steel; the premium disappears at USD 1.3/kg. China: at a zero CO2 price, H2 must fall to about USD 2/kg to compete with gas-DRI-EAF — https://globalefficiencyintel.com/green-steel-economics ; https://forourclimate.org/hubfs/GEI_SFOC_TA%20Green%20Steel%20Economics.pdf (date not shown, c. 2023-24) [secondary]
- Europe, Hydrogen Europe (2022 prices, dated): breakeven needed delivered H2 below EUR 3/kg in the high-energy-price scenario (about EUR 1.50/kg adjusted); breakeven CO2 price EUR 140/t. Converting all EU BF-BOF capacity would need up to 5.3 Mt/yr of green H2 — https://eurometal.net/hydrogen-below-3-kg-needed-for-breakeven-green-steel-in-europe/ (May 2022) [press]
- Gulf vs Europe: gas-fuelled DRI-EAF in the Gulf states expected to be about 30% cheaper than in Europe by 2030; BF-BOF will no longer be the lowest-cost route in any region by 2040 at the latest. BF-BOF in Central Europe uncompetitive after 2040 — PwC via GMK Center, https://gmk.center/en/news/bf-bof-in-central-europe-will-become-uncompetitive-after-2040-pwc/ (19 Aug 2026) [press]
- Wood Mackenzie (global): new technologies and low-carbon feedstocks likely to raise steel production costs by 15-20% — via https://www.theweek.in/news/biz-tech/2025/07/29/will-india-move-to-green-steel-quick-enough-to-escape-impending-carbon-taxes.amp.html (29 Jul 2025) [press]
- EY (India): current green steel premium about USD 210/t, expected to shrink as carbon prices rise; India emission intensity about 2.5 tCO2/t vs an EU benchmark of 1.28 tCO2/t — https://www.ey.com/en_in/newsroom/2025/07/india-s-green-steel-demand-to-surge (Jul 2025) [secondary]
- India press: green steel costs about USD 200/t more than conventional steel; green H2 costs about USD 5/kg and would need about USD 1/kg for H2-steel to be commercially competitive (single press account) — https://theprint.in/economy/csir-immt-led-consortium-to-develop-indias-first-100-pc-hydrogen-dri-pilot-plant/3041509/ (2026) [press]
- Stegra (then H2 Green Steel), company estimate quoted by an economist's blog: production cost premium about 25%, or USD 65/t, over BF steel (the "USD 325/t" base figure in the summary looks like a cost of conversion, not full cost; treat with caution) — https://johnquigginblog.substack.com/p/metallurgical-coal-is-coming-to-the (date not shown) [secondary]

### 1b. Physical inputs: hydrogen and gas per tonne
- Gas-DRI: Suez Steel reported natural-gas consumption below 2.2 Gcal/t DRI (about 9.2 GJ/t) at its ENERGIRON ZR plant in Apr 2026; Midrex reported below 2.40 net Gcal/t DRI (about 10 GJ/t) at ArcelorMittal Hamburg — LinkedIn industry post summarising plant data, https://lu.linkedin.com/in/chedli-h-9182778 (2026) [secondary — low reliability; verify against Midrex/Energiron]
- Gas-DRI flow: Midrex cases move from about 275-300 Nm3 natural gas per t DRI to about 850-900 Nm3 H2 per t DRI (gross, including recycle) at full H2 substitution — same source [secondary]
- H2-DRI net hydrogen: about 60-64 kg H2/t DRI, needing about 3.4-3.6 MWh (12.2-13 GJ) of renewable power per t DRI for electrolysis alone — same source [secondary]
- Pilot plants: 53-67 kg H2 per t DRI depending on ore quality and reduction conditions — https://discoveryalert.com.au/green-hydrogen-steel-production-2026-decarbonisation (2026) [secondary]
- Lhyfe: 54 kg green H2 per tonne of finished steel — https://eurometal.net/?p=26581 (date not shown) [press]
- Derived: the "50-55 kg H2/t" figure is per tonne of steel; per tonne of DRI it is closer to 55-65 kg. Each USD 1/kg change in H2 price moves cost by roughly USD 50-65/t (Derived from the kg/t ranges above; no published sensitivity found).
- Derived cost of gas per tonne of DRI at about 10 GJ/t (1 MWh = 3.6 GJ; 1 MMBtu = 1.055 GJ; gas prices from ../cbam/06): TTF EUR 43/MWh (H1 2026 avg) is about EUR 12/GJ, so about EUR 120/t DRI; TTF EUR 80/MWh (Sep 2026 peak) about EUR 220/t; Henry Hub USD 3.5/MMBtu about USD 33/t; India APM USD 7/MMBtu about USD 66/t. So an EU gas-DRI plant pays about 3-4x a US plant for its gas.
- Derived cost of hydrogen per tonne of steel at 54 kg/t: EUROFER's EUR 7-12/kg (EU renewable H2) gives about EUR 380-650/t of steel for hydrogen alone; India's Feb 2026 refinery tender at USD 3.08/kg gives about USD 166/t; USD 2/kg gives about USD 108/t.
- Scrap-EAF burner gas: one study finds an EAF uses 2.9 GJ of natural gas per tonne of steel; replacing it fully needs 23.5 kg H2/t. Using H2 costs about USD 144/t of steel (at USD 38/MWh wind power) vs USD 58/t with natural gas — via search summary of https://www.mdpi.com/2227-9717/12/9/1829 (2024) [secondary]

## 3. "Clean steel" definitions and labels

- EU Industrial Accelerator Act (IAA), proposed 4 Mar 2026: public procurement for buildings, infrastructure and vehicles must use at least 25% low-carbon steel, from 2029. No "Made in EU" requirement for steel (unlike aluminium and cement), with reporting linking this to the new steel trade regime. No voluntary low-emission steel label; the Commission will rely on a forthcoming ESPR delegated act — https://eurometal.net/2026/03/05/ ("European Commission mandates low-emission steel, drops 'made-in-EU'", 5 Mar 2026) [press]; https://gmk.center/en/news/steelmakers-insist-on-clearer-requirements-for-steel-in-the-industrial-accelerator-law/ (5 Mar 2026) [press]
- IAA status: proposal only, not in force (Oct 2026). EUROFER's 2026 annual report lists it as "Ongoing" ("Proposal creating lead markets for low-carbon products") — ../cbam/07-eurofer-annual-report-2026.txt [primary]
- Reactions: EUROFER wants tighter "Made in Europe" rules so that support does not flow to non-EU producers — https://www.eurofer.eu/press-releases/industrial-accelerator-act-must-prioritise-european-green-steel-industry-warns (2026) [primary-via-summary]; Outokumpu wants an EU low-carbon steel label — https://www.outokumpu.com/en/news/2026/outokumpu-welcomes-the-proposal-for-the-industrial-accelerator-act-3725452 (Mar 2026) [primary-via-summary]; Bellona: "welcome but incomplete step on lead markets" — https://eu.bellona.org/?p=42926 (Mar 2026) [secondary]
- ESPR delegated act on iron and steel: Call for Evidence published 19 May 2026, open to 12 Aug 2026. Steel is the first intermediate product group under the ESPR 2025-2030 working plan. Options: mandatory carbon-footprint, recycled-content and substance information; carbon-footprint performance classes and minimum thresholds; digital product passport — https://public.cdxsystem.com/de/web/cdx/w/espr-delegated-act-on-iron-and-steel-commission-opens-stakeholder-consultation (2026) [secondary]
- ESPR draft classes (JRC method, cradle-to-gate): for HRC, class B spans 1.79-2.66 tCO2e/t while average EU BF-BOF is 1.9 tCO2e/t. Recycling Europe, EEB and FEAD say this is too lenient and want public-procurement eligibility limited to classes A and B — https://eurometal.net/2026/06/15/ ; https://eeb.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/Recycling-Europe-EEB-letter.pdf (15 Jun 2026) [press]/[secondary]
- Bellona (Aug 2026 consultation response): class boundaries should tighten at each review and reflect products on the EU market, not global production — https://network.bellona.org/content/uploads/sites/6/2026/08/ESPR-Steel-Consultation-Response.pdf (Aug 2026) [secondary]
- Derived: if the ESPR class B upper bound stays at 2.66 tCO2e/t for HRC, typical Indian BF-BOF HRC (about 2.5-2.9 tCO2/t, ../cbam/02 section 4) could sit inside class B. This depends on final thresholds and on Scope boundaries, which differ from CBAM (CBAM steel = direct emissions only).
- EUROFER annual report lists a "Proposal introducing low-carbon steel credits" under the cars and vans CO2 regulation as "Ongoing" — ../cbam/07-eurofer-annual-report-2026.txt [primary]
- IEA near-zero (2022): 400 to 50 kgCO2e per tonne of crude steel on a sliding scale by scrap share (400 at 0% scrap, 50 at 100% scrap). ResponsibleSteel uses the same near-zero threshold (its Level 4); LESS, the Global Steel Climate Council and the China Iron and Steel Association have converged on similar approaches — https://www.iea.org/reports/definitions-for-near-zero-and-low-emissions-steel-and-cement-and-underlying-emissions-measurement-methodologies (2022) [primary-via-summary]; Clean Energy Ministerial, https://www.cleanenergyministerial.org/content/uploads/2026/01/definitions-for-cement-and-steel-production.pdf (Jan 2026) [primary-via-summary]
- GSCC's own threshold numbers were not in the summaries (see Gaps).
- India Green Steel Taxonomy (Ministry of Steel, Dec 2024): green steel below 2.2 tCO2e per tonne of finished steel; 5-star below 1.6, 4-star 1.6-2.0, 3-star 2.0-2.2; Scope 1+2 plus limited Scope 3; NISST is the MRV/nodal agency — already in ../cbam/02; reconfirmed by https://www.steelradar.com/en/amns-india-prepares-for-green-steel-taxonomy (2025) [press]
- India green public procurement: still "under consideration" in 2026 as a phased mandate (ET via GMK), covering iron and steel procurement above Rs 1 crore per a leaked draft cabinet note — https://gmk.center/en/news/india-is-considering-introducing-a-requirement-for-green-steel-in-public-procurement/ (5 Mar 2026) [press]; https://www.outlookbusiness.com/economy-and-policy/govt-working-on-modalities-to-mandate-green-steel-public-procurement-official (2025) [press]
- Proposed shares: 20% 3-star, 5% 4-star, 2% 5-star from 2026-27 (BigMint, Nov 2024) vs 20% / 5% / 1% (later leaked draft) — https://bigmint.co/insights/detail/india-govt-adopts-green-steel-definition-to-drive-demand-generation-via-sustained-policy-push-599966 (Nov 2024) [press]; https://www.steelradar.com/en/india-prepares-to-mandate-the-use-of-green-steel-in-public-projects/ (2025) [press]

- No notification of the India mandate or first NISST star ratings was found (see Gaps).
- Berkeley IECC: India's 2.2 tCO2e/t threshold is loose enough that existing lower-emission routes already qualify — https://iecc.gspp.berkeley.edu/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/IECC-Green-Steel-Turning-Point-Policy-Brief-May26.pdf (May 2026) [secondary]
- Derived comparison of thresholds (tCO2e per tonne): IEA/ResponsibleSteel near-zero 0.05-0.40 (crude steel, sliding by scrap) < Fastmarkets "green flat" 0.8 (Scope 1-3) < India 5-star 1.6 < EU average BF-BOF HRC about 1.9 < India "green" cut-off 2.2 (finished steel) < ESPR draft HRC class B ceiling 2.66. So India's 3-star "green" steel would be above the EU BF-BOF average and roughly 3-40x above the IEA near-zero level. Scope and product boundaries differ, so the comparison is indicative only.

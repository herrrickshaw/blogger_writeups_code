PM-MKSSY, the fisheries sub-scheme launched in 2023–24, has ₹6,000 crore allocated to it. As of this piece's publication, live registration data from the scheme's own digital platform show 36.88 lakh fishers, fish farmers and vendors signed up. Parliament's own answers show that of those, 127 have received the scheme's aquaculture-insurance incentive and 21 have actually been paid a performance grant. The parent scheme's infrastructure money moves at a respectable clip — it's the money meant to go straight into individual hands that hasn't.

India Allocated ₹6,000 Crore for Fisheries Under PM-MKSSY. It Has Paid Out ₹4.73 Crore So Far.

Skip to article content
Text Size

Three schemes, one department, very different money

India's fisheries programming runs through three overlapping instruments, all administered by the Department of Fisheries under the Ministry of Fisheries, Animal Husbandry and Dairying. Pradhan Mantri Matsya Sampada Yojana (PMMSY) is the parent scheme, running 2020–21 to 2026–27 at a total outlay of ₹20,750 crore, funding fishing harbours, cold chains, hatcheries and landing centres. The Fisheries and Aquaculture Infrastructure Development Fund (FIDF), running since 2018–19, is a ₹7,522.48 crore concessional-finance window for the same kind of infrastructure. And Pradhan Mantri Matsya Kisan Samridhi Sah-Yojana (PM-MKSSY), a sub-scheme launched in 2023–24 at an estimated outlay of ₹6,000 crore, is different in kind: instead of funding harbours and cold storages, it is meant to put money directly into individual fishers' and fish farmers' hands — an aquaculture-insurance subsidy, credit-linkage incentives, and performance grants for small fisheries enterprises — and it runs on a digital backbone, the National Fisheries Digital Platform (NFDP), launched 11 September 2024.

Parliament has been asked about all three this month. The infrastructure-money answers and the direct-benefit-money answers tell two very different stories.

The infrastructure money: roughly two-thirds released

A Lok Sabha answer on assistance to coastal fishermen (11 August 2026) carries the fullest state-wise breakdown of PMMSY's core infrastructure spending to date, covering approvals from 2020–21 through 2025–26.

₹6,205.78 crore released against ₹9,694.69 crore in approved central share is a 64% disbursement rate — not complete, but a functioning pipeline by the standard this blog has applied elsewhere on this platform (India's manufacturing-subsidy scheme, by comparison, had paid out a far smaller share of what Parliament had approved). Kerala's own state-level figures, reported separately in a Lok Sabha answer on welfare schemes, show a similar pattern at smaller scale: ₹1,413.91 crore approved, ₹598.58 crore central share, ₹462.22 crore released — a 77% release rate against the state's approved central share.

The direct-to-fisher money: registered by the million, paid by the dozen

PM-MKSSY is where the story changes. Two separate Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha answers, both posted 11–12 August 2026, give the scheme's actual beneficiary numbers against its digital-registration count.

Sources: Rajya Sabha, "Status of PM-MKSSY," and Lok Sabha, "Financial Assistance under PMMSY" / "Digital Transformation Strengthens Livestock and Fisheries Sectors," all posted 11–12 August 2026, cross-checked against NFDP's own live public registration counter (nfdp.dof.gov.in), read 30 August 2026. A separate Ministry answer on digital transformation, posted a day after the "Status of PM-MKSSY" answer, put the NFDP registration count at 35.85 lakh rather than 35.92 lakh — a small discrepancy between two Parliament answers from the same Department within 24 hours, not reconciled in either text. The live counter's higher 36.88 lakh figure, 24 days later, is consistent with ordinary registration growth (it shows 3,183 new sign-ups in the 24 hours before this piece checked it) rather than a further discrepancy.

Registering a digital identity on NFDP is a first step, not an entitlement — a fisher isn't owed an insurance payout or a performance grant simply for signing up. But three years into a four-year, ₹6,000 crore scheme, the total confirmed direct-incentive money that has reached individual beneficiaries — ₹40.03 lakh in insurance incentives plus ₹4.33 crore in performance grants — adds up to about ₹4.73 crore. Against a ₹6,000 crore outlay, that is roughly 0.08%.

The ₹28.04 crore in sanctioned bank loans sits in a different category — that's credit facilitated through the scheme's bank tie-ups, not central government money spent, and it's the one line in this table where PM-MKSSY looks like it's actually moving activity: 535 loans is a real number of transactions, even if small next to 36.88 lakh registrations. The cooperative-strengthening line (3,829 cooperatives, ₹72.75 crore) is described in the source answer as "approved," not confirmed as released, so it isn't counted in the ₹4.73 crore direct-incentive total above.

A state-wise table that doesn't add up to the national figure — and NFDP's own live data confirm why

The Rajya Sabha answer on PM-MKSSY's status also carries a state-wise annexure, titled "Support Provided to Small and Micro Fisheries Enterprises," with a national total of 20,09,341. That figure is roughly 56% of the 35.92 lakh national registration count quoted in the same answer's main text — and the per-state numbers in the annexure (2,04,967 in Andhra Pradesh; 2,15,000 in Maharashtra; 2,35,665 in Telangana) are far too large to plausibly be counts of "enterprises" in the ordinary sense of the word.

NFDP's own live public dashboard settles which side of that guess is right. It breaks registrations into two categories: 36,65,661 Individual Registrations and just 21,998 Organisation Registrations nationally — the latter split further into 21,998 tagged Enterprises, 11,392 Cooperatives and 2,604 FFPOs (Fish Farmer Producer Organisations; these sub-categories overlap rather than sum, per the portal's own presentation). Genuine "enterprises," in other words, number in the low tens of thousands nationwide — nowhere close to 20,09,341, and not close in a single state either. The Rajya Sabha annexure's title is confirmed mislabelled: whatever the table's 20,09,341 figure is actually counting, it is not "Small and Micro Fisheries Enterprises" in the sense NFDP itself uses that term. The most likely explanation, unconfirmed, is that the annexure reused a partial individual-registration count under the wrong header.

The EEZ Access Pass: a new entitlement, and a sharp coastline mismatch

A separate, newer instrument sits alongside PMMSY and PM-MKSSY: the Access Pass for fishing in India's Exclusive Economic Zone (EEZ), a free digital pass issued via the ReALCRaft Portal under the Sustainable Harnessing of Fisheries in the EEZ Rules, 2025 (notified 4 November 2025). It's required for mechanised vessels and large motorised boats over 24 metres fishing beyond 12 nautical miles; traditional non-motorised and small artisanal craft are exempt. As of 5 August 2026, a Rajya Sabha answer put the national total at 9,650 passes issued.

Tamil Nadu has one of India's longest coastlines and among its largest marine-fishing workforces, yet has issued just 6 Access Passes — a 400x gap against Karnataka's 2,563, a state with a considerably shorter coast. The source answer does not explain the gap, and this piece has not independently established whether it reflects lower deep-sea fleet size in Tamil Nadu, slower state-level rollout of the application process, or something else; it is reported here as a fact worth further reporting, not a conclusion.

What this piece does not establish. This piece does not know why Tamil Nadu's EEZ Access Pass count is so far below Karnataka's and Gujarat's, and did not attempt to reach the state fisheries departments to ask. It does not reconcile the 35.92 lakh vs. 35.85 lakh NFDP registration discrepancy between two Department answers a day apart — that one is reported as published, flagged, and left open. The 20.09 lakh "enterprises" mismatch is resolved as far as this piece can take it (NFDP's live category breakdown rules out the figure being a genuine enterprise count), but this piece does not know what the 20,09,341 figure actually represents, and did not contact the Department to ask. The ₹4.73 crore direct-incentive figure covers only the two PM-MKSSY components (aquaculture insurance One-Time Incentive and Performance Grant) where the source answers distinguish "approved" from "released" or "paid"; it does not capture whatever share of the ₹72.75 crore cooperative-strengthening approval or the scheme's platform/administrative costs may already have been spent, since the source answers don't break those out. Sources and caveats

Most figures are drawn from written answers given by the Minister of Fisheries, Animal Husbandry and Dairying, Shri Rajiv Ranjan Singh alias Lalan Singh, in the Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha between 11 and 20 August 2026, published via the Press Information Bureau (pib.gov.in): "Allocation for Fisheries Sector," "Financial Assistance under PMMSY," "Assistance to Coastal Fishermen," "Welfare Schemes for Fishermen," "Status of PM-MKSSY," "Salient Feature of PM-MKSSY," "Access Pass for Fishing in India's EEZ," "Digital Transformation Strengthens Livestock and Fisheries Sectors," and "Exports of Value-Added Tilapia Products" (Department of Fisheries press note, 20 August 2026). These were cross-checked, before publication, against two official platforms: the National Fisheries Digital Platform's own live public registration counter (nfdp.dof.gov.in), which supplied the live 36.88 lakh registration figure and the Individual-vs-Organisation registration breakdown used to resolve the "enterprises" mismatch in this piece; and the PMMSY public dashboard (pmmsy.dof.gov.in/dashboard), which was checked and found to be stale — its state-wise sanction data run only through FY2021-22, well short of the FY2025-26 figures the Parliament answers cover, so it was not used as a source for any figure in this piece. All are primary government sources; the parliamentary answers and the NFDP live counter are graded strong, the PMMSY dashboard is explicitly flagged as outdated rather than used. Where sources gave slightly different figures for the same metric (NFDP registrations across two Parliament answers a day apart), both figures are reported and the discrepancy is flagged rather than silently resolved in either direction.

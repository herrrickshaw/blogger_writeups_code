India's policy log, October 2026: ten dated changes with figures and effective dates

Skip to article content
Text Size

Top line

  • Rs 5 crore is the prosecution threshold the GST Council recommended on 9 October 2026, up from Rs 1 crore, with the maximum general penalty cut from Rs 25,000 to Rs 10,000 and arrest powers to be removed from the CGST Act.
  • 5 million dollars is the new limit for entering rupee currency derivative contracts without showing an underlying exposure, down from 100 million, in the RBI measures of 10 October; the daily Cash Reserve Ratio minimum rose from 90% to 99%.
  • 1 January 2027 is when the Bharatiya Vayuyan Niyam, 2026 takes effect, 62 days after its notification on 31 October 2026; it removes the aviation regulator’s power to act on excessive airfares.

Ten dated changes, 6 to 31 October 2026

  1. 6 Oct1. Cabinet sets up the Integrated Transport and Logistics Authority, which will appraise and monitor central transport projects costing above Rs 500 crore and prepare a 10-year national transport master plan.
  2. 9 Oct2. GST Council (57th meeting) recommends a prosecution threshold of Rs 5 crore against Rs 1 crore, a maximum general penalty of Rs 10,000 against Rs 25,000, no show cause notice under Rs 10,000 of tax, and refund acknowledgement in 10 days against 15. The CGST (Amendment) Rules, 2026 followed on 26 October.
  3. 9 to 10 Oct3. RBI raises the daily minimum Cash Reserve Ratio maintenance from 90% to 99% (notification of 9 October) and tightens rupee currency derivatives: the limit without an underlying exposure falls from USD 100 million to USD 5 million, and specified contracts above USD 2 million need a Foreign Exchange Risk Reserve of 20% of the rupee amount (press release of 10 October). A special dollar window for three oil marketing companies opened on 12 October.
  4. 12 to 13 Oct4. Draft Bharat Stage VII emission rules proposed: limits would apply to cars from April 2029, light goods vehicles from April 2030 and larger vehicles from April 2031; comments invited until 11 November.
  5. 14 Oct5. Cabinet continues the National Social Assistance Programme from 2026-27 to 2030-31 with an expected outlay of Rs 53,428 crore, renamed Pradhan Mantri Samajik Sahayata Yojana. SEBI the same day invites comments on SME IPO rules until 4 November.
  6. 26 Oct6. Defence Acquisition Council gives in-principle approval to acquisition proposals worth Rs 2.12 lakh crore, with about 82% to be bought from Indian industry.
  7. 28 Oct7. Cabinet approves the Container Manufacturing Promotion Scheme, Rs 10,000 crore to 2039-40, and the Pradhan Mantri Viksit Gram Swaraj Yojana, Rs 7,396 crore for 2026-27 to 2030-31, and the Khelo India Sports Goods Manufacturing Scheme, Rs 1,857 crore for 2026 to 2034.
  8. 30 Oct8. TRAI issues draft guidelines on responsible use of artificial intelligence in telecom networks; comments invited until 27 November.
  9. 31 Oct9. Ministry of Civil Aviation notifies the Bharatiya Vayuyan Niyam, 2026, in force from 1 January 2027.
  10. 30 Sep, in force Oct10. Tribunal rules under the Tribunals Reforms Act, 2026 apply to 15 tribunals; serving judges and government officers appointed to them must resign or take voluntary retirement from their parent service.

GST Council, 9 October 2026: prosecution threshold up fivefold, penalty cap down 60%

What it means: A recommendation of the Council takes effect only when the Act or the rules are amended, and PRS reports that rules were notified on 26 October 2026 only for the registration and refund changes.

What to do: The Ministry of Finance should publish the date by which each remaining recommendation, including the removal of arrest powers, will be put before Parliament.

What it means: Lowering the disability threshold from 80% to 40% widens who qualifies, and the summary gives no estimate of beneficiaries, so the outlay cannot be turned into a cost for each person.

What to do: The Ministry of Rural Development should publish the expected number of disability pensioners under the 40% benchmark and the central and state shares of the monthly pension.

What would fix this

  1. Ministry of Finance: publish a single table of GST Council recommendations with the legal change each needs and the date it takes effect.
  2. PRS and the ministries: give each scheme summary the full itemisation, so that parts sum to the outlay (the container scheme leaves Rs 100 crore and the sports scheme Rs 622 crore unexplained).
  3. Ministry of Rural Development: state the number of people expected to qualify under the lowered disability benchmark.
  4. Ministry of Road Transport and Highways: publish the cost and fuel-price impact of the proposed Bharat Stage VII limits with the draft.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

Method and definitions
  • The Monthly Policy Review for October 2026 (27 pages, dated 2 November 2026) was read in full as a PDF. PRS summarises official notifications and names their dates in its footnotes; this post did not open those primary notifications, so every item is secondary.
  • Items already published on this blog were left out: the repo rate increase, the IDBI Bank sale, the SME Growth Fund, the draft National Steel Policy, E100 flex-fuel rules and the Mobile Phone Manufacturing Scheme guidelines. Titles of the 523 posts in the archive were checked by keyword.
  • Dates are those PRS gives for the decision or notification; weekdays, window lengths, percentages and averages are computed here.
What this piece could not establish
  • The text of any notification, Cabinet note or Council press release: only PRS’s summaries were read, and PRS footnote links were not opened.
  • The exact date the Tribunals Rules took effect: PRS cites a notification of 30 September 2026 and describes them as notified in the October review.
  • The DAC figure: PRS text says about Rs 2.1 lakh crore and its footnote title says Rs 2.12 lakh crore; Rs 2.12 lakh crore is used and the 82% share is as PRS reports it.
  • The Pradhan Mantri Viksit Gram Swaraj Yojana split: PRS gives Rs 7,396 crore, with a central share of Rs 4,869 crore and a state share of Rs 2,526 crore, which sum to Rs 7,395 crore; the Rs 1 crore difference is rounding in the source.
  • What part of the Rs 10,000 crore container scheme (Rs 100 crore) and the Rs 1,857 crore sports goods scheme (Rs 622 crore after the itemised Rs 1,130 crore and Rs 105 crore) the summaries leave unitemised.
  • Whether any October item took effect after 2 November 2026: the review stops on that date.
  • Other items in the review that are not in this log: an expert committee report on pipeline safety, hydrogen blending guidelines, right-of-way compensation guidelines for transmission lines, Census Rules, Mineral Auction Rules and parliamentary committee reports, which the review also covers.

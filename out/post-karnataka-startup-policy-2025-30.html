Karnataka Startup Policy 2025–30: Rs 518.27 crore for 25,000 startups, Rs 2.07 lakh each

Skip to article content
Text Size

Top line

  • Rs 2.07 lakh is the policy outlay of Rs 518.27 crore divided by the target of 25,000 new startups (Rs 207,308 each, about Rs 41,462 a year); the grant caps per startup are Rs 12 lakh, Rs 50 lakh and Rs 1 crore, so the outlay cannot fund every target startup at a cap.
  • 29.8% of the 2,584 startups registered with the state’s Karnataka Innovation and Technology Society since 2021 are outside Bengaluru Urban, against a policy target of 40% outside Bengaluru; the series ends in March 2026, four months after approval.
  • 24,026 is the number of Karnataka entities recognised as startups by the Union government on 30 June 2026; it counts every startup recognised since 2016, so it is not progress against a target of new startups, and no count of startups created under the policy was found.

What would fix this

  1. Department of Electronics, IT and BT: publish the policy text and the split of the Rs 518.27 crore by measure, stating whether the Rs 500 crore fund of funds is inside it.
  2. Karnataka Startup Cell: define a startup for the 25,000 target, name the registry that counts it, and publish the count at approval and each quarter since.
  3. Karnataka Startup Cell: publish yearly district-wise registrations from April 2026 on the 2021–26 basis, with the share outside Bengaluru against the 40% target.
  4. Karnataka Startup Cell: publish for each Elevate call the winners, grant sanctioned and amount released, and reconcile the cohort counts given in different announcements.
  5. Department of Electronics, IT and BT: state how the Rs 1,000 crore LEAP, the Rs 200 crore and Rs 500 crore funds of funds and the Rs 518.27 crore outlay relate to one another.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

Method and definitions
  • The approval date is the Thursday before YourStory’s report of Friday 7 November 2025; the policy was unveiled on 17 January 2026, 72 days later, according to a department document.
  • The per-startup figure divides Rs 518.27 crore by 25,000 and is checked by an assertion; it is an average of an outlay, not a grant any startup receives.
  • Percentages, ratios and sums are computed here from the figures shown; each is checked by an assertion in the builder, and the district table was checked for row and column sums.
  • A target is a goal for 2030; a registration or recognition count is an outcome of a registry, not of the policy. The 22,600+ and 2,46,000+ figures are printed with a plus sign and are treated as lower bounds.
  • Page text was read for Entrepreneur India, Dataquest, YourStory and NammaWard. The NammaWard article reports a written Legislative Council reply; the reply itself and its annexure were not read. Official pages, the Lok Sabha reply, PIB and Moneycontrol were read as search excerpts. News sites are secondary.
What this piece could not establish
  • The text of the policy, and with it the split of the Rs 518.27 crore by measure and any spending against it. Whether the proposed Rs 500 crore fund of funds is inside the outlay is not stated, and the government told the Council on 20 August 2026 that a Rs 200 crore fund of funds under LEAP had not been set up.
  • How many startups the policy has created since approval. No count by the policy’s own definition was found; the KITS series ends in March 2026 and the central series counts recognitions since 2016.
  • Whether the target of 25,000 new startups counts registrations, recognitions or something else, and whether the 10,000 outside Bengaluru is a cap, a floor or a share.
  • A baseline at approval. Reports give over 18,000, over 14,000 and over 15,000 startups on different bases, and the central count was 22,600+ in March 2026.
  • Why Elevate totals differ (1,250 and 1,084) and why the 2025 cohort is 103 on the Startup Cell page and 143 in a news report.
  • Jobs created by policy beneficiaries. The 7,000-plus estimate covers the 2,584 registered startups and the 2,46,000-plus covers all recognised startups; neither is limited to policy support.
  • Funds actually released: the Rs 1,050 crore of venture commitments of 19 November 2026 are commitments, and grant releases against sanctions were not found.
  • How LEAP’s Rs 1,000 crore relates to the policy outlay, and which cities it covers: the reply lists eight, the Startup Cell page six clusters.

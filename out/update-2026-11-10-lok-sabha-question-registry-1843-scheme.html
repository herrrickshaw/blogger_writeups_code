Use the House filter to see one chamber. The data is unchanged: both Houses run to the end of the 2026 Monsoon Session (12–13 August), and the registry will be rebuilt after the Winter Session.

This registry is the parliamentary companion to the Quarterly Reportage: where the reportage tracks what government announced, this tracks what Parliament asked, in both Houses, and the ministry’s answer of record. Questions are classified by the same scheme-keyword map, so the two views filter identically. Use it to find the answer, then read the answer. The underlying data analysis and build scripts are backed up at github.com/herrrickshaw.

Method. Lok Sabha questions were harvested from sansad.in's Lok Sabha Q&A index by subject keyword (17th Lok Sabha: 2019–2024; 18th: 2024 to date), and Rajya Sabha questions from the rsdoc.nic.in per-question index with the same keyword lists, from 2019. Both were then classified against the same scheme-regex map and acronym-veto list as the Quarterly Reportage — so “Sanchar Mitra” does not file under PM MITRA and IIT (ISM) Dhanbad does not file under the Semiconductor Mission here either. Generated 2026-08-19 by scripts/build_pq_registry.py (Lok Sabha) and scripts/build_rs_pq_registry.py (Rajya Sabha) in digital-twin-for-ipa; the two were combined into one page on 10 November 2026 with no rows added or removed.

Known limits, stated rather than hidden. Both indexes match keywords against the subject line only, so a question whose subject names no scheme is not captured even when its answer discusses one at length — coverage is of scheme-titled questions, not of every mention. Ministries answer as of their date; figures in older answers are superseded by newer ones. A row whose subject has nothing to do with its chip (the coloured scheme tag) is a classification bug, not a parliamentary fact. The two Houses’ counts are not strictly comparable: the Rajya Sabha series starts in January 2019 and the Lok Sabha series in June 2019.

Why UDAN/RCS draws this much scrutiny. At 566 of 3,773 questions — 273 in the Lok Sabha and 293 in the Rajya Sabha, the most in each — UDAN’s lead isn’t really about the scheme’s size in rupee terms; several other schemes in this registry carry larger outlays. It’s about how the scheme is structured. UDAN’s viability-gap funding subsidises specific, named air routes between specific, named airports, so a route delay, a suspension, an airline leaving a route or a new route being announced is a local event with an obvious member to ask about it. A sector-wide capital subsidy or a national tax credit pays out through a formula with no geographic face and generates far fewer individually triggered questions. That logic is often argued for the Lok Sabha, whose members represent constituencies, but the counts here show Rajya Sabha members, who represent states, ask about UDAN just as often.

Why starred vs. unstarred matters. A starred question is one the member has flagged for an oral answer on the floor of the House, which also opens it to supplementary questions from other members in real time — the government has to defend the number, not just table it. An unstarred question gets a written answer laid on the table with no floor debate. Of the 3,773 questions here, 245 were starred (104 of 1,890 in the Lok Sabha, 141 of 1,883 in the Rajya Sabha). Oral slots are scarce, so a scheme that keeps attracting them is, by revealed preference, a live political concern rather than a routine ask.

What this registry is for, on this blog specifically. Like the Quarterly Reportage, this isn’t a standalone reference — it’s the search index this blog’s own articles use to find the parliamentary reply behind a disbursement figure. A scheme’s announced outlay is usually easy to find in a PIB release; how much of it has actually reached beneficiaries is, far more often, buried in a written Lok Sabha or Rajya Sabha answer. Where an article here cites a specific answer date, question number or minister for a disbursement figure, it was very likely found through this same subject-line classification rather than read directly off sansad.in.

What would fix this

  • The Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha Secretariats should make the full text of answers searchable on sansad.in, not only the subject line, so a question whose subject names no scheme but whose answer discusses one at length can still be found.
  • The two Secretariats should publish a bulk, machine-readable download of every question and answer — number, date, ministry, member, subject, starred or unstarred, and answer text — for the 17th and 18th Lok Sabha and the matching Rajya Sabha sessions, so registries like this one need not be built by keyword harvesting.
  • Both Secretariats should tag each question with the government schemes it concerns, using a scheme list supplied by ministries, so classification no longer depends on outside regex maps that must guard against false matches such as “Sanchar Mitra” filing under PM MITRA.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

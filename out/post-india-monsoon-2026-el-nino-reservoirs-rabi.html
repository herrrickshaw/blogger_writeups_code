India's 2026 monsoon: 87% of normal rain, reservoirs low, a very strong El Niño

Skip to article content
Text Size

Top line

  • 87% of the long-period average fell in the June–September 2026 monsoon, 109 mm short; east and northeast India received 74%, its lowest since 1901, and the south 76%, according to the India Meteorological Department (IMD).
  • 68.58% of capacity was held by 178 monitored reservoirs on 15 October, which the Central Water Commission figures put 24.53% below a year earlier and 16.44% below the 10-year average.
  • 2.7 times the buffer norm was the central pool’s 840.8 lakh tonnes of wheat and rice on 1 October, while IMD says very strong El Niño conditions are likely in November and December and November rain below 77% of normal.

What it means: June reached only 65% of normal and August 84%, and 17 of 36 sub-divisions covering 42% of the country’s area were deficient, so the national 87% hides a much drier east and south.

What to do: State governments in the dry regions should publish drought declarations at taluk or block level on one date-stamped list, since the national average cannot tell a farmer whether the year counts as a drought.

What to do: The Central Water Commission should publish reservoir storage by basin against the 10-year average each week through the rabi season, so irrigation releases can be matched to what is held.

What it means: A dry year cut the supply of hydropower as heat raised demand, and coal-based generation rose 29% in October to cover the gap, which links the monsoon to the coal stock position in a single month.

What to do: The Central Electricity Authority should publish a monthly table of the hydro shortfall against the coal and gas generation that replaced it, so the cost of a dry year to the power system can be read directly.

What it means: The central pool can supply rationing and market sales for months at 2.7 times its norm, so the exposure this winter is the rabi crop, vegetable prices and water rather than the grain in store.

What to do: The Agriculture Ministry should release the first advance estimates for kharif 2026-27 with the rainfall and reservoir overlay by district, and add a February–March heat-risk advisory for wheat.

What would fix this

  1. Ministry of Agriculture: release the first advance estimates for kharif 2026-27, which have not yet appeared, and publish them with district rainfall and reservoir data.
  2. Central Water Commission: publish storage by basin against the 10-year average weekly through the rabi season, with the drawdown plan for irrigation.
  3. IMD and the Agriculture Ministry: issue a February–March heat advisory for wheat with dated triggers for irrigation and sowing-time guidance.
  4. State governments: publish drought declarations by taluk or block on one dated list, and the relief tied to each.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

Method and definitions
  • Rainfall shares are IMD’s figures for the four months June to September against the 1971–2020 long-period average; percentages are recomputed from the millimetre values in the IMD tables.
  • The reservoir comparison values (about 90.9% a year earlier and 82.1% on average) are computed from the 68.58% reading and the reported gaps of 24.53% and 16.44%, read as relative differences; if the gaps are in percentage points the values would be 93.1% and 85.0%.
  • The buffer norm is the quarterly minimum the Cabinet Committee on Economic Affairs sets for 1 October; the stock is wheat and rice together.
  • Hydro and demand changes are year-on-year, from CREA for the July–September quarter and from Crisil for October.
What this piece could not establish
  • Crop output: the first advance estimates of kharif 2026-27 had not been released when this was written, so no production figure is given; sowing area (about 1% lower) is not yield.
  • The El Niño record and its probabilities (a +3.11°C daily anomaly on 21 September; an 83% chance above +2.5°C in October–December; 85–98% to March) come from a working paper and a magazine, not from NOAA or INCOIS directly; IMD’s own statement is “very strong El Niño conditions are prevailing”.
  • The reservoir figures are from a magazine report of the 15 October bulletin; the latest CWC bulletin was not read, and a figure of 71.84% on a later date appears in a USDA report without a date.
  • Drought declarations and the 70% share of land under dry or drought conditions come from a working paper citing IIT Gandhinagar’s monitor and state notices, and were not checked against the originals.
  • Effects on rabi yields, wheat and food prices are expectations in the sources, not outcomes; the October food-inflation figure (8.94%) is in this blog’s separate CPI post.

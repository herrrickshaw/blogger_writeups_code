Telangana Life Sciences Policy 2026-30: targets of USD 25 billion and 500,000 jobs

Skip to article content
Text Size

Top line

  • 23 May 2026, a Saturday, is the date reports give for cabinet approval of the Next-Gen Life Sciences Policy 2026-30, 122 days after its unveiling at Davos on 21 January; the USD 25 billion and 500,000 jobs are stated targets, and no result against them was found.
  • 20,000 jobs for each USD 1 billion is what the two targets imply together (computed), or USD 50,000 of investment for each new job.
  • 14.6% to 25.6% a year is the growth a separate target of a USD 250 billion life sciences economy by 2030 implies, depending on whether the base is USD 145 billion (May 2026 report) or USD 80 billion (October 2025 statement).

Cabinet approved the policy on 23 May 2026, 122 days after the Davos unveiling

  1. 21 Jan 2026 (Wednesday)Chief Minister A. Revanth Reddy unveils the Next-Gen Life Sciences Policy 2026-30 at the World Economic Forum meeting in Davos; the state press release says the aim is a top-three global cluster by 2030, while The Hindu and The New Indian Express say top five.
  2. 14 May 2026 (reported 15 May)Telangana Life Sciences Foundation board meeting; officials put the sector at USD 145 billion and investments at more than Rs 84,000 crore over two years (as reported).
  3. 23 May 2026 (Saturday)State cabinet approves the policy, with R&D units recognised as industries; briefing by Minister Ponguleti Srinivas Reddy; the stated aim is one of the top five life sciences hubs by 2030.
  4. 2026 to 2030Policy period. Targets: USD 25 billion of investment and 500,000 new jobs, over five years as reported.

What it means: The policy was announced in January and approved in May, so incentives in the text apply to investments made after the launch, and the date that launch counts from is not stated in the sources read.

What to do: The Industries and Commerce Department should state the date from which investments qualify, since the policy text says only that investments made after the policy launch are eligible.

What to do: The Planning Department should publish one dated definition of the life sciences economy, with the base year and the value, so every target can be measured against the same figure.

What would fix this

  1. Telangana Industries and Commerce Department: state the date from which investments qualify under the policy and publish the expected fiscal cost of the incentive list at the USD 25 billion target.
  2. Telangana Life Sciences Foundation: publish yearly investments grounded and jobs created against the two targets, separate from MoUs signed, with a stated definition of each.
  3. Telangana Planning Department: publish one dated definition of the life sciences economy, with base year and value, to replace the USD 80 billion and USD 145 billion figures.
  4. Telangana Information Technology and Industries departments: state whether the aim is a top-three or a top-five global cluster, and name the ranking used to measure it.
  5. Telangana Life Sciences Foundation: reconcile the three Genome Valley company counts and workforce counts on the state portals into one dated figure.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

Method and definitions
  • Implied growth is (target / base) raised to 1 over the years, in dollars. The five years run from October 2025 for the USD 80 billion base and the four years from May 2026 for the USD 145 billion base; both are assumptions, as the sources do not state the base year of each value.
  • Rupee figures are converted at Rs 8,333 crore per USD billion, which is the rate in the policy’s own pair (Rs 1.2 lakh crore equals USD 14.4 billion); its second pair (Rs 36,893 crore equals USD 4.43 billion) gives a rate within 0.2% of it.
  • The state press release and the policy text were read in full; the policy text was read through its Invest Telangana page, sections 6.5 and 7. News reports were read as search excerpts only and are labelled secondary.
  • Target means a goal stated by the state. Outcome means a figure the state or a report gives as already achieved. Neither has been audited here.
What this piece could not establish
  • Whether the target is a top-three or a top-five cluster: the state press release of 21 January and the policy text say top three, while The Hindu, The New Indian Express and the reports of the 23 May cabinet decision say top five. No ranking method was found.
  • The cabinet resolution itself: the approval date rests on three news reports (BusinessLine, Deccan Chronicle, PTI), and no cabinet note or government order was read.
  • What counts as investment and a job: no source defines whether the Rs 84,000 crore, Rs 63,000 crore or Rs 80,000 crore figures are realised spending or announced commitments, or whether the 500,000 jobs are direct or indirect.
  • The base year and definition of the sector value: USD 80 billion (October 2025) and USD 145 billion (May 2026) differ by USD 65 billion in about seven months with no reconciliation, and the USD 250 billion target for 2030 appears on the Invest Telangana pharma page and in the minister’s October 2025 speech, not in the policy text excerpts read.
  • The 32% export share: the policy text states 32%, but its own rupee and dollar figures give 30.7% and 30.8%; the total of Rs 1.2 lakh crore may be rounded, and which figure is right was not established. Exports after FY2023-24 were not found beyond April to December 2024 (more than Rs 26,000 crore, a nine-month figure).
  • The ranking of Genome Valley: Invest Telangana says it is ranked seventh among global life sciences hubs in CBRE’s Global Life Sciences Atlas 2025, but the CBRE profile read does not show a rank.
  • Genome Valley counts: organisations (200+, 250+, around 500) and workforce (15,000+, 18,000, 20,000) differ across official pages; none is dated.
  • The incentive terms for R&D units beyond their treatment as industrial units, any fiscal cost estimate, and whether the common-list items reproduce T-IDEA exactly; T-IDEA itself was not read.
  • Any outcome since 23 May 2026: no post-approval progress report on the policy was found.

## 1. New article topics, ranked

**A. The RBI's FCNR(B) swap window: about $127 billion of deposits in nine weeks, and a bill that falls due from 2029.**
- **What happened:**
  - The RBI announced it on 5 June and opened it on 8 June 2026. Banks take 3–5 year foreign
    currency deposits from non-resident Indians and swap the dollars to the RBI.
  - On 14 August the RBI brought the closing date forward from 30 September to 31 August.
  - Reported totals at 31 August: $136.4 billion across all channels, of which about $127.2
    billion was FCNR(B) deposits. The 2013 window raised about $26 billion. Reserves hit a
    record. Banks cut FCNR(B) rates from 1 September.
- **Angle:** the window did its job on the rupee, but the deposits are repayable in 2029–31.
  Who carries the exchange-rate risk at maturity, how big the bill is against reserves, and
  what has been published about the swap book.
  - Existing coverage: one row in an exhibit of the securitising-deficit post. No article
    covers the mechanics or the maturity ladder.
- **Data:** RBI weekly statistical supplement (reserves), RBI press releases, the circular
  (reported as FMOD.MAOG.No.S-56/01.06.016/2026-27), PIB 2302879 (25 August).
- **Fix candidates:**
  - Publish the swap book and a maturity ladder.
  - Publish the valuation basis for the RBI's forward position.
  - State the rollover policy.
- **Caution:**
  - That the RBI bears the full hedging cost is in this blog's earlier post and one trade
    source; read it from the circular.
  - The totals differ by source ($127.2 bn, about $133 bn, $136.4 bn). Use the RBI figure.
  - The "net pressure of $3.5 billion a year" is one columnist's estimate.

**B. A ₹2,500 crore areca-nut fraud, and how much of India's trade preference rests on a paper certificate.**
- **What happened:**
  - The DRI said on 16–17 August that areca nuts from Indonesia, Thailand and Malaysia were
    shipped via a Bangladesh export zone and declared Bangladeshi to claim duty-free entry under
    SAFTA (PIB 2300102).
  - Reported figures: revenue loss over ₹2,500 crore, nine arrests, about 160 tonnes seized,
    one customs broker's licence suspended. The duty avoided is 100%.
- **Angle:** the agreements now being brought into force (New Zealand from 20 October, and the
  others this blog has covered, such as EFTA) rely on origin certificates in the same way.
  What checks exist after the shipment clears, and how often are they used? Check each
  agreement's status before naming it.
- **Data:** DRI release, TRADESTAT areca imports from Bangladesh, Customs rules on verifying
  origin (the 2020 rules, to be checked).
- **Fix candidates:**
  - Publish origin-verification statistics each year.
  - Share electronic certificates with the exporting country's authority.
  - Publish the list of high-risk products.
- **Caution:** the figures are from press coverage of the DRI release. Allegations; no
  court finding yet.
- **Coverage:** areca appears only in the cocoa post, in passing.

**C. India's informal economy, district by district, for the first time.**
- **What happened:** the NSO released district-level estimates from the Annual Survey of
  Unincorporated Sector Enterprises 2025, press note of 10 September (PIB 2308690).
- **Findings so far:**
  - 757 districts covered.
  - Top 10 districts hold about a tenth of establishments, workers and value added; the top 50
    nearly a third, in 12 states.
  - Women are at least a third of the workforce in 237 districts.
  - Nationally: 12.81 crore workers (+6.18%, 74.52 lakh added), value added ₹19.92 lakh crore
    (+10.87%).
- **Angle:** which districts carry the informal economy, how it compares with the district
  figures for factories, and where employment grew. Pair with the ASI piece.
- **Data:** the ASUSE district report (needs a download on the Mac; mospi.gov.in is blocked here),
  the MoSPI ASUSE dataset for state-level figures.
- **Fix candidates:**
  - Publish unit-level data.
  - Publish district margins of error.
  - Repeat the district release every year.
- **Caution:** the district estimates carry sampling error, and the district figures include
  their large cities, so city and district totals cannot be added.
- **Coverage:** none.

**D. A new Seeds law to replace the 1966 Act.**
- **What happened:**
  - On 10 September the agriculture minister met about 20 farmer organisations on a draft law
    to replace the Seeds Act 1966 and the Seeds (Control) Order 1983 (PIB 2308922).
  - The draft reportedly raises the maximum fine for illegal seed production to ₹30 lakh,
    against ₹500 now, and adds QR-code traceability and registration of manufacturers and
    distributors.
  - About 18,000 responses came in during consultation.
  - It is not yet introduced.
- **Angle:** what the draft changes for the farmer who saves and swaps seed, how many seed
  samples fail testing today, and whether the penalties are enforceable by state seed
  inspectors.
- **Data:** Parliament answers on seed samples tested and found sub-standard; the draft text.
- **Fix candidates:**
  - Publish state testing capacity.
  - Publish prosecution rates.
  - Publish the draft with a comparison to the 1966 Act.
- **Caution:** the penalty figure is from the draft, and sources name it differently ("Seeds
  Bill 2025", "Seed Act 2026"). Read the draft before drafting.
- **Coverage:** none.

**E. The e-commerce rules that start on 1 January 2027.**
- **What happened:**
  - The Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) (Amendment) Rules, 2026 were notified on 10 September.
    They take effect from 1 January 2027.
  - Platforms must follow the 2023 dark-pattern guidelines, run an annual self-audit and show a
    compliance certificate.
  - "Prior price" means the lowest price in the previous 30 days.
  - Marketplaces must give a buyer the seller's details on request.
- **Angle:** what the rules oblige and what the regulator can check. The National Consumer
  Helpline returned ₹105 crore in 15 months (PIB 2308386). Set that against the platforms' own
  scale.
- **Data:** the Gazette text, CCPA orders, the helpline's complaint data.
- **Fix candidates:**
  - Publish audit certificates in one register.
  - Publish dark-pattern complaints by platform.
  - Report prior-price disputes.
- **Caution:** the text was not seen; the summary is from law-firm commentary.
- **Coverage:** none.

**F. Exporters now have nine months to bring payment home, not fifteen.**
- **What happened:**
  - The RBI's FEMA Export and Import Regulations, 2026 took effect on 1 October.
  - Commentary says the realisation period for general exports is nine months (rupee-invoiced:
    twelve), against fifteen (eighteen) in the January text.
  - Banks can extend on request. Set-off of goods receivables against services payables is
    now allowed.
- **Angle:** MSME exporters on long credit terms, the interaction with rupee invoicing and the
  new FTAs.
- **Caution:** commentary disagrees on whether the change came by an amendment of 22 September
  or one of 5 June. Do not draft before the Gazette text is read (Mac).
- **Coverage:** none.

**G. Money-mule accounts: the Supreme Court's order and the RBI's draft hold procedure.**
- **What happened:**
  - The Supreme Court on 4 August 2026 asked the RBI to set a procedure for temporary debit
    holds on suspected mule accounts.
  - The RBI's draft was issued on 11 September (comments closed 2 October; proposed from
    1 April 2027).
- **Caution:** whether holds are limited to the disputed amount is not confirmed; read the draft
  (rbidocs). Comment window has passed, so the piece would be explanatory only.

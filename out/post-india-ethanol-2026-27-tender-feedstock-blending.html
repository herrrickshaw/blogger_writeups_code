Ethanol 2026-27: 11 billion litres tendered, cane juice and B-heavy molasses excluded

Skip to article content
Text Size

Top line

  • 20% was the average ethanol blend by January 2026, reached in December 2025; the government says no decision has been taken to go beyond 20%, while a US agriculture attache projects 23.4% for 2026.
  • 11 billion litres are sought in the 16 October 2026 tender for 2026-27, which excludes sugarcane juice, sugar syrup and B-heavy molasses and allots 7.2 million tonnes of FCI rice, up to 3.24 billion litres.
  • Rs 73,996 crore was spent on ethanol in 2024-25, up 52% from Rs 48,757 crore; E20 petrol with a minimum RON of 95 became the national standard from 1 April 2026.

What it means: A rise of 4.64 points from 14.60% to 19.24% in 2024-25 was the last big step, and the 20% in 2025-26 is a plateau that the government says it will not leave without scientific and industry consultation.

What to do: The Ministry of Petroleum should publish the monthly blend by oil company and state, so that the plateau can be checked against the 20% claimed.

What it means: Grain and rice now carry the blending target, which moves the ethanol programme closer to food supply, since rice that FCI would otherwise hold is allotted a quarter of the tender.

What to do: The food and petroleum ministries should publish, before each quarter, how much FCI rice and maize the tender will use, so that grain markets and distilleries can plan together.

What it means: A price gap of Rs 13.89 a litre between maize and C-heavy molasses explains why distilleries lean on grain, and it is paid for by the procurement bill of Rs 73,996 crore in 2024-25.

What it means: Installed capacity of about 20 billion litres is nearly twice the 10 to 11 billion litres that E20 needs, so the tender volume is set by policy and not by what distilleries can make.

What to do: The Ministry of Petroleum should publish capacity utilisation by feedstock and the share of the 501 suppliers that supplied nothing in the last supply year.

What would fix this

  1. Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas: publish monthly blend and ethanol bought by company, state and feedstock.
  2. Department of Food and Public Distribution: publish the quarterly FCI rice and maize plan so grain markets can plan.
  3. Oil marketing companies: publish capacity utilisation and the number of registered suppliers that delivered.
  4. Standing Committee on Food, Consumer Affairs and Public Distribution: publish its quality-assurance findings with the test data it received.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

Method and definitions
  • An ethanol supply year (ESY) runs from 1 November to 31 October. Blending is ethanol by volume in petrol sold by public sector oil marketing companies.
  • RON is research octane number; E20 is petrol with 20% ethanol; FCI is the Food Corporation of India.
  • Rice litres a tonne is the tender allotment of 3.24 billion litres divided by 7.2 million tonnes.
What this piece could not establish
  • The quality-assurance findings of the committee: it heard a briefing on 17 November 2026 and has published nothing.
  • The tender result: the 16 October 2026 tender was reported, but offers and awards were not read.
  • A conflict on the blend: the government says 20% in 2025-26 with no decision beyond; USDA projects 23.4% for calendar 2026 and says industry sources call E30 attainable.
  • Price and price series for 2026-27, which the sources do not give.

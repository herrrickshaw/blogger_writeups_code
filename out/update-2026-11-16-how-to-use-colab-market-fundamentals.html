Why it is useful

  • Who benefits: students and researchers who want a table of annual revenue, profit, assets, debt and cash flow for many companies without paying for a data feed.
  • What it saves: building a rate-limited, resumable collector yourself. The notebook already handles Yahoo blocking, retries and checkpoints.
  • Evidence from the run: a probe of three large companies passed, and a live test of the collector on three German companies (SAP, Siemens, Allianz) returned 12 rows with 13 columns in a few seconds.
  • It stores one row per company per fiscal year with the fields needed for a Piotroski-style score, so the output slots straight into a screening notebook.

What it is for

Open collect.ipynb in Colab, press Run all, and download fundamentals.zip. The notebook reads tickers.csv, skips the US by default and the India set is stored with bare symbols, fetches income statement, balance sheet and cash flow for each ticker, and writes one parquet file per market.

The data come from Yahoo Finance through yfinance, so they are third-party and unaudited. Use them for research and teaching. Not investment advice: this is research and education material built from public-source or derived data; it is not a recommendation to buy or sell anything.

Who it is for

  • Students and analysts doing multi-market fundamentals research
  • People who want a reproducible, resumable collection job that runs in the free Colab tier
  • Non-programmers who want an AI assistant to walk through each cell
Not for.
  • Investment decisions. Not investment advice: this is research and education material built from public-source or derived data; it is not a recommendation to buy or sell anything.
  • Point-in-time backtests. The 'filed' date is an assumption (fiscal year end plus 90 days), not the real filing date.
  • Redistributing the collected data. It comes from Yahoo, whose terms apply.

Limits and honest caveats

  • Yahoo data are third-party, unaudited and can change or vanish; terms of service apply. Samples here are shown only to illustrate the table shape.
  • The 'filed' column is an assumption: fiscal year end plus 90 days. It is not the actual filing date, so do not use it for strict point-in-time work.
  • Yahoo blocks many datacentre IPs. The notebook probes first and tells you to restart the runtime if the probe returns zero; whether Colab's IP is blocked on a given day is out of the repo's control.
  • I ran the collector code locally (probe plus three German tickers), not a full Colab run of 30,000 tickers; total runtime and block rate at scale are not measured. The Colab upload/download cells were not run.
  • Banks, insurers and some foreign filers return only part of the 13 columns.
  • Not investment advice: this is research and education material built from public-source or derived data; it is not a recommendation to buy or sell anything.

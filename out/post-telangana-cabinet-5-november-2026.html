Telangana cabinet, 5 November 2026: Rs 4,193 crore for three Kaleshwaram barrages

Skip to article content
Text Size

Top line

  • Rs 4,193 crore is the estimated cost of restoring the Medigadda, Annaram and Sundilla barrages; the Chief Minister set the end of November for final approvals and the end of December to start work, and contractors are to bear repairs of construction defects, in a split no source gives.
  • 7 to 9 December is the Invest Telangana Global Summit 2026, 32 days after the cabinet meeting; the Chief Minister asked for a full plan by 15 November, and reports differ on the investors expected (3,500 or hundreds).
  • 136 posts were sanctioned for six courts (56 for two new courts, 80 for four additional ones) while Telangana’s subordinate courts held 1,011,003 pending cases on 16 July 2026; no report says how many of the posts are judge posts.

What it means: The estimate is the first rupee figure for the work, and reports differ on whether the cabinet approved spending of Rs 4,193 crore or the Chief Minister reviewed proposals estimated at that cost.

What to do: The Irrigation Department should publish the cost of each barrage, the defect-repair share borne by contractors and the share borne by the state, with the date of each approval.

Dates stated for the Kaleshwaram works, 30 June to 31 December 2026

  1. October 2023Medigadda barrage piers sink; Annaram and Sundilla develop leakages (Siasat).
  2. 30 Jun 2026Union Jal Shakti Ministry constitutes a Technical Oversight Committee; it later met on 31 July and 14 October (Irrigation Minister, as reported).
  3. 26 Sep 2026Irrigation Minister: works to start from the second week of November, with designs vetted by the Central Water Commission’s committee on 5 October (The Hindu).
  4. 24 Oct 2026 (Saturday)Minister’s roadmap: estimates and approvals by October-end, agencies mobilised by November-end, work on all fronts by December-end, repairs before the 2027 monsoon, subject to technical clearances (New Indian Express).
  5. 5 Nov 2026Chief Minister reviews the plan and the cabinet meets: final approvals by the end of November, work to begin by the end of December; the start has moved by about 47 days from mid-November (computed).
  6. 30 Nov 2026Target for final approvals (not reached on 21 November, as far as reports show).
  7. 31 Dec 2026Target for starting work. No completion date is given beyond the minister’s aim of the 2027 monsoon.

What would fix this

  1. Telangana Irrigation Department: publish the cost of each barrage, the contractors’ share and the state’s share of the Rs 4,193 crore, and the date of each final approval.
  2. Telangana Industries and Commerce Department: report, after the summit, investors attending and each agreement’s value, sector and timeline, apart from expressions of interest.
  3. Telangana Finance Department: publish the premium, insurer, budget provision and enrolment count for the Indiramma family life insurance, with one enrolment window.
  4. Telangana Law Department: publish how many of the 136 posts are judge posts and when each court is to start sitting.
  5. Telangana Information and Public Relations Department: release the cabinet decisions in one dated note, so that reports no longer differ on whether the cabinet approved or reviewed an item.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

Method and definitions
  • The cabinet meeting is dated 5 November 2026 (a Thursday) and called the 38th by UNI. Items the reports attribute to the Chief Minister’s review meeting of the same day are labelled as such.
  • The UNI report was read only as far as its first paragraph, as the rest is for subscribers. The Siasat report was read in full. Other outlets were read as search excerpts and are labelled secondary.
  • Extensions, shares, per-court averages and per-judge counts are computed here from the figures shown; none is a published figure. Per-court post counts are averages of group totals.
  • Pending cases and judge counts come from parliamentary replies and a High Court report, which carry different dates; the per-judge figure joins two dates and is indicative.
  • Political statements made at the Siddipet meeting and in the debate around Kaleshwaram are left out; the post keeps to dated decisions, figures and deadlines.
What this piece could not establish
  • Whether the cabinet approved spending of Rs 4,193 crore or reviewed proposals estimated at that cost: Siasat says the cabinet cleared the works, while Telangana Today and The Hindu describe proposals at that estimate after the Chief Minister’s review. The New Indian Express headline says the cabinet cleared restoration, and its first paragraph says restoration ‘by the end of December’, while its body says work is to begin by then; the start reading is used.
  • The split of Rs 4,193 crore between contractors and the state, the cost of each barrage, which contractors are involved, and whether they accept the liability: none is stated.
  • Whether final approvals were obtained by 30 November, the date work will start beyond the end-December target, and any completion date; the minister’s 24 October aim of finishing before the 2027 monsoon is subject to technical clearances.
  • The text of the cabinet decisions: no cabinet note was read; the UNI report is paywalled after its first paragraph.
  • The investors expected at the summit: Siasat says 3,500, UNI says ‘hundreds’, and no investment-value target was found. Whether the 15 November summit plan was completed.
  • That UNI’s ‘Fourth City’ and the state’s ‘Bharat Future City’ are the same site: the dates and the cabinet discussion match, but no source says so.
  • Premium, insurer, budget and enrolment of the insurance scheme; the saree distribution start date (19 or 26 November); and the enrolment window (20 to 30 November in cabinet-day reports, 19 November to 18 December in guidelines).
  • How many of the 136 court posts are judge posts, their cost, and the dates the courts start sitting. The 80 posts for four additional courts come from a Telugu report; the English reports read give the 136 total and the 56. The Siasat list of six places and the New Indian Express list of districts differ in naming (Siddipet is absent from the latter).
  • Andhra Jyothy, a Telugu daily, lists further items (886 municipal posts, a Rs 3,000 crore HUDCO loan for roads, a Telangana Road Development Corporation borrowing limit raised from Rs 5,000 crore to Rs 10,000 crore, an amendment to the Bhu Bharati law); no English report read confirms them, so they are not included.
  • Whether disposal of cases will in fact speed up: the minister’s statement is an expectation, and no target or measure was found.

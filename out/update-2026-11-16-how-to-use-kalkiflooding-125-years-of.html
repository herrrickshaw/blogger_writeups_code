Why it is useful

  • Urban flood questions need long rain records; the repo ships city daily series so you can recompute extreme-rain metrics without downloading raw grids.
  • It uses IMD's official intensity categories (heavy 64.5 mm, very heavy 115.6 mm, extremely heavy 204.5 mm), so results map to terms in weather bulletins.
  • A DATA_CAVEATS file documents where gridded data misleads (Pune cell, sea-masked Mumbai and Chennai points); this matters before citing any number.
  • Evidence from the run: city_rain_metrics.py regenerated 1,623 city-years in about 3 seconds and the output CSV was identical to the committed one.

What it is for

Recompute per-city yearly rainfall metrics (annual, monsoon, rainy days, heavy-day counts, wettest day) and charts from the committed daily CSVs.

The repo also holds collector.py and request_access.py for the IUDX flood-sensor network. Those need registered client credentials and provider approval and were not run.

Who it is for

  • Students, journalists and planners exploring long-run heavy-rain trends
  • Analysts who want a documented starting dataset for city flood-risk work
Not for.
  • Operational flood warning or engineering design (conceptual analysis only, not engineering advice)
  • Citing Pune cell trends or absolute city-centre extremes without reading DATA_CAVEATS.md
  • Running the IUDX collector without your own credentials and provider consent

Quick start: commands and what you should see

Rebuild the metrics and two charts

.venv/bin/python city_rain_metrics.py

Prints 'wrote .../city_rain_metrics.csv (1623 city-years)' and 'wrote city_monsoon_trend.png, city_extreme_days.png'. It overwrites those files in the folder, so run it in a copy if you want to keep originals.

Limits and honest caveats

  • Pune's grid cell is unusable for trends (documented artefact); Mumbai and Chennai points are shifted off the sea mask; Delhi extremes are muted by gridding.
  • Early-vs-recent differences are descriptive; no significance testing was done.
  • collector.py, request_access.py and the other analysis scripts were not run.
  • Not for operational flood forecasting or engineering design.

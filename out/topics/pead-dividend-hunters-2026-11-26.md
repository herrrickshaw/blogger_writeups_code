## The argument to test: is PEAD worth anything to a dividend hunter?

A dividend hunter buys liquid, established payers for income and holds. The honest question is whether the earnings-surprise drift adds anything to that. A straight reading of the evidence found so far:

1. **The return case is weak.** Drift is smallest in the large, liquid stocks that income investors hold (Martineau: gone from non-microcap stocks since about 2006), it is a few percent over weeks to months, and the one paper on payers reports no drift difference between payers and non-payers (untabulated). Transaction costs and the tax drag of trading a held position against a buy-and-hold income plan make a return-chasing argument hard to sustain.
2. **The risk case is stronger.** The payout-relevant move is the downside one. The omission drift in Michaely, Thaler and Womack (-11.0% over 12 months) is larger than earnings drift, and Benartzi, Michaely and Thaler found that cuts follow earnings declines that had already happened. So a run of negative earnings surprises is a documented early-warning pattern for a payout problem, and negative drift is where an income investor can lose twice, on price and on the cheque.
3. **Timing of additions is a defensible use.** Adding to a payer after a positive surprise, or waiting out the drift after a negative one, costs nothing against the income plan and uses the one part of PEAD that has survived in the literature. This is a rule of thumb for when to add, not a reason to buy a payer.
4. **Where it may matter more.** The retail-horizon paper and the illiquidity results in this blog's own study suggest drift is larger where trading is thin. For Indian small and mid-cap payers (NSE) the working papers on PEAD and the Indian insider and FII results apply; check whether any of them separates payers.
5. **What it cannot do.** It does not make a high yield safe. Payers' earnings surprises are less persistent (11% to 15% lower response), dividends mostly reflect the past (Benartzi et al.; Grullon et al.), and no paper found tests dividend growers as a group.

Suggested structure for the section in the post: "Does drift help an income investor?" with a three-row table (return case, risk case, timing case), each row with its source, its status (peer-reviewed or working paper) and what it cannot show. Do not present any of this as investment advice. Keep the conclusion as the evidence supports it: PEAD is better used as a payout-risk and entry-timing signal than as a source of return for large-cap dividend holders.

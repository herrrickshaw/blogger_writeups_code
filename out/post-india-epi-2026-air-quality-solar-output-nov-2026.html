Top line

  • 176 of 177 is India's rank in the 2026 Environmental Performance Index, with 175th in air quality; Laos is last and Bangladesh is 175th.
  • 37.1% of Delhi's PM2.5 on 15 November came from biomass burning (stubble, dung cakes, wood), with the AQI at 290; firecrackers contributed 1.6%.
  • 9.6% of India's solar output, about 15 terawatt-hours, was lost to aerosols in 2023; CREA found 261 of 267 cities above the WHO daily guideline in October 2026.

What it means: India ranks 175th on air quality, which measures health burden, but 88th on air pollution, which tracks emission trends, so the index penalises exposure and illness more than emissions.

What to do: The Ministry of Environment should publish the exposure data behind its objection to the index, since it rejected the 2022 ranking as based on unscientific methods and has not supplied a rival measure.

What it means: A 9.6% loss of solar output is about 15 TWh, so the study treats polluted air as a cost to the power system as well as to health.

What to do: The Ministry of New and Renewable Energy should publish the soiling and aerosol loss for its large solar parks each month, so that cleaning costs and output losses can be set against the cost of cleaner air.

What it means: A city can meet the national standard of 60 micrograms and still exceed the WHO guideline of 15 by a factor of four, which is why 38 cities breaching India’s limit understates how many breathe air above the health guideline.

What to do: The Central Pollution Control Board should publish city-wise PM2.5 against both the national standard and the WHO guideline each month on one page.

What would fix this

  1. Commission for Air Quality Management: publish daily source apportionment for Delhi and neighbouring districts on a standing page.
  2. Central Pollution Control Board: publish monthly city PM2.5 against the national standard and the WHO guideline.
  3. Ministry of New and Renewable Energy: publish monthly aerosol and soiling losses for large solar parks.
  4. Ministry of Environment: publish the data behind any objection to the EPI.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

Method and definitions
  • The EPI scores each country from 0 to 100 on 47 indicators in three objectives: environmental health (25% of the score), ecosystem vitality (45%) and climate change (30%).
  • PM2.5 is fine particulate matter under 2.5 micrometres; AQI bands: 201 to 300 poor, 301 to 400 very poor, 401 to 500 severe.
  • Implied solar output is 15 TWh divided by 9.6%; it is not a figure in the study.
What this piece could not establish
  • The 2023 solar output total used in the study; the totals here are back-calculated.
  • How representative 15 November is: the apportionment is a single day at an AQI of 290.
  • The CREA, Dalberg and State of Global Air figures were read through Drishti IAS, not from the reports.
  • Ecosystem vitality and climate-change scores for India; only ranks were read.

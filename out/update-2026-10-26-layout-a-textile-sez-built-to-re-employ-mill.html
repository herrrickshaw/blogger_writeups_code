On 31 August 2026, the Ministry of Commerce and Industry de-notified another 5.6 hectares from the GIDC Apparel Park Special Economic Zone in Khokhra, Ahmedabad. On its own, that is a routine administrative order — the kind of notification that runs a few hundred words and gets no coverage. Read against the SEZ's own history, it is something else: this zone has now shed 87% of the land it started with in 2007, and it was built, quite explicitly, to re-employ the workers a textile mill's closure had put out of work.

India's cotton and garment industry — the sector the GIDC Apparel Park SEZ was created to give former textile-mill workers a place in. Source: सीमा1 (Seema1), Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 4.0.

Land freed by one mill closure, meant to employ its workers

The Apparel Park was proposed on roughly 38 hectares that had belonged to the erstwhile Ambika Mills, a textile mill in Ahmedabad. The project was conceived specifically to give displaced mill workers somewhere to go: a garment-manufacturing SEZ replacing the industry that had just failed on the same ground. It did not move quickly. The scheme drew a legal dispute over the land in the Gujarat High Court and separate delays from the Finance Ministry and the Environment Ministry, and the zone did not become operational until 2013 — six years after its 2007 notification. Once running, its units were 100% export-oriented, manufacturing garments (jeans, menswear, womenswear, kidswear, hosiery) for outbound sale, the standard SEZ model.

Both de-notifications came at the request of the Gujarat Industrial Development Corporation (GIDC), the state body that developed the zone, with the state government's approval and a recommendation from the Development Commissioner of the Kandla SEZ. The stated justification in the notification itself is that the released land "would be utilized towards the creation of infrastructure which would sub-serve the objective of the SEZ as originally envisaged" — language that reads, on a zone that has lost seven-eighths of its land, less like expansion planning and more like a formal sign-off on contraction that had already happened on the ground.

Not an isolated case

SEZ de-notification is a recognisable pattern nationally, not a one-off. The income-tax exemption that made SEZ status valuable to developers and unit-holders carried a sunset clause: developer benefits under Section 10AA lapsed from April 2017, and unit-holder benefits from April 2020 (extended to September 2020 because of the pandemic). A wave of de-notifications has followed in the years since, across sectors and states — a single recent batch of notifications, unrelated to this one, de-notified 22.767 hectares combined across an IT SEZ in Greater Noida, a solar-panel SEZ in Kerala, and an IT/ITES SEZ in Telangana.

An older Comptroller and Auditor General review of a sample of 50 approved SEZs, published in 2014 and describing that period rather than today, found only 15 operational, 29 yet to start any commercial activity, and 6 already de-notified — a snapshot from over a decade ago, but the direction of travel it describes has not obviously reversed.

What this post does not establish. This is one zone's land-area history, sourced to its own de-notification notifications, plus national context on why SEZ shrinkage is a known pattern. It is not a claim about the Apparel Park's current employment, export revenue, or occupancy rate — none of which appear in the notifications themselves, and none of which were independently verified for this post.

The net read

A zone conceived to replace one failed textile enterprise with another, on the same ground, is now itself down to a fraction of its founding footprint — and the mechanism doing the shrinking is not a dramatic closure but two quiet, procedurally unremarkable notifications, three years apart, each one smaller than the last de-notification that preceded it. That is usually what SEZ contraction actually looks like: not a single failure announced at once, but a series of routine paperwork entries that only add up to something notable when read together.

What would fix this

  • The Department of Commerce's SEZ Division should publish a register of every SEZ's area history — original notification, each de-notification with its S.O. number, and resultant area, so an 87% contraction like Khokhra's is visible without reconstructing it from gazette OCR.
  • The SEZ Board of Approval should require every de-notification request to state the zone's current operating units, employment and exports, and publish these with its minutes, since the 2024 and 2026 notifications for the Apparel Park say nothing about any of them.
  • The Gujarat Industrial Development Corporation should publish what the 17.6 hectares and 5.6 hectares released from the GIDC Apparel Park have been used for, and by whom, so the claim that the land 'would sub-serve the objective of the SEZ as originally envisaged' can be checked.
  • The Development Commissioner of the Kandla SEZ should report how many former Ambika Mills workers the Apparel Park has employed since it became operational in 2013, so the zone is measured against the re-employment purpose it was created for, not just its land area.
  • The CAG should repeat its 2014 sample review of SEZs, reporting how many are operational, yet to start, or de-notified, so the decade-old snapshot of 15 operational out of 50 is replaced by a current one after the income-tax sunset clauses lapsed.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

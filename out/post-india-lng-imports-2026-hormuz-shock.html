India's LNG imports in 2026: up over 10% to September, Qatar's supply down 72%

Skip to article content
Text Size

Top line

  • 72% fall in Qatar’s LNG deliveries to India in January–September 2026, according to the IEA. Total imports still rose by over 10%, or 2.6 bcm, as supply from North America and Africa doubled.
  • $8.5 billion was the LNG import bill for April–September 2026, 23% higher for about 4.4% more gas (PPAC, provisional). That is about $11.8/MMBtu, against an Asian spot benchmark averaging about $20 in March–September.
  • 51% less gas reached petrochemical plants in March 2026 than a year earlier, and 25% less reached fertiliser plants. City gas use rose 11.5% under the government’s priority order.

Before the war that began on 28 February 2026, about 60% of India’s LNG came through the Strait of Hormuz, mainly from Qatar, on Kpler data printed by the Indian Express. This piece sets out what happened next, using PPAC reports, the IEA’s November review, company results and ship-tracking data printed in the press.

What it means: The shortfall lasted two months, and from April to September the extra LNG (775 MMSCM on PPAC’s provisional count) almost exactly matched a 779 MMSCM fall in domestic gas output, while total gas use stayed flat.

What to do: Gas buyers and planners should treat March as the stress case, about 19% below the 2025 monthly average, and size storage or swap arrangements to cover two months of it.

What it means: India replaced a supplier that depends on the strait with three that do not, and Kpler estimates that portfolio deliveries rose about 161% and spot purchases 35% to do it.

What it means: Most LNG still arrived on contracts linked to oil or to the US gas price, so the average import value rose about 18% while the Asian spot benchmark rose about 67%, and the spot price applied to the third or more of supply bought at short notice.

What to do: Importers should hold enough oil-indexed and Henry Hub-linked contracts to cover priority demand, and PPAC should publish the term and spot split each month.

Terminal use: Dahej handled 216 TBtu in July–September 2026, at 73% of capacity

Petronet’s Dahej terminal processed 216 trillion Btu in July–September 2026, against 211 a year earlier. Utilisation reads 73% against 92% because capacity grew from 17.5 to 22.5 million tonnes a year in March 2026.

The cargo mix changed. Brokerage summaries of the results put long-term volume at 21 TBtu, down from 95, and third-party cargoes at 188 TBtu, up from 116. About 6 of the 75–80 Qatari cargoes due in March–October arrived.

Kochi ran at 23.7%, against 26.9% a year earlier. PPAC’s terminal-by-terminal table for April–September 2026 could not be read for this piece, so the other six terminals are not covered here.

What it means: The March order kept household and vehicle gas whole and fertiliser at 70% of past use, so over March–September power, refineries, petrochemicals and other users took 3,031 MMSCM less gas than a year earlier while city gas took 2,062 more.

What to do: The petroleum ministry should keep the priority schedule as a standing plan with published triggers, so that plants able to switch fuel know they are first in line for cuts.

Outlook: the IEA expects India’s gas demand to end 2026 about 2% lower

The IEA puts India’s gas demand 3% lower in January–September and expects about 2% lower for the year. Domestic output fell for a 27th month in a row, which is why imports rose while demand did not.

An ICIS webinar deck of early November 2026, a single secondary source, expects South Asian LNG imports of 8.2 Mt in October–December, 18% lower than a year earlier, and India’s spot buying to strengthen below $12/MMBtu. Kpler’s October count for India points the other way so far.

What would fix this

  1. PPAC should publish LNG imports each month by source country and by contract type (term, portfolio, spot), and mark prorated months in every table.
  2. The Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas should keep the March 2026 priority schedule as a standing contingency plan, with published triggers for switching it on and off.
  3. Petronet LNG, GAIL and other importers should write origin flexibility and delivery-at-port terms into contract renewals, so a closed route does not stop supply.
  4. PNGRB should require every LNG terminal to publish monthly throughput split into long-term, third-party and spot cargoes.
  5. The Department of Fertilizers should publish each month the gas cost passed into the urea subsidy, so that the fiscal cost of spot LNG is visible.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

Method and units
  • Average import value. PPAC’s import bill divided by its import volume, using PPAC’s own 2025-26 conversion of 34,427 MMSCM to 1,366,138,186 MMBtu. The same division gives PPAC’s printed $9.8/MMBtu for 2025-26. The bill is printed to one decimal of a billion dollars, so the result is approximate.
  • Derived months. April, August, the June–July average and the 2025 monthly average are computed from percentage changes in the Indian Express reports of Kpler data, and are marked “derived”.
  • Sector figures. PPAC monthly reports, provisional. May 2026 uses the revised figures in the June report. March–September sums and changes are this blog’s arithmetic.
  • Units. Mt is million tonnes of LNG; PPAC converts 1 Mt to 1,325 MMSCM (million standard cubic metres). bcm is billion cubic metres. TBtu is trillion British thermal units.
  • Global frame. IEEJ counts world LNG imports at 303 Mt in January–September 2026, 1.8% lower, with Qatar’s exports at 22 Mt against 60 Mt.
What this piece could not establish
  • PPAC’s month-by-month LNG import table. The workbook on PPAC’s LNG import page could not be opened. Monthly volumes here are Kpler figures printed in the press.
  • An official country-wise series. None was found. Supplier shares rest on Kpler data printed by the Economic Times and the Indian Express.
  • The term and spot split. “More than a third” spot is attributed by the Economic Times to industry executives; an earlier report said 35–40%. No official split exists.
  • Final April–September figures. PPAC’s August and September 2026 imports are prorated from the last 12 months, not counted, so the $8.5 billion bill and the $11.8 average are provisional and will change when customs data arrive.
  • Terminals other than Dahej and Kochi. PPAC’s utilisation table for April–September 2026 could not be read.
  • The subsidy cost. No government estimate of the extra urea subsidy caused by costlier LNG was found.

Where sources disagree. PPAC’s prorated September import figure is 2,957 MMSCM, 8.5% lower than a year earlier; Kpler’s ship count shows September 7% higher. For September, Kpler via the Indian Express gives 2.24 Mt and the Times of India 2.1 Mt. For April–September PPAC shows +4.4%; Kpler shows +9% for March–September and the IEA over +10% for January–September.

City gas growth also differs by period and source: +22% in PPAC’s March–September sector tables, +16% in the IEA’s January–September text. Qatar’s 2025 monthly average is printed as 0.95 Mt and as 1 Mt in different Indian Express reports.

Top line

  • 91% of FY2026 electric registrations were two- or three-wheelers (2,232,481 of 2,451,719, Autocar Pro from Vahan); EVreporter’s April–September 2026 count gives 88.7% as cars gain.
  • 9.3× is how much higher e-3W penetration (60.9%) is than e-2W (6.5%), yet e-2Ws outsell e-3Ws 1.69 to 1 because 15.7 two-wheelers are sold for each three-wheeler.
  • 52% is the estimated share of FY2026 registered battery energy carried by two- and three-wheelers (19.3 GWh in all), against 90.5% of units; an estimate, with a range of 37% to 67% across pack-size scenarios.

This piece is about the mix of electric vehicles and why, and does not repeat the volumes and subsidy history in India’s EV sales: 12% of vehicles in April–September, cars in single digits. The CEEW-GFC dashboard (National Volume Monitor) was read through four screenshots supplied by the blog’s author on 27 November 2026, and the page itself and the Vahan dashboard could not be opened here (see the box at the end), so the monthly figures are from secondary reports of Vahan data and are labelled as such. Every conflict between sources is shown, not blended.

For scale: the 19.3 GWh is energy registered in one year, a flow, while the blog’s grid battery storage piece counts about 9.3 GWh installed in total by mid-2026, a stock; the two are different measures and the comparison is only an order of magnitude. About 47% of the estimated energy sits in cars and buses, which is where large cells are used.

What it means: Two- and three-wheelers are 90.5% of FY2026 units but an estimated 51.5% of battery energy, and the estimate stays between 36.5% and 67.0% across five pack-size scenarios, always at least 23 points below the unit share.

What to do: Cell and storage planners should size demand from kWh by segment and not from unit counts, and the Ministry of Road Transport should publish registered battery kWh by class so that the estimate becomes a count.

What to do: Fleet buyers should compare total cost at their own daily distance with the inputs in the box, and agencies should publish a three-wheeler cost sheet, because the blog’s own cost model has none.

What would fix this

  1. Ministry of Road Transport and Highways (Vahan): publish one monthly table of registrations by vehicle class, fuel, state and RTO, with the class definitions (L3, L5M, L5N, low-speed, goods, passenger) and a revision log, so that a month’s figure can be cited with its vintage.
  2. Ministry of Heavy Industries: report PM E-DRIVE claims by segment, state and battery kWh, so that registered battery energy by class can be counted instead of estimated.
  3. Vahan and the state transport departments: add Telangana to the national dashboard, or publish its monthly class counts in the same format, because the two largest secondary series differ by about 5% in e-2W units with one including Telangana and the other apparently not.
  4. FADA, SIAM and the trackers: state in each release whether e-rickshaws and low-speed e-2Ws are inside the three-wheeler and two-wheeler counts and penetration, so that 33.7% and 60.9% are never both called e-3W penetration.
  5. CEEW-GFC: offer the dashboard’s state and RTO tables as a downloadable file with a citation date, so that state concentration by segment can be checked without a browser.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

What this piece does and doesn’t cover

It covers the mix of electric registrations, not the number of vehicles on the road, and not electric cars or buses beyond their effect on the mix. It treats Vahan registrations as a proxy for sales: a registration lags a sale, some states report late, Telangana is handled differently by different trackers, and low-speed e-2Ws are in or out depending on the source. Source definitions drift: ‘e-3W penetration’ is 33.7% or 60.9% depending on whether e-rickshaws count. The October 2026 total of 354,284 is a first report and will rise; the 2.34 lakh figure that circulated with it describes October 2025. Battery energy is an estimate built on assumed pack sizes.

Method and definitions
  • One source per series. FY2022 to FY2025 shares use the CEEW brief (Vahan, Telangana excluded, 1,378 RTOs); FY2026 shares use Autocar Pro’s Vahan pull of 1 to 6 April 2026; April to October 2026 uses EVreporter’s monthly first reports (Vahan, low-speed e-2W excluded, Telangana excluded in the April report only). Autocar Pro’s FY2025 figures match CEEW’s to 0.01% for e-2W and 0.02% for e-3W, which is why those two can be read as one definition; EVreporter’s FY2026 figures are 4.0% higher in total and 5.0% higher for e-2W and say they include the Telangana transport portal.
  • Segments. E-rickshaw is EVreporter’s term for a low-speed (up to 25 km/h) electric three-wheeler carrying passengers, and e-cart for one carrying goods; L5M is the passenger and L5N the cargo three-wheeler; CEEW calls the low-speed passenger class ‘E-rickshaw’ and the rest ‘E3W’. Autocar Pro’s e-3W includes all of them. How Vahan codes these classes was not opened; the labels are the trackers’.
  • Registrations are not sales. Every figure is a Vahan registration, used as a proxy for retail sales; late registrations raised September 2026 by 1.3% in a month, so the latest month is always understated.
  • Derived counts. L5 passenger FY2026 (239,781) is 9.4% of 2,550,865 and carries the rounding of that percentage; the L5 cargo count (32,112) is the residual after every other count and includes a few hundred forklift-type vehicles; the e-bus count (5,039) comes from Autocar Pro.
  • Energy. Units times assumed typical pack kWh. Pack sizes come from manufacturer pages and guides for models on sale, not sales-weighted, so the mid values are assumptions. The estimate counts energy registered, not energy delivered, and counts lead-acid e-rickshaws at lithium-equivalent kWh.
  • Cost per km. Charging or fuel cost only; the blog’s model prices are July 2026 and Delhi-based; the vehicle efficiencies are WRI India’s 2020 inputs.
What this piece could not establish
  • The CEEW-GFC dashboard itself: only four screenshots were seen (supplied 27 November 2026), so state volumes, penetration lines and the unlabelled registration points are read by eye, and the top five states for e-2W and for e-3W by units remain unestablished. The dashboard pages (www.ceew.in, three addresses tried) returned a Cloudflare challenge and were not bypassed; only the CEEW issue brief PDF could be read. The Vahan dashboard did not load. State figures here come from the brief’s Table 1 (ten states, all EVs, FY2025) and an EVreporter page on e-2W (January to May 2026).
  • Any RTO-level ranking. EVreporter’s city and RTO tables are for data-portal subscribers; its FY2026 page names only Bengaluru (98,892 e-2Ws), Delhi (41,238), Lucknow (14,099 L5 passenger) and Kanpur (7,513).
  • FY2026 L5 passenger, L5 cargo and e-bus counts from one source. EVreporter’s public excerpt gives a 9.4% share for L5 passenger and no cargo or bus count.
  • Whether the 70,366-unit FY2026 e-2W gap between EVreporter and Autocar Pro is Telangana, late registrations or the low-speed classes. The EVreporter note names the Telangana transport portal; Autocar Pro’s inclusion of Telangana was not stated.
  • The FY2022 and FY2023 e-3W L5 counts (not labelled on CEEW’s chart), so those two shares are lower bounds.
  • Mercom’s January to March 2026 figures (696,769 EVs, 9% of vehicles; 427,773 e-2Ws, about 61%): the page was refused by the network and the figures are from a search excerpt only. They are not used in any computed figure here. January to March is also a different quarter from EVreporter’s April to June fiscal quarter of 834,222 EVs.
  • JMK Research’s reports (H1 FY2027 at 17.89 lakh, the FY2026 report card, the cargo three-wheeler white paper), NewsBytes (403) and CEEW’s cost-of-ownership PDF were outside the allowed network or blocked; they appear only as labelled excerpts, and the JMK total is used once, as a 0.8% cross-check.
  • S.O. 4424(E) of 10 August 2026 itself. PIB’s release of 1 October 2026 confirms its terms; the Gazette text was read for the blog’s August piece and not re-read. A July 2026 parliamentary answer found in search gives 31 July 2026 for the e-2W end date; it predates the 10 August notification.
  • Real-world electricity use and charging price for three-wheelers, and the lead-acid share of e-rickshaws.
  • Whether the 11.6% September 2026 e-2W penetration the August piece attributes to FADA appears on a FADA page; the only copy opened is EVreporter’s, with Nuvama and Angel One giving 11.4% and 11.5%.

Doing Business in India: a committee seeks audited reform claims and one PAN-based ID

Top line

  • 58 numbered observations and recommendations are listed in the press release for the 201st Report of the Rajya Sabha Standing Committee on Commerce (chair Dola Sen, presented 7 August 2026), after 4 meetings totalling 8 hours 11 minutes.
  • 47,123 compliances are reported as simplified, trimmed, digitised or decriminalised (computed sum of four counts; the press release says ‘over 47,000’), while one stakeholder told the committee a typical firm faces 69,233; the two are different measures.
  • 2 asks on state reforms: an audit by third-party sampling and industry interviews instead of state-submitted paperwork (para 4.4), and a single PAN-based Business ID across state-level clearances (para 4.5).

What to do: DPIIT should publish the compliance count on one base, central and state, so that reformed compliances can be set against the total that a firm faces.

The committee asks for third-party checks of state reform claims and one PAN-based ID

  1. Para 4.3A new edition of the Business Reform Action Plan (BRAP) with more weight on outcome-based indicators, on-ground implementation and the reduction in time and cost of compliance; best practices of leading states to be documented and shared.
  2. Para 4.4A mandatory Ground Reality Verification audit in the BRAP evaluation, using third-party random sampling and direct industry interviews instead of relying strictly on state-submitted paperwork.
  3. Para 4.5A single, PAN-based Business ID adopted as standard across all state-level clearances, ending re-verification of identity across local departments (as the press release words it).
  4. Paras 4.6 and 4.7Future BRAP metrics to reward states that close the gap between industrial hubs and rural areas, with capacity-building for lagging regions.

What it means: Both asks concern how states are scored and how a business is identified to them, and neither is yet a rule, since the committee recommends and the Department decides.

What to do: DPIIT should state whether the next BRAP edition will include the audit and the PAN-based ID, with the sampling method and the date from which they apply.

What to do: The Telangana Industries Department should add the processing time for each application to the TG-iPASS dashboard, which is the measure the committee asks the National Single Window System to publish.

What it means: Most of the report’s weight is on how long approvals take and who verifies reform claims, with sector items such as dark stores and logistics a smaller share.

What to do: Readers should check the full report for the evidence behind each item, because the press release lists recommendations and not the data the committee relied on.

What the committee examined
  • Meetings: four meetings totalling 8 hours 11 minutes (491 minutes, an average of about 123 minutes each, computed); the draft report was adopted on 5 August 2026 and presented on 7 August 2026 (PIB press release).
  • Witnesses named in the press release: the Department for Promotion of Industry and Internal Trade and the Department of Commerce, Government e-Marketplace, and the ministries of Ports, Shipping and Waterways; Electronics and Information Technology; Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises; Environment, Forest and Climate Change; Road Transport and Highways; Railways (Railway Board); and Civil Aviation, with CII, FICCI, FIEO, banks and financial institutions and other stakeholders.
  • Visit: the press release has a section on a committee visit to Jammu and Kashmir and Ladakh (items 53 to 56); its dates and findings were not read.
  • Data cited: the 47,123 reformed compliances (DPIIT) and a stakeholder’s count of 69,233 compliances; PRS says the India assessment under the World Bank’s third-cycle B-READY is due around September 2026. No state-level scores, survey or time-and-cost data appear in the press release or the PRS summary.

What would fix this

  1. Department for Promotion of Industry and Internal Trade: publish the sampling method, sample size and findings of any Ground Reality Verification audit, state by state, alongside the BRAP score it adjusts.
  2. Department for Promotion of Industry and Internal Trade: state which regulators and which state clearances a PAN-based Business ID would cover, since the sources describe state-level clearances only.
  3. Rajya Sabha Secretariat: publish the report’s evidence chapters and annexures with the press release, so that the 47,123 and 69,233 counts can be traced to their sources.
  4. The Karnataka and Telangana governments: publish processing time, not only counts of applications processed, on the Sakala and TG-iPASS portals.
  5. National Single Window System team: publish the list of states and central ministries onboarded, with dates, so that the committee’s call for mandatory onboarding can be measured.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

Method and definitions
  • The PIB press release of 7 August 2026 (Rajya Sabha Secretariat) lists all 58 observations and recommendations with paragraph numbers; it was read in full and is the source for every item quoted here. The PRS summary was read in full. The report itself was not read.
  • The committee is a Department Related Parliamentary Standing Committee of the Rajya Sabha, so the full report is hosted by the Rajya Sabha rather than on the Lok Sabha committee pages; the press release says it is under sansad.in/rs, Committees, DRPSC-RS, Commerce, Report.
  • Counts are summed or grouped here as stated beside each chart; the grouping into themes is this blog’s.
  • The Karnataka and Telangana figures are those of this blog’s service-delivery posts, which read the Sakaala Services Act (PRS compilation), the TS-iPASS Act 2014 and the two state portals; they were not re-read for this piece.
What this piece could not establish
  • The full report: its host was blocked for the research environment, so the committee’s evidence chapters, state-wise data, annexures and the reasoning behind each item were not read; everything here rests on the press release and the PRS summary.
  • The scope of the PAN-based ID: the press release and PRS say state-level (state) clearances; the brief for this piece said ‘across regulators’, which neither source supports. Whether it would also cover central regulators is not stated.
  • Whether the audit is a new exercise or a change to the existing BRAP process: the sources do not say how BRAP evidence is collected today beyond ‘State-submitted paperwork’.
  • Whether the four reform counts overlap: the sum of 47,123 is consistent with the press release’s ‘over 47,000’ only if they do not, and the sources do not say.
  • The stakeholder’s figures (1,500 Acts, 69,233 compliances, 6,618 filings): who gave them and how they were counted are not in the summary.
  • The result of the World Bank’s third-cycle B-READY assessment: PRS expected it around September 2026, and a search on 25 November 2026 found no published result for India.
  • Whether the Government has accepted any recommendation: no action-taken report was found.
  • Whether Karnataka or Telangana is onboarded to the National Single Window System, or uses a PAN-based business ID: no source was found for either; the comparison is limited to the time limits, remedies and dashboards the two posts document.
  • Conflicts between sources: PRS lists ‘compensation for delayed government refunds and subsidies’ (para 3.31) as a time-limit remedy; the Karnataka compensation of Rs 20 a day applies to citizen services, a different thing, so the two are compared only as remedies and not as like for like.

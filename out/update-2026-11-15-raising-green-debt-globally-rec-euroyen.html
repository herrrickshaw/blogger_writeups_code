On 28 November 2025 the Climate Bonds Standard Board approved post-issuance certification of REC Limited's EuroYen Green Bonds — the letter, signed by CBI chief Sean Kidney, sits in front of us. That one page is the end of a chain that starts with a ¥61.1 billion bond, runs through a verifier's audit of where every yen went — solar, wind, biogas, pumped storage, electric buses — and ends with access to a class of global investors that plain-vanilla debt never reaches. This piece uses the REC deal, the CBI standard, SBI's framework and India's own sovereign programme to map every avenue an Indian issuer has for raising sustainable debt globally, and what the green label is actually worth.

What it means: Covered interest parity means the hedge costs about what the cheap coupon saves, which is why hedged yen landed below 7% rather than near 3%.

What to do: Harvest the gap only where an issuer can leave part of it unhedged against matching foreign-currency cash flows, or where hedging is subsidised or partly waived.

Sovereign greenium: 6bp in April 2026 after the RBI rejected every bid in June 2025

  1. Jan 2023India's sovereign debut priced 5-6bp inside conventional G-secs
  2. Nov 2024₹1,502 cr accepted of ₹5,000 cr offered
  3. Jun 2025The RBI rejected every bid at a 30-year green auction
  4. Apr 2026The 7.50% GOI SGrB 2056 sold at a 6bp greenium, the largest since January 2023
  5. Aug 2026Reopened with ₹5,000 crore notified for 14 August; greenium reported at about 4bp, not checked against the RBI result
  6. Oct 2026Reopened again with ₹3,000 crore notified for 1 October

What it means: The sovereign programme now shows that the label pays when supply is kept short, with ₹15,000 crore of 30-year green tranches of ₹3,000 crore in the October 2026 to March 2027 calendar.

What to do: When the label stops paying, the correct response is issuance discipline rather than label inflation.

What it means: The economies with the cheapest funding are rated well above India, so their institutional investors have room to hold BBB- rated foreign paper.

What to do: A single-notch downgrade at Fitch or Moody's would push REC, PFC and EXIM out of many minimum-rating mandates, so debt trajectory is the metric to watch.

What would fix this

  • The Department of Economic Affairs should publish, after each sovereign green bond auction, the cut-off yield against the matching conventional G-sec and the amount devolved or rejected, so that the shrinking greenium behind outcomes like ₹1,502 crore accepted of ₹5,000 crore in November 2024 is on the record.
  • IFSCA should maintain a public register of every green, social and sustainability bond listed at GIFT City, with issuer, currency, label, external reviewer and links to allocation and impact reports, so that the roughly $14 billion of green issuance under IFSC rules can be checked bond by bond.
  • SEBI and IFSCA should require each green-bond issuer to publish one status table per bond stating which standards apply — ICMA principles, Climate Bonds certification, EuGB — and the date each pre- and post-issuance review was completed, the mismatch this post had to flag for REC’s yen bonds.
  • The RBI should add a green or sustainability flag, the currency, and whether the exposure is hedged to its monthly external commercial borrowing data, so that the shift into hedged yen loans by IREDA, PFC and NLC, and its scale after the February 2026 ECB revision, can be tracked from official statistics.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

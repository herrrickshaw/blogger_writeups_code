Oil PSU R&D: Rs 2,909 crore in FY2025-26, 2.16% of profit after tax at seven majors

Skip to article content
Text Size

Top line

  • Rs 2,909 crore is the R&D spend of 11 oil PSUs in FY2025-26, 4.9% below the Rs 3,058 crore peak of FY2024-25 and 39.6% above Rs 2,084 crore in FY2021-22 (the report’s own table; computed changes).
  • 2.16% of seven major PSUs’ profit after tax went on R&D in FY2025-26, from 1.27% at BPCL to 3.89% at OIL; the committee asked for a floor tied to profit before tax, a figure the report does not give.
  • No number is what the committee recommends: a minimum ‘preferably’ set as a percentage of profit before tax, and the Ministry told it there is no such guideline today.

What the committee recommended, by theme
  • Minimum spend (Observation 1): a benchmark of minimum R&D expenditure for all oil PSUs, ‘preferably a minimum percentage of their Profit Before Tax’, reviewed periodically; a dedicated fund to co-finance cross-sector R&D (green hydrogen, carbon capture, biofuels, waste-to-energy); measures for private co-investment.
  • People (Observation 2): a dedicated R&D career track, incentives for deployed patents and royalty-earning technologies, a common HR framework with a three-year manpower projection.
  • Centres and benchmarking (Observation 3): a standard benchmarking system for the 16 R&D centres and five regional laboratories (innovation productivity, technology transfer success rate, external collaboration, commercial adoption, clean-energy fit); board-level R&D representation (only IOCL has it today); funding for fundamental science.
  • Output and review (Observations 4 and 5): standard annual R&D reporting, since the four centres named published 363 papers in 2022 to 2025 (IOCL 127, BPCL 71, HPCL 144, EIL 21); a unified review body with the Centre for High Technology; a cross-PSU review of projects near technology readiness level 6; a public list of projects on the Centre for High Technology website.
  • Start-ups (Observation 13): IOCL, BPCL, HPCL, EIL and GAIL have funded 42, 30, 28, 29 and 38 start-ups, 167 in all (computed), consistent with the PRS summary’s ‘more than 150’; OIL reports 25 currently funded. The committee asks for a fund for full-scale trials of proven start-up technology.
  • Also covered: enhanced oil recovery and deep-water support, shale oil and gas hydrates, refining efficiency, an umbrella research organisation, patents and monetisation, net-zero research. These are in the report and are not examined here.

What would fix this

  1. Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas: publish one annual table with each PSU’s R&D spend, profit before tax and profit after tax, so that a benchmark on profit before tax can be checked.
  2. Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas: define what counts as R&D expenditure, including whether campus construction such as IOCL’s Rs 3,199 crore second campus is counted, before any floor is set.
  3. Lok Sabha Secretariat and the committee: issue a correction for the Rs 2,098.95 crore figure in Observation 1 and for the 3% printed for HPCL in para 1.5.
  4. Oil PSUs: report start-ups on one definition (funded, engaged or currently funded), since the counts in the report mix the three.
  5. Centre for High Technology: publish the list of ongoing and completed projects the committee asked for, with spend per project.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

Method and definitions
  • The report was read from the Lok Sabha Secretariat PDF (74 pages, text layer): Part I paras 1.1 to 1.9 (spend, profit, GERD), 1.10 to 1.22 (people, centres), the start-up replies 1.38 and 1.39, and Part II Observations and Recommendations 1 to 5, 6, 7 and 13. Other sections (refining efficiency, umbrella organisation, patents, net zero) and the annexures and minutes were not analysed. The PRS summary was read in full.
  • R&D spend is the Ministry’s figure for each company as printed; the report does not define what is counted. Profit is profit after tax (PAT) as printed by the Ministry; the committee’s recommendation refers to profit before tax (PBT), which the report does not give.
  • Shares are R&D divided by PAT, computed here and checked against the printed percentages. The illustrative floor in the fourth section lifts the two PSUs below the seven-PSU combined share to that share; it is a computation, not a proposal in the report.
  • Seven PSUs have PAT in the report; BLC, MRPL, NRL and CPCL appear as printed percentages only, so no PAT-based combined share for all 11 is computed.
  • BLC is Balmer Lawrie, EIL is Engineers India, MRPL is Mangalore Refinery and Petrochemicals, NRL is Numaligarh Refinery, CPCL is Chennai Petroleum, OIL is Oil India.
What this piece could not establish
  • Profit before tax for any PSU: the report gives only profit after tax, and company annual reports were not reachable from the research environment, so the committee’s own benchmark base cannot be computed here.
  • Profit for FY2021-22 to FY2024-25: the report gives profit for FY2025-26 only, so the share of profit over time is not shown, and whether FY2025-26 profit figures are final or provisional is not stated.
  • What the R&D spend includes: no definition is given, and it is not stated whether capital spending on campuses or grants such as MRPL’s Rs 6.7 crore from the Oil Industry Development Board are counted.
  • Why IOCL and GAIL spent less in FY2025-26 than in FY2024-25: the report does not say.
  • Source conflicts, not resolved: Observation 1 prints Rs 2,098.95 crore for FY2025-26 against Rs 2,908.95 crore in the table (the table matches its column sum); HPCL is printed as 3% against 3.37% from its own figures; the PRS summary says ‘around 2%’ and a range of 1.3% to 3.8% for the PSUs, while the report prints 2.16% for seven PSUs and 0.58% to 4.17% across all 11.
  • Comparability of the 1.3% share: the figure is the petroleum sector’s share of India’s gross R&D spend in 2020-21 (Rs 1,634.45 crore of Rs 1,27,380.96 crore), a different measure and year from the PSU spend table, so the two are not compared.
  • R&D headcount: the oral evidence of 24 February 2025 gives 1,619 across seven PSUs and also says ‘nearly 2,000’; the gap is unexplained, and the GAIL figure of 9 looks low.
  • Whether the Ministry has acted: no action-taken report was found, and the Ministry told the committee it ‘would explore’ a norm.

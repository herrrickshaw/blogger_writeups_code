India's power mix, Q2 2026-27: coal 42% of capacity but 66% of generation

Skip to article content
Text Size

Top line

  • 243 GW of renewable capacity (44% of 555 GW) passed 231 GW of coal and lignite (42%) by August 2026, with solar at 168 GW; in the July–September quarter renewables gave 19% of generation and coal and lignite 65%.
  • 73% of the year-on-year increase in electricity came from coal and lignite, against 27% from solar and 19% from wind; large hydro’s 20% fall subtracted 27 points (CREA, via PTI).
  • 5.5 GW was the most that battery storage supplied at the night peak, about 1–2% of demand, while 1,835 million units of solar and wind output were curtailed in July and August.

What it means: A coal unit ran at 68% of its rated output over the quarter, wind at 37% and solar at 18.3%, so a gigawatt of coal delivers several times the energy of a gigawatt of solar and the capacity share overstates what renewables supply.

What to do: MNRE and CEA should publish plant load factors and generation shares beside installed capacity in each monthly progress table, so that a capacity milestone is not read as a supply milestone.

What it means: Renewable generation grew by about 14 BU of solar and 10 BU of wind, but large hydro fell about 15 BU and demand rose about 53 BU, so coal and lignite added 39 BU in a quarter of weak rain and record peaks.

What to do: Planners should read renewable growth against the hydro and demand movements in the same quarter, since a year of poor rain turns the same solar addition into a smaller share of the extra supply.

What it means: The daily peak fell in daylight on 62 of 92 days, which favours solar, but the price after sunset ran three times the daytime price, so the scarce hours are the ones coal still covers.

What to do: CERC and Grid-India should publish hourly short-term market prices and energy not met by state each month, so the value of evening supply can be compared with the cost of storage.

What to do: The power ministry and CEA should publish tendered, awarded and commissioned storage in GWh each quarter, with state curtailment and its cause, so the pipeline can be tracked to delivery.

What would fix this

  1. MNRE and CEA: publish plant load factors and generation shares next to installed capacity in every monthly progress table.
  2. Grid-India and CEA: publish curtailment of solar and wind by state and cause each month; the CREA figure covers only July and August.
  3. Ministry of Power and CEA: publish tendered, awarded and commissioned storage in GWh each quarter and the dates by which awarded projects are due.
  4. CERC and Grid-India: publish hourly market prices and energy not met by state, to price the evening hours.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

Method and definitions
  • Capacity is at the end of August 2026 (555 GW, up 12% on a year earlier); generation is July to September 2026. The two periods differ by a month, which matters little for shares.
  • “Renewables” excludes large hydro, as in CREA’s tables. “Coal and lignite” is 231 GW of capacity; coal alone is 224 GW. CREA’s press release gives coal as about 64% of generation, and its snapshot gives coal and lignite as about 66% (351 BU of 536); this piece uses the 66%, which includes lignite.
  • Nuclear (16 BU) and gas and other sources (8 BU) are derived from CREA’s totals: non-fossil 177 BU less renewables 101 BU and large hydro 60 BU; total 536 BU less coal and lignite 351 BU and non-fossil 177 BU.
  • The shares of the increase are CREA’s as reported by PTI; the 53 BU increase and the BU split in the text are computed from the stated growth rates.
What this piece could not establish
  • CREA’s report was read through its own web page and press release, with Down To Earth, PV Tech and PTI, not as a full PDF; the exchange-price figures are Crisil’s, as reported by ETEnergyWorld.
  • The share of the extra generation that is solar-plus-wind against coal depends on weather: a year with normal rain would give hydro a larger share. This piece does not test the weather link.
  • Curtailment is reported for July and August only, so no quarterly share is given; JMK Research reports a different April–June figure from a different data set.
  • Storage tender volumes are tenders issued, not awards or commissioning dates; the 3 GWh due by December is CREA’s expectation.
  • Capacity after August: the government factsheet put total capacity at about 560 GW in October (secondary report); the generation and plant-load figures stop at September.

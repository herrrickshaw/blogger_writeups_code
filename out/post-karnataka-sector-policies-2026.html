Karnataka’s 2026 policies: data centre, aerospace and marine biotechnology targets

Skip to article content
Text Size

Top line

  • Rs 1,300.45 crore is the five-year budget framework of the Sustainable Data Centre Policy 2026–2031, approved at Mangaluru on 18 September 2026; the marine biotechnology policy approved the same day carries Rs 120 crore, and the aerospace policy of 9 September states no outlay in the reports read.
  • 1,000 MW is the data centre target for 2031, 2.5 to 7.3 times the capacity reported today depending on the baseline; a target, not an outcome.
  • $6–8 billion is the marine target for 2031 against about $2 billion, 3 to 4 times, while the aerospace policy targets Rs 60,000 crore of investment and 60,000 jobs; no source gives results for any of the three.

Rs 1,300.45 crore belongs to the data centre policy; the marine policy has Rs 120 crore

  1. 9 Sep 2026 (Wednesday)Cabinet approves the Karnataka Aerospace Policy 2026–31, reported by PTI from Bengaluru; no outlay or target figure appears in the PTI text.
  2. 18 Sep 2026 (Friday)Cabinet meeting in Mangaluru, chaired by Chief Minister D.K. Shivakumar, approves the Sustainable Data Centre Policy 2026–2031 and the Marine Biotechnology Policy 2026–2031 (Deccan Chronicle, ThePrint, BusinessLine, Dataquest).
  3. 19 Sep 2026ThePrint headline: ‘Rs 1,300-cr data centre policy, PLI-centred Blue Bioeconomy roadmap’; the text gives Rs 1,300.45 crore to the data centre policy and Rs 120 crore to the marine policy.
  4. 17 Nov 2026 (Tuesday)Marine policy ‘formally announced’ at the 29th Bengaluru Tech Summit, 60 days after approval (IASPoint, a study-notes site; secondary).

What it means: The Rs 1,300 crore figure in the headline belongs to the data centre policy alone, while the roadmap built around a production-linked incentive is the marine policy, whose budget is Rs 120 crore.

What to do: The Department of Electronics, IT, BT and Science and Technology should publish each approved policy with its budget by component, so that a headline cannot join two budgets.

What it means: The target is 2.5 to 7.3 times the capacity reported today and needs about 138 MW a year against the 13 MW a year Bengaluru added in 2020–25, and it equals the 1,000 MW already planned in April 2026, so it is a goal rather than a result.

What to do: The Electronics, IT and BT Department should publish a single dated series of installed and operational data centre capacity for the state, so that progress to 1,000 MW can be read against one baseline.

What to do: The Commerce and Industries Department should publish the investment made and jobs created under the 2022–27 policy, alongside the 2026–31 targets.

What it means: The marine target is a tripling to quadrupling of a base that the policy does not date or define, and recorded seaweed cultivation in Karnataka is close to nil, so the target rests on activity the state does not yet measure.

What to do: The Fisheries and Biotechnology Departments should define the $2 billion base, name its year and publish yearly seaweed area and production, so that the 2031 target can be tested.

What would fix this

  1. Department of Electronics, IT, BT and Science and Technology: publish the approved data centre and marine policies with budget by component and the incentive rates, including the seaweed production-linked incentive amount.
  2. Department of Electronics, IT and BT: publish one dated series of installed and operational data centre capacity for the state, with the definition used.
  3. Department of Commerce and Industries: publish investment and jobs achieved under the Aerospace and Defence Policy 2022–27, and state whether the 2026–31 policy replaces it.
  4. Department of Fisheries: define the $2 billion marine base, give its year, and publish yearly seaweed area and production.
  5. Karnataka Information and Public Relations Department: release each cabinet decision with its budget in one dated note, so that headlines do not join separate budgets.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

Method and definitions
  • Approval dates come from reports of 9, 18 and 19 September 2026; weekdays were checked against the calendar (Wednesday 9 September, Friday 18 September).
  • A target is a goal stated for 2031 or for the policy period; an outcome is a measured result. No outcome for any of the three policies was found, and the baselines shown pre-date them.
  • Ratios, percentages, growth rates and per-job figures are computed here from the figures shown, and each is checked by an assertion in the builder.
  • The page text was read for ThePrint, the Deccan Chronicle, MediaNama, Dataquest, PTI, HR Katha, GKToday and IASPoint. Other sources, including the Hindu, Deccan Herald, PIB, Lok Sabha replies, the policy documents and the market reports, were read as search excerpts and are labelled. News and study-notes sites are secondary.
  • The cooling-water figure reuses this blog’s data-centre cooling analysis, which treats 25–26 million litres per MW a year as a press and advocacy figure above the physical range, so the result is an upper bound.
What this piece could not establish
  • The text of any of the three policies. Incentive rates for data centres and for marine biotechnology, the amount of the seaweed production-linked incentive, and any aerospace outlay were not found; a search excerpt mentioned a Rs 4,000 crore aerospace support figure from a page not read.
  • What the data centre budget of Rs 1,300.45 crore was ‘revised’ from, and whether it is spending or a ceiling: the Deccan Chronicle says revised budget framework and Dataquest says proposed outlay.
  • One baseline for data centre capacity. Bengaluru figures run from 137 to 182 MW and the state figure is about 400 MW; an Anarock excerpt shows 137 MW in its text and 179 in an exhibit whose labels were unclear. The Deccan Herald of 18 July speaks of adding 1,000 MW over five years, while the approved text speaks of 1 GW cumulative; these differ by the existing capacity.
  • Whether 162 MW is a city or a state figure: The Hindu of 18 November calls it Karnataka’s operational capacity, while the market reports give it for Bengaluru and the IT secretary put the state at about 400 MW in July.
  • Whether the 10–12% share of India’s AI-ready capacity can be tracked: no source defines AI-ready capacity or gives the national total.
  • The investment and jobs achieved under the 2022–27 aerospace policy, and whether the 2026–31 policy replaces it before August 2027. The Economic Survey 2025-26 has an aerospace export row, but the year headings were not in the excerpt read, so it is not used.
  • Why the reports give 30% for Zone 1 and up to 35% for non-focus areas, and whether the 25–30% quoted for space manufacturing is inside or additional to it.
  • The year and definition of the $2 billion marine base; whether the seaweed incentive has been notified; whether the Fisheries College intake rises from 40 or from 60.
  • Whether any investment, application or job has followed from any of the three policies since approval.

State GCC policies in India: targeted jobs per new GCC range from 200 to 1,000

Skip to article content
Text Size

Top line

  • 200 to 1,000 jobs per new GCC is the range implied by seven states’ own targets, from Gujarat (50,000 jobs, 250 GCCs) to Maharashtra (4 lakh jobs, 400 GCCs); the targets are goals, and no count of GCCs opened under any policy was found.
  • 1,375 would be Karnataka’s total after 500 new GCCs if the policy’s own statistics panel of 875 existing GCCs is right; the target of 1,000 fits a baseline of 500, and the March 2026 budget says more than 550 are operating.
  • 1,500 to 2,000 new GCCs is the sum of the state targets in the primary documents (six states, then with Uttar Pradesh), against 1,380 in the national estimate that a comparison site attributes to CBRE; the job targets sum to 12.2 lakh against 11.8 lakh.

What it means: A five-fold gap in jobs per targeted GCC means the targets do not describe the same kind of centre, so a state’s count of GCCs cannot be compared with another’s without a common definition of size and function.

What to do: The Union Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology, with the states, should publish one definition of a GCC and a yearly count of GCCs and jobs by state against each policy target.

What it means: The ceilings differ by a factor of 20, and Gujarat’s lowest-tier ceiling is above the average investment per GCC implied by its own target, so the ceiling binds only for large centres while the rate decides the cost to the state.

What to do: Each state’s IT department should publish, for each GCC supported, the investment, jobs and incentive sanctioned and paid, so that the cost per job under each policy can be computed.

What it means: Karnataka’s own documents give three baselines for the number of GCCs, 500, 550 and 875, and the target of 1,000 matches only the first, so progress cannot be read until the state says which count the target uses.

What to do: The Karnataka IT, BT and Science and Technology Department should state which unit the target counts, give the number at 3 December 2024, and publish the number registered with the Karnataka Innovation and Technology Society each quarter.

What it means: The state targets cannot be reconciled with the national estimate of 1,380, which suggests that the estimate and the states count different things or different years, and neither gives the basis.

What to do: The Union IT Ministry should publish the states, horizons and definitions behind the national estimate, and each state should publish its target and baseline on one common basis.

What would fix this

  1. Union Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology: publish one definition of a GCC and a yearly count of GCCs and jobs by state, against each state target.
  2. Karnataka IT, BT and Science and Technology Department: state the unit the 1,000 target counts, the number at 3 December 2024 and a quarterly count of registered GCCs.
  3. Each state IT department: publish, for each GCC supported, the investment, jobs and incentive sanctioned and paid, so that cost per job can be computed.
  4. Andhra Pradesh, Bihar and Tamil Nadu: publish targets and a baseline for their GCC policy and scheme, as the other eight states do.
  5. Authors of national estimates: publish the states, horizons and method behind the 1,380 GCCs and 1.18 million jobs, so that they can be reconciled with the state targets.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

Method and definitions
  • A target is a goal stated for 2029 to 2031; an outcome is a measured result. No state publishes an outcome under its GCC policy in the sources read.
  • Jobs per GCC, investment per GCC, sums, differences and ceilings are computed here from the figures shown, and each is checked by an assertion in the builder. Dates to operating rules use the dates of file listings and are approximate.
  • Primary documents (policies, resolutions, gazettes, press releases, operational guidelines, the Karnataka budget, Bihar’s resolution) were read as search excerpts; none was read in full. Haryana comes from a PwC alert on the policy, and Maharashtra’s rates, Tamil Nadu and Andhra Pradesh from secondary summaries. The comparison page of AIM Research, a commercial data provider, was read in full and used for items no primary excerpt gave; it is labelled secondary.
  • Targets differ in horizon (2029 for Karnataka, 2030 for Rajasthan, 2031 for the national estimate and Uttar Pradesh) and in what they count, so sums are indicative.
What this piece could not establish
  • Any GCC policy in full. The incentive rates for Maharashtra and the targets for Rajasthan, Haryana and Odisha come from secondary summaries.
  • Targets for Andhra Pradesh and Bihar, and any target in Tamil Nadu’s scheme beyond the 50-GCC limit and a reported 2030 goal of 460+.
  • Bihar’s incentive rates from the policy itself: the resolution was read as an excerpt of an OCR’d Hindi PDF without the attached policy, so the rates are from secondary summaries. Its date (cabinet 29 January 2026, e-gazette listing 31 January, a start of 5 February in two secondary sources), whether benefits are limited to the first 25 units, and the first eligibility threshold (OCR reads 4000, the band implies 1,000) were not settled.
  • Which count of GCCs in Karnataka is right (500, 550 or 875), whether the policy’s statistics panel was read correctly from a jumbled extraction, and why the March 2026 budget gives a lower number than the panel.
  • The basis of the national estimate of 1,380 GCCs and 1.18 million jobs, which was read only through AIM Research.
  • How many GCCs have opened, and how many jobs have been created, under any state policy, and how much incentive has been paid.
  • The notification date of Rajasthan’s policy (2 December 2025 in one secondary source and an order file dated 12 December), and the Bengaluru ceiling for Karnataka’s innovation labs (Rs 4 crore or Rs 5 crore).
  • Whether incentives such as Maharashtra’s payroll subsidy limited to salaries above Rs 1 lakh a month, or Uttar Pradesh’s domicile conditions, change the cost per job materially.

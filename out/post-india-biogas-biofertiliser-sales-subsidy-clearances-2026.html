Biogas manure: 20.65 lakh tonnes sold, Rs 111.72 crore subsidy, 149 clearances

Skip to article content
Text Size

Top line

  • 20.65 lakh tonnes of fermented organic manure, liquid and phosphate-rich organic manure were sold from 2023-24 to 28 February 2026, 81% of it in 2025-26 alone.
  • Rs 111.72 crore of the Rs 1,500 a tonne subsidy had been released by 28 February 2026, about 36% of the Rs 310 crore that the rate implies on the tonnage reported; the scheme ends this year and is being evaluated.
  • 149 entries in ten gazette orders from 14 November 2023 to 27 July 2026 authorise biogas plants to sell the manure in bulk to farmers; 122 are current and 27 lapsed on 14 November 2026.

What it means: Liquid manure is 65% of the 20.65 lakh tonnes, and the jump from 3.36 lakh tonnes to 16.73 lakh tonnes in one year comes with 15,690 farmer camps and 44 marketing agreements between fertiliser companies and biogas operators.

What to do: The Department of Fertilizers should publish sales by plant and by quarter with the realised price per tonne, so that the volume can be tied to named plants and to what farmers paid.

What it means: The gap between Rs 111.72 crore released and about Rs 310 crore implied may reflect lags in claims, plants not registered or tonnage that did not qualify, and the reply does not say which, so the subsidy bill for a scheme ending this year is not yet known.

What to do: The Department of Fertilizers should state the claims received, paid and pending as of the scheme end date, so that a decision to extend or replace the subsidy rests on the full liability.

What would fix this

  1. Department of Fertilizers: publish sales and subsidy claims by plant and quarter, with the realised price per tonne.
  2. Department of Fertilizers and NITI Aayog: state claims received, paid and pending at the scheme end date before deciding on extension.
  3. Department of Agriculture and Farmers Welfare: publish one live list of authorised plants with expiry dates, and say whether the Seventh Amendment Order of 29 June 2026 makes the per-plant orders unnecessary.
  4. State agriculture departments: publish the hectares and crops reached by biogas manure, so effects on chemical fertiliser use can be measured.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

Method and definitions
  • FOM is fermented organic manure, a solid by-product of biogas plants; liquid FOM is the liquid form; PROM is phosphate-rich organic manure.
  • Sale (Annexure I) and production (Annexure II) are separate series in the reply and cover different dates; production to 4 March 2026 is 1.43 times the sales to 28 February 2026, and the reply does not reconcile them.
  • The implied subsidy multiplies the Rs 1,500 rate by the total tonnes sold and assumes every tonne qualifies, which the reply does not say.
  • The gazette register counts each entry in each order; plants that appear in more than one order are counted more than once.
What this piece could not establish
  • Sales after 28 February 2026 and subsidy released after the same date.
  • Why sales (20.65 lakh tonnes) and production (29.57 lakh tonnes) differ, and how many tonnes were sold at what price.
  • The number of distinct authorised plants, and whether each is selling.
  • Orders before 14 November 2023, and any order after 27 July 2026.
  • The reasoning for the 27 entries lapsing: whether the plants have applied again is not shown.

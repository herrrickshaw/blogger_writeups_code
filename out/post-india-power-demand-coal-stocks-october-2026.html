India's power demand in October 2026: 160 billion units, coal stocks at six days

Text Size

Top line

  • 160 BU of electricity was consumed in October 2026, 22% above a year earlier and, because October 2025 fell 6%, about 15% above October 2024; the peak was 261 GW (Crisil).
  • 20 Mt of coal sat at power plants on 31 October, a third of the 58 Mt norm and down from 59 Mt on 1 March; that is about six days of burn, against 16 days a year earlier.
  • 86 of 191 coal plants held under 25% of their required stock on 31 October, against 11 a year earlier, even as Coal India sent a record 57.1 Mt to power plants in the month.

What it means: October 2026 sat about 15% above October 2024 once the weak October 2025 base is removed, and the growth came from heat and weak rain that the Power Line report ties to El Niño, so part of the 22% is a base effect.

What to do: Planners should read monthly growth against two-year comparisons, since a 22% rise on a 6% fall overstates the underlying trend and understates the base to which stocks and capacity must be scaled.

What it means: Stock fell by about two-thirds between March and October while consumption stayed near 79 Mt a month from June to September, so the margin that absorbs a supply or demand shock is now about six days.

What to do: The coal and power ministries should publish a dated target for rebuilding stocks and report progress weekly, since the Ministry of Coal itself says rebuilding has not yet begun.

What it means: Record dispatch more than halved the daily drawdown, from 0.243 Mt in September to 0.116 Mt in October, but it only slowed the fall, because stocks remained near 20 Mt on 31 October.

What to do: Coal India, Indian Railways and the power ministry should publish weekly dispatch against consumption for the power sector, so the date when stocks start rising can be seen and not forecast.

What would fix this

  1. Ministry of Coal and Ministry of Power: publish a dated stock-rebuilding target and a weekly dispatch-against-consumption table for the power sector.
  2. Central Electricity Authority: state in each daily coal report the plant list, ownership split and normative stock behind the critical-plant count.
  3. Ministry of Power: state whether a blending directive for imported coal will be issued for the winter, since trade reports say domestic plants have not been told to import for blending.
  4. Grid-India: publish evening energy-not-met by state each month, since reports name several states with evening supply gaps but give no figures.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

Method and definitions
  • Demand is electricity consumed (energy met); generation is a separate, larger measure (about 173 BU in October, Crisil). The two are not interchangeable.
  • A “critical” plant holds under 25% of its normative coal stock (CEA definition, as stated by CREA and Hindu BusinessLine). Normative stock is the number of days of burn set for each plant by its distance from the mine, so the national norm is an aggregate.
  • The two-year comparison multiplies the October 2026 growth (+22%) by the October 2025 growth (−6%): 1.22 × 0.94 = 1.147.
  • Peak growth is recomputed from the stated peaks (261 GW against 221 GW) because the report’s own +24% does not follow from them.
What this piece could not establish
  • CEA’s daily coal report and Grid-India data were not downloaded for this piece; stock, demand and price figures are read from Crisil (through ETEnergyWorld), Power Line, CREA, Coal India, the Ministry of Coal and trade press. The CEA report of 30 October was found but its totals were not read.
  • Plant counts differ across reports (84–91 in CREA, 85 and 91 in Hindu BusinessLine, 86 in Crisil, 98 in Argus Media) because the plant set and the date differ; the 86 of 191 is Crisil’s end-October count.
  • The cause of the demand surge: Crisil and Power Line cite heat, weak rain and El Niño; this piece does not test it. The 24% peak-growth figure in Crisil’s report is not reproduced.
  • Whether stocks have risen since 31 October: the latest figure read is 31 October, and a figure of about 19 Mt is quoted by Power Line for a nearby date.
  • Import figures: the 15.06 Mt October estimate is a Kpler estimate quoted by Argus Media, not a reported figure.

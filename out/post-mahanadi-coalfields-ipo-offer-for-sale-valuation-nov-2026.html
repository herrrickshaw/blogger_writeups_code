Mahanadi Coalfields IPO: Rs 8,981 crore offer, all proceeds to Coal India

Skip to article content
Text Size

Top line

  • Rs 8,981 crore is the offer at the top of the Rs 129 to Rs 136 band, 10% of Mahanadi Coalfields sold by Coal India; the company receives nothing, and the issue opens 19 November.
  • 8.4 times FY26 earnings and 4.2 times book value is the price at Rs 136, a market value of Rs 90,009.74 crore on 6,618,363,000 shares.
  • Rs 10,678 crore was FY26 profit, 10% below FY24, on output of 218.31 million tonnes, 3.0% below FY25; the first half of FY27 shows profit up 6.7%.

What it means: Because the whole issue is a sale by Coal India, the Rs 8,981.12 crore goes to the parent and not to a company that has identified 12 first-mile connectivity facilities to build by FY30, and the reported size is Rs 19.85 crore below the share count times the top price, a gap the sources do not explain.

What to do: Coal India should state in the offer summary what it will do with the proceeds of the sale, and the exchanges should show the gross and net issue sizes at both ends of the band.

What would fix this

  1. Coal India: state in the offer summary how the proceeds of the sale will be used, since the company being listed receives none of them.
  2. Stock exchanges and the SEBI offer-document summary: show gross and net issue size at both ends of the band, with any employee or shareholder discount.
  3. Coal India and the Ministry of Coal: publish one table of the listed subsidiaries (BCCL, CMPDI, Mahanadi, SECL) with price-to-earnings and price-to-book at the offer and at today’s price.
  4. Mahanadi Coalfields: publish monthly production, offtake and pit-head stock after listing in one standard table.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

Method and definitions
  • An offer for sale is a sale of existing shares by a shareholder; here Coal India sells 10% of a wholly owned subsidiary, so Mahanadi Coalfields issues no shares and receives no money.
  • Gross offer size is shares offered multiplied by the price; the reported figure is as given by CNBC TV18 on 16 November and is compared, not substituted.
  • Price to earnings divides the price by FY26 basic earnings per share (Rs 16.13) and price to book divides it by net asset value per share at 31 March 2026 (Rs 32.29); both are from the offer document.
  • Profit is the figure attributable to owners of the company, in Rs crore; production and offtake are in million tonnes from the offer document’s key performance indicators.
  • The grey-market premium is an unofficial figure and is not used in any calculation.
What this piece could not establish
  • The reason the reported offer (Rs 8,981.12 crore) is Rs 19.85 crore below the share count times Rs 136: the gap is consistent with a discount on the employee reservation of about 3.31 crore shares, but the discount is not in the sources read.
  • Any comparison with Coal India, Bharat Coking Coal or CMPDI: the sources give no multiples for them, and their post-listing price performance was not read.
  • The cause of the lower June 2026 quarter output, and a conflict on FY26 dispatches: the offer document table shows offtake of 211.35 million tonnes while a Mint report gives 210.74.
  • Coal India’s share of national output: CNBC TV18 gives about 74% in FY26 and a Mint report says more than 80%; the 74% fits MCL’s 21% and 28.4% shares and is used.
  • The date of SEBI’s observations on the draft offer document; the draft was filed on 1 September and the final document on 13 November.
  • What happens after 23 November: subscription, the allotment price and listing day are not covered. SECL’s draft filing of 1 November (up to 668,056,100 shares) is noted but not analysed.

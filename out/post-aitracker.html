What changed in the method

Edition 01 copied its numbers. Edition 02 queried them.

Edition 01 promised that “GitHub stars are checkable” and then took its star counts from a third-party roundup article. That is not the same thing. For this edition every figure in the table below was pulled directly from the GitHub search API on 8 August 2026, which also resolved each project to its actual repository — useful, because two of the names in Edition 01 are ambiguous and one of them ranks below an unrelated project of the same name.

The growth percentages are softer than they look, and it matters. Edition 01’s baselines were rounded to two or three significant figures (“32K”, “18K”, “10K”) and dated only as “mid-to-late July 2026”. So both ends of every calculation are approximate: the starting number is rounded and the interval is somewhere between two and four weeks. Read the ordering and the orders of magnitude, not the decimal place. From Edition 03 onward the baseline will be an exact API figure with an exact date, and the growth rates will mean what they say.

What the ordering says

The fastest movers are not the biggest projects.

Vendor-backed tooling is compounding hardest. xAI’s grok-build more than doubled and a half. It was the smallest repository on the Edition 01 list at 9,300 stars and is now larger than seven of the others. A coding-agent reference architecture published by a frontier lab appears to accumulate attention faster than an independent tool solving the same problem.

Plumbing is outgrowing products. Three of the top five — OmniRoute (provider routing), hallmark (output validation) and codebase-memory-mcp (agent memory) — are infrastructure that sits between a model and an application. None of them is a model or a chat product. That is the layer where the star velocity is.

Graphify crossed 100,000, up from 63,200, and remains the largest project this tracker follows. Its growth rate has slowed relative to the smaller entrants, which is what usually happens at that size and is not a negative signal.

What a star is, and is not. A GitHub star is a bookmark. It measures attention, and attention is what this tracker set out to rank. It is not a download, not a deployment and not revenue — a repository can gain 20,000 stars and be used in production by nobody. Where a project also publishes package downloads, that is the better number, and future editions will prefer it where it exists.

New this cycle, and explicitly unverified

The model releases could not be checked to the same standard.

Several frontier and open-weight releases were reported during this cycle, including Qwen3.8 Max (Alibaba, reported 2 August 2026), Muse Spark 1.2 (Meta, reported 5 August 2026), DeepSeek-V4-Flash and further GPT-5.6 updates. These are listed as pointers only.

None of the model releases above was verified against a vendor source for this edition. They come from model-tracking aggregators and startup newsletters, several of which disagree with each other on dates and on whether a given entry is a new model, a point update or a marketplace listing. A model release is not a checkable quantity in the way a star count is: there is no API to query, and the primary source is a vendor blog post that has to be read. Do not cite the dates above. Treat them as a list of things to go and confirm. The GitHub table is this edition’s evidence; this section is its to-do list.

Carried forward

Entries from Edition 01 with no measurable movement.

Eight Edition 01 entries carry no GitHub figure and so cannot be re-measured on this edition’s basis: Cursor v3.11 (Anysphere), Kolibri (Konecta), Ruflo, Claude Opus 5 (Anthropic), GPT-5.6 (OpenAI), Grok 4.5 (xAI), the open-weight wave (GLM-5.2, DeepSeek V4 and others) and Klon AI (Cheer Holding). They remain in scope. Their Edition 01 signals were press coverage, funding and reported adoption, none of which this edition attempted to re-measure, and a ranking that mixes an API-verified star count with “major coverage” is not a ranking.

The five scope categories are unchanged — coding and developer tools, agentic platforms, foundation models, consumer AI, automation — so edition-to-edition comparison holds.

What this edition does not establish. Adoption, revenue or production use of anything listed. Whether star growth reflects organic interest or promotion. Any model release date. And the precise growth interval, because Edition 01 dated its snapshot to a three-week range rather than a day — the single change that would most improve Edition 03.

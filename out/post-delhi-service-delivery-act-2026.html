Top line

  • Rs 5,000 is the most an official can be fined per case under section 8: Rs 250 a day of delay, reached after 20 days, paid into the government treasury and not to the applicant.
  • 12.5 times Karnataka’s daily rate of Rs 20, with a cap 10 times Karnataka’s Rs 500; Karnataka pays the citizen, Delhi does not.
  • 65.02% of over 36 lakh applications are shown as disposed in time on the e-SLA dashboard, a count of 567 services in 46 departments that carries no date.

Delhi Act timeline: passed 11 Aug 2026, gazetted 27 Aug, notification reported 9 Nov

  1. 2011The Delhi (Right of Citizen to Time Bound Delivery of Services) Act, 2011 applies; a 2012 PRS table records its cost of Rs 10 an application on a defaulting servant.
  2. 11 Aug 2026The Legislative Assembly of the National Capital Territory passes the Bill.
  3. 25 Aug 2026The Lieutenant Governor gives assent.
  4. 27 Aug 2026The Act is published in the Delhi Gazette Extraordinary as Delhi Act 10 of 2026; it comes into force on a date the Government appoints by notification (section 1(3)).
  5. About 9 Nov 2026 (as reported)News reports say the Government notified the Act; the notification text was not read, and whether it sets the commencement date under section 1(3) is not confirmed.
  6. 10 Nov 2026The IT Department notifies the Delhi (Right of Citizen to Time Bound and Ease of Delivery of Services) Rules, 2026 under section 19: online filing and tracking, a five-day deficiency memo, third-party audits and transfer of pending 2011 applications; they take effect when the Act does (Gazette Tracker, SCC Times, TeamLease; search excerpts).
  7. On commencementThe 2011 Act is repealed; applications pending under it are deemed applications under the 2026 Act (section 21).

What it means: The Act applies only after a commencement notification, so a citizen can rely on its penalties from the date the notification names and not from the date of passage.

What to do: The Government should publish the commencement notification, the Schedule of services and the Commission’s constitution order together so that applicants can see which deadlines now apply.

What would fix this

  1. Delhi Government: publish the Schedule of services, time limits, Designated Officers and Authorities with the commencement notification, and say whether the services were already notified as the Chief Minister’s office stated on 9 November.
  2. Information Technology Department, Delhi: print the as-of date on the e-SLA dashboard and report delivered-in-time, delivered-late, rejected and pending counts by department every month.
  3. Delhi Right to Service Commission: publish the annual report required by section 15(6) with the number of penalties imposed and the rupees recovered.
  4. Delhi Government: state whether any part of a penalty reaches the applicant, and publish the count of 2011-law applications carried over under section 21(3) and the Rules.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

What the Act provides, section by section
  • Right and scope (sections 1 to 4): every citizen has the right to notified services within the Schedule’s time limit; the Act applies only to services in the Schedule and to departments, local bodies, autonomous bodies or statutory organisations the Government notifies; the Government may amend the Schedule by notification, laid before the Assembly.
  • Designated Officer (sections 2, 4, 5): the officer responsible for a service, with a Link Designated Officer who covers absences; the officer must give the service or reject with written reasons within the time limit, which starts on receipt of a complete application, and an application submitted online counts as received.
  • Appeals (sections 6, 7, 9): the Authority must be at least one or two levels senior to the Designated Officer; the Authority cannot send an appeal back; a second appeal lies to the Commission within 30 days.
  • Penalty (section 8): if the default is without sufficient and reasonable cause, the Authority or Commission may impose Rs 250 a day up to Rs 5,000, or a lump sum of Rs 250 to Rs 5,000 for unreasonable rejection, after hearing the officer; the penalty may fall on any official involved in the default, and the Authority recovers it from salary under section 7(9).
  • Commission (sections 10 to 15): a Chairman not below Chief Secretary rank and three Members; the Information Technology Department is its administrative department; it may inspect offices, recommend departmental action and issue instructions, and must prepare an annual report.
  • E-governance (section 16): the Government may provide online filing, status updates and auto-escalation on default, and must set up an audit mechanism.
  • Service conditions (sections 17 and 18): the Act is deemed part of the service conditions of government servants and adds to, and does not replace, disciplinary rules.
What this piece could not establish
  • The Schedule of services and time limits: it is not in the PRS copy of the Act, so no notified service and no deadline is quoted here. Reports conflict on whether it has been notified: the Chief Minister’s office statement, carried by the Pioneer and the Economic Times on 9 November, says a separate notification specified the services and days, while the Indian Express, the Tribune and News On AIR say the Government will notify them. No such notification was found.
  • The Rules: the Delhi (Right of Citizen to Time Bound and Ease of Delivery of Services) Rules, 2026 were notified on 10 November 2026 and were read only as summaries in search excerpts (Gazette Tracker, SCC Times, TeamLease); their text was not read, and the excerpts do not say whether they address who receives a penalty.
  • The structure of the penalty: the Act text gives Rs 250 a day up to Rs 5,000 for delay and a separate lump sum of Rs 250 to Rs 5,000 for unreasonable rejection, but the Indian Express describes a one-time Rs 250 rising to Rs 5,000 and the Hindustan Times a daily Rs 250 to Rs 5,000. The Act text is used here.
  • Whether the notification reported on about 9 November 2026 is the commencement notification under section 1(3). It was read only in search-result excerpts from News On AIR and NewKerala, and one secondary analysis found in a search excerpt said no commencement notification could be located as of 4 September 2026. The text of any notification was not read.
  • The daily rate: the Gazette text prints Rs 250 a day, but the PTI report of 11 August 2026 on the Bill’s passage, seen in search excerpts from ptinews.com and the Pioneer, said Rs 249 a day. The Act text is used here.
  • The e-SLA dashboard figures: 567 services, 46 departments, over 36 lakh applications and 65.02% disposed in time were read from a search-result excerpt of esla.delhi.gov.in, which prints no date. The page also prints 26.73% disposed beyond the prescribed days and 0.19% under processing, which with 65.02% leave 8.06% not accounted for; the meaning of in time was not read, and the dashboard belongs to the 2011 law’s system. Other Delhi government sources seen in the same search show other service counts for other portals, such as 453 services on eDistrict, so the counts are not interchangeable.
  • Pending or disposed applications under the 2011 law at the date of transition: none was found.
  • Whether the Delhi Right to Service Commission has been constituted; section 10 lets the Public Grievance Commission act until it is.
  • The Act’s name differs between sources: the Gazette says ‘Right of Citizen to Time Bound and Ease of Delivery of Services’, while some reports say ‘Right of Citizens to Time-Bound and Easy Delivery of Services’. The Gazette form is used. The PRS copy carries a disclaimer that it is not the authoritative text, and the Hindi text was not compared with the English.
  • Karnataka comparison: the figures are from the PRS compilation of the Karnataka Act; Karnataka’s 2026 amendment allows its Schedule to set other rates, and a reported 2026 slab is covered in the Karnataka post.
  • Whether the Act reduces delays: the sources read describe the law and a dashboard, not outcomes under it.

India made 247.87 million tonnes of milk in 2024-25 — more than any other country — and sells roughly two-thirds of it exactly the way it left the buffalo: liquid, in a pouch, same day. The remaining third is where cheese lives, and cheese is currently a rounding error inside it. A Maharashtra cooperative-adjacent brand betting ₹100 crore on a mozzarella plant, and an Indian cheesemaker just medalling in Brazil, are both symptoms of the same gap closing.

Top line

  • 247.87 MT of milk in 2024-25, with roughly a third left after liquid milk, on the order of 82-83 MT. The processed share is contested: about 25% to 40% depending on the source.
  • ₹128.9 billion cheese market in 2025, projected by IMARC to reach ₹619.9 billion in 2034 at about 19.1% a year; mozzarella is about 35% of the category.
  • ₹403.07 crore cheese trade surplus in 2024-25. Dairy is excluded from the EFTA agreement, so tariff access is not what is driving it.
Updated 7 November 2026. Milky Mist’s first full quarter as a listed company (July–September 2026) brought revenue of about Rs 1,089 crore, up about 42%, and profit of about Rs 78 crore, up about 174% (AgroSpectrum); the results do not break out cheese. Trade deals: India kept dairy out of EFTA (2025) and UAE (2022) and left RCEP (2019)

  1. November 2019India walks out of RCEP, the only prospective member to do so. Dairy was a named reason: GCMMF, which markets Amul, led opposition to tariff cuts on dairy imports from New Zealand and Australia.
  2. May 2022The India-UAE CEPA comes into force. Dairy sits inside the 1,157 tariff lines (about 9.7% of all lines) that India kept off the table.
  3. 1 October 2025The EFTA-India TEPA comes into force. India agreed no line-item reductions for dairy, and Switzerland grants no commitments on butter (heading 0405) or cheese and curd (heading 0406).
  4. 20 October 2026The India-New Zealand agreement comes into force with no tariff concession on cheese, milk, cream, whey, butter or caseins; only milk albumins get a half-duty quota.

What it means: So cheese-export growth is not an EFTA story: the post points to APEDA market access and buyer linkage in the Gulf and Singapore, rising value-added dairy demand and the mozzarella-and-PLI capacity build.

What to do: The Department of Commerce should host the full India-EFTA TEPA tariff schedules line by line on its own website, so the exclusion of ghee, paneer, butter and milk powder can be confirmed from the treaty text.

Who is making cheese
  • Amul (GCMMF): cooperative leader with a full portfolio. It invested ₹200 crore from 2021 to scale buffalo-milk mozzarella production for domestic and export markets, and mozzarella came under the government’s PLI scheme in 2021.
  • Milky Mist: private challenger, heavy in South India. Listed in August 2026; an estimated ~19% of India’s organised packaged paneer market (~60% within South India) and ~12% of South India’s organised cheese market; one integrated plant at Perundurai, Erode (Tamil Nadu), with 192 tonnes/day paneer capacity; South India supplied 69.23% of FY26 revenue.
  • Britannia and Go Cheese (Parag Milk): retail-distribution challengers. Britannia uses its biscuit and FMCG network; Go Cheese markets itself on a “cow milk” positioning.
  • Nandini (KMF), Aavin, Verka: state cooperatives, strong and often cheaper in home states, without the national cold-chain and distribution reach to compete pan-India.
  • D’lecta and Lactalis: B2B and premium niche, focused on hotel and restaurant supply and natural cheese.

Amul figures per Business Standard’s 2021 coverage; Milky Mist figures per its IPO disclosures and dairy-trade press. Other players’ positioning is qualitative and not independently verified.

Case study: Paras Dairy’s ₹100 crore cheese plant

Paras Dairy built a cheese plant in Ahmednagar, Maharashtra, making its Galacia brand from cow’s milk with Individual Quick Freezing (IQF) so shredded and diced product stays uniform for commercial kitchens and smaller HoReCa outlets. It has put over ₹100 crore into the plant.

Its stated goals, as of its most recent public announcements, are more than 2,000 outlets (from 500-plus) and 100 new distributors by March 2026, and a place among India’s top five cheese brands within the following year. These are the company’s own targets, not verified outcomes.

One claim that did not survive checking

A figure in circulation says Amul “expanded mozzarella production capacity by 1,200%”. The sourced figure is that India’s mozzarella exports reached USD 3.29 million in April-September 2025, up more than 1,200% year on year: export growth off a small base, not Amul’s capacity, which was built through a separate ₹200 crore programme.

A second data point needs no correction: at the Mundial do Queijo do Brasil 2026, Indian cheesemakers won a Super Gold, two Golds and a Silver among entries from over 30 countries. That is a signal about India’s small artisanal segment, not the mass mozzarella market.

Cold chain: the size of the gap is unsettled

Regional cooperatives (Nandini, Aavin, Verka) stay in their home states mainly because of cold-chain logistics. Estimates of the gap range from a shortfall of roughly 8 million tonnes of cold-storage capacity to the National Centre for Cold-Chain Development’s figure of an additional 35 million tonnes, depending on baseline and demand assumptions; the post presents the spread deliberately rather than collapsing it.

What is consistent: only around 4% of India’s perishable-food transport moves in refrigerated vehicles, the other 96% is unrefrigerated, and cold storage is concentrated in a handful of states, leaving central and eastern India under-served.

Cheese is a small part of India’s dairy exports
  • Per an APEDA-commissioned CRISIL Intelligence 2024 basket breakdown, ghee was 42% of dairy export value, butter 18%, paneer/cottage cheese 15%, processed cheese 6% and fresh cheese 1%.
  • Cheese in both forms combined is a smaller export category than butter alone; HSN 0406 is the 4th-largest export subchapter in Chapter 04, narrowly ahead of heading 0408 (₹494.60 crore).
  • Ghee exports are projected to grow at roughly 35% CAGR from 2021 to 2026, priced 30-35% below European ghee on a September-2025 snapshot.
  • The main destinations for Indian cheese are the UAE, Saudi Arabia and Singapore.
How the trade figures were sourced

The ₹ crore series (FY2018-19 to FY2024-25) was checked against the DGCI&S TradeStat portal and matches to the paisa. A second, differently based series covers calendar years 2012-2023 in US dollars: exports roughly quadrupled from about $13.4m to about $53.2m, while imports grew more slowly and dipped sharply in 2020. The two bases were not reconciled; both show the same growing-surplus trend.

What the treaty text shows for ghee, paneer, butter and milk powder
  • India’s schedules to Switzerland, Iceland and Norway mark ghee (04059020), mozzarella (04061010), the residual cheese line that covers paneer (04061090) and the other cheese lines as “Exclusion”; Switzerland’s Annex 2.F marks headings 0405 and 0406 as “NC”, meaning no commitments on import duties.
  • Butter shares heading 0405 with ghee, and the milk-powder lines of heading 0402 are marked “Exclusion” in the Swiss schedule too. Dahi and yoghurt fall under heading 0403.
  • Dairy is not shut on the Swiss side: Annex 2.F gives a few reductions within 0402 and 0403, such as a preferential rate within a tariff quota on one milk-powder line and 10 off the MFN rate on flavoured yoghurt.
  • EFTA’s offer covers 100% of non-agricultural tariff lines duty-free, plus some processed agricultural products; the carve-out is dairy, soya, coal and select other categories, not agriculture as a whole.
  • EFTA’s own website blocked access, so the schedules were read from the copies published by India’s Department of Commerce.

What would fix this

  1. Department of Animal Husbandry and Dairying: publish an annual estimate of milk utilisation by product, split between organised and informal channels.
  2. National Centre for Cold-Chain Development: publish a dated, state-wise cold-chain gap assessment stating its baseline and demand assumptions, so the gap is not quoted from roughly 8 million to 35 million tonnes.
  3. Department of Commerce: host the full India-EFTA TEPA tariff schedules, line by line, on its own website.
  4. Ministry of Food Processing Industries: publish the capacity added and output achieved by each PLI beneficiary in mozzarella and other dairy.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

What this piece does not establish. The share of India's milk production that is "processed" varies materially across the sources used here (40% processed vs ~25% through organised dairies specifically vs the ~35% implied by liquid-milk share alone) and was not reconciled to one authoritative figure; the "headroom" framing in Section 1 should be read as order-of-magnitude, not a precise number. India's total milk production was originally reported here from secondary aggregators that disagreed with each other (216.5 million tonnes for 2025 from a market-data aggregator vs. 228 million tonnes for 2025 and 239.2 million tonnes for 2023-24 from a CRISIL/APEDA dashboard) — since resolved to a primary source: a Lok Sabha answer from the Department of Animal Husbandry and Dairying, posted 12 August 2026, gives 247.87 MT for 2024-25 and 239.30 MT for 2023-24, the latter matching the CRISIL/APEDA figure to within 0.1 MT. The 216.5 MT and 228 MT aggregator figures for 2025 remain unreconciled against the government series and are no longer used in Section 1. Cheese-market valuation and CAGR figures come from a single market-research firm (IMARC) and were not cross-checked against a second independent estimate. Player market-share figures (Amul's 40-50%, Milky Mist's various shares) come from company disclosures and trade press, not independently audited; Amul's overall share in particular is a widely repeated but not freshly re-verified figure. The cold-chain shortfall estimate has the widest spread of any figure in this piece (8 million to 35 million tonnes) and that spread is presented deliberately rather than collapsed into one number. Paras Dairy's expansion targets (2,000 HoReCa outlets, top-5 brand ambition) are the company's own stated goals as of its most recent public announcements, not independently verified outcomes. Section 7's HSN 0406 fiscal-year trade figures were checked against the DGCI&S TradeStat portal (tradestat.commerce.gov.in, commodity-wise export and import reports) directly and match to the paisa; the calendar-year USD series is a separate dataset that was not re-checked, and the two cited series use different bases (Indian fiscal year in ₹crore vs calendar year in USD) that were not reconciled into one number — both are presented because they independently confirm the same growing-surplus trend, not because either is definitive. The claim that EFTA TEPA excludes dairy is well-sourced and specific (multiple independent legal/trade-advisory summaries agree), but this piece did not check whether any other trade agreement India has signed or is negotiating does offer cheese-specific tariff concessions. The exclusion of ghee (HSN 0405), butter, milk powder (0402) and the cheese lines of 0406 (including the residual line covering paneer) from India's concessions was checked against the treaty appendices published by the Department of Commerce (read via an archive copy, because efta.int is behind Cloudflare). Read line by line: India's schedule to Switzerland; for Iceland and Norway the butter, ghee and cheese lines were checked; the Iceland and Norway annexes of their own concessions to India were only partly read, so this piece does not state what they offer on cheese.

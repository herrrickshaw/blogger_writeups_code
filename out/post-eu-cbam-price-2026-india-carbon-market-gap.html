EU CBAM price: €82.32 in Q3 2026, with no carbon certificate traded in India

Top line

  • €82.32 a tonne of CO2e was the EU's CBAM certificate price for Q3 2026, 9.4% above Q2. India’s carbon market has traded no certificate; the €82.32 is about Rs 8,880, roughly 10 to 22 times the Rs 400–900 analysts expect (one source).
  • 12 Jan 2027 is the next date CERC lists the three exchange petitions, 8 days after the Q4 price (4 January) and 20 days before CBAM certificate sales open on 1 February; BEE’s trading procedure is still a draft.
  • 0 NABCB-recognised CBAM verifiers on 6 February 2026, then 6 applications by 12 October against a government aim of at least 10. Steel is 83% of the €5.30 billion of CBAM goods the EU imported from India in 2025.

What it means: Each 2026 quarterly price is set from EU emissions-trading auctions and applies to goods imported in that quarter, so a shipment in July to September carries €82.32 whenever the certificate is bought, while an Indian certificate has no price to compare or deduct.

What to do: Exporters and EU buyers should index CBAM cost clauses to the published quarterly price now and to the weekly price from 2027, and budget 2026 shipments with no Indian carbon-price deduction.

India's certificate market: two rulebooks pending before the first trade

  1. 27 Feb 2026CERC notifies the regulations for purchase and sale of Carbon Credit Certificates, with trading on power exchanges.
  2. 31 Jul 2026Original deadline for first-year compliance filings (Form A); a three-month extension was reported on 8 October by Carbon Pulse, as cited by Climate Decode.
  3. 16 OctBEE lists 18 accredited carbon verification agencies for the scheme (S&P Global).
  4. 19 OctBEE issues the draft Detailed Procedure for transacting certificates, open for comments for 15 days; it gives no date for a first session.
  5. 23 OctCERC admits petitions from PXIL, IEX and HPX, notes that BEE has not yet formulated the procedure, and lists them for 12 January 2027.
  6. 4 Jan 2027European Commission publishes the Q4 2026 CBAM price.
  7. 12 Jan 2027CERC hearing on the exchange petitions.
  8. 1 Feb 2027CBAM certificate sales open to importers; 2026 declarations and surrender are due by 30 September 2027.

What it means: The petitions return to CERC 81 days after the 23 October hearing, so first exchange trading can come only after BEE finalises its procedure and CERC approves the exchanges’ contracts, and officials’ start dates range from early 2027 (S&P Global) to the end of 2027 for most sectors (one 4 November report).

What to do: BEE should publish the final procedure, the floor and forbearance prices and a first-session date, and CERC should list the exchange approvals together with the procedure.

What it means: Almost all of the exposure is steel and aluminium, the sectors where India’s own scheme has no traded price, with iron and steel targets still in draft and no fertiliser target, so little carbon price paid at home can yet count at the border.

What to do: The Commerce Ministry and the Ministry of Steel should publish EU-bound exports by CBAM product for FY2025-26 from TradeStat on one schedule, so exporters can size the 2026 bill against the €82.32 price.

EU talks on widening CBAM to downstream goods
  • 17 December 2025: Commission proposes about 180 downstream steel and aluminium products from 1 January 2028.
  • 12 June and 15 September 2026: Council and Parliament adopt positions; Parliament’s vote was 464 to 50 with 159 abstentions, and trade press puts its list at about 457 product types.
  • 20 October: first trilogue ends with no overall agreement, according to press reports; four issues went to technical work (an aluminium threshold, scrap, online platforms, default values).
  • 20 November: Council ambassadors (Coreper II) prepare the trilogue, per the Council agenda of 13 November. The second trilogue is reported for 30 November, not confirmed by an EU readout.
  • The counts of 180, about 200 and 457 use different units (products, codes, types) and are not final.

What would fix this

  1. BEE and CERC: publish the final Detailed Procedure, the floor and forbearance prices and a first-session date, and decide the exchange petitions in one listing.
  2. Ministry of Power and BEE: notify the iron and steel and fertiliser targets so the scheme covers the sectors the EU charges.
  3. Commerce Ministry and NABCB: publish the verifier applicant list, the assessment calendar and the EU’s decision date on recognising NABCB.
  4. Commerce Ministry: publish FY2025-26 EU-bound exports for each CBAM product from TradeStat.
  5. European Commission: state which carbon prices it will recognise under its draft Article 9 rules and what evidence an Indian certificate must carry.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

Method and definitions
  • CBAM prices are read from the European Commission’s own page. The percentage changes and day counts are computed by this blog.
  • One Indian certificate (CCC) is one tonne of CO2e; the EU price is also per tonne of CO2e. The two are not like-for-like: the Indian scheme sets intensity targets and prices only the gap to target, and the EU price is an auction average.
  • The rupee comparison uses International Carbon Trade’s own conversion of €82.32 to about Rs 8,880 and its analyst range of Rs 400–900; both come from one publisher.
  • Dates for CERC and BEE come from their own documents; exchange trading rules (monthly sessions, last Thursday) are from secondary summaries of BEE’s draft and may change.
What this piece could not establish
  • EU-bound exports by CBAM product for FY2025-26 from TradeStat: not obtained here. The sector split is Eurostat for calendar 2025 through a secondary page; the FY2025-26 steel figure is an official’s round number.
  • Whether any Carbon Credit Certificate has been issued: no source confirms issuance, and the Q3 price comparison in rupees rests on one publisher’s conversion and analyst range.
  • The start date for Indian trading: S&P Global reports officials projecting early 2027; a 4 November report (OneStop ESG, single page) cites the BEE director for the end of 2027 for most sectors. The two are not reconciled.
  • Cement: sources disagree (under €1 million, 84,000 tonnes, US$162 million in 2024), so it is not charted.
  • The deduction rules: the Commission’s draft Article 9 rules of 13 May 2026 are cited through Climate Decode only.

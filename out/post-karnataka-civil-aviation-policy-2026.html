Top line

  • 19 November 2026 is the date the Karnataka cabinet approved the proposal to implement the State Civil Aviation Policy 2026, in one wire report; no policy text, target, outlay or incentive rate was found, so only the minister’s statements of 10 and 18 November describe it.
  • Rs 542.96 crore is the revised cost approved the same day for Hassan airport, 2.8 times the Rs 193.65 crore sanctioned in 2021.
  • 59.2% is the fall in aircraft movements at four Karnataka UDAN airports from 2021-22 to 2024-25, and the three-year subsidy at Shivamogga ended on 21 November 2026, with the last subsidised flights on 20 November.

Cabinet approved the policy on 19 November 2026; no text, target or outlay was found

  1. 10 Nov 2026 (Tuesday)Infrastructure Development Minister M.B. Patil says a comprehensive civil aviation policy will go to the next cabinet; it covers financial assistance, viability gap funding (VGF) and the management of state-run airports (The Hindu, ANI, Deccan Chronicle).
  2. 18 Nov 2026 (Wednesday)Minister: the policy will be approved at the next cabinet meeting, with priority for MRO, aircraft manufacturing, components and the supply chain, and a suppliers’ conference before the Global Investors Meet (IANS, ANI).
  3. 19 Nov 2026 (Thursday)Cabinet approves the proposal to implement the Karnataka State Civil Aviation Policy-2026, and administrative approval for Hassan airport at a revised Rs 542.96 crore (IANS; the Hassan item is also in PTI and The Hindu).
  4. 20 Nov 2026 (Friday)Star Air ends its UDAN flights from Shivamogga as the three-year subsidy period closes; it takes weekend bookings on the Hyderabad route only, at commercial fares without subsidy (The Hindu).
  5. 21 Nov 2026 (Saturday)Date from which no scheduled flights were expected at Shivamogga (The Hindu, 6 and 12 November).

What to do: The Infrastructure Development Department should publish the approved policy with its targets, VGF rates, outlay and list of airports, so that its provisions can be read rather than inferred.

What to do: The Infrastructure Development Department should publish the cost build-up, the completion date and the operating model for Hassan airport, since the new policy is meant to cover state-managed airports.

What would fix this

  1. Karnataka Infrastructure Development Department: publish the approved Civil Aviation Policy 2026 with its targets, VGF rates, outlay and the list of airports it covers.
  2. Karnataka Civil Aviation Department: publish yearly movements, passengers and subsidy for each state airport on one basis, with sources.
  3. Union Ministry of Civil Aviation and the state: state in one note which support period applies to routes awarded under the original UDAN scheme and which under Modified UDAN.
  4. Karnataka Infrastructure Development Department: publish the cost build-up, completion date and operating model for Hassan airport.
  5. Karnataka Information and Public Relations Department: release cabinet decisions in one dated note, so that a policy approval does not rest on a single wire report.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

Method and definitions
  • The approval date comes from a cabinet briefing reported by IANS on 19 November 2026; the Hassan item in the same briefing is also reported by PTI and The Hindu, which do not mention the policy in the excerpts read.
  • Percentage changes and ratios are computed here from the figures shown. The 33% passenger fall is computed from figures the source gives to one decimal in lakh and is approximate.
  • News reports were read as search excerpts and are labelled secondary. Parliamentary replies and Government of India releases were read as excerpts; the Modified UDAN components are those in the excerpts.
  • Statements by ministers and members of Parliament about the Centre, other states or each other are not reported; the piece keeps to dated decisions, figures and the stated scope of the policy.
What this piece could not establish
  • The policy text, and with it any target, outlay, VGF rate, eligibility rule or list of airports: none was found. Its coverage is known only from the minister’s statements of 10 and 18 November.
  • That the policy was approved as drafted: the report says the cabinet approved the proposal to implement it, one wire service carries this, and a second report was not found. A republished summary calls it ‘formally adopted’; that wording is not in the wire text.
  • Whether the policy replaces or adds to the state industrial and aerospace policies, including the Aerospace Policy 2026-2031 approved on 9 September 2026 that this blog has not reviewed.
  • The amount the state spent on Kalaburagi airport: the minister said Rs 1,000 crore, the Times of India says over Rs 175 crore, and the central UDAN expenditure shown for Kalaburgi is Rs 28.64 crore. The three may measure different things, and none was reconciled.
  • Why the Airports Authority’s FY2025-26 traffic for Hubballi, Belagavi and Mysuru (7.7 lakh) is more than twice the Times of India figure for four airports in 2024-25 (3.3 lakh): the basis of the Times of India series is not stated.
  • Whether Modified UDAN’s five-year VGF applies to Shivamogga’s routes, and why the minister describes the support as three years: the scheme documents read do not say. The count of stopped airports also differs (15 on 20 January, 17 on 10 November).
  • Four Modified UDAN components sum to Rs 28,440 crore against a total of Rs 28,840 crore; the fifth component was not in the excerpts read.
  • Whether any flights resumed at Shivamogga after 21 November, and whether the state will fund VGF for Star Air: the airline’s condition of 100% VGF was reported on 20 November and no decision was found.

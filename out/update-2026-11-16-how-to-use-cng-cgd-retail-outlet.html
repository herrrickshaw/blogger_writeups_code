Why it is useful

  • Who benefits: CNG and EV-charging developers, gas-distribution companies, consultants and regulators-watchers who need to know who may supply CNG at a given pump.
  • What it replaces: reading the regulator's state-by-state PDF list and matching districts to pumps by hand.
  • Evidence from the run: 17 of 17 tests pass; the linkage table shows 82,261 outlets in an authorised area, 305 in districts with no authorised company and 27 outside the district map.
  • The per-state view is instantly useful: Gujarat has 23.9% of its outlets already CNG-equipped, Haryana 22.1%, against about 10% in West Bengal, Karnataka and Rajasthan.

What it is for

The repo parses the regulator PNGRB's list of authorised City Gas Distribution geographical areas (309 areas, 58 companies), assigns each petrol pump to a district by point-in-polygon, and publishes the result as a table plus a self-contained map webpage.

Authorisation is not the same as a built pipeline: an outlet inside an authorised area can be supplied by the authorised company, but the list does not say how far its steel network has reached.

Indicative planning data, not legal advice or an official registry.

Who it is for

  • CNG and EV-charging site planners
  • Energy consultants and analysts
  • Students of India's gas-distribution regulation
Not for.
  • Legal conclusions about who holds a licence in a specific place (check the regulator)
  • Assuming pipeline gas is available; the data is about authorisation only
  • Latest bidding rounds; the committed list runs through round 12A and was a 22 Jul 2026 snapshot

Install

  1. Clone the repository (needs git)
    git clone https://github.com/herrrickshaw/cng-cgd-retail-outlet-mapping.git
cd cng-cgd-retail-outlet-mapping
  2. Create a virtual environment
    python3 -m venv .venv && source .venv/bin/activate
  3. Install the dependencies
    pip install -r requirements-dev.txt

Quick start: commands and what you should see

Run the tests

pytest -q

17 passed in under a second. The tests use small fixtures and do not touch the network.

Summarise the linkage table

python3 - <<'E'
import csv,gzip,collections
rows=list(csv.DictReader(gzip.open('data/outlets_cgd.csv.gz','rt')))
print(len(rows));print(collections.Counter(r['cgd_status'] for r in rows))
print(sum(r['has_cng']=='True' for r in rows))
E

Limits and honest caveats

  • Authorisation, not pipeline reach, and not licence status; verify with the regulator before acting.
  • Snapshot of 22 Jul 2026; the SSRI source has since grown to 107,380 raw records and newer PNGRB bidding rounds are not included.
  • The pipeline cannot be fully rerun from a fresh clone: join_final.py needs a 47 MB district geojson that is not committed, and parse_ga.py expects the regulator PDF, which the public repo has replaced with a markdown extract. I ran only the tests and read the committed outputs.
  • Chhota Udaipur and several districts are left unassigned rather than guessed; 45 districts fall under more than one area and the map colours the first-listed company.
  • Counts differ slightly from the README (11,198 vs 11,194 CNG outlets).
  • The raw outlet source contains duplicate rows; the repo removes them before linking.

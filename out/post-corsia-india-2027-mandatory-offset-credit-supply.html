CORSIA from 2027: India joins with about 44 million eligible units labelled

Skip to article content
Text Size

Top line

  • 134 states are in CORSIA offsetting from 1 January 2027, up from 130. ICAO’s July 2026 list adds Brazil, China, India and Russia. India’s 2018 traffic share was 1.35%, above the 0.5% threshold.
  • About 44 million units were labelled as eligible by late September 2026, ICAO says. Demand for 2024 to 2026 is estimated at 105 to 150 million (ICAO) or 169.5 to 214.8 million (IATA), due by 31 January 2028.
  • 2.58 million tonnes is the offset requirement ICCT projects for Indian carriers in 2027, rising to 4.61 million by 2035. Their first cancellation deadline is 31 January 2031.

CORSIA is the offsetting scheme of the International Civil Aviation Organization (ICAO) for international flights. Airlines offset growth in emissions above 85% of the 2019 level by cancelling approved carbon credits, called eligible emissions units.

The European Parliament’s COP31 study of October 2026 says participation becomes mandatory from 1 January 2027 for states above a 0.5% traffic share. ICAO’s own Resolution A42-22 confirms the rule, with exemptions for least developed, small island and landlocked developing states.

What it means: India did not volunteer for the 2021 to 2026 phases, and ICAO’s 2027 list applies the Assembly’s traffic rule, so routes between India and the other 133 listed states carry offsetting requirements from 2027.

What to do: Airlines flying to and from India should check their 2027 schedules against ICAO’s list of 134 states, because only routes between two listed states count.

What it means: Demand is 2.4 to 4.9 times the labelled supply, Guyana alone issued more than half of it, and ICAO puts the potential pool at 700 million to 1 billion units, of which IATA counts about 72 million under host-country letters of authorisation.

What to do: Host governments, India included, should publish how and when they will issue letters of authorisation, because that step decides how many units reach airlines.

What it means: On ICCT’s projection Indian carriers need about 8.3 million units for 2027 to 2029, and ICAO’s Annex 16 timeline sets cancellation for that period at 31 January 2031.

What to do: Indian airlines should plan purchases from their 2027 emissions onward, because supply for 2024 to 2026 had reached only about 44 million labelled units in the third year of that period.

Cost to Indian carriers: $781 million over nine years, $2.6 billion if units are scarce

What it means: The bill depends more on the unit price than on traffic, and first-phase units were assessed at $12.40 a tonne in late August 2026 (Fastmarkets) and averaged $12.85 in October (General Index), below the $21 to $27 that ICCT assumed.

What to do: The DGCA should add a penalty provision to its CORSIA requirements, which this blog did not find in the October 2024 text, so that airlines that buy units are not undercut by those that do not.

What India has put in place

Rules. The DGCA’s Civil Aviation Requirement, Section 10, Series C, Part I was first issued on 24 December 2018 and reissued on 8 October 2024. It covers monitoring, reporting, verification and the cancelling of units. The DGCA is to tell each operator its requirement by 30 November each year.

Airlines. A DGCA presentation to an ICAO seminar in May 2025 lists IndiGo, SpiceJet, Air India (with Vistara merged into it), Air India Express and Akasa Air as the Indian operators under CORSIA.

Preparation. The civil aviation minister reviewed readiness on 29 July 2026. The meeting discussed letters of authorisation and a CORSIA-compliant carbon market framework. This blog found no published outcome of either.

Reservations. India filed reservations to the CORSIA resolutions in 2016 and 2019. Its 2019 text asked for a baseline “closer to 2027” for developing states. ICAO’s lists for 2022 and 2025 record no reservation by India.

Sustainable aviation fuel covers little of the requirement

Airlines can also reduce their requirement with CORSIA-eligible fuels. IATA estimated in June 2026 that sustainable aviation fuel output will be about 2.4 million tonnes in 2026, 0.8% of aviation fuel use.

India’s indicative targets are 1% by 2027, 2% by 2028 and 5% by 2030 for international flights. ICCT estimates the 1% blend cuts the 2027 requirement by about 26 thousand tonnes, roughly 1%, and that eligible fuels meet 9.2% of the 2027 to 2035 total.

Two earlier posts cover the fuel side: corn ethanol jet fuel under CORSIA and hydrogen needed per litre of sustainable aviation fuel in India.

What would fix this

  1. DGCA: publish the enforcement and penalty provisions for airlines that do not cancel units, and the cancellation-report template, before the first 2027 emissions report.
  2. Ministry of Civil Aviation, with the environment ministry: publish the outcome of the July 2026 review on letters of authorisation, including whether Indian projects may supply units to airlines.
  3. Indian airlines: disclose projected CORSIA requirements and unit purchases in annual reports, starting with 2027.
  4. ICAO: report labelled units for the 2027 to 2029 period separately on its dashboard, next to an updated demand estimate.
  5. Host governments of approved projects: issue pending letters of authorisation before the 31 January 2028 deadline for 2024 to 2026.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

Phase 1 and phase 2: the dates that apply to Indian airlines
  • 2021 to 2023 (pilot) and 2024 to 2026 (first phase): voluntary for states. India stayed out; its airlines have reported emissions to the DGCA since 2019.
  • 31 January 2028: airlines of the 130 states in the first phase cancel units for 2024 to 2026. This deadline does not cover routes brought in by India’s entry.
  • 1 January 2027: the second phase starts (134 states). It runs to 2035 in three-year compliance periods.
  • 30 November 2030: states notify airlines of final requirements for 2027 to 2029.
  • 31 January 2031: cancellation deadline for 2027 to 2029, or 60 days after the state’s notification if that is later.
  • Units: eight programmes are approved for 2027 to 2029 after the ICAO Council’s November 2026 session. Units must come from emission reductions between 1 January 2021 and 31 December 2029.
Method
  • Traffic shares are ICAO’s 2018 figures; each was recomputed from ICAO’s million-RTK column and its total of 735,574 million.
  • Supply uses ICAO’s “approximately 44 million” labelled units. The multiples 2.4 and 4.9 are 105 ÷ 44 and 214.8 ÷ 44.
  • The Environmental Defense Fund’s “up to four times”, quoted by the European Parliament study, is 150 ÷ 41 million, which is 3.7.
  • The host-country table lists the rows legible in ICAO’s newsletter. They sum to 42.2 million; the rest of the table was cut off in the copy read.
  • India’s requirement and costs are ICCT projections. The 8.3 million for 2027 to 2029 is the sum of ICCT’s three yearly figures.
  • The $408 million bar is 31.76 million units at $12.85, held flat. It is an illustration, not a forecast.
What this piece could not establish
  • Whether India has notified penalties for non-compliance. The October 2024 DGCA text read here has none, and ICCT says no national penalty policy had been adopted when it published.
  • Each Indian airline’s share of the requirement. ICCT publishes a national total only.
  • Whether India has issued any letter of authorisation for CORSIA units.
  • A price for units eligible for 2027 to 2029. The prices quoted are for first-phase units.
  • How many units are labelled for 2027 to 2029. ICAO’s dashboard figure covers the first phase.
  • ICAO’s live dashboard itself. The figure used is from ICAO’s newsletter.

Caveats: Counts of labelled units differ by date and method: ICAO counted 32.4 million on 9 March 2026, the Environmental Defense Fund read about 41 million in late July, ICAO about 44 million in late September, IATA about 43 million in October, and Carbon Pulse counted about 42 million on 30 October. Demand estimates differ more: ICAO’s committee gives 105 to 150 million tonnes for 2024 to 2026 (the Environmental Defense Fund quotes 100 to 150), IATA gave 200 to 250 million in September and 169.5 to 214.8 million in October. The European Parliament study describes the 0.5% rule as a share of traffic; ICAO’s text specifies 2018 revenue tonne-kilometres and adds a second test, the states that together make up 90% of traffic. Unit prices are from price-reporting agencies, not ICAO. India’s requirement and cost figures are projections by one research group.

India's retail inflation: 6.09% in October 2026, above the RBI's 6% upper limit

Skip to article content
Text Size

Top line

  • 6.09% was India’s retail inflation in October 2026, up from 5.54% in September and 0.09 points above the RBI’s 6% upper limit; it has risen in every month since January’s 2.74%.
  • 116.22% was onion inflation, with garlic at 60.74% and ginger at 48.32%; the five fastest-rising items weigh 1.78% of the basket and add roughly 1.4 points on a weight-times-inflation estimate.
  • 5.94% would be the average monthly rate for November to March if the RBI’s October projection of 5.2% for 2026-27 holds (a simple average, computed here); the next MPC meets on 2–4 December.

What it means: October is the highest of the ten monthly readings published on the 2024-based series, and the RBI’s own projection of 5.2% for the year implies about 5.9% a month for the remaining five months, so the path from here depends on whether October is the peak.

What to do: Readers tracking rates should watch the 2–4 December MPC decision and the 14 December release of November CPI, the two dates that show whether the October reading is a peak.

What would fix this

  1. MoSPI: publish each item’s and division’s contribution to headline inflation with every monthly release, with the method and the base-price adjustment stated.
  2. Department of Consumer Affairs: publish onion buffer-stock quantities, release dates and prices with its daily price bulletin during the lean months.
  3. Reserve Bank of India: publish the monthly path behind its 2026-27 projection in the December policy statement, so the 5.2% can be compared with the readings after October.
  4. MoSPI: state in each release which items have thin price quotations in the state-level tables, since the release itself warns that some state item indices use thin samples.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

Method and definitions
  • All CPI figures are from MoSPI’s press release of 12 November 2026 (base 2024=100, provisional for October). Inflation is the year-on-year change in the index.
  • The contribution estimates use weight times October inflation divided by 100. They treat each weight as the item’s share a year ago, which is only approximately true when relative prices have moved, so they are rough points, not MoSPI figures.
  • The implied November-to-March average is the RBI’s 5.2% projection times 12, less the sum of the seven April-to-October readings, divided by five. It treats the projection as a simple average of monthly rates; the RBI made the projection before the October reading.
  • Press reports compare October’s 6.09% with 6.21% in October 2024. That earlier figure is from the old 2012-based series and is not on the same base; the 2024-based series publishes inflation rates only from January 2026.
What this piece could not establish
  • The causes. The RBI Bulletin lists a deficient monsoon, El Niño conditions and high energy prices; the release gives item and division rates, not a decomposition. Trade-press reports of depleted onion stocks and uneven rain are not tested here.
  • Item contributions in MoSPI’s own terms: the release does not publish them, so the 1.4 points and 0.8 points are this blog’s approximations.
  • Whether October is the peak. November data is due on 14 December; the repo-rate decision is due after the 2–4 December meeting. Economists’ expectations of a second rise (DBS, CareEdge) are forecasts, not decisions.
  • State rankings below the top five and above the lowest: Annexure III is listed in full in the release, but only the chart values for the top five are given by the release itself.
  • October values are provisional and are revised the following month.

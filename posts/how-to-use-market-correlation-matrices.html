Why it is useful

  • Who benefits: students and analysts who want to see how tightly stocks in a market move together, for diversification studies or teaching.
  • What it replaces: dense CSV files of up to 837 MB that were never committed. The README says the same information is about 24 times smaller here, in regular git files.
  • Evidence from the run: the rebuild script ran end to end in about 17 seconds on a local copy of the price warehouse, and the six summary files load instantly. The rebuilt Europe dense matrix is 1,420 by 1,420, and a lookup of the most similar stocks to a name takes one line.
  • The summaries are derived aggregates (mean, median, counts, top pairs) computed from daily price returns, so you can compare markets without any raw price data.

What it is for

For each market the repo stores three files: a dense correlation matrix (float16, with a symbol list), a table of pairs whose correlation is at least 0.6 in absolute value, and a small summary. Correlation is the Pearson measure on daily log returns over the last 252 trading days, using the 3,000 most traded symbols.

It is a descriptive snapshot, not a model and not a signal. Not investment advice: this is research and education material built from public-source or derived data; it is not a recommendation to buy or sell anything.

Who it is for

  • Students learning about correlation, diversification and market structure
  • Analysts who want a quick cross-market comparison of how closely stocks move together
  • Non-programmers who want an AI assistant to explain what the numbers mean
Not for.
  • Trading signals. The README says the pairs file is descriptive, not a signal by itself.
  • Current conditions. The committed windows end between 29 June and 21 July 2026, and a rebuild on 23 October 2026 ends 21 to 22 October (China 1 July); windows are not aligned across markets.
  • Full-precision work. Values are stored as float16, about three decimal places.

Quick start: commands and what you should see

Read the per-market summaries

import json
for m in ['IN','US','JP','KR','CN','EU']:
    s = json.load(open(f'data/{m}/summary.json'))
    print(m, s['window'], s['symbols'], s['mean_pairwise_corr'])

One line per market with the window dates, symbol count and mean correlation.

Load a dense matrix and find a stock's closest neighbours

import numpy as np
z = np.load('data/EU/corr_dense.npz', allow_pickle=True)
c, sy = z['corr'].astype(float), list(z['symbols'])
k = sy.index('SIE.DE'); c[k,k] = -9
print([(sy[i], round(c[k,i],3)) for i in np.argsort(-c[k])[:3]])

The three symbols most correlated with Siemens (SIE.DE), with their r values.

Limits and honest caveats

  • Snapshot, not live: windows end 21 or 22 October 2026 for India, US, Japan, Korea and Europe, but 1 July 2026 for China (the warehouse copy has no later China bars). Cross-market comparisons therefore mix dates.
  • Data-quality finding: in the warehouse copy, many recent days (Sep to Oct 2026) hold only a small share of symbols (for example Europe days with 1 to 30 symbols, Japan days with 8 to 150, Korea days with 3 to 4). The repo script counts any date as a trading session, so the unmodified script's 252-day window absorbs these partial days and its Europe row differs (928 symbols, mean r 0.148). The numbers shown here come from a locally patched copy that counts a date only if at least half the market's usual symbol count traded and requires volume above zero; the unpatched run is reported in the status notes. The repo itself was not changed.
  • Some pairs are stale-price artefacts rather than co-movement: in the unpatched run a Korean pair showed r of exactly 1.0 because both series had a constant price and zero volume on recent days. The patch removes zero-volume bars.
  • The India universe includes ETFs and index funds (the README says the NSE panel is not ISIN-filtered), and several top India pairs between small caps and large banks (for example 0.96) are unexplained and not validated here. Filter before reading them as stock pairs. The US universe also contains trusts and ETFs.
  • Pairwise correlation on thinly traded stocks is noisy even with a 200-observation floor; float16 storage rounds to about three decimals. Europe's low mean is also affected by non-overlapping trading hours across venues.
  • Not investment advice: this is research and education material built from public-source or derived data; it is not a recommendation to buy or sell anything.

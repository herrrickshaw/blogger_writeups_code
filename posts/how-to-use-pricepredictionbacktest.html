What it is for

The repo has two parts. First, a walk-forward harness for three model families (a Markov regime chain, a Kalman trend filter, and LSTM/GRU nets) that forecast next-day returns and are scored on directional accuracy, RMSE and a frictionless long/short Sharpe against baselines.

Second, replay.py replays Darvas-style breakout and breakdown signals over ten-year panels for US, India, Japan, Korea and China, labels the market regime point-in-time, and reports excess returns versus the market median with t-statistics clustered by date.

The repo's conclusion is modest: daily large-cap returns are close to unpredictable, and the Markov layer is useful as a regime gate rather than a forecaster.

In this refresh I re-ran both parts from scratch on 23 October 2026: the model harness (including LSTM and GRU with PyTorch) and the replay, on panels rebuilt from a local copy of the author's price warehouse, then compared them with the committed results.

Who it is for

  • Students and researchers learning walk-forward evaluation and honest baselines
  • Developers who want a regime-gate template for breakout signals
  • Anyone checking whether a price-prediction claim survives costs and baselines
Not for.
  • Investment advice or trade signals. Not investment advice.
  • Live trading: P&L is frictionless and daily sign-flipping would be eaten by real costs
  • Trusting t-statistics at face value: the repo itself says overlapping 21-day windows make them optimistic

Quick start: commands and what you should see

Re-run the repo's regime statistics on the committed US signals.

PYTHONPATH=. python3 - <<'X'
import pandas as pd
from price_prediction.replay import clustered_stats
s=pd.read_parquet('reports/replay_signals_us.parquet')
print(len(s), s.Date.min().date(), s.Date.max().date(), s.Symbol.nunique())
print(clustered_stats(s,21,['signal','trend']).to_string())
X

On the committed file: 110,852 signals from 2016-09-15 to 2026-07-17 across 1,000 symbols, then the table shown in the sample outputs. With panels rebuilt to 21 Oct 2026 and `python -m price_prediction.replay --market US`, the same function gave 109,127 signals.

Summarise the model comparison.

python3 -c "import pandas as pd; d=pd.read_csv('reports/price_prediction_us.csv'); print(d.groupby('model')[['dir_acc','strat_sharpe','bh_sharpe']].mean().round(3))"

One row per model averaged over the 20 US symbols.

Full walk-forward run (needs panels and torch).

python -m price_prediction --market US --top 20 --test-days 500

Ran on 23 October 2026 against panels rebuilt from the warehouse: 20 symbols, 499 scored out-of-sample days each, about 15 minutes on a laptop CPU (the LSTM and GRU refits dominate).

Limits and honest caveats

  • Not investment advice. Research code on historical data; P&L is frictionless and ignores costs, slippage and borrow.
  • Universe membership uses full-sample dollar volume, a mild lookahead the repo documents. The top-20 US universe includes ETFs (SPY, QQQ, GLD).
  • Overlapping 21-day windows make the clustered t-statistics optimistic, as the repo says.
  • Data was rebuilt from a local warehouse copy, not the repo's own ten-year panels, which are not available. Recent warehouse days with only a few symbols (16 US days dropped) were removed before running, and India used split-adjusted prices, so differences from the committed tables mix data and universe changes. China's data ends 1 July 2026.
  • The full model harness did run, but it is slow: about 15 minutes for 20 symbols on a CPU with LSTM and GRU refits. The LSTM and GRU numbers come from a single run with one random initialisation, so small differences between them are not meaningful.
  • Passing seq_models={} to evaluate_symbol does not skip the neural nets (an empty dict is treated as 'use defaults'); a one-line patch is recorded in the status notes. The repo was not changed.
  • The repo recommends adjusted series for India because of raw-close split risk.

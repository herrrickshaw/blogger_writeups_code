Why it is useful

  • Who benefits: students, policy analysts and journalists who want to see why distribution company debt grows and which levers change it, with the consumer bill trade-off shown next to the debt.
  • What it replaces: a hand-built spreadsheet of the cost-of-supply versus revenue gap. The calculation is a short, readable loop of about forty lines.
  • Evidence from the run: on the default inputs, doing nothing takes debt from 40,000 to 175,288 crore rupees over seven years, and only the most aggressive scenario brings it down, at a 50.4 percent rise in the household bill.
  • Every input can be changed from the command line, so you can test your own what-ifs in seconds.

What it is for

discom_debt_calculator.py models the yearly economics: energy billed after losses, revenue collected, cost of supply, state subsidy actually received, interest on the debt, and the resulting closing debt. It compares five scenarios and prints a debt trajectory for the best one plus a household bill table.

It is a conceptual scenario calculator, not a forecast. Its own header says it uses flat cost growth, a single blended tariff and no demand response, and that it is no substitute for a regulatory tariff filing or audited accounts. The default company is illustrative. The repository is archived.

Who it is for

  • Students and analysts learning why electricity distribution companies accumulate debt
  • Writers who want a transparent model to illustrate policy trade-offs
  • Non-programmers who want an AI assistant to explain each line
Not for.
  • Forecasting any real company's debt. The defaults are illustrative and the model is deliberately simple.
  • Tariff petitions, rating work or investment decisions.
  • Concluding that a real policy mix will deliver the numbers printed here.

Quick start: commands and what you should see

Run the default illustrative company

python3.13 discom_debt_calculator.py

A header of inputs, a five-scenario table, a year-by-year trajectory for the best scenario, a household bill table and a short interpretation.

Change the inputs

python3.13 discom_debt_calculator.py --debt 50000 --acs 7.2 --arr 6.1 --loss 18

The same report for a company with a wider cost-revenue gap.

Get machine-readable output

python3.13 discom_debt_calculator.py --json

Inputs and year-by-year rows for every scenario as JSON.

A bare percent sign in an argparse help string. Newer Python versions check help strings when the option is defined.

Limits and honest caveats

  • A conceptual scenario calculator, not a forecast. The defaults are illustrative, not any real company's accounts.
  • Flat cost growth, one blended tariff, no demand response, and a fixed subsidy amount that does not grow with costs, so debt is very sensitive to those assumptions.
  • It crashes at start-up on Python 3.14 (ValueError: badly formed help string). I ran it on Python 3.13.
  • The script's interpretation text says operational levers cut debt without the full tariff burden, but on the default inputs reform alone ends with higher debt (139,081 crore) than a tariff-only path (108,496 crore).
  • The README's heading says subscription-model-revenue, and the second file (a Dirichlet random-numbers script exported from a notebook) is unrelated to power. I did not run it.
  • Not a tariff filing, rating opinion or investment advice.

Coal gasification: 9 applications in two rounds for a Rs 37,500 crore scheme

Skip to article content
Text Size

Top line

  • 9 applications came in under the Rs 37,500 crore scheme, 7 in Round 1 and 2 in Round 2 (both NTPC), from 5 companies; the scheme was sized for about 25 projects.
  • Rs 235.52 crore of the Rs 8,500 crore approved in 2024 had been paid by August 2026, 3.8% of the Rs 6,233.06 crore earmarked, all to Jindal Steel.
  • Rs 1.5 lakh crore is the forex saving the Coal Secretary cited on 5 November over five to seven years; a July Lok Sabha reply gave the same figure as an annual saving at maturity.

What it means: The ministry called Round 1 an overwhelming response and the trade press called Round 2 thin, but both rounds together are 36% of the projects the scheme expects and neither round published the capacity or cost of any application.

What to do: The Coal Ministry should publish the capacity, product and project cost of each application received, so the share of the 75 million tonnes a year that applicants cover can be counted.

What it means: The Rs 46,000 crore quoted for both schemes is a ceiling and not spending, since the Rs 8,500 crore scheme has paid 3.8% of its earmarked amount, with one project through financial closure and Jindal Steel’s first instalment equal to 41% of its own Rs 569.05 crore.

What to do: The Coal Ministry should publish the incentive earmarked, paid and still conditional for each project every quarter, so a reader can tell progress from approvals.

What it means: A Rs 1.5 lakh crore saving a year would offset 54% of the FY2025 import bill for LNG, urea, ammonia and methanol, while the same sum over five to seven years is Rs 21,429 to 30,000 crore a year, so the two readings differ by a factor of five to seven and only one can be right.

What to do: The Coal Ministry should publish the method behind the saving, with the import price, product volumes and years assumed, so that the annual and the multi-year readings can be tested against each other.

What would fix this

  1. Ministry of Coal: publish the capacity, product, cost and state of every application received in each round, with the names of those shortlisted.
  2. Ministry of Coal: publish a project-wise register that reconciles the 22.6 million tonnes a year across the Parliament replies, with status and incentive paid.
  3. Ministry of Coal and the Department of Economic Affairs: state the method for the forex saving, including whether Rs 1.5 lakh crore is annual or cumulative.
  4. Department of Economic Affairs: consider gasification for the infrastructure master list, which the Gasification Technology and Research Council says would ease financing.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

Method and definitions
  • Applications are those the Coal Ministry announced for the Rs 37,500 crore scheme: Round 1 on 8 September 2026 and Round 2 on 10 November 2026; this piece counts one application per project, as the ministry lists them.
  • Incentive figures are rupees crore from Parliament replies; “earmarked” is the amount set aside for approved projects and “paid” is the amount disbursed.
  • MTPA is million tonnes of coal a year. The 22.6 is as stated in the Rajya Sabha reply of 20 July 2026; the 97.6 is this blog’s sum of that and the 75 expected from the 2026 scheme.
  • The annual reading of the Secretary’s Rs 1.5 lakh crore divides it by 5 and by 7 years; this is an interpretation of an ambiguous statement and is labelled as such.
What this piece could not establish
  • The size, product and cost of each application: the ministry lists applicants and products but not capacity or project cost, so the share of the 75 million tonnes a year applied for is unknown.
  • Whether any project has been selected under the Rs 37,500 crore scheme: none was announced in the sources read.
  • Which of the two Parliament counts of the 22.6 million tonnes is right, and whether Jindal Steel’s plant is operational or under construction; the replies differ.
  • Whether the Rs 1.5 lakh crore saving is annual or over five to seven years; the Lok Sabha reply and the Secretary’s remark read differently, and the method is not published.
  • The composition of the Rs 2.77 lakh crore import figure: the ministry names LNG, urea, ammonia and methanol, while the 2024 scheme’s projects also replace ammonium nitrate, acetic acid and direct reduced iron.
  • The reasons for the thin Round 2 beyond one industry-body view in Business Standard; lower gas and chemical prices, financing, coal supply and Chinese equipment were cited, none measured.
  • The state of the Talcher project, covered in a separate post, and any application figure after 10 November.

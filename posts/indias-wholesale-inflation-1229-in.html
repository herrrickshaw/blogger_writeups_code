India's wholesale inflation: 12.29% in October 2026, fuel and power up 32.84%

Skip to article content
Text Size

Top line

  • 12.29% was India’s wholesale price inflation in October 2026, up from 11.39% in September and 9.92% in August; April to October averages 10.14%.
  • 32.84% was inflation in fuel and power, which has 14.11% of the weight and about 32% of the year’s rise; crude petroleum and natural gas rose 70.80% and mineral oils 45.57%.
  • 6.2 points separate wholesale inflation (12.29%) from retail inflation (6.09%); the retail index for electricity, gas and other fuels rose 3.71%, against 32.84% wholesale.

What would fix this

  1. DPIIT: publish each group’s and item’s contribution to the year-on-year change with every monthly WPI release.
  2. DPIIT and MoSPI: publish a concordance between WPI items and CPI items, starting with petrol, diesel, LPG, electricity, onion and edible oils.
  3. Ministry of Petroleum and PPAC: publish oil marketing companies’ monthly marketing margins, so that frozen pump prices and rising wholesale prices can be reconciled.
  4. DPIIT: publish a bridge between the 2011-12 and 2022-23 WPI series for 2025-26 and 2026-27, since earlier releases this year were on the old base.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

Method and definitions
  • WPI figures are the Ministry of Commerce and Industry’s provisional October 2026 release (base 2022-23), read through DD News, PTI and the PNB Economic Intelligence Cell. WPI inflation is the year-on-year change in the index.
  • The all-commodities index is the weighted sum of the three group indices; the group shares of the rise are weight times the change in each group index, with the October 2025 index inferred from the October 2026 index and the published inflation rate. The inferred indices are rounded, so the shares are approximate.
  • Retail figures are MoSPI’s CPI for October 2026 (base 2024=100): general 6.09%, food 8.94%, electricity, gas and other fuels 3.71%, operation of personal transport equipment 7.52%.
  • WPI and CPI cover different goods at different stages with different weights, so the gap is a guide to where price pressure sits, not a measure of pass-through.
What this piece could not establish
  • The Ministry’s own release: the DPIIT page read here still held the May 2026 release (old 2011-12 base), so every WPI figure rests on press and bank reproductions of the October release. Item weights below the three groups are not given.
  • Pump-price history: the report that Delhi petrol and diesel prices are unchanged for weeks is a single source, and PPAC’s own price series was not read.
  • Whether the series change affects comparisons: releases earlier in 2026 were on base 2011-12, so figures such as April’s 8.3% are not on the same basis as October’s 12.29%.
  • Causes: the release names mineral oils, crude petroleum and natural gas, electricity, food articles, basic metals, chemicals and others as drivers, but not their order. The West Asia conflict is cited in trade coverage of the energy rise, not in the release.
  • October values are provisional and are revised after ten weeks.

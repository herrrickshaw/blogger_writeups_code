India’s approved list of solar-cell factories now totals 37,796 MW a year of enlisted capacity, from 19 company names running 21 units, according to my sum of the Ministry of New and Renewable Energy’s (MNRE) 12th revision of ALMM List-II, dated 8 October 2026. That is the number that decides how many rooftop and open-access solar projects can be built with Indian cells. Under MNRE’s office memorandum of 18 July 2026, those projects can still commission without List-II cells until 31 December 2026. After that the list applies to them in full.

A date to get right. The cell rule for net-metering and open-access projects dates from 1 June 2026, not 18 June. The 18 July memorandum is the document that moved the exemption from 31 May to 31 December. I read the scanned memorandum page directly to confirm this, because text extraction misreads “1st” as “18”.
Updated 7 November 2026. A 13th revision of List-II has been reported since this piece was written: Energetica India says it added 7,063 MW from two new entrants, GREW Energy and Ampin Solar One (a joint venture of Ampin and Jupiter International, 1,160 MW of mono-PERC cells at Bhubaneswar), taking enlisted cell capacity to 44,858 MW (Energetica India). That is a single trade report, not checked against the MNRE memorandum, and other October coverage still gives 37.8 GW; the tables below remain on the 12th revision of 8 October.

What the rule says, and what the July memorandum changed

The framework dates from MNRE’s memorandum of 9 December 2024. Modules must come from ALMM List-I, and those modules must in turn use cells from List-II. For net-metering and open-access renewable projects, the memorandum of 18 July 2026 recites that the rule bites on projects commissioned on or after 1 June 2026. It then does three things:

  • It refuses a blanket extension of List-II for solar power projects.
  • It gives net-metering and open-access projects a limited window to commission without List-II cells until 31 December 2026. In its words, such projects commissioned after 31 December 2026 “will be required to comply” with List-II.
  • It supersedes MNRE’s memorandum of 25 May 2026 and four June memoranda (8 June twice, 15 June and 23 June).

The earlier 25 May memorandum was stricter. It also refused a blanket extension, but offered relief only case by case. A developer had to show that every module was already installed on site (Category I), or that “effective steps” had been taken (Category II). The second category needed at least 75% of the land, financial closure, a connectivity grant, approved electrical drawings and modules on site. Claims had to be filed on a National Institute of Solar Energy portal by 30 June 2026. The 18 July text, as I read it, lists no such conditions for the window. The test is the commissioning date. I could not confirm how states and distribution companies are applying it.

The list has roughly tripled in fourteen months, from 13,067 MW to 37,796 MW. The biggest single jump was the third revision (15 December 2025), when Waaree’s 5,251 MW went on. My running total for the ninth revision, 35,468 MW, is within 1 MW of the 35,467 MW that trade outlet Energetica India reports for that revision. That is a useful independent check on the method.

Cells are the narrow end of the pipe

Modules are far easier to build than cells. Energetica India, which tracks MNRE’s List-I, reports cumulative enlisted module capacity of 228,524 MW after the September 2026 update. That is six times the cell capacity: 228,524 divided by 37,796 is 6.0. A module line needs cells, so List-II is where the constraint would show first.

Compare the cell capacity with what India installs. JMK Research, citing MNRE, puts solar additions in FY2026 at about 44.6 GW, of which about 34.8 GW ground-mounted and 8.7 GW rooftop. Enlisted cell capacity of 37.8 GW is about 85% of the all-segment total and 4.3 times the rooftop figure.

Do not read this as supply versus demand. The comparison is indicative only. Enlisted capacity is a factory’s annual nameplate, not what it ships, and a plant listed this month may not be at full output. The installation figures may mix AC and DC reporting bases, which I could not check. And List-II binds only some projects. Utility-scale projects won on bids submitted before 31 August 2025 are exempt, so part of the 34.8 GW of ground-mounted additions never needed List-II cells. The segment that does need them from January is net-metering and open access, and I have no published count of that demand.

What to watch before 31 December

  • Whether the window moves again. MNRE issued five memoranda on the deadline between 25 May and 18 July, and the 18 July one itself records that the earlier dispensation ran only to 31 May 2026.
  • Which plants ramp. The list shows capacity MNRE has enlisted, not cells delivered. Avaada’s entry went from 610 MW to 3,621 MW in a single month (22 July to 21 August).
  • Geography. A developer in a state with no enlisted cell plant must buy from Gujarat, Tamil Nadu or Telangana, which together hold 67% of the list.
  • Efficiency claims. The list carries manufacturer-claimed efficiency and wattage ranges, not tested values.

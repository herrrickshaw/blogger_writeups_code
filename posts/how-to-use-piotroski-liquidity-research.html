piotroski-liquidity-research asks whether the Piotroski F-score premium is explained by firm size or by stock liquidity, using point-in-time backtests. Its headline is that the premium tracks illiquidity, with a capacity of about $250,000. I re-ran its US scripts on 23 October 2026 against a US price panel whose last session is 21 October 2026 and SEC EDGAR filings filed up to 20 October 2026. Not investment advice. Educational and research use only; historical averages on short samples, and the repo's own numbers are provisional.

Why it is useful

  • Who benefits: students and independent researchers who want a worked, honest example of a point-in-time factor study, including the data bugs found along the way.
  • What it replaces: guessing whether an F-score screen survives size and liquidity controls; the repo ships the scripts, the paper and the result tables.
  • Evidence from the run: the US scripts (completeness audit, daily Darvas x F-score scan, weight-preset sweep, cost-versus-edge) all ran end to end on 23 October 2026 and regenerated their result files in under a minute each.
  • Evidence from the run: in the regenerated daily signal file (16,333 signals, 1,802 tickers) the median 12-month forward return rose from -2.8% for the lowest-turnover tier to +8.9% for the highest, the same direction as the committed file (-3.1% to +8.2%). Almost all of those signals fall in 2023 to 2026, so it is a short, regime-dependent window.

What it is for

The repo studies one question: is the Piotroski F-score premium conditioned by firm size or by liquidity? It double-sorts liquidity within size, uses point-in-time market cap and SEC EDGAR filing dates, keeps delisted names, and reports medians rather than means.

Its headline table says the premium is largest in large but illiquid firms and comes from weak-F names falling, not strong-F names rising. It then estimates execution costs and puts a capacity ceiling near $250,000.

The README opens with a warning that its own figures are being regenerated after data defects were found, so treat every number as provisional. That candour is part of what makes it worth reading.

The scripts need an EDGAR filings table and a US price panel that the repo does not ship. For this post I re-ran the US scripts on a US price panel (last session 21 October 2026) and an EDGAR filings table (filed up to 20 October 2026), with the hard-coded file paths pointed at those copies in a scratch clone. The EDGAR table is dense only from fiscal 2021, so the re-run covers roughly 2023 to 2026, not the repo's 2016 to 2025. I did not use the India scripts or file, which derive from screener.in data.

Who it is for

  • Students learning point-in-time backtesting, survivorship handling and why medians beat means
  • Researchers who want a template for liquidity-conditioned factor tests
  • Anyone sceptical of factor claims who wants to see the validation failures documented
Not for.
  • Investment advice or trading signals. Not investment advice.
  • Reproducing the headline tables without the EDGAR and price panels the README lists
  • Trusting any number as final: the README says the tables are provisional

Quick start: commands and what you should see

Check the sample-size claim before trusting anything (run this first, as the README says).

python3 data_completeness_audit.py

One line: fundamentals tickers, price tickers and their overlap. On 23 Oct 2026: 6,897 | 10,110 | 6,889 (100% / 68%).

Regenerate the daily Darvas x F-score signal file and the tier table.

python3 daily_breakout_combo_us.py

About 40 seconds. Prints signal counts, a tier table and a per-year table, and rewrites reports/daily_breakout_combo_us.csv.

Run the weight-preset sweep, then the cost test that reads its output.

python3 sweep_piotroski_plus_us.py && python3 cost_vs_edge.py

About 15 seconds each. The cost test prints net edge by tier at ten portfolio sizes.

Two things. The README's capacity ceiling of about $250k is consistent with these costs (15% of daily volume at $250k). But the script defines a 20%-of-volume ceiling (ADV_CEILING_PCT) and never applies it, so it still prints SURVIVES at $500k and $1M, where positions are 30% and 60% of daily volume.   • Using reports/daily_breakout_combo_us.csv, compute the median and win rate of wret by tier and by year and tell me which results are most regime-dependent.
  • List every data defect the README says was found, and for each say whether it is fixed and which results it could change.

Limits and honest caveats

  • Not investment advice. Educational and research use only.
  • The README states that numbers in reports/ are provisional and are being regenerated after eight defects were found; one (lender EBIT and ROCE) is documented but unfixed. My re-run does not settle any of them.
  • Data freshness: US prices run to the 2026-10-21 session and EDGAR filings to 2026-10-20, but the price panel is patchy: on several recent days only 2,000 to 3,000 of roughly 7,000 US symbols have a bar (for example 15 and 16 October). Anything that needs a clean daily history should be checked with the audit first.
  • The EDGAR table I used is dense only from fiscal 2021 (about 4,700 tickers in 2021 versus a few dozen per year before 2020). So the re-run is a 2023 to 2026 test with 98.6% of signals in those four years, not the repo's 2016 to 2025 sample, and the results are not comparable one-for-one with the committed tables.
  • Power is low and windows overlap: the sweep has only four rebalance years with forward returns, no standard errors are reported, and outliers (mean 34% versus median 15% in the universe) suggest unadjusted corporate actions. The India scripts were not run and the India file is not shown because it derives from screener.in data.
  • The cost test's gross edges are hard-coded constants, and its 20%-of-volume ceiling is defined but unused (patch saved, not pushed).

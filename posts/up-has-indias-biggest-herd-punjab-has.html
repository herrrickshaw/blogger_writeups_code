UP Has India's Biggest Herd. Punjab Has India's Best Yield. The Census Behind Both Numbers Is Six Years Old.

India is the world's largest milk producer, and the government's own December 2025 answer to the Rajya Sabha proves it with a state-by-state livestock count. What it doesn't mention: that count is the 20th Livestock Census, taken in 2019. A fully digitised 21st census finished its fieldwork in February 2025 — ten months before that answer was tabled — and its results still hadn't been published. Laid against the government's own current-year production data, the stale headcount and the fresh output numbers tell two different stories about which states actually run India's dairy sector.

The headcount Parliament was given is a 2019 snapshot

A newer, fully digitised census exists. Its results just aren't out yet.

On 17 December 2025, the Ministry of Fisheries, Animal Husbandry and Dairying answered a Rajya Sabha question on livestock population with a state-wise table of cattle, buffalo, sheep and goat numbers — sourced, the answer says explicitly, to the “20th Livestock Census All India Reports.” That census was conducted in 2019 and put India's total livestock population at 536.76 million, a 4.8% rise over the 19th Census in 2012. It is six years old at the point it was cited in Parliament.

A newer count exists. The 21st Livestock Census — fully digitised, with mobile-app data collection, GPS-tagged enumeration and breed-level detail across all 219 ICAR-NBAGR-recognised indigenous breeds — ran its field enumeration from October 2024 to February 2025. That fieldwork finished ten months before the December 2025 Rajya Sabha answer was tabled, and this article could not locate a published state-wise result set from it as of research. Until the 21st Census numbers are released, every state-wise livestock-population figure the government cites — including the one placed on the record in December 2025 — is a 2019 measurement of a sector that has since grown at nearly 13% a year.

Uttar Pradesh, West Bengal and Madhya Pradesh carry India's largest cattle herds; Uttar Pradesh's buffalo count — 3.3 crore — is more than double the next-largest state's. Rajasthan tops goat population outright and ranks third in sheep. This is the population base the country's dairy economy sits on, as counted in 2019.

What the same states actually produced in 2024-25

Scale and efficiency turn out to be two different rankings.

The Basic Animal Husbandry Statistics 2025 report — DAHD's current-year survey, covering March 2024 to February 2025, published 18 November 2025 — measures milk production directly rather than inferring it from herd size. National milk production reached 247.87 million tonnes in 2024-25, up 3.58% on 2023-24, with per-capita availability rising to 485 grams/day. The sector's compound annual growth rate from 2014-15 to 2023-24 was 12.77%, and livestock now contributes more than 31% of agricultural GVA and 5.5% of India's total GVA. Globally, India ranks 1st in milk, 2nd in eggs, and 4th in meat.

Uttar Pradesh's position holds on raw output: it produces 15.66% of India's milk, more than any other state, consistent with its lead in cattle-plus-buffalo population. But its 2024-25 growth rate was 0.09% — essentially flat — the slowest of any of the 15 major producing states charted in the report, and its yield rate for exotic/crossbred cattle, 9.16 kg/day, sits in the middle of the pack. Punjab is the inverse case. It does not appear among the top 10 states by cattle-plus-buffalo population in the 2019 census — its herd is a fraction of Uttar Pradesh's — yet it posts the highest cattle yield rate in the country (14.10 kg/day for exotic/crossbred cattle, against a national spread that tops out elsewhere around 11), the highest per-capita milk availability (1,318 g/day, more than two-and-a-half times the national average), and the fastest milk-production growth among the 15 major states in 2024-25.

Scale and efficiency are answering different questions. Uttar Pradesh's milk lead is a herd-size story: more animals, more raw tonnes, a stable but not fast-growing share. Punjab's numbers describe something else — a smaller base producing disproportionately, through yield per animal rather than animal count. A livestock-population count, however current, only measures the first of these. The production data is what actually shows which states are getting more out of what they have.

Rajasthan sits between the two patterns: it ranks second in both population (goat and cattle-plus-buffalo counts are both large) and milk share (14.82%), and also posts strong growth (5.78%) and the second-highest per-capita availability (1,229 g/day) after Punjab — a state that is both large and getting more efficient at the same time, which the other two single-axis stories in this article are not.

What this article does not establish. Whether the 21st Livestock Census's state-wise results have been finalised internally but not yet published, or whether processing is still underway — this article's research found no public state-wise release and cannot distinguish between those two explanations for the gap. Whether the population changes implied by six years of 12.77% CAGR production growth are evenly distributed across states or concentrated in a few — the 2019 census cannot answer this, and no interim state-wise population update was located. Why Andhra Pradesh's and Bihar's per-capita/growth figures are marked unavailable in Exhibit 2 — BAHS 2025's charted state subsets differ slightly across its milk-share, growth and per-capita graphs, and this article reports only what each specific chart covers rather than interpolating. Whether the 20th-Census population rankings (Exhibit 1) still hold in the same order today — plausible given the sector's growth has been broad-based per the report's own framing, but not verifiable without the 21st Census.

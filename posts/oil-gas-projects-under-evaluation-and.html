Oil & Gas Projects Under Evaluation and Design: A Segment-Wise Read of 2026 (Upstream to Petchem)

Data caveat. GEM has not published a 2026 global pre-construction tally in the material I could reach, and S&P Global's project-level pre-FID data is paywalled. Every number below is attributed to its source and year. Where a figure is old (GEM's pipeline numbers date from 2022, its LNG numbers from early 2024), the chart says so. The big picture

The pipeline is bifurcating. Capital is flowing to low-breakeven offshore and to US-centred LNG, while a very large stock of proposed gas infrastructure sits unsanctioned, and Middle East and pipeline projects face delay and stranded-asset questions. Wood Mackenzie counts 23 projects sanctioned in 2026 so far, carrying about $96bn of commitments, with up to 10 more (about $70bn) possible but described as unlikely to all land.

Segment 1: Offshore and deepwater (FPSO-led)

This is the healthiest segment. ADI Analytics reports offshore FIDs of about $164bn, the highest in over a decade, and Wood Mackenzie notes FPSO projects dominated Q2 committed investment. The economics explain it: deepwater breakeven is roughly $40/bbl against about $65/bbl for new US shale wells, which matters when ADI sees Brent at $55-60 on a 2-4.5 MMbpd surplus.

Segment 2: LNG liquefaction

LNG is where the volume is, and also where the slowdown is. 2025 was a record: 9 projects, 72 Mtpa sanctioned (Wood Mackenzie), over 100 bcm/yr by the IEA's count. For 2026, Wood Mackenzie expects just 4-5 FIDs, and every 2026 FID so far is American (over 31 bcm/yr). Behind them sits an enormous unsanctioned stock: roughly 648 Mtpa proposed in North America alone, 170 in Russia and 133 in Africa (IGU, 2024 data). Whether even a fraction is built depends on contracting in a softer market.

Segment 3: Gas pipelines and import terminals

GEM's last global pipeline census (2022) found 122,500 km in pre-construction against 70,900 km being built, about $486bn of capex flagged as stranded-asset risk. The pre-construction stock was nearly 1.7 times what was physically under way. The regional picture is more concrete: GEM's Asia Gas Tracker says India has the world's third-largest buildout, with nearly three-quarters already under construction, so there the pipeline is converting to steel rather than lingering in design.

Segment 4: Onshore upstream and new fields

GEM's extraction tracker (GOGET) found at least 20 fields approved in 2023, around 8 billion boe, with pre-production fields concentrated in Russia, Norway, Nigeria, Australia and the US (34% combined). Southeast Asia is a regional hotspot: GEM flagged a decade-high number of gas decisions for 2025. In 2026 the economics are tougher onshore. At $55-60 Brent, shale at a $65 breakeven is the marginal barrel, which is why capital discipline rather than new onshore design work dominates.

Segment 5: Oil pipelines

GEM's 2022 oil pipeline report had the US ahead of every other country in new capacity by length, with pre-construction projects adding about 21.8 mb/d. I found no 2026 update, so treat this segment as the least evidenced here.

Segment 6: Refining

Refining is a growth story with an expiry date. BloombergNEF projects net capacity growth of about 4.2 mb/d over 2026-2030, with retirements (mainly Europe and North America) taking roughly 0.2 mb/d out. About half of the planned additions are already operating or under construction; the 2028-2030 tranche is at earlier stages and carries more delay or cancellation risk. OPEC's regional split puts 2.8 mb/d in Asia-Pacific, 1.0 in the Middle East and 0.8 in Africa. An earlier IEA outlook called this wave of growth likely to be the last. Near term, the IEA's May 2026 Oil Market Report expected 2026 refinery throughput to fall by 1.6 mb/d to 82.3 mb/d on infrastructure damage and supply disruption, a reminder that projects in design compete with a shaky operating base.

Segment 7: LPG

LPG is the clearest case of midstream capex chasing associated-gas growth. US Gulf Coast export capacity is forecast to rise from about 60 to 100 mtpa by 2029. Projects in design or build include Enterprise's Houston Ship Channel expansion (+300 kb/d, a 36% lift to 1.135 mb/d dedicated, due late 2026), Targa's Galena Park (444 to about 625 kb/d by Q3 2027), and a new 400 kb/d Texas City terminal from Oneok and MPLX. Canada shows the risk: AltaGas's REEF terminal slipped to March 2027 with costs up 12% to about CAD 1.5bn. I found no India demand or import data this round, so the demand side is unaddressed here.

Segment 8: Petrochemicals

This is the weakest segment on the margin. China added more than 10 Mt/y of ethylene capacity in 2024-25, Asian olefin margins have been negative for years, and some Northeast Asian crackers have shut. Even so, SunSirs counts over 13 Mt/y of world-scale cracker capacity in late construction (e.g. Huajin Aramco, Fujian Gulang), against only about 6.87 Mt/y of estimated capacity reductions (CGS International), so supply keeps outrunning demand. Mysteel expects an H1 2026 lull in new polyethylene capacity, with most arriving in H2. Evidence points to delays rather than cancellations, and CERA does not expect margins to turn positive until after 2030. The design-phase signal is that new Chinese greenfield investment slows after 2029, while integrated refinery-petchem complexes remain the preferred form in Asia and the Middle East.

What to watch

  1. LNG contracting. The 4-5 FID forecast versus 9 last year is the clearest slowdown signal.
  2. Brent versus breakevens. Anything with a breakeven near $65 is at risk of deferral.
  3. Middle East timing. Delays shift capex into 2027.
  4. Downstream timing. Watch whether 2028-2030 refinery tranches slip, whether LPG terminals hit 2027 dates, and whether Chinese crackers start in H2 2026 as scheduled.
  5. Fresh GEM data. A new GOGET release or gas tracker update would replace the stale numbers above.

A sensible order for learning AI at no cost: concepts first (3Blue1Brown, StatQuest), then hands-on basics (fast.ai or Kaggle Learn), then building a language model (Andrej Karpathy), then applied work with provider documentation and DeepLearning.AI short courses. The tables below list the channels, resources, tools and methods that recur most often in that route.

How the list was chosen. Channels are included because they are long-standing and widely used for AI instruction, not because of a measured ranking. Subscriber and view counts were not checked for this piece, so none are given.

What this piece could not establish

Scope limits. The list is not exhaustive and is not a ranking. Channel audience sizes, tool versions, free-tier limits and course availability change often and were not re-checked at publication; each provider's own page is the reference. Papers with Code, a former research index, is not listed because it no longer operates as a separate site.

Sources: provider and course pages linked in the tables above (fast.ai, DeepLearning.AI, Hugging Face, Kaggle, Google, Microsoft, Harvard CS50, Elements of AI, d2l.ai, arXiv). YouTube channels and tools without links are named from general knowledge and were not individually verified.

Consistency & methodology notes

A small verified sample was checked first (Ruflo, Graphify, Kolibri, HappyRobot), each fact checked against its primary source, then scaled to the full board across all five categories. Items are labelled Verified where a figure came from GitHub itself or reputable first-party press, and reported where it rests on a single aggregator or secondary source a lab or company has not directly confirmed.

What changed this refresh (as of 11 August): six new movers added, headlined by Cloudflare's “Agents Week” — Kitesurf (agent-first browser) and Cloudflare Wallets / cloudflare.pay (agent identity + stablecoin payments), both confirmed via TechCrunch and Cloudflare's own press/changelog. Also new: Meta's Muse Code (repo-scale terminal agent) and Muse Glimmer model, OpenAI Agent Plugins (interop standard), and Drata AI Agent Governance. No new funding rounds surfaced this window; Ruflo's star count and prior funding figures are unchanged (flat).

  • Foundation-model dates carry caveats. Most August-2026 model details come from release-tracker aggregators, not lab pages, so specs and parameters are marked “reported.” Example discrepancy: Claude Opus 5 is dated 24 July 2026 by one tracker but “August 2026” by another — carried here as late July, flagged.
  • Pricing and single-source claims (the Codex price cut, Gemini Spark's $99.99/mo, per-token model pricing) are marked “reported” pending first-party confirmation.
  • Funding figures (HappyRobot, Convex, Sapiom) are from a 5 August 2026 TechStartups VC roundup — a secondary but editorial source; round sizes and valuations should be treated as reported until an official announcement is located.
  • Freshness bias: the board prioritises items from roughly the last 2–4 weeks. Where an item is a still-dominant carryover, the “why it matters” note says what changed this week.

How this refresh relates to Edition 03's own rule. Edition 03 was built specifically to prove a GitHub-API-only, no-roundup discovery method for star velocity — and that method, and its data, are unchanged here. This refresh adds a second, separate board for launches, funding and model releases that GitHub's API cannot see at all, sourced more broadly but labelled honestly per item. The two aren't merged into one number; treat the star-velocity table from the original edition and this launch board as two different instruments measuring two different things.

Sources. Star/fork counts are read directly from the GitHub API. Launch, funding and model-release items are sourced per-item as linked in each card above and in the notes; see each card's “Source” link for the specific citation. Prior editions are linked from the AI Tracker hub.

Why it is useful

  • Who benefits: fuel-retail, CNG and EV-charging planners, consultants and students who need a pump-level list of India with location, company and fuel type.
  • What it replaces: collecting listings from each oil company's locator one by one.
  • Evidence from the run: counted naively, Delhi appears to have 21,939 pumps; after removing duplicate rows it has 419. The raw file also holds 22,343 duplicate rows (21.3%). If you skip the cleaning step, every state count is wrong.
  • After cleaning, the per-capita view shows a clear spread: Bihar 2.7, West Bengal 2.8 and Jharkhand 3.4 pumps per 100,000 people, against Punjab 11.5 and Haryana 11.3.

What it is for

This repo collects Indian petrol, diesel and CNG outlet listings from public sources (the SSRI petrol-pump API, the BPCL dealership locator and CashAtPOS), with two small web apps for the map and a coverage gap-analysis heatmap.

The data is indicative, for planning and visualisation. It is not an official registry. Outlet records are public business listings.

Who it is for

  • Energy and mobility analysts sizing pump or charger networks
  • Students working on India fuel-retail geography
  • Anyone who needs a raw outlet list to join with district or population data
Not for.
  • Regulatory or legal use; it is not an official registry (PPAC publishes the official totals)
  • Using raw counts without removing duplicates first
  • Real-time prices; the prices in the crawl are a snapshot

Quick start: commands and what you should see

Load the committed crawl and deduplicate it the way the repo's notes describe

python3 -I - <<'E'
import gzip,json,collections
p='api-data-integration/outlet_data_ssri_complete/ssri_pumps_raw_20261016.jsonl.gz'
rows=[json.loads(l) for l in gzip.open(p,'rt') if l.strip()]
k={}
for r in rows:
    key=(r['name'],r['address'],r['company'],r['latitude'],r['longitude'])
    if key not in k or r['id']>k[key]['id']: k[key]=r
u=list(k.values())
print(len(rows),len(u),len(rows)-len(u))
print(collections.Counter(r['company'] for r in u).most_common(5))
E

104945 82602 22343, then the company counts: BPCL 25,795, HPCL 23,543, IOCL 19,446, Nayara 9,007, Unknown 2,504.

Limits and honest caveats

  • Raw data contains about 21% duplicate rows; always deduplicate. In the 16 Oct 2026 file Delhi shows 21,939 rows before cleaning and 419 after.
  • Even after cleaning, 82,602 outlets compares with a PPAC official baseline of 103,450 (May 2026) in the repo's notes, so coverage is roughly 80%.
  • State names are inconsistent (53 spellings) and all 9,007 Nayara outlets have state 'unknown', so state tables miss them unless you geocode by coordinates.
  • Population figures in the gap-analysis data.js are older census-based numbers; per-capita values are indicative.
  • Not an official registry. Prices and timings are a snapshot.
  • The README refers to files removed in the LFS-free rebuild (derived maps and unified DB); they must be regenerated by the scripts. I did not run the extractors, web apps or any network call; only the committed crawl was read.

India runs 14 Production Linked Incentive schemes and a handful of major competitive-auction programmes, and almost every one of them names its winners in public — in Press Information Bureau releases and, less consistently, on ministry websites, but never on one aggregated portal. This piece compiles all 14 PLI schemes plus four auction categories, reading the actual release and portal text rather than trusting a headline. It also asks the harder question — how many companies actually competed, not just who won — and reports plainly, scheme by scheme, what is and isn't publicly known. No names or numbers are invented where none exist.

India's 14 PLI Schemes, and Every Company Publicly Named as a Winner, 2019–2026

Skip to article content
Text Size

India's competitive government tenders and Production Linked Incentive (PLI) schemes almost always end the same way: a Press Information Bureau release, or occasionally a ministry's own scheme portal, naming exactly who won, for how much, and where. There is no single place that aggregates these across ministries — each release or portal stands alone, and finding them means knowing which ministry ran which scheme and reading the actual text rather than trusting a headline. This piece does that reading for four competitive-auction categories and all 14 of India's original PLI schemes, spanning eleven ministries, going back to 2019 where the trail is verifiable. Two rules throughout: every company name below comes from text this piece actually read, not a title, URL, or inference; and where a process is still open or a source was simply unreachable, that's stated plainly rather than papered over.

Part 1: Competitive auctions

Four categories where the government sells or leases a resource or capacity via competitive bidding, rather than incentivising private investment.

Inter-state transmission projects (Ministry of Power)

Tendered via REC Power Development and Consultancy Limited under the Tariff Based Competitive Bidding (TBCB) route.

PIB PRID 2005066 (11 Feb 2024) named the successful bidders for a set of ISTS transmission projects as Power Grid Corporation of India Limited (PGCIL), the consortium of Indigrid 2 Limited & Indigrid 1 Limited, and Apraava Energy Private Limited; five project SPVs were handed over 9 February 2024. The same release separately named Avaada Energy Private Limited and Juniper Green Energy Private Limited as Letter-of-Award recipients for a 100 MW wind-power supply contract to Damodar Valley Corporation. The release does not state how many companies bid for the ISTS projects in total, and no companion release with a bidder count was found.

Part 2: India's 14 Production Linked Incentive schemes

The PLI programme, approved across November 2020 to March 2021 with later additions, is structurally different from the auctions above: instead of the government selling something, it's paying companies to invest, ranked by application quality rather than a single winner-take-all bid. Every scheme below is numbered against the same canonical list of 14, so gaps are visible as gaps, not silently skipped.

PLI 1 of 14: Mobile Manufacturing & Specified Electronic Components (MeitY)

Outlay ₹38,645 crore, notified 1 April 2020, incentive of 4–6% on net incremental sales; tenure extended by a year (to FY2025-26) for 15 of the original 16 approved companies. The first round approved 16 companies (5 global mobile-phone makers, 5 domestic, 6 for specified electronic components); a second round for electronic components added another 16. As of a 24 July 2024 Lok Sabha reply, 32 companies were approved in total — ₹8,282 crore cumulative investment against ₹11,324 crore committed, 7 Greenfield and 25 Brownfield projects.

PIB's disbursement-approval releases name individual beneficiaries at specific Empowered Committee meetings rather than publishing one full 32-company roster. Confirmed by name: Padget Electronics Private Limited (a Dixon Technologies subsidiary, first disbursement ₹53.28 crore, later ₹58.29 crore for Jan–Mar 2022) and Foxconn Hon Hai Technology India Mega Development Pvt. Ltd (₹357.17 crore for Aug 2021–Mar 2022). The same release names other scheme participants without individual figures: Samsung, Pegatron, Rising Star, Wistron (global) and Lava, Micromax, Optiemus, United Telelinks (Neolyncs) (domestic).

Sources: PIB PRID 1858066 (9 Sep 2022), PRID 1885189 (20 Dec 2022, NITI Aayog), PRID 2036448 (24 Jul 2024). A complete 32-name roster with individual figures was not found in the PIB corpus checked.

PLI 6 of 14: Specialty Steel (Ministry of Steel)

When this piece was first published, this was the one scheme in the list it could not verify at all — not because the data doesn't exist, but because steel.gov.in itself was unreachable: every attempt to fetch the ministry's PLI pages failed with a broken TLS certificate chain, a fault on the ministry's end rather than a content gap. PIB gave counts only across rounds ("57 MoUs/27 companies," "67 applications/30 companies"), no names. Search-engine snippets describing steel.gov.in content (not independently fetched or read at the time, so not verified per this piece's own rules) suggested PLI 1.0 covered 19 companies/44 projects, PLI 1.1 a further 25 companies/42 projects (signed 24 Mar 2025), and PLI 1.2 a further 55 companies/85 projects (signed 9 Feb 2026) — reported at the time as an unverified lead, not a fact. Update, 27 September 2026: the PLI 1.2 figure is now directly confirmed by a primary source, PIB PRID 2225496 ("Ministry of Steel Signs MoUs with 55 Companies under PLI Scheme for Specialty Steel," 9 Feb 2026): 55 companies, ₹11,887 crore in committed investment, 8.7 million tonnes of committed capacity. A further, more recent figure not available when this piece was first written: cumulative investment under the scheme has since reached ₹26,320 crore of ₹56,106 crore total committed, as of 30 June 2026.

PLI 14 of 14: IT Hardware, PLI 2.0 (MeitY)

The original PLI Scheme for IT Hardware (notified 3 March 2021; Laptops, Tablets, All-in-One PCs, Servers) drew 19 applications by 30 April 2021, naming 5 IT Hardware Companies (Dell, ICT/Wistron, Flextronics, Rising Stars Hi-Tech/Foxconn, Lava) and 14 Domestic Companies (Dixon, Infopower, Bhagwati/Micromax, Syrma, Orbic, Neolync, Optiemus, Netweb, VVDN, Smile Electronics, Panache Digilife, HLBS, RDP Workstations, Coconics) — 14 were ultimately approved, ₹2,517 crore committed investment.

PLI 2.0, Cabinet-approved 17 May 2023 with expanded scope (adding Ultra Small Form Factor devices), approved 27 IT hardware manufacturers on 18 Nov 2023, citing well-known brands including Acer, Asus, Dell, HP, Lenovo among them — expected outcomes of ~2 lakh jobs, ₹3,50,000 crore production value (~US$42 billion), ₹3,000 crore investment; 13 of the original 14 approved companies migrated into PLI 2.0. By July 2024, cumulative investment of ₹464.66 crore across the 27 companies was reported (6 Greenfield, 21 Brownfield).

Sources: PIB PRID 1715871 (4 May 2021, original scheme, full 19-name list), PRID 1977846 (18 Nov 2023, PLI 2.0 approval), PRID 2036448 (24 Jul 2024). What this sweep does not cover

This is not an exhaustive census of every Indian government tender or PLI approval ever announced — it's what surfaced from a systematic but time-bounded search of a local PIB index (2017 to date) plus a set of ministry-website checks, across ministries known to run competitive, publicly-announced processes. Several leads were identified but not run to completion: full technical-bidder lists for coal tranches other than the 19th (coal.nic.in appears to publish a "List of Bidders" PDF per tranche as standard practice, so equivalent documents for other rounds almost certainly exist); the MSTC e-auction platform's "Event Results" sections for mineral blocks, critical minerals and offshore minerals (real, live, but dynamically loaded in a way this piece's fetch method couldn't open); dot.gov.in's and civilaviation.gov.in's own PLI beneficiary PDFs, both of which returned access-denied responses to automated fetching (a real browser session might succeed where this method didn't); and dghindia.gov.in's current OALP/DSF results pages, whose previously-indexed direct PDF links now appear stale. Categories searched and found genuinely empty, which may mean the winner-naming release exists under different wording rather than that it doesn't exist at all: Ports, Shipping and Waterways PPP concessionaires; Ministry of Power's TBCB process beyond the one ISTS release above; Railways' Vande Bharat manufacturing-tender winner; the Ministry of Defence's Strategic Partnership model shortlists (Project 75(I) submarines and similar); and results from India's first offshore mineral block auction, launched November 2024.

Sources and caveats

Every company name in this piece was transcribed from the body text of a specific government release cited beside it — a PIB press release (via its PRID, Press Release ID) or a document/page hosted directly on a ministry's own website (texmin.gov.in, mofpi.gov.in, mnre.gov.in, coal.nic.in, mines.gov.in) — never inferred from a title, URL, or search-engine snippet. Where information came only from a search snippet describing a page this piece could not itself open (originally the Specialty Steel round figures, now resolved via PIB PRID 2225496 as noted in an update; the Kavach vendor names, still unresolved), that is explicitly flagged as unverified or secondary-sourced rather than presented as a confirmed government finding. Ministry websites are, on the evidence gathered here, wildly inconsistent in accessibility: some (Textiles, Food Processing, Mines, Coal, MNRE) serve plain, readable data; others (Steel, DoT, Civil Aviation) actively blocked automated access during this research. This piece does not verify that any contract or approval was subsequently completed, cancelled, or modified after its cited release date — the Nayakkarpatti Tungsten annulment noted in Part 1 is the one exception found in passing, and even that was not independently confirmed. Very long applicant rosters (Textiles, ~170; Food Processing, 119; Auto Components, ~75) are given as representative samples with the source URL for the full list, not reproduced in full, to keep this piece readable.

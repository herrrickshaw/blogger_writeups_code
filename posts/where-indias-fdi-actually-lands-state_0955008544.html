DPIIT's own quarterly FDI newsletters carry a district-by-district breakdown of foreign investment — not just a state-level headline number. Reading five of those primary tables directly, spanning October 2019 to December 2022, shows the same three districts capturing the bulk of every single quarter's inflow: Bangalore Urban, the New Mumbai/Thane belt, and Delhi. Everything else, across roughly 30 states and union territories, splits what's left.

The short version.

  • District-wise FDI equity-inflow data is real and DPIIT publishes it every quarter — a "Statement on State-wise FDI Equity Inflow" table, with a District Name column, inside each Quarterly FDI Newsletter. This piece reads five of those tables directly (user-supplied PDFs, not a web fetch): Oct–Dec 2019, Jul–Sep 2020, Jan–Jun 2021, the full Jan–Dec 2021 calendar year, and Oct–Dec 2022 — roughly 130 state-level and several hundred district-level line items in total.
  • Three districts dominate every single period: Bangalore (Urban) in Karnataka, New Mumbai in Maharashtra, and Delhi. Across the five snapshots read here, those three alone account for anywhere from 46% (Jul–Sep 2020, when a single Gujarat district spiked) to over 75% (Jan–Jun 2021) of all district-level equity inflow reported nationally in that period.
  • One outlier is worth flagging on its own: in Jul–Sep 2020, Ahmedabad (Gujarat) alone reported ₹1,16,379 crore ($15,585 million) — 66.6% of that entire quarter's national FDI equity inflow. The newsletter itself doesn't attribute inflows to individual transactions, so this piece cannot say what drove it; it is reported as a fact from the primary table, not explained.
  • The five-period window is bounded by DPIIT's own methodology: a footnote on the Oct–Dec 2019 table states plainly that "RBI regional office wise data has been discontinued and it has been replaced with State wise data w.e.f. October, 2019" — meaning this exact state-and-district format has no predecessor before that date in a comparable form.
  • For the years this piece's five primary-source snapshots don't cover — FY2022-23 through FY2025-26 in full — national and state-level totals below come from search-indexed coverage of DPIIT's later tables, not a primary document read directly, and are flagged at correspondingly lower confidence. Two real, unresolved press contradictions turned up over which state actually led FY2024-25 and FY2025-26 (Maharashtra vs. Karnataka); both are reported rather than resolved.

What DPIIT actually publishes, and why this dashboard starts in October 2019

The headline "India got $50 billion in FDI this year" figure that shows up in press coverage is a national roll-up of a much more granular document DPIIT (Department for Promotion of Industry and Internal Trade, Ministry of Commerce & Industry) actually maintains: a quarterly Newsletter, each carrying a table titled "Statement on State-wise FDI Equity Inflow" — and, inside that table, a District Name column against every state. This piece was written after a background research pass using web search alone concluded, reasonably given what it could access, that "no evidence was found that DPIIT... publishes FDI data broken down below the state/UT level." That conclusion turns out to be wrong, and the reason is instructive: the district breakdown lives inside PDF tables that aren't well indexed by search engines, so a search-only pass never surfaces them, even though the tables themselves are DPIIT's own primary publication, refreshed every quarter. Five of those PDFs were supplied directly for this piece, and their contents are what this dashboard is built from.

The five periods read here — Oct–Dec 2019 (Table No. 16 in that newsletter), Jul–Sep 2020 (also Table No. 16), Jan–Jun 2021 and the full Jan–Dec 2021 calendar year (Table No. 9), and Oct–Dec 2022 (Table No. 16 again, the table numbering isn't consistent release to release) — are not a continuous run; they're five snapshots roughly two years apart at points, missing most of 2020 and all of 2022 outside one quarter. That's a real gap, not a stylistic choice: DPIIT was not among the PDFs supplied for the periods in between, and this piece's network access could not fetch dpiit.gov.in directly to fill them in (see Sources and caveats). What the five snapshots do share is a common table format, which is itself informative: a footnote reproduced verbatim on the Oct–Dec 2019 table reads "RBI regional office wise data has been discontinued and it has been replaced with State wise data w.e.f. October, 2019." Before that date, FDI inflow was tracked by which RBI regional office processed the transaction, not by the investee company's actual state or district — a materially different, and less locationally precise, method. So October 2019 isn't just where this piece's source documents happen to start; it's close to where this exact state-and-district reporting format itself starts.

The "Statement on State-wise FDI Equity Inflow" table title, its District Name column, and the RBI-regional-office-discontinued footnote are transcribed directly from the five DPIIT Quarterly FDI Newsletter PDFs supplied for this piece (periods: Oct–Dec 2019, Jul–Sep 2020, Jan–Jun 2021, Jan–Dec 2021, Oct–Dec 2022), read in full rather than fetched from the web. The "no evidence district-wise data exists" finding being superseded is this piece's own comparison between an earlier web-search-only research pass run for this piece and these primary documents; it is not a claim that search-only research is unreliable in general, only that it missed a real, quarterly-published, PDF-only table in this specific case.

The district dashboard: three places absorb most of it

Going one level down changes the picture from "which state" to "which city, specifically." Below are the top 10 individually-named districts by FDI equity inflow in each of the five periods, again transcribed directly from the primary tables. Bangalore (Urban), New Mumbai, and Delhi are not just usually near the top — they are the top three, in some order, in four of the five periods (the exception being Jul–Sep 2020, when Ahmedabad's spike pushed Delhi to fourth). Gurgaon (Gurugram), Chennai, Kancheepuram, Pune, Hyderabad, Ahmedabad and Gandhinagar round out most of the rest of each period's top 10 — meaning a handful of specific metro districts, not entire states, are functionally where most of India's reported FDI equity actually lands.

How the state ranking has shifted since, per later (search-sourced) coverage

This piece's primary-source snapshots stop at Oct–Dec 2022. For a sense of where things went after that, search-indexed coverage of DPIIT's later full-year tables (FY2021-22 through FY2025-26, not read directly — see confidence flags in §2) shows Karnataka's 37.55% share in FY2021-22 — a genuine GCC/tech-investment boom in Bengaluru — receding to second or third place for the next two years as Maharashtra took over the top spot, before Karnataka surged back in FY2025-26 (nearly doubling year-on-year to $12.9–13.0 billion, again attributed in coverage to Global Capability Centre and AI-sector investment). Delhi's share has structurally eroded across the period, from around 14% in FY2021-22 down toward mid-single digits by FY2025-26, while Gujarat and Tamil Nadu have held reasonably steady mid-table positions throughout.

Two real, unresolved contradictions turned up in this later coverage and are reported here rather than silently resolved. For FY2024-25, most outlets and the DPIIT table itself put Maharashtra clearly first at 39.16% (nearly ₹1.65 lakh crore), but one outlet's headline read "Karnataka tops FDI chart... Rs 50,000 crore in FY 2024-25" — a framing that appears to describe a different metric (possibly a state-government investment-facilitation figure rather than DPIIT's own equity-inflow ranking), since Karnataka's own reported DPIIT figure that year was roughly ₹56,030 crore against Maharashtra's ₹1,64,875 crore. The same pattern recurred for FY2025-26: most outlets report Maharashtra retaining the top spot at $18.4bn against Karnataka's $12.9bn, but one outlet headlined "Karnataka overtakes Maharashtra to become No.1 in FDI with $13 billion inflow." This piece could not resolve either discrepancy and reports both sides rather than picking one.

What doesn't follow from any of this

This piece's five-period primary-source window (Oct 2019–Dec 2022, missing most of 2020 and 2022) is not a complete multi-year time series, and nothing here should be read as one. The concentration finding — that Bangalore Urban, New Mumbai and Delhi dominate — describes reported FDI equity inflow location as DPIIT's own attribution method records it; it says nothing about where the underlying economic activity, jobs, or manufacturing capacity funded by that investment actually end up, which can differ from where a holding company or headquarters is registered. Similarly, the Ahmedabad and Nawan Shaher single-quarter spikes are reported as facts from the primary tables, not explained — this piece did not attempt to identify or verify the specific transaction(s) behind either, and readers should not assume a particular deal without independent confirmation. The Section 2 and Section 5 figures, being search-sourced rather than read from a primary document, should be treated as directionally reliable rather than exact wherever a range or a "~" appears; where this piece found a genuine contradiction between outlets, it reports the contradiction rather than picking a side, and a reader who needs a definitive answer on either dispute should consult DPIIT's own factsheet directly. Finally, this dashboard covers equity inflow only, per DPIIT's own table footnote; total FDI (which also includes re-invested earnings and other capital) is a larger, differently-distributed number this piece does not attempt to break down by state or district.

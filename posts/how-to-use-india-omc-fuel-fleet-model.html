Why it is useful

  • Energy analysts, students and journalists get a transparent, editable model of an argument that is usually made in prose: blended fuel has less energy per litre, so vehicles buy more litres, and per-litre margins and taxes scale with it.
  • It replaces a spreadsheet nobody can audit: the main model is one readable script using only the standard library, and every assumption is in the code.
  • Evidence from the run: omc_model.py, fleet_from_fuel.py and energy_blend_comparison.py all ran in a fresh clone with no installs and printed the headline numbers the README quotes, and the extra-litre figures in two different scripts reconcile (2.16 billion litres at E20).
  • The run also surfaced a modelling choice worth knowing: the OMC model uses a 4% mileage drop at E20, while the pure energy-content table implies about 7%. The post explains the difference.

What it is for

The repo models India's oil marketing company (OMC) retail margin on petrol and diesel from PPAC reference data, and what changes when petrol is blended with 20, 25 or 30 percent ethanol. The key mechanism is that a mileage penalty makes drivers buy more litres, which raises pump throughput and so OMC income.

Other scripts estimate how many vehicles are actively driven from fuel sales, compare energy content of blends, test price-parity scenarios and sketch CBG, distillery, DME and isobutanol investment cases. This post focuses on the blend-scenario model and the fleet estimate.

Who it is for

  • Students and analysts studying India's ethanol blending policy
  • Journalists and researchers who want to test assumptions rather than quote headlines
  • Developers extending a small, readable fuel-economics model
Not for.
  • Investment advice: this is not investment advice, and the OMC margin is an assumption, described by the README as the key lever
  • Policy or tax decisions without checking inputs against current official figures
  • Detailed engineering or project-finance design; the pitch models are conceptual

Quick start: commands and what you should see

Run the OMC profitability and blend-scenario model

python3 omc_model.py

Five lines: base OMC gross, extra pump income at E20/E25/E30, mix scenarios S0 to S4, the YoY projection, and the files it wrote under outputs/.

Estimate the actively driven fleet from fuel sales

python3 fleet_from_fuel.py

Totals by fuel and a segment table; about 24.5 crore actively driven vehicles.

Compare blends by energy content and who collects the extra spend

python3 energy_blend_comparison.py

An energy table for E10 to E30, isobutanol and biodiesel blends, then consumer spend, excise, VAT, dealer and OMC shares per blend.

Limits and honest caveats

  • Not investment advice. The README itself calls the headline results illustrative and the OMC margin the key lever.
  • The mileage-drop assumption (4% at E20) is lower than the energy-content estimate (about 6.8% less energy per litre, 7.3% more litres). The scripts reconcile with each other, but the chosen real-world mileage drop drives the extra-income result.
  • The YoY table labels its first row FY25-26 while the base is labelled FY24-25 in the model output; both show the same 46,449, so read the first row as the base year.
  • Inputs are PPAC and other figures embedded in the repo; I did not re-verify them against the sources and they will age.
  • I ran only the Python models. The Excel builder, the Node slide and document builders and the investment-pitch models were not run.
  • The CBG, DME and isobutanol pitch models are conceptual and are not investment advice.
  • The README says CBG fed into CNG does not cut mileage, so the extra-throughput mechanism applies to ethanol in petrol only.

Why it is useful

  • Who benefits: students and policy readers who want one worked narrative on reserves, the trade balance and debt service, with the working shown in plain markdown tables.
  • What it replaces: stitching together RBI reserve tables, Budget annexes and TradeStat extracts by hand for a first-pass picture.
  • Evidence from our read: the reserve table adds up (552.28 + 115.40 + 18.62 + 4.81 = 691.11) and the tax-share percentages recompute correctly.
  • Evidence of a problem: the stated 2023 exports of US$875.4 bn are exactly twice the 437.7 bn total row in the sibling india-trade-export-analysis data, so the headline deficit looks unreliable.
  • Treat it as a worked example to audit, not a source to cite.

What it is for

The repository is nine numbered markdown documents plus an index. They cover export growth 2020-2023, import and export analysis, RBI reserves versus the trade deficit, a reconciliation of Budget 2026-27 tax revenue, debt and borrowing trends, a five-year post-pandemic comparison, year-on-year deterioration, multi-source integration and an executive summary. There is no code to run.

It rates the situation an 'amber alert' and gives scenario probabilities. Those ratings and probabilities are the author's judgement, not outputs of a documented model. This is economic commentary for learning, not investment, trading or policy advice. The repository is archived (read-only).

Who it is for

  • Students of macroeconomics practising how to audit a report
  • Journalists who want a checklist of numbers to verify before quoting
  • Analysts looking for a structure to rebuild with sourced data
Not for.
  • Investment, trading or currency decisions
  • Quoting the trade-deficit headline (US$567 bn for 2023, US$769.5 bn for 2026) without re-checking it
  • Real-time reserve monitoring: the figures are dated March 2026 and some are projections

Quick start: commands and what you should see

Start with the index and README, then the reserves document

less README.md
less 3_RBI_RESERVES_vs_TRADE_DEFICIT_ANALYSIS.md

You see the executive summary, the amber-alert framing and the reserve composition table.

Re-compute the stated figures

python3 -I check.py   # a short script that re-adds the tables you copy from the documents

Five lines of arithmetic checks, shown in the sample outputs below. Our check script is not part of the repository.

Search for a number across all documents

grep -n "875.4" *.md

The same figure appears in several documents, which makes a single error spread.

Where the export figure goes wrong

grep -n '875.4' 2_IMPORT_EXPORT_ANALYSIS_2025_26.md; then read the 'India's Total Export' row of the sibling repo CSV
2_IMPORT_EXPORT_ANALYSIS_2025_26.md:13:- **Exports**: $875.4B
2_IMPORT_EXPORT_ANALYSIS_2025_26.md:79:Export Growth (2020-2023):       +44% ($583.6B → $875.4B) ✓ Strong
total row 2020: 291808.48  2023: 437704.58 (USD mn)   <- india-trade-export-analysis CSV
2 x total row: 583616.96  875409.16

US$875.4 bn is 2 x 437.7 bn. The sibling data has one 'India's Total Export' row plus the commodity rows, so adding both appears to have double-counted. The imports figure may have the same problem, which we did not check, so the stated deficit cannot be trusted in either direction until it is re-derived.

Sample prompts for your AI assistant

Copy, paste and edit the parts in [brackets].

  • Read README.md and 2_IMPORT_EXPORT_ANALYSIS_2025_26.md in this repo and list every number that cannot be derived from another number in the repo.
  • Recompute the reserve composition percentages in 3_RBI_RESERVES_vs_TRADE_DEFICIT_ANALYSIS.md and tell me if any do not add up.
  • The repo says exports were US$875.4 bn in 2023. Compare with [the official figure from the Commerce Ministry] and explain the gap.
  • Rewrite the executive summary so every claim states its source and whether it is actual, estimated or projected.
  • List the assumptions behind the scenario probabilities (25/60/15) and say what evidence would be needed to justify them.

Limits and honest caveats

  • The 2023 export total (US$875.4 bn) and the 2020 total (US$583.6 bn) are exactly twice the totals in the sibling trade data, which points to double counting; the 2023 deficit headline therefore needs re-deriving.
  • Scenario probabilities and 'confidence before and after PDFs' percentages are stated without a method.
  • Many 2026-27 figures are projections; the 'amber alert' is a judgement.
  • The analysis is not independently validated and the data-quality line in the README says HIGH without evidence.
  • Not investment, trading or policy advice. The repository is archived and will not be corrected.

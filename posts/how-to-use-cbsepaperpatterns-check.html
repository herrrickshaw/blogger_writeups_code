Why it is useful

  • Teachers, students and tutors get an evidence-based answer to 'do the papers repeat?' by subject, instead of coaching-industry folklore.
  • It replaces gut feeling with one number per subject: the share of marks in 4-mark-or-higher questions sitting on a phrase that recurs in at least 3 of 5 exam years.
  • The result is uneven: Accountancy 49%, Physics 44%, Economics 38% versus Chemistry and Biology at 5%. The repo explains this measures repeated wording, not repeated concepts.
  • Evidence from the run: the subject table and the mined topic list load from CSV in seconds and match the README; the heavy corpus rebuild was not run.

What it is for

The repo mines 2 to 4 word phrases that recur across at least three exam years in high-mark questions, and reports a repetition index per subject. It also documents paper structure, syllabus coverage and three parsing traps for anyone processing CBSE PDFs.

This post uses only the derived tables in data/ (subject_repetition.csv and recurring_topics.csv phrase counts). It does not reproduce question text.

Who it is for

  • Teachers and tutors planning revision emphasis by subject
  • Researchers studying exam design and assessment patterns
  • Data people who want a worked example of parsing messy PDF exam papers
Not for.
  • Predicting next year's paper: lexical repetition is not a guarantee that a topic will appear
  • Skipping syllabus topics. Revise the whole syllabus; this is a descriptive pattern, not advice on what to leave out
  • Redistributing CBSE question papers: they remain CBSE's copyright (see limits)

Quick start: commands and what you should see

Read the repetition index by subject

python3 -I -c "import csv;[print(r['subject'],r['papers'],round(float(r['repetition_index'])*100)) for r in csv.DictReader(open('data/subject_repetition.csv'))]"

One line per subject with paper count and repetition percentage.

List the recurring content topics for one subject

python3 -I topics_for.py Physics   # 8-line filter on data/recurring_topics.csv, kind=content_topic

Phrases with the years they recurred in, number of papers and total marks (see sample output).

Limits and honest caveats

  • Only the derived aggregate CSVs were used. The 6 GB corpus download, the parsing scripts and the OCR step were not run.
  • The index measures lexical repetition, not conceptual repetition; the repo itself warns that Chemistry and Biology are understated.
  • Marks across the full archive are regex-parsed and approximate; only the 32-paper subset is checked against stated maximums.
  • Hindi-medium and image-only papers are only partly recovered; 8 files still lack text.
  • Copyright: the repo's description says papers are not redistributed, but the README says the corpus Markdown of CBSE question papers is included for research use under a take-down offer. Check CBSE's terms yourself before reusing the corpus text.
  • Do not treat recurring topics as a reason to skip other syllabus content. This is a descriptive analysis, not exam or educational advice.

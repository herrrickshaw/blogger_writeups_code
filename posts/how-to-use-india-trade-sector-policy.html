Why it is useful

  • Who benefits: policy students, think-tank staff and journalists who want sector calls laid out with the numbers and the reasoning attached.
  • What it replaces: a long hunt through ministry releases. Each recommendation links an HS chapter, a FY2025-26 value, a growth rate and any existing scheme.
  • Evidence from our read: the main JSON sorts import chapters into 'Act now', 'Scale existing scheme' and 'Different lever' tiers, naming chapters such as inorganic chemicals (+86.0%) and plastics (+45.8%).
  • It flags two risks it says it cross-checked against NITI Aayog's Trade Watch: EU CBAM exposure on iron and steel exports, and a long decline in gems and jewellery exports.
  • Claims are explicit, so you can disagree with a specific tier rather than a vague essay.

What it is for

The repo takes the chapter rankings and country concentration from india-trade-data-analysis, checks them against PLI scheme coverage and policy history, and produces tiered recommendations, a scheme report card, a quarterly report and dashboards. Open the live site or read the JSON.

This is policy analysis written by the repo author. It is not investment advice, legal advice or an official government view.

Who it is for

  • Policy students and researchers
  • Journalists looking for sourced sector talking points
  • Analysts who want machine-readable tier lists to challenge or extend
Not for.
  • Investment, trading or legal decisions
  • Treating the tiers as official or settled policy
  • Anyone needing data newer than the July 2026 snapshot

Quick start: commands and what you should see

List the import-substitution tiers

python3 -c "import json;d=json.load(open('data/sector_and_policy_recommendations_2026-07-18.json'))
for t in d['import_substitution_tiers']:print(t['tier'],t['label'],[(c['hs'],c['name'],c['growth_pct']) for c in t['chapters']])"

Three tiers, each with HS chapters and growth percentages, as in the sample output.

Sample prompts for your AI assistant

Copy, paste and edit the parts in [brackets].

  • Read data/sector_and_policy_recommendations_2026-07-18.json and explain in plain English why plastics is in Tier 1 while fertilisers is in Tier 2.
  • Argue the strongest case against the 'De-risk China' tier using only the numbers in this repo.
  • Turn the three export-growth tiers into a one-page briefing with the figures and the file each came from.
  • Which recommendations here depend on a PLI scheme being in place, and where could I check each scheme's actual status?
  • List the caveats the author documents and which ones weaken the tier assignments.

Limits and honest caveats

  • These are the repo author's tiers, based on a July 2026 snapshot of provisional data. They are not official policy or advice.
  • We read the committed JSON and README only. We did not rebuild the PDFs, run the scripts or re-verify the NITI Aayog and PIB cross-checks.
  • Tier labels are judgments; a different weighting would move chapters between tiers.
  • The repo has a quarterly refresh process, so figures here will go stale; check the date in each file.
  • Policy analysis only, not investment or legal advice.

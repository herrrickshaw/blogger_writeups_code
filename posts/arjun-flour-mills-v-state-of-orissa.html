Top line

  • 27 years after the reference to a seven-judge bench in October 1999, the Supreme Court disposed of Arjun Flour Mills on 4 November 2026, holding the question infructuous after GST and stating that S. Kodar (1974) continues to govern.
  • 10% and 15% were the additional tax rates on sales tax payable in Section 5A of the Orissa Sales Tax Act, 1947, by turnover band, according to the Supreme Court Observer; other reports state only the 10% rate.
  • 2 opinions were pronounced by a bench of 7 judges: Chief Justice Surya Kant for the Bench and Justice K.V. Viswanathan concurring separately; the judgement runs to 93 pages (as summarised by the Supreme Court Observer).

The question, the law and the cases

  1. StatuteSection 5A of the Orissa Sales Tax Act, 1947 added an additional tax on dealers by annual turnover, calculated on the sales tax payable. The Supreme Court Observer dates the insertion to 15 November 1997.
  2. QuestionIs a levy computed with reference to an existing sales tax a tax on the sale of goods, which a State may impose under Entry 54 of the State List, or a tax on the dealer’s income, which lies outside State power?
  3. ConflictThe Court that made the reference saw conflict between Hoechst Pharmaceuticals (three judges, upholding a State surcharge under Entry 54) and India Cement (seven judges, in a case on the Madras Panchayats Act).
  4. Anchor caseS. Kodar v State of Kerala (1974, five judges) upheld the Tamil Nadu Additional Sales Tax Act, 1970 as a tax on sales and not on income.
  5. Answer on 4 November 2026The Bench held that Kodar governs: an additional tax computed as a percentage of sales tax enhances the rate and falls under Entry 54, and that the reference was academic because Entry 54 and the State laws were replaced when GST began (101st Amendment).

Section 5A: 10% extra tax on sales tax payable, 15% above Rs 1 crore of turnover

The stakes, without a prediction
  • Under GST: Business Standard reports that the judgement creates no new liability under GST. Entry 54 no longer exists after the 101st Constitutional Amendment, according to the Supreme Court Observer.
  • Old disputes: Business Standard reports the observations could influence unresolved disputes on sales tax and surcharges under earlier State laws, and that individual challenges to old demands remain open; the result in each depends on the law and facts of the case.
  • Connected cases: CourtBook reports that the Registry was directed to place the lead and connected matters before regular benches; their number, the States and the amounts involved were not found.
  • Open question: whether a regular bench will accept the Kodar reasoning for a particular State levy has not been decided, and this post makes no forecast.

What would fix this

  1. Supreme Court Registry: publish the 4 November 2026 order and the list of connected appeals with the State and the amount involved in each.
  2. State finance departments with pre-GST sales tax disputes: publish the number and value of appeals that turn on a surcharge computed on sales tax.
  3. Supreme Court Observer and similar trackers: update a case page when a judgement is delivered, so that the page does not show a hearing date after disposal.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

Method and definitions
  • Sources are legal-news reports and the Supreme Court Observer case archive, all labelled secondary; the judgement of 4 November 2026 (93 pages, per the Supreme Court Observer) was not opened because the Court’s server was not reachable from the analysis environment.
  • Intervals between dated steps are computed here in years of 365.25 days, rounded to one decimal; the illustration of the levy on Rs 1 lakh of sales tax is computed and is not from the case.
  • ‘Infructuous’ means the question no longer needs an answer; ‘academic’ is used by the reports in the same sense.
  • The State is spelled Orissa in the statute and the older case title and Odisha in the 2026 reports.
What this piece could not establish
  • The text of the judgement and of Section 5A: neither was read, so every statement of law and every rate is as reported by secondary sources.
  • The date Section 5A was enacted: the case page says it was inserted on 15 November 1997, yet the appeal was filed on 11 February 1994 and the reference was made in 1999, so the 1997 date cannot be the whole history; LiveLaw says only that it was introduced in 1997 and another report says the provision dates from the 1947 Act. Not resolved.
  • Whether a 15% band for turnover above Rs 1 crore applied: two sources say so, three do not mention it.
  • Whether the Court found there was no conflict between the earlier judgements (the Supreme Court Observer judgement summary, CourtBook) or disposed of the reference because it was academic (all reports): the two statements are both reported and may be two limbs of one reasoning.
  • The number of connected appeals, the States concerned and the money in dispute.
  • The 30 November 2026 hearing: not found in any source; the case was decided on 4 November.
  • Whether Hoechst Pharmaceuticals and India Cement are cited by the reports as three-judge and seven-judge decisions respectively with the year of each: the years were not stated in the text read, so none is given here.

Green Hydrogen · State Clusters · Pipeline vs. Built
India's Green Hydrogen Mission has commissioned 8,000 tonnes a year against a 5-million-tonne target. The 126-project pipeline behind that gap tells you where the real bets are being placed.

A September 2024 project registry compiled by the Indo-German Green Hydrogen Task Force names 126 production projects, 21 electrolyser/fuel-cell manufacturing plants, and 40 other related projects across India. Almost none of it is built yet — but the geography of where it's being announced is itself the story.

Roughly a third of the registry's 126 rows carry no capacity or tonnage figure at all, so these are project-count rankings, not true installed or planned capacity totals — the real gap between announced states is almost certainly wider than this table shows.

Mangaluru, Karnataka: the tightest cluster in the country

No other Indian city hosts as many named green-hydrogen or green-ammonia projects in one place as Mangaluru: ACME, ReNew, Avaada Energy, Petronas, JSW, and O2Power all have projects sited there, alongside Mangalore Refinery and Petrochemicals' own 3.5 MW refinery-hydrogen unit. Most are ammonia-export plays rather than domestic-blending projects — a coastal port city with existing refinery and export infrastructure functioning as a natural staging ground for shipping green ammonia out rather than consuming hydrogen locally.

Odisha's approval rounds — a bigger, messier picture than one headline number

Odisha's state government approved green hydrogen and derivative projects in at least two separate, overlapping tranches reported months apart: one widely cited round of 9 projects worth ₹1.53 lakh crore (covering, among others, ACME's Kujanga/Jagatsinghpur ammonia project), and a second, distinct round of 12 projects worth ₹84,918 crore reported separately (covering ReNew's Malkangiri methanol project and REFPL's Rayagada methanol project). The registry's source citations for these rows don't fully reconcile which project belongs to which approval batch — a reminder that state government "project approved" announcements in this sector often stack on top of each other rather than replacing one another, and a single "₹1.53 lakh crore" headline number undercounts the state's actual pipeline.

Of the ten largest named projects in the registry, only one — ACME's Thoothukudi plant in Tamil Nadu — was marked commissioned as of September 2024. Every other top-10 project remains at the announcement or pre-construction stage. That pattern matches the national numbers: 8,000 tonnes/year actually commissioned against a 5-million-tonne 2030 target, even as the SIGHT scheme has signed manufacturing-capacity agreements with 15 electrolyser companies (3,000 MW of manufacturing capacity, backed by ₹4,440 crore of incentives) and production agreements with 18 companies for a combined 862,000 tonnes/year — agreements, again, not yet delivered tonnage. Mission funding itself has ramped slowly: ₹100 crore in FY24, ₹300 crore in FY25, ₹300 crore in FY26.

Reading the map correctly: this registry is a snapshot of announcements and MoUs as of September 2024, not a survey of built infrastructure — by the time you're reading this in 2026, some of these projects will have been commissioned, resized, or quietly dropped (one Ladakh pilot in the same registry is already marked "Annulled"). What the map still tells you reliably is where developers and state governments are choosing to place their bets: Gujarat's broad industrial-corridor spread, Odisha's aggressive multi-round approvals, and Karnataka's Mangaluru cluster are the three clearest signals in a pipeline that is, so far, mostly still on paper.

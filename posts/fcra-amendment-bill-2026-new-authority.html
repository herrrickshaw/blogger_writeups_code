Top line

  • 1 year would be the maximum jail term for violating the Act under the Bill, down from 5 years; a Designated Authority would take over assets of organisations whose FCRA certificate ends.
  • No appeal is provided in the Act or the Bill when the government refuses to renew a certificate, and no hearing before refusal, according to PRS.
  • 19 November is the date of the next Joint Committee sitting, with the Home and Law ministries; the committee heard stakeholders including Oxfam International and a law-firm chairman on 12 November.

What it means: The lower jail term and the new asset provisions cut in different directions, since an organisation that stops renewing loses assets that foreign funds paid for, even if it breaks no rule.

What to do: The Home Ministry should state how many organisations would have assets vested under the Bill in the last five years of certificate cancellations and non-renewals.

PRS flags four issues: asset loss on exit, unequal treatment, no appeal and no hearing

  1. Loss of assets on cessationAn organisation that has not renewed would see assets created from past foreign funds vested in the Designated Authority.
  2. No clean exitAn organisation cannot leave the FCRA framework without losing assets built with foreign funds, so it has to keep renewing, and the Rules add spending obligations.
  3. Different routesThe Bill may treat assets created by certificate holders differently from those created through the prior-permission route.
  4. No appeal or hearingNeither the Act nor the Bill provides an appeal when renewal is denied, or a hearing before denial.

What it means: Each of the four points concerns what happens to an organisation after a decision by the government, and in each the Bill supplies a consequence without supplying a remedy.

What to do: The Joint Committee should ask the Home Ministry to table the renewal-refusal numbers and the stated reasons for them, since the Bill’s effect depends on how often renewal is denied.

The Bill has been with a joint committee for 99 days; it sits again on 19 November

What it means: A committee that has met stakeholders on 12 November and the ministries on 19 November is at the stage of taking evidence, and its report date was not found.

What to do: The committee should publish the written submissions it receives, so that the organisations the Bill affects can see what has been argued.

What would fix this

  1. Ministry of Home Affairs: publish yearly counts of FCRA certificates cancelled, surrendered, not renewed and denied, with the assets and funds involved.
  2. Joint Committee: publish stakeholder submissions and the ministry replies.
  3. Parliament: consider an appeal and a hearing before denial of renewal, as PRS notes neither exists.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

Method and definitions

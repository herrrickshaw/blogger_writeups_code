Digital Bharat Nidhi: 61.5% of the BharatNet budget spent in 2023-24 and 2024-25

Skip to article content
Text Size

Top line

  • Rs 40,635 crore had been disbursed from the fund for BharatNet phases I and II by 28 February 2026, and the committee found that operational gram panchayats remain below universal coverage; four states account for 44.9% of the money (Annexure III, computed).
  • 61.5% of the BharatNet budget allotment was spent in each of 2023-24 and 2024-25 (Rs 3,075.54 crore of Rs 5,000 crore, then Rs 3,995.01 crore of Rs 6,500 crore), against 100% in 2022-23.
  • 60.7% of 4G saturation towers were commissioned by 28 February 2026 while 30.8% of the project cost had been disbursed; the project is due in December 2026.

How the fund is financed and spent

  1. LevyLicensees pay a Universal Access Levy of 5% of Adjusted Gross Revenue (report, para 5).
  2. Consolidated FundThe levy is credited to the Consolidated Fund of India, so the fund does not hold the cash itself.
  3. AppropriationMoney reaches Digital Bharat Nidhi only through a budget provision that Parliament appropriates; the fund is non-lapsable.
  4. SchemesBharatNet fibre to gram panchayats, 4G saturation, mobile services in left-wing extremism, aspirational-district, border, island and north-eastern areas, and research and pilots.
  5. Name and scopeThe Telecommunications Act, 2023 widened the former Universal Service Obligation Fund to urban areas and research; the Rules of 30 August 2024 renamed it Digital Bharat Nidhi.

Where the report’s own figures differ
  • Operational panchayats: the report gives 78,899 gram panchayats with operational points of presence, said to include 40,610 under phase I, 38,289 under phase II and 2,884 under the Amended BharatNet Programme; 40,610 plus 38,289 is 78,899, so the third term is either not included or the total is stated without it (computed sum of all three: 81,783).
  • Project costs: the border outpost scheme costs Rs 737 crore in Annexure I and Rs 1,546 crore in the status table; Lakshadweep Rs 63 crore and Rs 36 crore; the Andaman and Nicobar project Rs 110 crore and Rs 130 crore.
  • 4G saturation cost: the Cabinet approved Rs 26,316 crore excluding taxes on 27 July 2022, and the report tables show Rs 30,620 crore; the Rs 4,304 crore difference is not explained in the passages read (taxes are the likely cause but the report does not say so).
  • Mobile project totals: the status table covers Rs 42,467 crore of projects and Annexure I Rs 40,009 crore, as the two lists are not the same projects.
  • Phase I coverage: the scope was raised to 1,20,210 gram panchayats, of which 40,610 are operational (33.8%, computed); the report also says over one lakh were completed by December 2017, so ‘completed’ and ‘operational’ mean different things.

What would fix this

  1. Department of Telecommunications: publish annual levy collections, budget allotment, appropriation and actual spending of the fund in one table, so that collections can be read against disbursals.
  2. Digital Bharat Nidhi administration: publish for every BharatNet phase and state the panchayats connected, lit and in use next to the money disbursed.
  3. Department of Telecommunications: reconcile the project costs, tower counts and operational panchayat totals in the report, and publish one dated project table.
  4. Department of Telecommunications: publish liquidated damages by scheme, operator and days of delay, with the reason for any waiver.
  5. Ministry of Communications: table the action-taken reply to the Committee’s seven highlighted recommendations with a date for each.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

Method and definitions
  • The report (105 pages, presented to Lok Sabha on 6 August 2026 and signed on 5 August 2026) and its two-page press release were read in full as PDFs from sansad.in; paragraph and annexure numbers are cited beside each figure.
  • Expenditure figures are in Rs crore and are as on 28 February 2026 unless stated. ‘Disbursed’ is the report’s word for money released from the fund.
  • Shares, sums, multiples and the per-panchayat figure are computed here; none is published in the report, except 60.7%, 69.6% and 41.7% tower completion and Rs 40,635 crore, which the report states and the build re-derives.
  • The Telangana figures of Rs 3,189 crore and 12,769 panchayats are from the PIB release of 6 November 2026, read in full.
  • The recommendation numbers are those printed in the press release; the report contains more recommendations than the seven in the release.
What this piece could not establish
  • Collections against disbursements: the report states the levy rate (5% of Adjusted Gross Revenue) and gives disbursals, but no table of annual levy collections or of the fund balance appeared in the text read, so the fund’s collections cannot be set against what was disbursed from this report alone.
  • Scheme-wise outlay and spend for every scheme: spend figures are given for BharatNet by state and by year and for eleven mobile projects; the outlay for the Amended BharatNet Programme (Rs 1.39 lakh crore, approved 4 August 2023) has no matching spend because the report gives none for it.
  • How many of the 78,899 operational panchayats are in use: the report gives usage figures for some projects, but no figure for connected panchayats with active traffic.
  • The cause of the 38.5% shortfall in BharatNet spend in 2023-24 and 2024-25: no reason is given in the passages read.
  • Whether the Rs 3,189 crore for Telangana is a ceiling or a commitment, and how much of it falls in each year: the PIB release says financial support is approved.
  • The Department’s reply to the Committee: an action-taken report had not been located at the time of analysis.

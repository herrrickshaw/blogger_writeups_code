India's crude basket: $120 in October, $89 on 10 November; pump prices frozen

Skip to article content
Text Size

Top line

  • $89.29 a barrel was the Indian basket on 10 November, $34 (28%) below $123.72 on 26 October; October averaged $120 to $121.85, the highest since March 2012, and Brent fell about 21% from its $118 high on 30 October to $92.84 on 11 November.
  • $90.6 billion was India’s crude import bill in April–September, up 46% on volumes that were flat at about 122–123 million tonnes, so the whole $28.7 billion rise is price.
  • Rs 12 and Rs 27 a litre are Jefferies’ estimates of marketing losses on petrol and diesel on a $97 basket (Rs 22 and Rs 28 in October); Delhi pump prices have not changed since May.

What to do: Refiners and the petroleum ministry should plan on a range rather than the 11 November level, since the sources give both a supply glut and a closed strait as live explanations.

What to do: The petroleum ministry should publish the under-recovery on petrol, diesel and LPG each fortnight with the basket price used, so the Jefferies and ICRA numbers can be compared with an official one.

What would fix this

  1. PPAC: publish the daily Indian basket, the monthly average and the days it covers in one table, with the Brent weighting used.
  2. Ministry of Commerce and Industry: publish crude import volume, price and the term-spot split each month so the lag between the basket and the import bill is visible.
  3. Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas: publish fortnightly under-recoveries on petrol, diesel and LPG, with the basket price behind them.
  4. Ministry of Finance and the Ministry of Petroleum: state how the March excise cut will be treated as OMC margins recover, since it was introduced to compensate losses.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

Method and definitions
  • The Indian basket is the PPAC series of the crude oil India buys, weighted between sour and sweet grades; this blog’s earlier piece on the basket explains that Brent has a 61% weight in it. Basket values here are as quoted in press reports of the PPAC series; PPAC’s own table was not read.
  • Brent values are futures settlements as reported by Indoen Research, except the 30 October high and the 13 November intraday readings.
  • The import-volume conversion (7.33 barrels a tonne) is an assumed factor; the per-barrel sensitivity is gross and does not net product exports, which is one reason the quoted sensitivities differ.
  • Marketing loss is the gap between the cost-based price and the frozen retail price before taxes and dealer margins as each analyst defines it; Jefferies and ICRA do not use the same basis.
What this piece could not establish
  • PPAC’s own daily basket was not read; the $89.29 for 10 November is a Business Standard figure attributed to PPAC, and two other sources put the basket near $96–97 for 11 November and the 15-day average.
  • Why the pump prices have not changed since May, and whether the Rs 10 excise cut will be reversed: the sources describe the freeze and the cut, not the policy intent beyond compensation.
  • A Mumbai diesel price site reports no change since 5 September; Delhi sources report none since 25 May. The two are not reconciled here and may reflect state-level changes.
  • The Brent rebound after 11 November rests on intraday readings reported on 13 November; later settlements were not read.
  • The petrol and diesel order of ICRA’s October figures is reversed against Jefferies’; the difference is not explained in the reports.

Why it is useful

  • Who benefits: policy researchers, journalists, civic-tech teams and students who want to see which subjects and ministries draw parliamentary scrutiny.
  • What it replaces: searching the Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha sites session by session and merging two different party and ministry naming systems.
  • Evidence from the run: the party table sums exactly to the 59,381 questions, and one SQL query ranks the ministries.
  • The repo also computes a 'lift' score for each party-and-ministry pair (how much more a party asks about a ministry than average), a quick way to see signature interests.

What it is for

The repo pulls every question since the 18th Lok Sabha convened in June 2024 from the public Parliament sources, matches each to the asking member's party and state, and aggregates by party, ministry, state and topic. It also compares scrutiny with what ministries publish through the Press Information Bureau.

This post shows aggregates only. It reports no individual MPs' records. Not political or legal advice; counts of questions say nothing about the merit of the questions.

Who it is for

  • Policy and government-affairs researchers
  • Journalists and civic-tech volunteers
  • Students of Indian parliamentary practice
Not for.
  • Judging a party or an MP on question counts alone; seat numbers and sitting time differ
  • Reading the answers; the data has questions and metadata, not the replies
  • Anything needing the question text from the Lok Sabha (only subject and ministry are in the data)

Install

  1. Clone the repository (needs git)
    git clone https://github.com/herrrickshaw/lok-rajya-sabha-qa.git
cd lok-rajya-sabha-qa
  2. Create a virtual environment and install DuckDB
    python3 -m venv .venv && source .venv/bin/activate && pip install duckdb

Quick start: commands and what you should see

Rank parties by questions asked

python3 - <<'E'
import duckdb
c=duckdb.connect('data/pq_ledger.duckdb',read_only=True)
for r in c.sql('select * from party_summary order by total_questions desc limit 6').fetchall(): print(r)
E

BJP 22,881, INC 11,184, DMK 3,537, AITC 2,544, SP 2,331, TDP 2,178, each with Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha splits.

Rank ministries by questions received

python3 - <<'E'
import duckdb
c=duckdb.connect('data/pq_ledger.duckdb',read_only=True)
print(c.sql('select * from ministry_summary order by questions desc limit 6').fetchall())
E

Health and Family Welfare 3,550, Railways 2,900, Education 2,802, Agriculture and Farmers Welfare 2,754, Finance 2,253, Civil Aviation 2,129.

Limits and honest caveats

  • Question counts are not a measure of an MP's or a party's quality; they depend on seats, sessions attended and rules on notices.
  • Lok Sabha data holds subject and ministry only; the question text and every answer are in PDFs not fetched here. Rajya Sabha answers are null.
  • The sources are undocumented APIs; party and state names from the two houses were reconciled by alias tables and a table of unmatched members exists.
  • Some scripts need external inputs (a PIB index and the sibling yellow-pages repo's minister directory) that are not in the repo, so a full rebuild is not possible from a fresh clone.
  • I ran only SQL on the committed database; none of the build, comparison or network scripts were run.
  • The repo links to a minister directory; this post uses no personal data and shows no individual MPs.

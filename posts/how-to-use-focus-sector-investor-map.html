Why it is useful

  • Who benefits: policy analysts, investment-promotion teams and researchers who need to know which companies, in which countries, belong to each focus sector without building the sector classification themselves.
  • What it replaces: hand-matching a stock universe to sector names, and hunting through press releases to find which foreign company sits behind which incentive scheme.
  • Evidence from the run: the country matrix sums to exactly 19,795 companies across 107 countries; Electronics & Semiconductors is the largest sector (3,449 companies, 52 countries) and Shipbuilding & Marine the smallest focus sector (359).
  • The pairing file keeps its negative findings (a stalled steel JV, a lapsed battery award, a shrinking beneficiary list), so it is a safer starting point than a list of announcements.

What it is for

The repository joins India's trade-policy focus sectors to the global listed-company universe. You get a coverage layer (every company classified into the twelve sectors), a screened set of liquid names, a shortlist checked for profitability, and a file that pairs foreign investors with the Indian initiative that is their vehicle.

It is a research starting point, not a recommendation. Nothing in the files tells you to buy or sell anything.

Not investment advice.

Who it is for

  • Researchers and students of Indian industrial policy
  • Investment-promotion and business-development teams looking at which countries to court
  • Analysts who want a sector-tagged global company list to filter further
Not for.
  • Investment decisions; profitability is trailing and from a free data source, not audited
  • Rebuilding the data from scratch without access to the author's local market-data files (the build scripts read private local inputs)
  • Treating 'prospective' pairings as announced deals

Quick start: commands and what you should see

Count companies per focus sector and country from the committed matrix

python3 -I - <<'E'
import csv,collections
tot=collections.Counter();cn=collections.defaultdict(dict)
for r in csv.DictReader(open('data/focus_sector_country_matrix.csv')):
    tot[r['FocusSector']]+=int(r['Companies']);cn[r['FocusSector']][r['Country']]=int(r['Companies'])
for s,t in tot.most_common():
    top=sorted(cn[s].items(),key=lambda x:-x[1])[:3]
    print(s,t,len(cn[s]),top)
E

Twelve lines, one per sector, with the company total, the number of countries and the top three countries. The totals add up to 19,795.

List the investor-to-scheme pairings

python3 -I - <<'E'
import json
d=json.load(open('data/foreign_investor_initiative_pairs.json'))
for k in ('verified_pairs','prospective_pairs'):
    for x in d[k]: print(k[:4],x['company'],'|',x['sector'],'|',x['status'])
E

19 lines: 12 verified and 7 prospective pairings with their status.

Limits and honest caveats

  • Not investment advice. Profitability and growth fields come from trailing, unaudited free data; UNVERIFIED does not mean unprofitable.
  • The ticker-to-country join is deliberately conservative: of the 38,495-name screening universe only about 2,217 were joined; unmatched names are dropped, not guessed.
  • The build scripts (build_screen.py and others) read local parquet files from the author's market-data stack that are not in the repo, so the screen cannot be rebuilt from a fresh clone. I did not run them; only the committed outputs were read.
  • 'Expansion' is a stated proxy (growth thresholds), not evidence of intent. Intent is evidenced only in the pairing file.
  • Found in the data: Medical Devices has 7 foreign names and 0 India names in the final list, so that sector looks one-sided; check why before relying on it.
  • Pairings were retrieved on 2026-07-20 and policy status changes quickly.

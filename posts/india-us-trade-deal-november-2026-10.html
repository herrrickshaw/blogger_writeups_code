India-US trade deal, November 2026: 10% duty in force, agreement text unsigned

Skip to article content
Text Size

Top line

  • 10% Section 301 duty applies to goods of India since 24 July 2026, on top of the normal duty; USTR proposed 12.5% in June. About 55% of India’s exports to the US pay it and about 45% do not (a PIB estimate).
  • 0 signed texts of an India-US interim agreement. The 6 February framework’s 18% rate was never applied, and on 20 February the Supreme Court held that the emergency-powers law behind it does not authorise tariffs.
  • 16 economies, India among them, are in a second Section 301 inquiry, on excess capacity, with no result; trade-press reports say India wants it before settling tariffs. The G20 summit in Miami, 14–15 December, is the next likely date.

What it means: With the duty covering about 55% of exports, on the ELP figure of about $87 billion of goods exports that is roughly $48 billion a year of trade paying an extra 10%.

What to do: The Commerce Ministry should publish the exempted share by HS chapter and value, since the 45% is an estimate and exporters cannot see which lines it covers.

What it means: Because India’s 10% is added to the normal rate while the EU’s and Taiwan’s totals are capped at 10%, an Indian exporter pays at least as much in total as an EU or Taiwanese competitor on every line, and more wherever the normal duty is above zero.

What to do: Exporters should compare total landed duty by product line against EU, Taiwanese and Japanese rivals, not the headline tier, because the headline is not the competitive measure.

The 18% rate announced in February never applied; three legal bases replaced it

  1. 6 Feb 2026India and the US announce a framework for an interim agreement with an 18% US rate; the extra 25% Russian-oil tariff is removed from 7 February (ATB Legal).
  2. 20 FebThe US Supreme Court holds, 6 to 3, that IEEPA does not authorise tariffs; the reciprocal tariff behind the 18% ends.
  3. 24 FebA 10% temporary surcharge under Section 122 starts for 150 days (MEA reply to the Lok Sabha, 27 March).
  4. 17 MarUSTR opens a second Section 301 inquiry, into excess capacity, covering 16 economies including India (91 FR 12886).
  5. 24 JulThe Section 301 forced-labour duty of 10% on India takes effect as the Section 122 surcharge expires (PIB; USTR).

What it means: The 18% was tied to a statute the Court then excluded, so what India pays today is a different duty under a different law with no signed agreement beneath it.

What to do: The US and Indian governments should publish the framework text and the legal basis intended for the agreed rate, since the February joint statement named a rate but not an authority.

Four items are open: the text, the excess-capacity result, the Russia-oil law and textiles

  1. PendingInterim agreement text: not published; officials describe a last stretch, and the US has promised India a comparative tariff advantage with safeguards against extra duties (PTI, 14 November).
  2. PendingResult of the excess-capacity Section 301 inquiry into 16 economies; trade-press reports say India wants it before settling tariff terms.
  3. Oct to DecThe Sanctioning Russia and Iran Act allows duties of up to 100% on the largest buyers of Russian oil from 18 October unless waived; a 60-day waiver was issued in October (Hindu BusinessLine, 12 November).
  4. PendingTextile-specific mechanism referenced in the July measures: not yet set up, with India not among the economies contemplated (PIB; ELP).
  5. 14–15 DecG20 summit in Miami; a Modi-Trump meeting is likely and an announcement is speculated, not confirmed (PTI; CNBC).

What it means: India wants protection from the sanctions law and clarity on the excess-capacity result before it settles tariff terms, and the US side still has to finish its own tariff framework, so the order of those decisions sets the timing more than the December meeting does.

What to do: The Commerce Ministry should state which of the four items it treats as conditions for signing, so exporters can plan around the likely order.

What would fix this

  1. Ministry of Commerce and Industry: publish the share of exports outside the 10% duty by HS chapter, with the annexes of the Federal Register notice that define it.
  2. Ministry of Commerce and Industry and USTR: publish the framework text, the legal authority intended for the agreed rate, and the safeguards promised against additional duties.
  3. Ministry of Commerce and Industry: publish a list of conditions for signing, naming the excess-capacity result, the sanctions-law protection and the textile mechanism.
  4. Export promotion councils: publish landed-duty comparisons against EU, Taiwanese and Japanese suppliers by product line.

These are proposals from this blog, not recommendations any agency has adopted.

Method and definitions
  • “Section 301 duty” is the additional duty imposed on 24 July 2026 after USTR’s forced-labour investigations of about 60 economies; it is charged in addition to the ordinary (MFN) duty for India. EU, Taiwan, Japan, South Korea and Switzerland are capped on a total basis.
  • The 45% and 55% are the Ministry’s estimate of the share of exports to the US outside and inside the duty, as published by PIB on 25 July; the dollar value of the 55% multiplies a secondary-source export total by that share.
  • The stacking chart is an illustration with an assumed 5% normal duty; the rule for each tier is USTR’s, and real rates depend on the product line.
  • The dates of the Supreme Court judgment, Section 122 surcharge and the Section 301 notice are from official documents (CRS, the MEA reply and the Federal Register); the framework’s 18% and the 25% Russian-oil removal are from a law-firm summary.
What this piece could not establish
  • The framework text of 6 February: it was not read; the 18% rate and other terms are from a law-firm summary and Indian parliamentary replies.
  • Whether Vietnam pays 10% or 12.5%: USTR’s own list of the 10% tier does not name it, while a third-party tracker lists it at 10%; this piece does not rely on it.
  • The waiver’s end date under the Sanctioning Russia and Iran Act: Hindu BusinessLine reports a 60-day waiver issued in October without a date, and it could not be confirmed here.
  • The result and timing of the excess-capacity inquiry, and the effect of the Democratic win in both houses of Congress on 3 November: reports say the executive keeps Section 232 and 301 tools, not that the timing is changed.
  • Refunds: IEEPA and Section 122 refunds go to the importer of record, not the Indian exporter, and their status was not tracked here.
  • Whether a deal will be announced at the G20: reports say it is likely or speculated; no source confirms it.

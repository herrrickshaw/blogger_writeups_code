Why it is useful

  • Economists, journalists and students who need the commodity leg of India's trade gap get 8 years of chapter-level exports and imports in two small JSON files, with no portal scraping.
  • It replaces manual TRADESTAT downloads. The 4-digit file splits bullion out of chapter 71, which the 2-digit data cannot do (gold 7108 and silver 7106 separately).
  • Evidence from the run: the totals reproduce the README headline (deficit widening from -$184.0 bn to -$334.3 bn), and a one-screen script ranks chapters by deficit: mineral fuels, then precious stones and metals, then electrical machinery.
  • A useful catch: silver imports rose from $4.8 bn to $12.1 bn (+150%) in one year, while platinum-group imports fell 92%, which looks like trade moving between customs lines, not demand disappearing.

What it is for

The repo isolates the commodity part of India's merchandise trade deficit using DGCI&S TRADESTAT figures (US$ million). data/ holds the chapter-level export/import file and the 4-digit bare-metal file with country breakdowns, plus a validation file on listed metal recyclers. blog/ holds a finished HTML post on the recycling counter-force.

This post uses only the two TRADESTAT files, read with the Python standard library.

Who it is for

  • Trade and macro analysts
  • Journalists writing on gold, silver and copper imports
  • Students looking for a clean, citable trade dataset
  • Researchers testing the recycling-substitution argument
Not for.
  • Trading or investment decisions: this is not investment advice
  • Real-time monitoring: the data is annual and FY2025-26 is provisional
  • Product-level (HS8) analysis: the files stop at chapter and selected 4-digit codes

Quick start: commands and what you should see

Print the trade balance by fiscal year

python3 - <<'E'
import json
d=json.load(open('data/tradestat_hsn_export_import_2018-19_to_2025-26.json'))
t=d['totals']
for y in t['import']:
    print(y, round(t['import'][y]/1e3,1), round(t['export'][y]/1e3,1), round((t['export'][y]-t['import'][y])/1e3,1))
E

Eight rows of year, imports, exports and balance in US$ billion.

Rank chapters by FY2025-26 trade balance

python3 - <<'E'
import json
d=json.load(open('data/tradestat_hsn_export_import_2018-19_to_2025-26.json'))
i,e=d['by_chapter']['import'],d['by_chapter']['export']
y='2025 - 2026'
r=sorted((e.get(c,{}).get(y,0)-v[y],c,v['name'][:40]) for c,v in i.items())
for b,c,n in r[:8]: print(c,n,round(b/1e3,1))
E

The eight chapters with the largest deficits; see sample outputs.

Sample prompts for your AI assistant

Copy, paste and edit the parts in [brackets].

  • Load data/tradestat_hsn_export_import_2018-19_to_2025-26.json and chart the trade balance by year, with chapters 27 and 71 shown separately.
  • Which chapters contributed most to the rise in imports between FY2024-25 and FY2025-26? Show a table and explain the top three.
  • From the 4-digit metals file, show gold imports by partner country for both years and flag the biggest changes.
  • Compare silver (7106) and platinum group metals (7110) import values across the two years and suggest, with caveats, why they moved in opposite directions.
  • Read the README and tell me which numbers in the headline table I can reproduce from the JSON files and which I cannot.

Limits and honest caveats

  • Not investment advice. Annual trade statistics only.
  • FY2025-26 is provisional (source last updated 19 May 2026 per the file); the data was retrieved 18 July 2026 and 3 August 2026, so later revisions are not included.
  • Commodity share depends on the definition. The README's 48% is oil plus chapter 71 plus metal chapters; my simpler chapter 27 plus 71 figure is 40.3%, so state your definition.
  • The metals file covers 14 selected 4-digit codes, not all of HS; 'diamonds' (7102) sits there only for chapter-71 context.
  • Partner-country values are as served by TRADESTAT; re-exports and misclassification can shift flows between codes, which is what the platinum-to-gold shift may reflect.
  • I did not run or validate the metal-recycler business-model file; it contains company financial claims that need their own check.

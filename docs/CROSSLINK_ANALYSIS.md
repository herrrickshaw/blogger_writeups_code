## Headline numbers

- **264 live posts** considered (files in `posts/` that also have a manifest entry — matches the rule `index_graph.py` already uses).
- **734 directed cross-link edges** across the whole corpus — 2.78 outbound links per post on average, but this average is misleading: it's carried almost entirely by posts published since the "Related on this blog" convention started.
- **Only 121 of 275 post files (44%) contain the phrase "Related on this blog" at all.** The convention is real but not retrofitted onto the older majority of the archive.
- **94 posts (36%) have zero outbound content links** — no Related-on-this-blog section, no inline link to another post. One of those, `indias-oil-psus-say-they-clear-their_01269248608`, is a true orphan: nothing links to it either.

The highest-scoring ~30 are a small, specific, genuinely actionable set.

## What this means

The blog's cross-linking is a **recent-post phenomenon, not a site-wide practice**. Every post from the last few weeks' pipeline/gas series links tightly to its siblings (see the fertiliser/CBG/ethanol hub cluster below), but the majority of the ~250-post back catalogue was published before "Related on this blog" existed as a convention and has never been retrofitted. That's a real SEO and reader-retention gap: 93 posts that other posts already point readers toward have no return link sending that reader anywhere else on the site.

## Method notes / limitations

- Candidate scoring uses only the taxonomy labels already assigned in `meta/manifest.json` (the same labels used for topic hubs), not full-text entity matching against `index_graph.py`'s geography/entity extraction — that's the natural next pass if the label signal alone under- or over-recommends.
- Labels carried by more than 40 posts are excluded from candidate scoring entirely (too generic to mean two posts are actually related); labels carried by fewer than 3 posts are excluded too (not enough of a cluster to call it a pattern).
- A pair is only counted as "linked" if one links to the other in either direction — the analysis doesn't currently distinguish a one-way link from a reciprocal one, which would be a reasonable follow-up refinement.
